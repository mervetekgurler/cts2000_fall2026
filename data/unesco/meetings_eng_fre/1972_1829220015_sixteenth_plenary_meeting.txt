Sixteenth plenary meeting
21 October 1970 at 10.20 a.m,  President: Mr, DELL'ORO MAINI (Argentina)  Seizième séance plénière
26 octobre 1972, à 10 h. 15  Président : M, HAGUIWARA (Japon)  Décimosexta sesiôn plenaria
1. Le PRESIDENT, -  Mesdames, Messieurs, je déclare ouverte la seizième séance plénière, Nous allons  poursuivre le débat de politique générale, et je donne la parole à S, Exc, M. Leuam Insi-  sienmay, chef de la délégation du Laos.
2,1 M. INSISIENMAY (Laos). -  Monsieur le Président, je voudrais d'abord, comme mes collègues qui m' ont précédé  à cette tribune, vous féliciter de votre élection à la présidence de la présente session,  Etant donné la manière dont vous dirigez les débats, la Conférence générale peut être cer-  taine qu'elle s'est donné un guide digne d'elle, En tant que représentant d'un pays asiatique,  je ne puis m'empêcher d'exprimer ici notre fierté en même temps que notre admiration.
2.2 La délégation du Laos profite également de cette occasion pour adresser ses chaleu-  reuses félicitations à la délégation de la République populaire de Chine pour sa triomphale  entrée dans le concert des nations. Nous nous réjouissons que l'universalité de notre Orga-  nisation soit encore renforcée par l'apport de la civilisation et de la culture chinoises plu-  sieurs fois millénaires. Nous nous réjouissons également de voir entrer dans cette Maison  de la culture et de la paix des pays tels que le Bangladesh, Bahrein, les Emirats arabes
16  unis, Oman et Qatar. Nous tenons à adresser aux délégations de ces nouveaux Etats membres  nos plus chaleureuses félicitations,
2.3 Enfin, Monsieur le Président, je vous remercie d'avoir bien voulu m'autoriser a sou-  mettre à cette assemblée les vues de mon pays, le Laos, sur les activités présentes et fu-  tures de 1'Unesco,
2,4 Je me réjouis sincèrement de constater qu'au cours de ces dernières années l'Unesco  a beaucoup fait pour l'expansion de l'éducation dans le monde, et particulièrement dans les  pays en voie de développement, Mon pays, comme beaucoup d'autres, a bénéficié des pro-  grammes de formation et de perfectionnement à l'étranger mis en oeuvre par l'Organisa-  l'on, ainsi que des services d'experts dans le domaine de la planification de l'éducation et  de l'alphabétisation fonctionnelle. Après avoir bénéficié d'une aide du Programme des Na-  tions Unies pour le développement (PNUD) et de l'Unesco pour la formation de moniteurs  de l'enseignement technique et professionnel, qui constituent actuellement le premier noyau  du corps enseignant national des lycées et collèges techniques, nous bénéficions maintenant  de leur aide pour la formation de professeurs de sciences et de mathématiques du second  degré. Voir notre Unité de développement de l'enseignement des sciences et des mathéma-  tiques figurer parmi les quarante-six grands projets assurés de l'aide internationale en
1973-1974 nous fait évidemment beaucoup de plaisir, En outre, nous sommes heureux de  déclarer que, en dépit des difficultés d'application inhérentes à la situation de guerre-im-  posée à notre pays, la réforme de notre éducation, élaborée il y a dix ans avec l'aide de  l'Unesco, est conforme dans ses grandes lignes aux orientations du rapport de la Commis-  sion internationale sur le développement de l'éducation, dont les principales recommanda-  tions, qui ont trait à la nécessité de reconsidérer globalement et en profondeur les poli-  tiques et pratiques éducatives, à la nécessité de mettre l'accent sur les stratégies natio-  nales, à l'éducation permanente et 4 la nécessité d'une éducation ouverte, souple et diver-  sifiée, seraient de nature à nous aider à faire face plus efficacement aux problèmes de  l'éducation de demain,
2.5 Pour ce qui est du Projet d'esquisse de plan à moyen terme et du Projet de programme  et de budget pour 1973-1974, nous avons pris connaissance avec un vif intérêt de l'énorme  travail accompli par le Secrétariat de l'Unesco et son Directeur général. Il va sans dire  que nous accueillons favorablement les grandes orientations proposées car, comme le Di-  recteur général, nous pensons que les services consultatifs mis à la disposition des Etats  membres pour les aider à élaborer leur politique nationale de l'éducation, l'intégration de  l'action intellectuelle et de l'action opérationnelle et l'accentuation des efforts tendant àla  rénovation de l'éducation renforceraient l'armature morale et intellectuelle de l'Unesco et  donneraient plus de poids et d'efficacité à son action,
2.6 Cependant, il faudrait, dans la pratique, se garder de multiplier le nombre d'organes  exécutifs et de projets, et ce, pour ne pas gaspiller les ressources budgétaires dont l'aug-  mentation proposée tant pour l'exercice 1973-1974 que pour les deux suivants nous inquiète  vivement. En effet, un pays comme le Laos, dont les disponibilités, particulièrement les  disponibilités en devises étrangères, sont plus que limitées, ne saurait supporter sans  ris que de déséquilibre pour son propre budget une augmentation de 23 % de sa contribution,  même en tenant compte des effets de l'inflation et des modifications survenues dans le cours  des changes de certaines monnaies principales, Il est urgent, je le répète encore une fois,  de freiner sérieusement l'augmentation du budget de l'Unesco ; pour ce faire, il faudrait  ralentir la croissance du Secrétariat, chercher à substituer, en ce qui concerne le person=  nel, la qualité à la quantité, tout en tenant compte de l'impératif d'une répartition géogra-  phique équitable, réduire les dépenses d'ordre administratif, diminuer le nombre des con-  férences et des réunions ainsi que le volume de documents,
2.7 Un soutien financier ne devrait être accordé aux centres ou instituts régionaux d'édu-  cation ou de recherche pédagogique que si ces instituts sont en mesure de servir efficace-  ment les pays de la région, Sauf dans des cas strictement limités - par exemple ceux de  l'Institut régional pour l'enseignement supérieur et le développement en Asie, à Singapour,
16  et de l'Institut national de recherches pédagogiques du Japon - toutes les activités devraient  être le plus rapidement possible assurées par les pays intéressés eux-mêmes, .
2.8 La tendance à créer des centres ou des organismes régionaux risque aussi de réduire  l'efficacité de l'Unesco, car il pourrait y avoir une trop grande dispersion des efforts ou  même des doubles emplois, Ce serait dans une certaine mesure le cas du Centre asien  d'innovation éducative en vue du développement, qui ferait partie du Bureau régional. pour  l'Asie, à Bangkok, et du Centre régional d'innovation et de technologie éducatives de 1'Or-  ganisation des ministres de l'Education des pays de l'Asie du Sud-Est, Je suis bien que tous  les pays de l'Asie du Sud-Est ne font pas partie de cette Organisation et que la création du  Centre asien a été recommandée par la Conférence régionale des ministres de l'Education  et des ministres chargés de la planification économique en Asie, réunie à Singapour, mais,  afin d'éviter des doubles emplois et des gaspillages de ressources et d'énergie, je demande  instamment qu'entre ces deux organismes s'instaurent des consultations permanentes et  une étroite co..aboration,
2.9 Monsieur le Président, la structure de l'Unesco vient d'être réformée d'une façon .  harmonieuse et équilibrée, Au Conseil exécutif, le système des groupes électoraux a déjà  donné de bons résultats, et le fait que la durée du mandat non renouvelable des membres  du Conseil ait été ramenée de six à quatre ans permettra à l'avenir une meilleure repré-  sentation des cultures et des langues, grâce à une rotation plus rapide, Nous souhaitons  que ces modifications de base dotent l'action de l'Organisation d'une efficacité supplémentaire,
2,10 Un autre point sur lequel j'aimerais attirer l'attention de cette assemblée concerne  les programmes scientifiques intergouvernementaux, dont l'utilité n'est plus à démontrer,  Un programme comme "L'homme et la biosphère'', pour ne citer que celui-là, mérite  d'être soutenu activement, car la pollution et l'environnement intéressent l'humanité tout  entière, C'est pourquoi nous avons décidé de nous y associer en créant un Comité national  à cet effet, Est-il besoin, cependant, de rappeler que les moyens exigés, en hommes et en  matériel, font gravement défaut aux pays en voie de développement, y compris le mien ?  Aussi serait-il nécessaire que l'Unesco et les pays développés aident ces pays à se doter  d'une infrastructure scientifique et à former le personnel nécessaire,
2,11 Voilà, Monsieur le Président, Messieurs les délégués, les questions qui nous pré-  occupent actuellement, Vous constaterez qu'elles ne sont pas uniquement d'ordre technique,  mais qu'elles ont des incidences économiques et sociales qui en accroissent à la fois l'in-  térêt et l'urgence,
2,12 Je terminerai en souhaitant que cette session de la Conférence générale, au succès  de laquelle nous coopérons de notre mieux, donne à toutes les délégations l'occasion d'amor-  cer en commun, et avec le concours des organismes centraux spécialisés, l'étude fruc-  tueusedes problèmes qui se posent à chacun de nous, Je vous remercie, Monsieur le Président,
3. Le PRESIDENT, - .  Je remercie M, Insisienmay, Je donne la parole à 5, Exc. M. Duma, chef de la délé-  gation de l'Albanie.

(4.1) Mr. DUMA (Albania) (Translation from the Russian):  The Director-General's report on the activities of the Organization for 1970-1971 deals with  a number of important problems of education, science and culture which are causing alarm to thc  States Members of our Organization. The delegation of the People's Republic of Albania proposes  to state its point of view on some of these problems.
(4.2) First of all, on behalf of the Albanian delegation I wish to express our great joy at the pre-  sence here of the People's Republic of China. The restoration to the Republic of its legal rights  in Unesco is an event of very great significance for our Organization. The completely unlawful  occupation of China's seat in Unesco for many years by the representative of Chiang Kai- Chek did  great harm to the Organization's work. The representation of China by its only legal government,  the Government of the People's Republic of China, has enhanced Unesco's authority and prestige.  We warmly welcome the representatives of the great Chinese nation, 700 million strong, and of  the Government of the People's Republic of China; and we are certain that they will make a very  great contribution to the fulfilment of our Organization's lofty aims.
(4.3) The Albanian delegation feels that a number of questions touched on in the recommendations  of the Director-General, Mr, René Maheu, and by the delegates of various countries in connexion
16  with the report of the International Commission on the Development of Education in the world, de-  serve very close scrutiny. It goes without saying that the problems of education, science and  culture are closely linked with those of freedom, emancipation, progress and the national indepen-  dence of each country. For these reasons our Organization has an extremely important duty to  see that they are correctly examined,
(4.4) Statistics on the development of education in the world put forward in various reports cause  quite understandable alarm, Today, throughout the world, an extremely high percentage of  school-age children have not received primary education. An equally high percentage of children  begin their primary education and do not finish it or give it up for various reasons, generally the  difficult economic position of their families. At the end of the primary education in many coun-  tries, schoolchildren have no opportunity to continue their education in secondary and higher  establishments. This means that there is an alarming backwardness in education and a fairly ob-  vious difference between what is said from the podium of our General Conference and the real si-  tuation in various countries. '
(4.5) We greet the recently-liberated countries of Africa and Asia and other developing countries  and express to them our great sympathy with their persevering efforts to develop the education,  science and culture which are an integral part of their struggle to safeguard their national sover-  eignty and national culture. The peoples of these countries are in no way to blame for the back-  wardness of their education and culture, This inherited backwardness is the consequence of long  and cruel colonial oppression, aggression and robbery by imperialists and colonizers. To emerge  from this an alarming situation, One suggestion has been made: that it would be valuable to intro-  duce a planned basis for education. Unesco has its own special Institute which has been and is  still working in this direction,
(4,6) Educational development planning was introduced in Albania immediately after the establish-  ment of the Popular Government: that is 28 years ago. We began to plan the development of «du-  cation in a country where the population was 80% illiterate, a situation not greatly different from  that of quite a large number of countries which have won their independence in recent decades.  Today in Albania, of slightly over two million inhabitants, 725,000 are studying in various schools.  This means that one person in every three is a student. The results of educational planning are  thus clearly visible.
(4.7) It goes without saying that educational planning cannot be a magic wand solving all problems.  Planning is impracticable outside the frameworks provided by the country's political, economic  and social structure, There exist in Albania today genuine conditions and opportunities for the  full, systematic and planned development of education. Over the last 30 years, we have developed  education along with the industrialization of the country and the modernization of its agriculture.  Education was a problem for the whole of our society. The State invested large sums in it, and  the people also made their valuable contribution. In many villages which had been reduced to  smouldering ruins by the Nazi fascist invadors, the first task to be shouldered by the peasants was  the building of schools, and only then did they build their own homes. Conditions were established  not only for the opening but also for the maintenance and development of schools. In this way  Albania eliminated illiteracy in just one decade. It democratized education on a really massive  scale.
(4.8) Particular attention was given in the Director-General's report to the "right to education"  about which many delegates have spoken here. No one can deny this right. However, this right,  acknowledged in words by the statesmen of many countries and enshrined in the constitutions of  many States, is often in fact repudiated. Data collected in Unesco surveys show that in Europe  only 10 to 12% of students in higher educational establishments come from the least well-to-do  classes, which make up 70 to 75% of the population. This means that in this instance the right  does not exist or is being infringed, since families from the poor sections of society are unable to  send their children to school because of unemployment or other economic or social factors.
(4.9) In Albania the right to education is put into practice as an integral part of those complete  rights and freedoms which the working masses gained through the victory of the people's revolution  and the establishment of the Popular Government. We are convinced from our own experience that  only political, economic and social equality of rights solves likewise the problem of the right to  education.
(4.10) It is frequently argued in many countries, to justify infraction of the right to education, that  to assist the most able and talented students first is in the interests of the nation itself and of pro-  gress in general. We feel, and our experience confirms, that it is first of all necessary to bring  all children to school and give them all an all-round development and education. Only then and on  €
16  that foundation would the greater attention be justified which the best deserve. To restrict educa-  tion to the "talented" and to exclude from school the broad mass of children means to pursue a  policy of selection which in fact turns out to be a policy of educational discrimination. Bitter ex-  perience and the actual situation in many countries, including countries regarded as "developed",  shows that those who suffer from this policy of selection are always the children from the pour  parts of the city, and more so of the countryside, The Albanian delegation feels that Unesco should  intervene resolutely and use all its authority to bring to an end practices which deprive millions of  children in various countries of the right to learn.
(4.11) In Albania, particularly in recent years, most attention in education has been concentrated  on qualitative improvements, on better training in political ideology, and on better cultural, scien-  tific, professional and technical training for the rising generation. This has not been neglected in  the preceding twenty years, but it is now given priority. The reason is that we have almost com-  pletely solved the problem of the full enrolment of children, young people and workers in school,  Compulsory eight-year education has been introduced in Albania, and approximately 70% of those  completing it continue their studies in the various schools which follow on. They generally finish  complete sec ‘ndary education, The proportion of the total population studying in higher educa-  tional establishments in Albania is larger than the Central European average; and it should not be  forgotten that our university is possibly the youngest in Europe, having recently celebrated its  fifteenth anniversary. In one year alone we train about 2, 500 graduate specialists, about six times  as many as in 1938. During the period of the fifth five-year plan (1971-1975) as many specialists  with secondary and higher education will be trained as during the whole 25-year period 1945 to 1970.  From what I have just said it is clear that there is a great forward impetus in the development of  education and the training of specialists in each faculty. It should be added here that all the pro-  fessors in our higher educational establishments, and likewise all the scientists in our scientific  institutes, are Albanian, We mention this because in 1945 we had neither our own university pro-  fessors nor our own scientists.
(4.12) In this task of raising the quality of schools, we shall strive to reach the targets laid down.  We require our education to train, to give an all-round education, to educate persons dedicated to  the cause of socialism who will be ardent patriots, consistent revolutionaries and unshakeable in-  ternationalists. We are educating Albanian schoolchildren to love and defend the freedom and in-  dependence of their motherland and at the same time to respect the freedom and independence of  other countries. We are educating them in such a way that they will condemn any aggressive pol-  icy or aggressive act and will always uphold, ardently and decisively, the just cause of nations  struggling for their freedom, their independence and their rights.
(4.13) Through measures adopted in the last three or four years, we are taking a new step to raise  education to a still higher level so that it may meet the new requirements of our time and of the  present stage of our economic and social development. We are already acquainted with the problem  causing anxiety to many States Members of our Organization and forming the substance of measures  submitted to this session by the International Commission on the Development of Education, namely  the adaptation of the system of school education to the new requirements of contemporary society.  We know the complaints and criticisms levelled by young people in many countries against an edu-  cation which has become enclosed within its aging structures and is already failing to meet the new  requirements and problems which young people are having to face in their work and lives.
(4.14) In our socialist society the demands made by life itself for the improvement of the structure  of institutions, including education, are satislied spontaneously and punctually precisely by the Popular  Government, which involves the broad participation of the general public, especially young students  and scholars. Throughout the thirty years since liberation, at each separate stage of the country's  development, Albanian education has been continually improved. The current changes and improve-  ments made over recent years aim in particular at raising the education of schoolchildren and stu-  dents to the even higher level required by the high level of economic development. For this pur-  pose we have reshaped and improved all our school curricula and textbooks.
(4.15) We have abandoned the old patterns of classical education and traditional routine in the organ-  ization and conduct of the school, We consider that educating young people only through the study  of traditional disciplines and through teaching in its traditional sense is inadequate and one-sided.  We feel that work, too, is a school where knowledge and culture may be acquired. In our country  schoolchildren and students study and are trained both through books and through industry, which  is part of the school curriculum and has a definite time allotted to it in the academic year. School-  children over sixteen work in industry, Generally speaking, our aim in this is educational, that  young people shall learn about life and its problems and about industry from teachers who are not  only school-teachers but also workers in factories and co-operative farms. While young people  work in industry they study the practical application of the theoretical knowledge they have gained
16  in school and become acquainted with the fourdations of broad social production; they learn how to  handle tools, acquire their first working skills on very simple machines, widen their technical  knowledge, and as a result make a better-informed choice of employment.
(4.16) We thus have the sort of education where students not only study but study and work. When  our new intelligentsia have completed this kind of education, they feel themselves closely linked  with industry and productive workers. In this way we are taking the necessary steps to narrow the  gap between intellectual and physical work, an important task of our social structure. We are con-  cerned with another important aspect of this problem as well: that those engaged in physical labour,  such as factory and co-operative farm workers, shall continually raise the level of their theoretical,  scientific and cultural knowledge. This is being achieved by way of a broad and solidly-based adult  education system involving no break from production. It is sufficient to say that in Albania today
57% of students at universities and other higher educational establishments are actually workers in  factories, farms and administrative or other social or cultural departments, who work and study  just as young students study and work. '
(4.17) Albanian education likewise plays a significant part in narrowing the gap between town and  country, At the present time in town and country alike eight-year education is being fully practised.  Schools have been built in the most distant mountainous regions of the country, even where the num-  ber of pupils is small. In recent years the network of schools giving a full secondary course has  been considerably extended, Over 80% of the secondary schools which it is planned to open in the  next four or five years will be situated in country districts and be generally geared to country life.  At the present time over 50% of pupils in vocational technical schools in our country come from  country distriets, This educational policy of our State is accompanied by cultural measures.  There is no village today without its cultural centre, and none in which an explosive development  of cultural, artistic and sporting life cannot be observed.
(4.18) Besides study and industrial work, students in secondary schools and higher educational ins-  titutes undergo military and physical training. We try to make physical education and sport in  schools as widely available, as systematic and as scientifically organized as possible. All young  people at school aged sixteen and over undergo military training to fit them to defend their socialist  homeland. A student's period of military training in school counts, as the period of military ser-  vice though it is shorter, which all other Albanian citizens have to undergo.
(4.19) In the general qualitative side of the studies and education of our country's rising generation,  there are still areas where we are making more effort, I might mention here the improvement of  the training and the continual raising of the professional and technical standards of teachers to en-  able them to meet the demands of the new education system, Teaching methods are being improved,  educational research and surveys are being widened, better methods of study and training are being  introduced and young students are being given a greater part to play in the organization and conduct  of school life, Renouncing obsolete traditions, we feel that the students are an active and decisive  force in the solution of any problems connected with education.
(4.20) At the present session of its General Conference our Organization has included among the  problems on the agenda Unesco's contribution to the preservation of peace and to respect for human  rights, and its tasks in the elimination of colonialism and racial discrimination. We energetically  protest against those States which proclaim only in words that they associate themselves with the  effort proposed by Unesco in the struggle for peace and respect for human rights, but really act  against the freedoms and rights of nations, All this demagogy and hypocrisy is aimed at pulling  the wool over people's eyes and concealing the general policy adopted by their countries against  the interests of various nations and sovereign States, and against the cause of peace throughout  the world, The representatives of the United States of America frequently employ a variety of paci-  fist, demagogic words and phrases at various international conferences while bombs of every pos-  sible kind have been supplied to American airmen to bomb Viet-Namese schools in the regions of  Tchao Tri, Haiphong, Hanoi and others, Compare the words of various American delegates who  express their willingness to assist the development of education and culture in undeveloped coun-  tries with the photographs in which you can see Viet-Namese teachers and children, their textbooks  in their hands, barbarously murdered by American air pirates, or with the photographs where you’  can see temples, pagodas and cinemas destroyed by American aircraft in Viet-Nam, Cambodia anc  Laos. As you know, our diplomats, too, accredited to North Viet-Nam recently suffered from these  bombing raids, as well as the French diplomat who was only recently buried here in his native land.  How can such actions fit the tasks proposed by Unesco for the defencr: of peace throughout the world  and respect for human rights? On the other hand, what explanation is there for the Soviet Socialist  imperialists attacking Czechoslovakia with their tanks, a country which to this day they still occupy?

6.1 Mr, GABITES (New Zealand):  Mr, President, since this is my delegation's first formal intervention at this Confer-  ence, may I offer you our congratulations, This is time-honoured custom, but if I may  say so, it is particularly agreeable for New Zealand to see a member of your country in  the office of President.
6.2 Mr. President and fellow delegates, the last three sessions of the General Conference  have been characterized by more determined long-term planning of the Organization's ac-  tivities. While the Conference must always be concerned with the immediate future, ithas  an equal obligation to ensure that its plans and activities will lead to long-term benefits in  a logical plan of action. At the sixteenth session we looked at the first long-term outline  plan and at this session we have a much more elaborate and detailed Draft Medium-Term  Outline Plan for 1973-1978. We congratulate the Director-Genéral and his associates on  their careful elaboration of this document, However, while we find it helpful to be able to  place the proposed programme for 1973-1974 in the context of a broadly described pro-  gramme for the next six years, we must agree with the views expressed by the Executive  Board and by the Director-General himself that the preparation of documents 17 C/4 and
17 C/5 has resulted in repetition and too much documentation. We hope that, with this  experience, the Director-General will be able to prepare for the eighteenth session a single  document setting out problems, projects and goals ir a more logical and complete manner,  eliminating the imperfections of these two documents.
6.3 I should like to comment briefly on the main lines of emphasis in the programme for  the next six years. In his introduction to document 17 C/4 the Director-General tells us  that his prime and constant concern has been to give Unesco's activities the most intimate  possible bearing on the problems and efforts of Member States, He considers this to be  not merely an essential condition for effective action, but also the justification for the real  significance of Unesco's work. While proposing that the Organization should continue with  its operational projects designed to meet urgent needs, the Director-General suggests that  Unesco's more significant rôle might be in helping Member States with the formulation of  their national policies in fields of Unesco's competence, This emphasis on technical assist- -  ance in formulating policy has for some time been evident in the education and natural sci-  ences sectors and more recently in culture.
6.4 We share the Director-General's view that Unesco does not have the human or the fi-  nancial resources to contribute significantly to the quantitative development of its Member  States. The main effort for such development must be national, perhaps with bilateral ex-  ternal assistance, However, during its 26 years of existence Unesco has accumulated a
16  wealth of experience on which it can draw, to give help and guidance in matters of policy  which will have qualitative significance for its Member States.
6.5 We support the. general lines of the programme for che Education Sector and the main  emphasis of this programme during the next six years. There are three groups of prob-  lems around which the programme has been built, they are, first, policy and planning of  education; second, structure, curriculum and methods in the field of school and out-of-  school education; and thirdly, post-secondary education, including training activities. When I had the privilege of speaking to you two years ago I said,  and I want to repeat it today, we offer this partnership to everyone: in our European policy,  in our relationship with our allies, and with the countries of Eastern Europe; and - let me  add this - in our relations with the People's Republic of China. That partnership is also,  of course, the yardstick of our policy on development.
8.19 All of us have to mobilize everywhere in the world the intellectual forces that are  needed so that the nations of the world may live side by side. We must set about realizing  something we have been talking about for years: co-operation, partnership and solidarity.  My Government will continue and, if possible, expand its close co-operation with Unesco.  It will do all in its power to promote Unesco's work, Thank you Mr. President.
9. Le PRESIDENT, -  Je remercie M, Eppler, Je donne la parole à S, Exc, M, Ngo Khac Tinh, ministre de  l'Education de la République du Viêt-nam.
10,1 M. NGO KHAC TINH (République du Viêt-nam), -  Monsieur le Président, puisque c'est la première fois que je prends la parole depuis  votre élection à la présidence, permettez-moi tout d'abord, au nom de la délégation de la  République du Viêt-nam, de vous adresser nos chaleureuses félicitations pour votre acces-  sion à ce poste vers lequel vous êtes porté par l'accord unanime et enthousiaste de toutes  les délégations, Comme représentant d'un Etat d'Asie, je ne peux m'empêcher d'éprouver  une légitime fierté en voyant un homme d'Asie, et parmi les plus illustres, appelé à diri-  ger les travaux de la plus haute instance culturelle du monde, :
10.2 Au nom de ma délégation, je désire aussi exprimer au Directeur général et atous ses  collaborateurs du Secrétariat mes félicitations et mon admiration pour le Projet de pro-  gramme et de budget et le Projet d'esquisse de plan à moyen terme qu'ils ont établis,
10.3 Nous sommes bien loin de considérer ces deux documents comme de simples projets  de classification des propositions désirables pour l'avenir, À nos yeux, il s'agit de deux  oeuvres considérables, dont chaque élément est le fruit d'un immense effort d'observation,  de systématisation, d'imagination et de réflexion, et dont la connaissance est indispensable  à tous ceux qui, dans le public ou parmi les initiés, cherchent à mieux comprendre l'Unesco  et surtout son orientation dans les six années à venir.
10.4 En ce qui concerne le C/4, c'est à la seizième Conférence générale qu'il a été pré-  senté pour la première fois, et ma délégation a accueilli alors avec une grande satisfac-
16  tion cette innovation importante dans 1'évolution des méthodes de travail de 1'Organisation,  Ce plan à moyen terme n'est pas seulement utile àl'Unesco, ill'est aussi aux Etats membres,  qui y trouvent à coup sûr une source d'inspiration inépuisable pour l'établissement de leurs  propres lois-programmes dans les domaines de l'éducation et de la culture,
10.5 On s'est demandé s'il fallait rédiger pour chaque session un C/4 distinct. À notre  avis, l'Unesco étant une création continue face aux changements rapides de la société de  notre temps, changements auxquels sont aussi soumis les Etats, il serait opportun que,  dans la période de six ans que couvre chaque C/4, soient rédigés des C/4 "complémen-  taires'' qui apporteraient les adaptations nécessaires,
10,6 Certeg, il n'est pas question que j'analyse dans mon exposé toutes les activités de  l'Unesco que les documents 17 C/4 et 17 C/5 nous révèlent dans leurs multiples aspects.  Je souhaite seulement faire quelques modestes observations à propos de certains points  fondamentaux,
10,7 Mesdames, Messieurs, la société internationale d'aujourd'hui demeure confrontée  aux graves problèmes que posent les différences de niveau de développement qui séparènt  les Etats, Nous n'en sommes qu'au début de la Deuxième Décennie du développement, et ce  n'est pas en douze ans d'efforts que le monde peut parvenir à combler le fossé entre les  Etats développés et ceux qui le sont moins, Aussi bien, l'objectif majeur de l'Unesco, ce-  lui qui commande tout le reste, a été et doit rester l'objectif du développement. Ce n'est  pas un objectif facile à atteindre, car il est extrêmement complexe,
10.8 Lorsque les peuples défavorisés ont commencé à prendre conscience de leur sous-  développement, ce qui les a frappé le plus, c'est qu'ils sont tous pauvres alors que d'autres  sont beaucoup plus riches, Pour cette raison, les économistes se sont emparés aussitôt de  la notion de sous-développement, dont l'aspect économique a été, de ce fait, considérable-  ment valorisé, Bien loin de négliger cet aspect, on s'est aperçu cependant assez rapide-  ment que les problèmes du développement débordent largement le domaine de l'économie  et embrassent tous les aspects de la vie, non seulement de la vie nationale mais aussi de  la vie internationale, non seulement de la vie sociale mais aussi de la vie individuelle des  hommes qui se déterminent d'après leurs idéaux, leur mentalité, leur savoir et leur envi-  ronnement. La lutte contre le sous-développement est tributaire, d'autre part, des progrès  des sciences physiques et naturelles et des techniques d'exploration et d'exploitation des  richesses naturelles, des ressources minérales, végétales et animales que renferment la  terre, la mer, le sol et le sous-sol des régions sous-marines, sans oublier la biosphère,  De même, pour mener à bien cette lutte, on doit avoir recours à l'ensemble des sciences  sociales et humaines, Enfin, si tout cela est en cause à la fois, comment les peuples peu-  vent-ils forger eux-mêmes les instruments de leur développement sans instruction ? L'édu-  cation à tous les niveaux devient ainsi un facteur primordial du développement.
10.9 C'est cette conception générale du développement que nous propose effectivement le  Secrétariat de l'Unesco, Tous les aspects du problème sont abordés à travers les nom-  breuses rubriques qui remplissent son projet de programme et qui sont établies unique-  ment au service du développement, Comment dès lors pourrait-on ne pas l'approuver plei-  nement ? En adoptant cette conception et en la mettant en oeuvre, l'Unesco se comporte  comme la véritable organisation internationale universelle pour le développement. Certes,  d'autres institutions spécialisées s'intéressent, elles aussi, au développement, mais elles  n'y consacrent qu'une partie de leurs activités. Nous serions tentés de dire que la Confé-  rence des Nations Unies pour le commerce et le développement elle-même, en associant  commerce et développement, reste influencée par l'aspect économique du problème, C'est  dire que nous approuvons dans leur ensemble les différentes parties du Programme, con-  sidérées comme les différentes branches de ce "tronc commun" qu'est le développement,
10.10 Seulement, si l'Unesco veut être une institution tournée totalement vers l'objectif  du développement, elle ne saurait entreprendre simultanément toutes les tâches avec la  même intensité, C'est ainsi que se pose à elle l'inévitable question des priorités, qui est  celle de la hiérarchie des urgences.
10,11 Notre délégation est heureuse de constater que les auteurs du Projet de programme  et de budget l'ont résolue non par suite d'un raisonnement purement déductif, mais en s'ap-  puyant sur des données concrètes, à la lumière de l'expérience, Nous ne doutons pas que  leurs choix aient été judicieux, Nous voudrions simplement ajouter que, dans cette appré-  ciation, le facteur déterminant devrait être l'avis des intéressés eux-mêmes, c'est-à-dire  des pays en voie de développement. Bien sûr, il n'existe pas d'uniformité dans les besoins  propres à chacun d'eux, Mais, au sein de l'Unesco, ils forment un groupe solidaire et struc-  turé. Us sont parvenus, dans leurs délibérations, à dégager des solutions d'intérêt commun,  A cet égard, la priorité accordée à l'éducation, clairement affirmée dans les deux projets,  répond aux voeux de tous les pays en voie de développement parce que, nous l'avons dit, le  développement de toute société est conditionné à la base par le développement de l'éducation,
10,12 Les auteurs des projets s'engagent aussi délibérément dans la voie de la rénovation  de l'éducation. D. as ses traits essentiels, cette rénovation devrait se traduire d'abord par  des mesures tendant à intégrer l'éducation dans le plan général de lutte contre le sous-dé-  veloppement, ce qui consisterait à harmoniser ces mesures avec les efforts paralièles en-  trepris pour faire progresser les autres secteurs de la vie sociale, L'éducation apparaît  ainsi par excellence comme une matière intersectorielle qui appelle une exploration et une  analyse interdisciplinaire ; c'est dans le cadre de cette harmonisation nécessaire qu'il con-  viendrait, ensuite, d'obtenir une amélioration de l'éducation en accordant, en l'état actuel  des choses, une plus grande considération à l'aspect qualitatif. -
10.13 Qu'il me soit permis d'indiquer que c'est dans cette double direction rénovatrice que  s'engage la République du Viêt-nam, Le but principal recherché par notre gouvernement est  la démocratisation de l'enseignement, grâce surtout à l'éducation des masses qui, elle-  même, repose sur trois idées directrices : donner aux citoyens des chances égales de s'ins-  truire, assurer à chaque individu le droit d'accéder au type d'éducation de son choix, faire  en sorte que chaque homme puisse apporter sa contribution au développement de l'éducation  nationale,
10.14 Nous visons aussi à la diversification des branches de l'enseignement, afin que les  jeunes puissent, selon leurs aptitudes, accéder aux professions de leur choix, et ce.à tous  les stades de leur éducation, Le Sud Viët-nam, en tant que pays en voie de développement,  étant un pays à la fois agricole et en voie d'industrialisation, nous avons notamment tout  mis en oeuvre pour encourager tant l'enseignement agricole que l'enseignement technique  et professionnel.
10.15 C'est avec une vive satisfaction que nous constatons que notre programme éducatif  coîncide en grande partie avec celui qu'élabore l'Unesco, Cette convergence est pour nous  très encourageante car, tout en justifiant les décisions que nous avons prises, elle nous  permet d'espérer une continuation - sinon une extension - de l'assistance de l'Unesco, as-  sistance dont nous avons tant besoin en cette période difficile que traverse notre pays.
10,16 Comme la culture est le complément indispensable de l'éducation, c'est à elle que  je consacrerai maintenant quelques brèves remarques, L'importance de la place que le  projet réservé à l'action del'Unesco dans le domaine culturel au cours -des deux prochaines  années est parfaitement justifiée. Mais, originaire d'une contrée de l'Asie, je désire aller  plus loin encore en proposant que soit reconnue expressément la primauté de la culture  dans la vie sociale, ce qui implique naturellement le droit d'accès à la culture comme com-  plément du droit à l'éducation, C'est sous le signe général du développement que nous sou-  haitons cette reconnaissance, Nous l'avons déjà dit et nous tenons à le répéter, la recherche  des mesures tendant à traduire la primauté de la culture dans les politiques culturelles na-  tionales devrait être le souci constant des pays développés comme des pays sous-développés,
10.17 Pour les premiers, l'accent mis sur la culture devrait jouer le rôle de prévention  souhaitable contre les excès que peut engendrer la récherche incessante des progrès ma-  tériels. Nous sommes convaincus que le développement ne saurait se confondre avec le  matérialisme, ‘
10. 18 En ce qui concerne les pays en voie de développement, par un besoin venu peut-être  de leur dénuement, leurs peuples connaissent traditionnellement une vie socio-culturelle  intense, Selon un spécialiste européen de géographie humaine qui connaissait bien le Viêt-  nam, nos paysans n'étaient parvenus à supporter leur pauvreté que grâce aux charmes eta  la joie morale que leur offrait la vie culturelle de la communauté villageoise. Certes, ces  peuples se sont engagés sans retour dans la voie du développement et du progrès social.  Mais, pour plusieurs raisons, ils doivent maintenir en même temps la primauté de la cul-  ture, D'abord, parce qu'ils ne pourront rattraper avant longtemps l'écart qui les sépare  des pays développés et que la culture demeure pour eux la seule richesse pleinement dis-  ponible, Ensuite, parce qu'en croyant en cette primauté et en l'appliquant ils donnent  l'exemple du refus de la poursuite exclusive et illimitée du progrès matériel et contribuent  à entretenir et à enrichir ce qui est proprement le facteur déterminant de ce que nous pou-  vons appeler l'harmonie de la vie, ’
10.19 En tant que représentant d'un peuple qui tout au long de son histoire a forgé une ci-  vilisation aujourd'hui plusieurs fois millénaire, j'insiste particulièrement sur la nécessité  de préserver la culture traditionnelle comme racine du génie des peuples et fondement de  la continuité et de l'identité des nations.
10,20 Mais il importe de souligner immédiatement que l'aire de culture et de civilisation  ne coincide pas toujours avec les frontières nationales, et qu'en conséquence cette préser-  vation ne devrait pas être comprise dans le sens d'un nationalisme étroit. Il faudrait donc  mettre l'accent autant sur les cultures nationales que sur les cultures régionales, Nous  nous félicitons que le Projet de programme et de budget s'engage dans cette voie en préco-  nisant une série de mesures relatives aux différentes cultures régionales,
10,21 Nous approuvons aussi entièrement la notion de préservation du patrimoine culturel  de l'humanité, C'est particulièrement à ce titre que nous attirons l'attention sur la néces-  sité de restaurer les monuments historiques, témoins du passé des peuples, qui peuvent  être détruits ou menacés par des événements naturels ou par la guerre, À ce sujet, il m'est  agréable d'exprimer à l'Unesco et à son Directeur gunéral mes remerciements pour l'aide  et l'assistance qui ont été et seront accordées à la République du Viêt-nam en vue de la  restauration des sites et monuments de la Cité impériale de Hué qui ont été détruits par la  guerre,
10.22 Inutile de souligner aussi que préservation ne doit pas être confondue avec conser-  vatisme. Comme les systémes éducatifs, les cultures nationales traditionnelles ont besoin  aussi d'une rénovation dans un monde en pleine et constante évolution, Tous les programmes  nationaux de rénovation culturelle devraient bénéficier de l'aide appropriée de l'Unesco,
10.23 Ainsi, la poursuite de ces deux objectifs complémentaires en matière d'éducation et  de culture ouvre un champ immense aux activités opérationnelles de l'Unesco, activités  dont chaque pays sous-développé, j'en suis sûr, a pu apprécier les bienfaits et qui doivent  évidemment être maintenues, voire accrues.
10,24 Afin de donner à l'action de l'Unesco son maximum d'efficacité, nous suggérons que,  là où existent déjà des institutions régionales constituées par un groupe d'Etats membres,  notre Organisation vienne les épauler au lieu d'en créer d'autres qui feraient double em-  ploi avec elles,
1N,25 Mesdames, Messieurs, je serais coupable d'une omission inexcusable si je n'évo-  quais, comme tous les orateurs qui m'ont précédé à cette tribune, les problèmes des droits  de l'homme et de la paix, problèmes qui, sur le plan technique, appartiennent à la catégo-  rie des problèmes intersectoriels, et qui, sur le plan politique, dominent les relations in-  ternationales actuelles,
10.26 Quelque part dans le Projet de programme et de budget, on lit : "Les droits de  l'homme et les problèmes de la paix sont des thèmes qui se retrouvent en filigrane dans
16  tout le programme de l'Organisation et qui constituent des objectifs fondamentaux des Na-  tions Unies." C'est à juste titre que ces deux objectifs se retrouvent partout, Ils sont en  effet intimement liés au développement.
10,27 Nous parlons, hélas, par expérience et non dans l'abstrait. En réponse à tous ceux  qui ont évoqué ici la guerre du Viêt-nam, tout en l'interprétant à leur façon, nous rappe-  lons que la République du Viêt-nam subit depuis un quart de siècle une guerre injuste, En  même temps qu'elle exerce son droit de légitime défense contre ceux qui l'attaquent, elle  s'efforce sans cesse de réduire son sous-développement, Mais c'est sa lutte pour sa sur-  vie qui conserve la priorité. Aussi bien, quand nous approuvons l'objectif de paix, nous le  faisons en pleine connaissance de cause, parce que nous savons que seul le retour de la  paix nous permettra de nous livrer totalement et fructueusement à la tâche exaltante du  développement,
10.28 Mieux que quiconque, nous savons pourquoi nous combattons, pourquoi cette guerre  nous est imposée, De l'extérieur, on veut introduire au Sud-Viêt-nam, contre la volonté de  l'écrasante majorité du peuple sud-vietnamien, un régime et une idéologie qu'il rejette,  Cette tentative, qui constitue une des causes de cette guerre, confirme la relation souvent  établie entre la rupture de,la paix et la méconnaissance, ainsi que les violations; des droits  de l'homme, Nous nous réjouissons donc de l'importance de la place qui est réservéedans  le Projet de programme et de budget à la promotion et à la défense des droits de l'homme,
10,29 Le peuple et le gouvernement de la République du Viêt-nam se conforment à l'idéal  de paix des Nations Unies ; ils sont respectueux des droits de l'homme, de la digrité'hu-.  maine et de la liberté réelle des peuples, Ayant trop souffert dés violences, ils demandent  qu'un terme soit définitivement mis aux guerres d'agression et que tout soit ‘mis en oeuvre  pour en supprimer les causes. Pour atteindre ces objectifs, ils sont prêts à participer ac-  tivement à toutes les activités entreprises par l'Unesco, dans le cadre de ses compétences.
10.30 Mesdames, Messieurs, j'ai l'impression que j'ai utilisé largement mon temps de pa-  role, en dépit du soin particulier que j'ai mis à me limiter à quelques questions essentielles.
10,31 Le retentissement mondial de chaque session de notre Conférence générale consti-  tue tout à la fois une confirmation du rayonnement de l'Unesco et un rappel de ses respon-  sabilités. Nos délibérations vont porter sur des questions relatives au développement, à  l'éducation, à la science, à la culture, à la paix et aux droits de l'homme, qui sont des.quec-  tions majeures de la société contemporaine, Aucune instance internationale n'est:plus qua-  lifiée que la nôtre pour examiner de telles questions, puisque aucune n'est aussi proche de  l'universalité que la nôtre, Sachons mériter notre rang en nous haussant au niveau de nos  responsabilités. - '
10.32 Afin de ne pas décevoir, nous devons adopter résolument une attitude constructi ye  et de compréhension mutuelle, en insistant sur ce qui nous unit au lieu de prononcer des  paroles et d'accomplir des gestes qui mettent inutilement en relief ce qui nous divise encore.
10.33 Le voeu le plus cher de la délégation de la République du Viêt-nam est que les déci-  sions que nous allons prendre reflètent fidèlement les aspirations fondamentales des peuples  que nous représentons, C'est à ce prix qu'elles pourront acquérir une autorité morale suf-  fisante pour servir de modèle à tous. :

(12.1) Mrs. ZAKHARIEVA (People's Republic of Bulgaria) (Translated from the Russian)  Mr, President; on behalf of the delegation of the People's Republic of Bulgaria, it gives  me also great pleasure to congratulate you on your election to the responsible position of President  of the seventeenth session of the Geueral Conference of Unesco. We are sure that under your guid-  ance this session will go down in the annals of the Organization as a session which made a valuable  contribution to the further development of peaceful international co-operation in education, science  and culture,
(12,2) Ladies and gentlemen, distinguished delegates: the preservation of peace, the elimination  of theatres of war, and the establishment of a climate of growing international co-operation have  always been the chief concerns of all peace-loving governments. Peace on our earth, freedom and  independence for all nations, and mutually-advantageous international co-operation are the unchang-  ing principles governing the countries of the socialist camp to which my country belongs. Since the  sessions of the General Conference began - and I think that most delegations here present will agree  with me - the time has never yet been so ripe nor the international climate so favourable as now  for the settlement of difficult problems of concern to all mankind.
(12. 3) Since the sixteenth session, positive steps have been taken, particularly in Europe, towards  the settlement of the most difficult problems. Agreements of exceptional importance have come  into force and strengthened the principles of peaceful coexistence. Talks are being held between  countries which only yesterday seemed to be divided by insuperable barriers. Fresh impetus has  been given to the question of progressive disarmament, security and co-operation. The trend to-  wards the search for political and not military solutions to a number of difficult problems is grow-  ing stronger.
(12.4) While all this is true, we are not at the same time justified in shutting our eyes to obvious  facts in the present situation which have a direct bearing on the Constitution and practical activity  of Unesco. The aggressive war against the people of the Indo-Chinese peninsula rages unabated;  the blood of the peaceful Viet-Namese nation is being spilt; a barbaric aerial war is being waged  on a scale which has surpassed the bombing of the whole of the Second World War; every day and  every hour an ancient and rich culture is being extinguished and monuments dating back thousands  of years are being destroyed. In the Near East a situation of dangerous tension still exists. The  consequences of the aggression against the Arab nations remain. The situation there is an ex-  tremely serious threat both to the countries of this region and to the whole world, Nor can we ig-  nore the fact, which disgraces our times, that on the map 0° the world are still to be found coun-  tries and territories whose people are languishing under the yoke of colonialism, and that racism  in all its evil forms has not yet been eliminated and in places even finds support.
(12.5) We were completely bewildered when the leader of the Albanian delegation stated that the  Soviet Union and the countries of the Warsaw Pact are responsible for the tension existing in va-  rious parts of the world, when all nations of the world know not only the consistent position of the  Soviet Union and the countries of the Warsaw Pact, but also their genuine peace-loving policy.
(12,6) We think that the situation in which our Conference is being held makes it even more im-  perative that Unesco's prime task should be to prepare and implement an effective long-term  programme of action directed towards the strengthening of peace and security in the whole world  and the complete elimination of colonialism and racism, The moral strength of ULesco and  some of the positive steps it has taken which have made possible an improvement in the interna-  tional climate give us grounds for thinking that it possesses unused potential which we must reso-  lutely seek and uncover at each session of the General Conference. In this connexion should be  noted the important draft resolution proposed by the Soviet Union entitled "The Preparation by  Unesco of a Draft Declaration on the Fundamental Principles Governing the Use of the Mass Me-  dia with a view to strengthening Peace and International Understanding and combating War Propa-  ganda, Racism and Apartheid’, The Bulgarian delegation is associated with this resolution as  co-sponsor.
(12,7) The problem of maintaining world-wide peace is indissolubly linked with the strengthening  of peace and security in Europe. In this respect our delegation expresses satisfaction at the ac-  tion taken by the Director-General, Mr. René Maheu, in making available valuable Unesco docu-  mentation for the forthcoming Pan-European Conference on Security and Co-operation, This  suggestion was put forward and expanded at the Helsinki Conference and expressed in resolution No. 32.
(12,8) At the same time it is not out of place to mention that Unesco's contribution would be even  more significant if it put the principle of universality more quickly into practice, The admission  of China and Bangladesh is a success for Unesco, We greet the newly-admitted Member States  and those taking part in the General Conference for the first time, We should also like to express  our confidence that the representatives of the German Democratic Republic will also take their  rightful seat in this hall in the very near future, since their country is one of the ten countries in  the world which have reached the highest level of development in science and technology, and has  been outstandingly successful in education, art and culture,
(12,9) The Bulgarian National Commission, the appropriate scientific and cultural institutes of  our country, and likewise a very wlde circle of specialists have carefully studied documents 17 c/4  and 17 C/5 prepared by the Secretariat. I should like to mention some of our observations and  recommendations concerning these documents. We feel that they contain, especially 17 C/5, a  number of valuable ideas and projects which we support and which are a sound basis for the Or-  ganization's future activity, However, we cannot say that we are fully satisfied with the form and  content of document 17 C/4, which is unnecessarily detailed and difficult to use. It repeats the  two-year programme in many respects, and we recommend that efforts be continued to simplify  and improve it.
(12,10) The reshaping and democratization of education, heiping it to catch up with the explosive  revolution in science and technclogy, the development of science, technology and the mass infor  mation media and the mitigation of their frequently adverse consequences and influence on the life  of society, all call for enormous efforts and resources, We therefore feel that Unesco must  change itself first cf all into an effective international forum for the exchange of useful experience  and knowledge concerning education, science, technology, culture and communication, Unesco's  operational activities, whatever their scale, cannot by themselves solve the great problems fac-  ing each country, We feel that the Organization's chief rôle must be to stimulate the national ef-  forts of Member States. In this respect the Bulgarian delegation notes that the question of increas-  ing the effectiveness of Unesco's activity is to be discussed at the present session, We feel that  this question is linked with the need to obey unswervingly the principle of selecting the great prob-  lems of importance to mankind on whose solution Unesco must concentrate its efforts and financial  resources by establishing priorities in its programme.
(12,11) We are pleased to note the trend towards concentration in the sectors of Education and  Natural Science, We support the execution of the programmes of UNISIST: Man and the Bio-  sphere, Geological Correlation, the Hydrological Decade and the Intergovernmental Oceano-  graphic programmes, These programmes are of great significance for both the developed and  the developing countries. In connexion with them and in answer to a request from the Secretariat,  national committees headed by outstanding scientists have been set up in Bulgaria, These com-  mittees, which enjoy the full support of the Government, are engaged on extensive activities, We  should like to express our appreciation here of the support given by Unesco and the Director-  General to the establishment in our country of an automated science and technology information  centre.
(12.12) The Government of the People's Republic of Bulgaria also pays close attentionto Unesco's  educational development programme. A reform of secondary and higher education is under way  at present in our country. Its aim is to establish the closest possible link between science and  life and to integrate scientific research with teaching in institutes of higher education. We should  be very happy to share our experience of this work. At the same time we study the useful expe-  rience of other countries with interest and attention,
(12,13) The Bulgarian delegation is cenvinced of the need to continue its work on concentrating  Unesco's resources on solution of the most important problems in education, science, culture  and communication, We are also convinced that to concentrate attention on the important pro-  grammes will help significantly in solving the Organization's financial problems. We are not op-  posed to a reasonable increase in the budget when it is connected with the Organization's devel-  opment and the expansion of its programme. What does cause us concern is the excessive and  continuing growth of the budget for administrative expenditure, and the passion for secondary and  not always sufficiently reasoned projects and expensive building, On top of all this a significant  proportion of Member States are obliged to bear the burden of the currency fluctuations and infla-  tionary processes occurring in a few others,
(12,14) True to the principles of peaceful coexistence and the development of equal and mutually-  advantageous relationships between States, the People's Republic of Bulgaria attributes great im-  portance to the various world, regional and sub-regional meetings and conferences, In recent  years, the Bulgarian Black Sea town of Varna has welcomed delegates to big international con-
16  ferences, In 1970 the World Congress of Sociologists was held in our country, The World Con-  gress of Architects, devoted to the important problems of "Man and Leisure", has only just  finished, and next year the fifteenth World Congress of Philosophers is to be held in Varna, It  is a pleasure for me to state from this podium that the Bulgarian Government is re dy to provide  all necessary facilities for one of the big international conferences contemplated fn Unesco's  programme for the next few years, namely the Second Conference of Ministers Responsible for  Science and Technology Policy in the Member States of Europe and North America, to be held  in 1977,
(12,15) Before ending, i should like to mention two further matters, I am happy to say that the  programme for the Study of Slav Cultures, which is in full accord with the recommendations of  the Venice and Helsinki Conferences on cultural matters, is proceeding well, This programme,  like many other similar Unesco programmes covering various cultures and civilizations, will  play an important part in making the age-old and mighty culture of the'Slavic nations available to  all mankind, The rich and interesting programme prepared by specialists from a number of  countries gives us grounds for supporting the continuation of this project beyond 1978.
(12.16) The second matter is this, Two years ago International Education Year was organized  with success, On the proposal of the Soviet Union, 1972 was proclaimed an International Book  Year, International action of this kind is highly important. It gives us the opportunity to reflect,  to appraise’ what has been'done, and to become aware of future requirements. Measures of this  kind and the national activity of Member States give a new image to our Organization a d awaken  public interest in the aims and achievements of Unesco, which at other times usually remain the  preserve of a limited circle of educational, scientific and cultural workers.
(12.17) Guided by the considerations I have just mentioned, we are proposing on behalf of the  People's Republic of Bulgaria to introduce at the present session of the General Conference a  draft résolution requesting that 1976 be declared an International Year for Oral Traditions and  for Folk Music and Dancing, We are sure that action of this kind will facilitate the collection,  preservation and popularization of the vast cultural heritage of nations. It also provides an op-  portunity to solve another problem: it will be close to the hearts of young people and will asso-  ciate wider sections of young people with Unesco's activity and ideals,

(14.1) Mr, ISHTSEREN (People's Republic of Mongolia) (Translation from the Russian):  Mr, President, allow me first of all, on behalf of the delegation of the People's Republic of  Mongolia, to congratulate you on your election as President of this seventeenth session of --  Unesco's General Conference and to wish you great success in your work in this distinguished  and responsible office,
(14,2) The two years which have gone by since the last session of Unesco's General Conference,  the sixteenth, have, as you know, been marked by great events in international life of great sig-  nificance for the preservation of peace, the strengthening of mutual understanding between na-  tions, and the further development of international co-operation. It is sufficient to mention such  encouraging moves as the success of the Soviet-American talks which took place in May this  year; the entry into force of the agreements between the USSR and the Federal Republic of  Germany and between the People's Republic of Poland and the Federal Republic of Germany; the  further return to normal of the relations between the two German States, the German Democratic  Republic and the Federal Republic of Germany, on the basis of international law; and the clear-  ing of the way for the practical preparation of the Pan-European Conference on Security and Co-  operation. All this will undoubtedly have a beneficial influence on the activities of our Organiza-  tion, since the successful development of education, science and culture is possible only in con-  ditions of peace and normal relations between States.
(14,3) We are therefore bound to express our serious concern at the persistence of dangerous  situations in various parts of the world, The flame of war continues to burn in South East Asia,  where children and peaceful citizens are perishing beneath American bombs, and schools and  cultural establishments are being destroyed. The situation is tense in the Middle East, and  colonial oppression, racism and apartheid persist in South Africa and the Portuguese African  colonies. We feel that Unesco, by reason of its constitutional provisions, cannot look with  equanimity on these dark aspects of international life but must, on the contrary, further a very  rapid positive solution of these problems,
(14.4) It is a pleasure to state that the past two years have been a period in which Unesco's  activity has expanded still further, Its efforts to spread literacy, to develop and improve edu-  cation, to organize international co-operation in the varions departments of natural science, and  to study and preserve the cultural heritage of mankinc, are all producing definite results. The  Reportof the Director-General, Mr, René Maheu, on Unesco's work in the last period and its  Programme for 1973-1974, which was listened to with great attention by all delegates, throws  light on the results of measures taken by Unesco and expresses interesting thoughts on the tasks  of future biennia. The Mongolian People's Republic is deeply interested in the development and  improvement of Unesco's work to fulfil its fundamental purposes, and we must therefore wish  the Organization all success in that work. .
(14.5) The delegation of the Mongolian People's Republic feels that Unesco must, first and fore-  most, pay continual attention to ways of reinforcing the peace and security of nations, defending  human rights and eliminating colonialism and racism. To do this it will be necessary in the  future to envisage more specific and important measures to make the greatest possible impact in  solving this problem, In particular we attribute great significance to the draft resolution sub-  mitted by the USSR and other delegations at this session, "Strengthening Unesco's Rôle in the  Fight Against Racism and Apartheid', which we fully support and of which we are a co-sponsor.  The cause of peace and the strengthening of mutual understanding between nations is also served
16  by the draft resolution ''Measures for the Preservation of National Cultures as the Objective  Foundation for Mankind's Cultural Progress and the Development of International Cultural Links",  The adoption and implementation of these resolutions would be a tangible contribution by Unesco  to the cause of peace, An important place must be given in Unesco's work to spreading the ideas  of peace and friendship between peoples, and to the struggle against the ideology of militarism  and against enmity and hatred between nations, Particular attention should therefore be given to  the international education of young people,
(14,6) The Government of the Mongolian People's Republic feels it of great importance that the  principle of universality should be practised, for this would increase atili further the authority  of Unesco as one of the great and foremost specialized organizations in the United Nations system,  and would facilitate a fuller and more competent solution of the various problems connected with  the development of education, science and culture, Thanks to the tireless efforts of many States  members of Unesco, the principle of universality is making still further progress. An obvious  confirmation of this statement is the restoration to the People's Republic of China of its legal  rights in Uuesco, and the admission to membership of Unesco of the People’s Republic of  Bangladesh, The time has now come for a rapid and favourable solution of the problem of admit-  ting to membership of Unesco, a sovereign State of Europe, the German Democratic Republic,  which fully supports the aims and principles of Unesco and which, once it hecame a member of  our Organization, could make a great contribution to the development of international co-operation  in education, science and culture, The fact that the GDR is still excluded from Unesco is con-  trary to the interests and true yearnings of its people and to the interests of Unesco itself, ‘I had  the good fortune some years ago to attend the Seventh Educational Congress in the GDR; and I  remember very clearly the thousands of representatives of the teachers of that country who en-  thusiastically carried a resolution expressing their ardent hope to see the GDR admitted to mem-  bership of Unesco. The delegation of the Mongolian People's Republic would like to state once  more that the question of the admission of the GDR to Unesco cannot be put off again, We also  feel that the People's Democratic Republic of Korea, the Democratic Republic of Viet-Nam, and  the real representative of the people of South Viet-Nam, the Interim Revolutionary Government  of South Viet-Nam, should be admitted to the ranks of our Organization,
(14,7) Where Unesco's individual programmes are concerned, we think that in forthcoming years,  in education for example, various forms of action against educational discriminatiofi, and in favour  of equality of access to education and its democratization should be maintained and-mnde more  specific, In this connexion I should like to refer to the Conference of Ministers of Education of  the Socialist countries, recently held in Prague, at which the present state and future prospects  of general and higher education in these countries were discussed and a convention signed on the  equivalence of general educational certificates, of higher education diplomas, and of academic  degrees and nomenclature, I think that this instrument is a very important example of how these  problems may be solved. Implementation of the Experimental World Literacy Programme is im-  portant for our Organization, and, so far as we can see, there is much to be done in this direc-  tion, The Mongolian People's Republic has solved the problem of eliminating illiteracy, and its  successes in this work have been marked by the N.K. Krupskaya Unesco Prize. We cannot, how-  ever, fail to express our alarm that nearly a thousand million people in other countries are still  illiterate; and we are convinced that the problem of literacy will be reflected in Unesco's future  programmes, At the same time we should like to say that the fight against illiteracy requires,  above all, the efforts of those very States which have an illiterate population, and that to exert  them is their duty as members of Unesco,
(14.8) It is useful to note the extension of Unesco's assistance to the National Commissions of  Member States to increase their establishment of links between Unesco and the governments of  the various countries for the preparation of Unesco's future programmes and for increasing the  effectiveness of the various measures being implemented under the auspices of our Organization,
(14,9) The continual growth and complicated structure of Unesco's governing bodies and its re-  gional organizations lead to considerable and unceasing increases in the Organization's budget.  The greater part of the resources which could be apportioned among the various parts of Unesco's  aid programme goes on expenditure connected with administration, Ve believe that Unesco could  do much to mitigate or stabilize budgetary fluctuations by limiting the rate of staff growth, stop-  ping the construction of Unesco's new buildings, cutting down the number of its conferences and  meetings, and simplifying-its administrative structure. (16.1) Mr, KHALFAN NASSER ALWUHEIBI (Sultanate of Oman) (Translation from the Arabic):  Mr, President: first of all, I should like to add the voice of my country, the Sultanate of  Oman, to those of the other States members of this Organization to congratulate you on your elec-  tion to the Presidency of this Conference, a choice which will undoubtedly be one of the factors  contributing to its success,
(16.2) I am most happy that my country, the Sultanate of Oman, should participate in this Con-  ference for the first time since we broke off the shackles of isolation and began to wipe off the  cobwebs of backwardness which had accumulated over long decades of darkness, Furthermore,  our entry into this noble Organization is an expression of our faith in its lofty ideals and in the  values of education and civilization which it represents,
(16.3) Since most of the delegates to this distinguished Conference are not aware of many of my  country's problems in the various domains of development, I ask leave to talk briefly about one  of these problems and the steps we have taken to overcome it.
(16,4) Before 23 July 1970, that is to say before the enlightened era in which the Sultanate of  Oman now lives, my country was deprived of education in the accepted sense, Until that year it  had only three primary schools with a tota) enrolment of 900 pupils, Many Omanis quenched  their thirst for learning, brought on by long deprivation, by smuggling their children out to
16  neighbouring States to have them educated. After 23 July 1970, however, the number of schools  rose to 45 and that of boys and girls ‘enrolled to 15,000, This year we have 81 schools with
30, 000 pupils,” There is an urgent and pressing need for more and yet more schools; but our  lack of resources frustrates this because of the large number of priorities which we must meet,
(16,5) The efforts exerted in my country to expand education must not prevent us from recogniz-  ing the problems we face in this domain, We have not yet a definite policy on educational curri-  cula, for we have as yet been unable to develop a philosophy or long-term objectives for the pri-  mary education which we are in the process of establishing, or for the preparatory education  stage which we inaugurated this year. As is usual in the developing countries, steady and mas-  sive expansion of education has created a chain of imbalance and disparity, There is, for ex-  ample, a disparity between the numbers of boys and of girls as a result of prevalent social cus-  toms and traditions, the proportion of girls to boys being 17.5%. There is also an imbalance in  the distribution of schools, and many densely-populated areas are short of educational services  owing to rommunication difficulties, ‘There is indeed an urgent need to correct this state of  imbalance.
(16,6) Another of our problems relates to the quality of education, The rapid increase in the  numbers of schools opened and of students enrolled during the past two years has not allowed us  enough time to evaluate the quality of the education which we have established, However, we be-  lieve that the time has come to study the situation carefully, in order to correct it in conformity  with the economic and social conditions and requirements of the country, In Oman's present cir-  cumstances as a developing State, it needs an educational system which shall not only play a part  in the economic development of its people but also be an effective means of changing the way of  life of the people.
(16,7) Another of our educational problems is the training of teachers, I must stress that the  steady increase in the number of schools has compelled us to use unqualified teachers, I do not  think that this problem is restricted to us alone; but our rapid expansion has caused our percen-  tage of unqualified teachers to rise to alarming proportions. Faced with this situation, our only  recourse is to bring in teachers from abroad, We also believe, however, that the time has come  to start planning programmes for the pre-service and in-service training of teachers, for we can-  not otherwise hope to raise our educational standards.
(16.8) At this stage of development in Oman we need a bilateral approach to educational planning.  First, we must provide primary education for children over ten years of age who have so far re-  ceived no education. The second problem is to plan accelerated courses in vocational education  as well as in other basic areas of the primary education curriculum, In addition, we must ini-  tiate a literacy programme, to relieve the pressure on our crowded primary schools and enable  them to absorb the largest possible number of school-age children,
(16.9) Needless to say, in the face of these numerous difficulties and in order to develop school  curricula and improve existing education, we must consider and apply the experience of other  nations, At the same time we ourselves must admit our need to exert a great deal of sincere  and persistent effort, At a time when it behoves us to do our utmost in this direction, we re¢al-  ize that, unless we give equal attention to the different aspects of educational development, our  endeavours will result in great waste of effort and money, and we shall lose sight of our basic  goal, namely the overall development of our country. It is therefore vital that we should start  to frame a sound policy and avoid as far as possible the errors made by other countries in simi-  lar situations.
(16.10) These are some of our achievements and some of our problems, To overcome these  problems we need resources and experts to organize our educational system, to train our teach-  ers, to establish centres of educational media and to lay down the broad lines of suitable curri-  cula and textbooks. Unfortunately our other development commitments stand between us and  these hopes, Our basic aim at this juncture of our development is to give all our children equal  opportunities and to guarantee that they receive a modern education according to their individual  interests and abilities, While we express our grateful thanks for the assistance which we have  begun to receive from this Organization and from several sister States, we hope that the Organi-  zation, as well as friendly and sister States, will help us on a larger scale to solve our educa-  tional problems,
(16, 11) The progress of a State must not be measured by material achievements alone, however  great these may be. It must be measured by the changes in its life and thought which can only be  accomplished through planned purposeful education. My country began to live in this world only  two years ago, We started from nothing, But our achievements in two years, despite great prob-
16  lems and enormous difficulties, give us cause to be optimistic and to realize that we are follow-  ing the right path.
(16.12) At a time when we are devoting our entire attention to development problems in our  country, we find that we cannot remain silent about the racial discrimination practised in cer-  tain countries, nor about the evil attempts which are being made :o obliterate the national heri-  tage of certain peoples by use of overwhelming force. Israel's acts in the Arab City of Jerusalem  aimed at destroying one of the greatest civilizations, which has contributed most to the human  heritage, is an insolent violation of the United Nations Charter and of the Unesco Constitution,  and a flagrant breach of the resolutions of this Conference, We urge this Conference to make  every effort to induce Israel to implement the resolutions adopted on this subject,
