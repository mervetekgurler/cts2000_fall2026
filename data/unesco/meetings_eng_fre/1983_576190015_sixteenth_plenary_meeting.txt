10.1 Mr SANKATSING (Suriname) :  distinguished delegates, ladies and gentlemen, it is a great honour for me to  address this session of the General Conference on behalf of the Revolutionary  Government and the people of the Republic of Suriname. First of all, allow me to  extend to you the warmest greetings from the leader of the Suriname Revolution,  Lt. Col. Desi Bouterse.  Mr President of the General Conference, Mr Chairman of the Executive Board,
10.2 Mr President, we congratulate you on your election to this important office,  and we are confident that your firm guidance will make an important contribution to  the success of this Conference. We further express our appreciation and gratitude  for the successful effort made by the Director-General and his staff in the excel-  lent preparation of this Conference. We welcome the new members of Unesco. We  also welcome the Netherlands Antilles, a country with which we have always had very  special ties of friendship, as an Associate Member.
10.3 Mr President, it is with pleasure that we take this opportunity to inform  so many prominent representatives from the world of education, science, communica-  tion and culture about our government's point of view concerning the fields  covered by this important Organization. But before I do so, I would like to refer  to an issue that has been raised at this Conference. Grenada, an independent,  sovereign and non-aligned country, is now the victim of an unprovoked armed aggres-  sion, which cannot be justified for whatever reasons. The Government of the Repub-  lic of Suriname unequivocally condemns this foreign armed intervention in Grenada,  which amounts to a serious violation of the norms of international law and the  principles of the Charter of the United Nations. Moreover, this armed aggression  seriously tramples upon the inalienable right of the people of Grenada to deter-  mine their own destiny free from any interference and intervention. The Govern-  ment of the Republic of Suriname therefore appeals to the international community  to remedy the situation and to take appropriate measures to secure a speedy with-  drawal of all foreign troops and respect of the sovereignty, independence and ter-  ritorial integrity of Grenada.
10.4 Mr President, my government considers the work Unesco is doing and which  it still has to do as extremely important, because only educated, well-informed  and conscious people can lay the foundations of strong, stable and just societies.  Although it is difficult to compare the importance of the different United Nations  organizations, we feel that Unesco occupies a very special place. For this reason,  we cannot support the arguments presented by some delegations in favour of a sharp  reduction of the Unesco budget.
10.5 Mr President, Unesco must apply its greatest efforts where the problems  within its field of interest have their sharpest manifestations. Without suggest-  ing that it should lay emphasis only on Third World countries, we have to realize  as undeniable the fact that problems in the educational, scientific and cultural  fields have special manifestations in the former colonies and the oppressed coun-  tries in the world. Although Unesco is aware of this, we feel that even more  emphasis must be put on it.
10.6 Mr President, education is taken very seriously in my country. Some 25 per  cent of our national budget is dedicated to education. But this is the case thanks  only to the Revolution which began on 25 February 1980. Before that date, our  country was moving closer and closer to an abyss, and in a few years, almost a  third of our population migrated to Holland, because there was no confidence any  more in the future. It is not surprising, therefore, that army cadres, as van-  guards of the progressive movement in our country, aware of their responsibility,
867    took action and set in motion a process of true transformation of society. On
25 February 1980, a state power was taken over. A process of decolonization and  national reconstruction was initiated under the guidance of the leader of the  Revolution, Desi Bouterse. That was the beginning of a new development policy in  Suriname to satisfy the basic needs of the entire people in the social, economic  and cultural fields. Our struggle for national liberation is not an easy one. The  former élite, linked with foreign capital groups which have lost their dominant  position, is continuously trying to frustrate our revolution.
10.7 The people of Suriname are determined to continue their struggle for  sovereignty and self-determination, first and foremost by working steadily and  consciously towards creating a true national economy, which aims at an increase in  production and a just distribution of the riches and products of our national  endeavours. Our people are also determined to create new democratic structures to  guarantee themselves full participation in the process of economic, political,  social and cultural development of our country.
10.8 Mr President, the struggle for national liberation of our people must find  reflection in our educational policy, because educational development is an essen-  tial step for liberation. Our position is that if a national, independent economy  is to be developed by increasing indigenous production, if true democratic struc-  tures in which everyone can participate are to be built, and if national unity and  consciousness are to be encouraged, then an educated people is needed.
10.9 Development of education serves as a good yardstick of the soundness of any  government, because knowledge and consciousness are powerful weapons for the people  in defending their real interests. Therefore, an anti-popular government will always  hesitate to educate people.
10.10 Mr President, the problem of illiteracy takes an important place in Unesco  policy, and there has been almost no head of delegation who has not mentioned  illiteracy in his statement. My government strongly supports every initiative in  this respect to solve this major problem of mankind. In this connection, may I  state that in Suriname, at the national level, a decisive step will be taken next  year. Two months ago, on 8 September, International Literacy Day, the Leader of  our Revolution, Lt. Col. Bouterse, announced a massive national literacy campaign  to begin on 1 August 1984, with the goal of eliminating illiteracy in nine months.  This implies that 70,000 people, i.e. 35 per cent of our adult population, will be  tzught to read and write, This is no easy task for my country, and we have  extremely difficult problems to solve, due to our complex ethnocultural and linguis-  tic situation, the absence of a written tradition in most of the spoken languages,  and a vast territory with a bad communications infrastructure.
10.11 Because of its specific peculiarities, this may be considered an important  experiment which will provide valuable information and experience for literacy  campaigns yet to be started in other countries. It therefore merits special atten-  tion and care on the part of Unesco,
10.12 On the occasion of the official launching of the campaign on International  Literacy Day, the Leader of the Revolution stated, and I quote: 'We shall never be  paralysed or diverted from the enormous tasks which we have. We have made a funda-  mental choice for our people and now we are facing one of the tasks which can no  longer be postponed. Because our struggle for a truly independent and just society  in which we are the architects of our own future can only be successful if we can  mobilize the full creativity and participation of our people'.
10.13 At the national level we count on the support of all national organizations  in the social, cultural, educational and religious fields. We also have support  from the labour organizations as well as youth and women's organizations. All of  them will be directly involved in the campaign. We are very grateful to Unesco and  UNDP for the financial resources already allocated, which will be used in the com-  ing months to develop and test the literacy teaching method used among various  linguistic groups of our population.
10.14 Mr President, our national literacy campaign will require quite an amount  of financing which Suriname, as a Third World country, is unable to furnish alone.  Our government is convinced that it can count on further support from Unesco and on  the support of Member States and organizations within the framework of Unesco. I  am referring not only to financial and material aid but also to know-how based on  the experience of those countries in Africa, Asia, the Middle East, Latin America  and the Caribbean which have already carried out literacy campaigns. Our national  literacy campaign is the first step in our struggle to eliminate arrears in educa-  tion among large parts of our population. It will be followed immediately by an  extensive adult education campaign. A team of experts has already begun to pre-  pare the programme which will commence in 1985.
10.15 Our literacy campaign which will put such a heavy strain on human and finan-  cial resources in solving the problem of illiteracy at one stroke is only one con-  crete example of our educational policy. A series of measures are being taken  simultaneously, to restructure our whole educational system which is of colonial  origin. Our educational system and curricula have always been based upon those of  our former Dutch colonizer and not on our own conditions and needs; they could  never, therefore, offer equal opportunities to everyone, but were available only  to a small privileged group. Equal educational opportunities can only bear fruit  if the socio-economic conditions of our people are also improved. Therefore, our  educational policy is only one expression of our government's intention to create  a just society, and measures are now being taken for a complete restructuring of  our education, so as to link intellectual education to productive work. Education  will be given special attention in the rural areas and in the interior, where the  percentage of drop-outs varies from 40 to 80 per cent. Adapted training for teach-  ers in these areas will commence in 1984, preparing them also to contribute to the  socio-economic development of these areas.
10.16 Mr President, another matter to which we should like to draw the attention  of the Conference is the necessity for the developing countries to write their own  history, instead of depending on history seen through the eyes of the former colon-  izers. It is therefore important that Unesco support the writing by nationals of  the history of the struggle against slavery, indentured labour, colonialism and all  forms of oppression. My country supports every initiative of this nature. And,  closely linked with history, we should mention the return of all cultural proper-  ties and archives to their countries of origin, for they are invaluable to the  political, social and cultural development of a country. We therefore strongly  support the work of Unesco in this field, as well as the United Nations Convention  on the matter.
10.17 Our country has enormous cultural diversity because of its multiracial  composition. Our cultural policy aims at preserving the authenticity of the various  cultures to the greatest extent possible, while trying to encourage cultural and  political unity. We shall thus support programmes of cultural co-operation with  countries whose cultures show great affinities wl.th ours. There are already bilat-  eral programmes on such cultural co-operation.
10.18 Mr President, to make our position quite clear, we wish to inform you that  we shall support all initiatives which will benefit the countries and movements  which have decided to struggle against underdevelopment, poverty, oppression and  exploitation and which struggle for national socio-economic and cultural indepen-  dence. In these countries, a process of decolonization is equivalent to the  struggle to escape from foreign cultural domination. We therefore feel that Unesco  should place even more emphasis on the Third World countries. We are convinced  that Unesco has accomplished much in this field and will continue to accomplish  much in the future. However modest our contribution, we shall do our utmost to  strengthen Unesco and to make this Organization into one which gives tangible sup-  port to the Third World countries by all means. Mr President, fellow delegates,  we feel quite sure that this twenty-second session will be fully successful. Thank  you very much.

12.3 Mr President, distinguished delegates, ladies and gentlemen, my delegation  attaches great importance to this session of the General Conference, which is  called upon to sum up the results of the Organization's activities over recent  years and to determine specific tasks for the subsequent period on the basis of the  second Medium-Term Plan. At the fourth extraordinary session last year, the del-  egation of the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan supported Unesco's Medium-Term  Plan for 1984-1989 as a vehicle for directing the activities of the Organization  towards the solution of global problems which affect the fundamental, vital inter-  ests of all countries and peoples, opening up new broad prospects for enhancing  its role in the world arena.
12.4 My delegation recognizes the importance of Major Programme I, 'Reflection  on world problems and future-oriented studies'. It is extremely desirable at pres-  ent to ensure that this programme has a content which will enable Unesco to contrib-  ute actively to the attainment of its primary objectives, namely the consolidation  of peace and security and the solution of the main global problems facing mankind,  while at the same time tackling the complicated socio-economic problems of the  developing - particularly the least-developed - countries, and developing and  enriching international scientific and cultural co-operation.
12.5 The draft programe for 1984-1985 submitted for consideration by the General  Conference generally corresponds to the guidelines provided in the second Medium-  Term Plan. All of us are  well aware that the prevention of nuclear war and the transition to disarmament,  being intrinsically global problems, directly affect the destinies of the develop-  ing countries, among them the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan. It is only in  conditions of durable peace and détente, through the redistribution of resources  released as a result of disarmament, that the developing countries can get broad  opportunities to overcome their economic backwardness inherited from the colonial  past. In this respect, I should like to refresh memories by stressing the content  of the New Delhi Message adopted by the Seventh Summit Conference of the Non-  Aligned Countries, which placed greater emphasis on peace and security, détente  and disarmament and peaceful coexistence and co-operation among nations. It is  important, therefore, to ensure that in terms of content, the relevant major pro-  gramme should be in line with the task of strengthening peace and should contrib-  ute to the solution of the complex socio-economic problems facing the developing  countries.
12.6 We fully support the efforts to intensify Unesco's activities aimed at  enhancing the role of the mass media in the struggle for peace and international  co-operation and against war and tension in relations among nations. We firmly  oppose the use of the mass media for the dissemination of false and slanderous  information, preaching the ideas of war and confrontation and publishing reports  sowing the seeds of conflict and enmity. My delegation stresses this matter  because the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan is subjected to slanderous propa-  ganda campaigns by a number of western media. They disseminate misleading infor-  mation and distort the real facts about the situation around Afghanistan which is  a direct outcome of foreign armed intervention in our internal affairs. There-  fore, while sharing the just demands of the developing countries concerning the  elimination of the monopolistic domination of the transnational corporations over  the flow of information, my delegation fully supports the activities aimed at  establishing a new international information order.

12.8 Major Programme VIII, 'Principles, methods and strategies of action for  development', and Major Programme XII, 'The elimination of prejudice, intolerance,  racism and apartheid', enjoy our full support. We believe that there is a close  link between the problems of peace, détente and disarmament and those of restruc-  turing international economic relations on a just and democratic basis and estab-  lishing a new international economic order. 12.10 It is the firm conviction of my delegation that the strict observance of  the principle of equitable geographical distribution of posts which would enable  Unesco to make the utmost use of the greatest achievements and experience of all  countries and peoples constitutes one of the essential elements in raising the  effectiveness of the Organization's activities.
12.11 Mr President, the April Revolution of 1978 created favourable conditions  and opened up broad prospects for the rapid social, economic and cultural develop-  ment of Afghan society. Proceeding from the vital interests of the people, our  government not only began to implement the planned development of the national  economy but also embarked on comprehensive programmes for public education, the  elimination of illiteracy and the development of science and technology in accord-  ance with the requirements of the present stage of socio-economic development of  our society. The peoples of all the different nationalities of our country are  now being taught in their native languages. Efforts have also been made to pre-  serve and enrich the cultural heritage and cultural identity of Afghanistan.
12.12 As all of you are well aware, however, the undeclared war being continuously  waged by the enemies of our revolution against the determination of our toiling  people to choose their own way of life has inflicted tremendous damage not only on  the economy but also on education, scientific work and the cultural heritage in the  Democratic Republic of Afghanistan. Besides committing inhuman terrorist acts  against the innocent civilian population of my country, the counter-revolutionary  bandits have brutally destroyed or burned thousar,ds of schools, libraries, other  educational institutions, hospitals and some elerrients of the cultural heritage.  The ominous source of the armed intervention against the Democratic Republic of  Afghanistan is no longer a secret. The criminal subversive activities of the  Afghan counter-revolutionary genocidal bandits are being inspired, organized and  financed by world imperialism and the reactionary, militaristic, fanatic and des-  potic regimes in the region in collaboration with hegemonistic circles.
12.13 Such unlawful and inhuman activities constitute a flagrant violation of the  United Nations Charter and the Constitution of Unesco, and are completely in con-  travention with the established rules and standards of international relations.  Nobody would dare deny the fact that it is not only Afghanistan which is a target  of imperialist intervention and aggression. The people of Palestine, Lebanon and  other countries of the Middle East, Chad, Angola and other frontline States of  Africa, El Salvador, Nicaragua and Caribbean countries are also the victims of  imperialism's expansionist policy pursued by war-mongering circles in a number of  western countries. On the day we attended the opening ceremony of the twenty-  second session of the General Conference of Unesco, all of us learnt of the un-  justified imperialist invasion of independent and non-aligned Grenada by foreign  occupation forces, In this respect, allow me, Mr President, to draw the attention  of the participants in the Conference to the 'Declaration of the Co-ordinating  Bureau of the Non-Aligned Countries on Grenada's situation'. At an urgent session  held at United Nations headquarters in New York on 26 and 28 October 1983, the Co-  ordinating Bureau of the Non-Aligned Countries, and I quote, 'expressed its grave  concern at the latest developments in Grenada, a member of the movement of non-  aligned countries ...'. The Bureau, in which the Democratic Republic of Afghanistan  is represented, further 'condemned the armed intervention in Grenada ... and called  for an immediate cessation of intervention and the immediate withdrawal of all  foreign forces from Grenada'.
12.14 In conclusion, I should like to stress once again that we in the Democratic  Republic of Afghanistan highly appreciate Unesco's activities. We believe that  this authoritative Organization will continue, as in the past, its fruitful endeav-  ours to serve its lofty goals of strengthening peace and security and broad inter-  national co-operation through education, science and culture. The adoption of the  Draft Programme and Budget for 1984-1985, following consideration of the construc-  tive and fruitful resolutions and proposals submitted by Member States, would be  an important contribution to Unesco's activities. I wish the Conference every  success. Thank you, Mr President.
