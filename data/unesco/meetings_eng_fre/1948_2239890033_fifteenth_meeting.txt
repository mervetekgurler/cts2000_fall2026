Proposal of the Arab States concerning the  translation into Arabic of certain publica-  tions of Unesco (document 3C/93).  The SECRETARY announced that the Budget  Sub-Commission had examined the resolution  and had provided for an allocation of $20,000; it  remained for the plenary meeting to consider the  resolution and authorize the work to be carried  out. He suggested that the matter should be  discussed at the same time as the report of the  Committee of Fifteen.  M. HUYSMANS (Belgium) feared that the  adoption of the resolution might create a precedent  for languages less widely used than Arabic.  M. SAGLAM (Turkey) remarked that, if such  a favour were granted to the Arabic-speaking coun-  tries, the Turkish delegation would present a  similar resolution and other delegations might  wish to follow suit. He therefore thought it  would be preferable to postpone further discus-  sion of the item until the following day.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) pointed out  that Unesco documents could not be published  anywhere in the world unless there was a re-  sponsible salaried officer in each area; the calls  made on the budget were therefore bound to be  higher than $20,000. He seconded the proposal  that further discussion should be postponed until  the following day.  Dr. MOLINARI (Argentina) said that he would  support the Arab resolution and the resolution of  any country that wished Unesco documents to be  translated into its own national language; that  was, in fact, the best method of spreading  Unesco’s ideas throughout the world.  M. SAGLAM (Turkey) agreed with the principle  postulated by the Argentine delegate. He feared  that, if a special favour were granted to the Arab  countries, other language groups would resent it;  if the Conference adopted the Arab resolution, he  hoped that a larger budgetary allocation would be  made for the item in 1950, so that the privilege  could be extended to all major language groups.  He reiterated his proposal that the item should  be postponed until the following morning, in the  hope that a solution might be found before the  resolution was submitted to the plenary meeting.  M. HUYSMANS (Belgium) was impressed by  the enormous ambition of Unesco compared with  its meagre budget; the Organization was  attempting to save the world with $8,000,000 a  year, and it would make itself ridiculous 1f it  attempted to undertake anything but the most  vital tasks. He, therefore, hoped that the Arab  resolution would not be adopted.  It was agreed that the discussion should be  continued the following day.
