Mr. HOLLAND (United States of America)  (Chairman of the Drafting Committee) read his  Report, calling attention to an amendment to  document 3C/98, contained in the Addendum to  document 3C/PRG/0.5, which recommended that,  in Part Ill, item 4 (Social Sciences and Huma-  nities) should be divided into two sections :  item 4 : “Social Sciences” and item 5 : ‘‘Philo-  sophy and Humanistic Studies”. ‘Cultural Acti-  vities would then become item 6, and “Com-  munication” item 7.  Mr. Holland proposed the adoption of the reso-  lutions contained in document 3C/98 and its  Addendum.  The CHAIRMAN asked whether there were any  general comments.  Sir John MAUD (United Kingdom) said that  the drafting committee, of which he had been a  member, had been faced with the difficult  question would their fellow delegates in the  plenary meeting be confused if the drafting com-  mittee attempted to change either the order of  the items inside each Chapter, or the order of  the Chapters in the Programme as a whole. He  himself was convinced that the Drafting Com-  mittee had taken the right decision when it  decided to try to improve on the programme  passed at the Mexico City Conference the previous  year, and which had come down from the Exe-  cutive Board almost unchanged in document 3C/5.  The Programme adopted at Mexico City was  a great deal better than any programme the  Conference had had to work on before, but it  had proved rather difficult to explain at home  some of the chapter titles (for instance “Cultural  Interchange” and “Human and Social Relations”)  and to justify them either as coherent parts of  Unesco's work, or as intelligible sub-headings of  the total Programme. The Programme presented  to the Commission differed, in respect of those  two Chapters, from the plan agreed at Mexico  City the previous year.  The second difficulty which had been found  in the previous year’s programme was the lack  of any very clear logic in the order of the six  Chapters into which the programme had been  divided; there again the drafting committee pro-  posed to the Commission an arrangement of the  Chapters which Sir John Maud thought was more  sensible.  Various delegates, and the Director-General in  his Report, had suggested that a phrase which  might sum up the whole work of Unesco was  “to help the world to become more civilized.”  As Sir John saw it, the programme presented for  the Commission's discussion showed, under seven  headings, the seven ways in which Unesco dared  to think that it could help to make the world  more civilized : firstly, by helping to reconstruct  those parts of the world devastated by the war  (therefore Reconstruction was the first Chapter);  secondly, by helping the world to become better  educated (the second Chapter was, therefore,  Education). The three succeeding Chapters dealt  with the “SS” of Unesco (natural sciences, social  sciences and humanistic studies). For the “C”  of Unesco, Chapter 6 was called “Cultural Acti-  vities , and dealt with the part of Unesco’s pro-  gramme which aimed at helping the world to  become a place in which the creative spirit of  man could work more freely, and the works of  that creative spirit could be more widely and gen-  erally enjoyed. The last Chapter was still called       “Communication” and set out the various ways  by which Unesco could make the world more  civilized by assisting people to meet more freely.  Speaking as a member of the United Kingdom  delegation, Sir John explained that his delegation  strongly supported the new arrangement of the  programme.  Dr. De MENDOZA (Cuba) asked the Com-  mission to include in its Report to the General  Conference his resolution (already mentioned in  the Budget Sub-Commission’s Report, page 8)  concerning a Regional Centre in the western  hemisphere; the resolution read as fellows :  The General Conference decides to prepare the  execution of the resolution of the General Confer-  ence regarding the establishment of a Regional Centre  in the western hemisphere;  (a) the preliminary studies and consultation  required to prepare for the creation of the proposed  centre will be entrusted to a responsible member of  the Secretariat;  (b) the Director-General is instructed to submit  a financial plan to the Executive Board as soon as an  agreement for the creation of a Regional Centre in  the western hemisphere has been reached with the  Organization of American States, and the Admini-  strative Committee on Co-ordination of the United  Nations and Specialized Agencies, with a view 10  financing the proposed Centre during the remaining  months of 1949.  The first months of 1949 would be taken up  with study and consultation and the details of the  agreement; it was, therefore, unlikely that the  Centre could be established before May or June  and the cost would consequently be reduced by  approximately half.  M. FLORES (Venezuela) supported the proposal.  The CHAIRMAN reminded the delegate of Cuba  that the question had already been dealt with in  the Joint Programme and Official and External  Relations Commission, and then submitted to the  Budget Sub-Commission, which had informed the  Programme and Budget Commission that funds  were not available to carry out the project. Later,  of course, the Report of the Official and External  Relations Commission, which contained a para-  graph concerning Regional Centres, had been sub-  mitted to, and approved by the General Conference.  The Chairman proposed that the Cuban dele-  gate’s proposal should be noted and transmitted  to the next plenary meeting, which was to deal  with the Report of the Programme and Budget  Commission.
