TENTH MEETING  Tuesday, 30 November 1948, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman:  Programme of meetings for the day.  M. MONTAGNIER (Head of the Bureau of Con-  ference Planning) presented the proposed pro-  gramme of meetings for the day.  On a point of order raised by Mr. ALLEN (United  States of America), the DIRECTOR-GENERAL  stated that the Budget Sub-Commission would  meet immediately after the Nominations Com-  mittee had taken the first item on their agenda  (the nomination of members of the Budget Sub-  Commission).  In reply to a question from Professor KABIR  (India), M. MONTAGNIER (Head of the Bureau  of Conference Planning) referred to the resolution,  adopted at the first plenary meeting, which laid  down that the Budget Sub-Commission should  consist of twelve persons chosen by the Nomi-  nations Committee, giving special consideration to  those delegates who had worked most closely with  the three main Commissions, especially the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission.  The programme of meetings for the day was  adopted.  Amendments to the Constitution (proposal of  the delegation of India) (continuation).  Professor KABIR (India) withdrew his dele-  gation’s second proposed amendment to the Con-  stitution, which had been considered at the pre-  vious meeting of the General Committee. as they  feared it might be a cause of embarrassment to  the new Director-General.  Establishment of a special Committee to  consider preparations for the Fourth Ses-  sion of the General Conference.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) thought  that the creation of a special Committee to consi-  der preparations for the Fourth Session of the  Conference was quite unnecessary. That was a  matter within the competence of the Executive  Board, and the establishment of a special com-  mittee would only lead to duplication of work.  Hamid Bey FRANGIE FE (Lebanon)  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) confirmed that, under the terms of the  Constitution, planning of the general programme  of work for Conferences was the responsibility of  the Board. There had already been preliminary  discussions of the arrangements for the Fourth  and Fifth Sessions of the Conference, although no  decision had so far been reached on the site of  the next session. The Board, however, wished for  guidance from the General Conference, and for  that reason a special Committee had been sug-  gested by the Secretariat.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) wel-  comed the proposal for a special Committee,  which would give delegations who were especially  interested in the problem, and yet not represented  on the Board, an opportunity to air their views.  He proposed the establishment of a committee of  ten persons chosen from those delegations which  had shown special interest in the problem.  Dr. MOLINARI (Argentina) seconded the pro-  posal, but suggested that countries represented on  the Executive Board should not be represented on  the Committee.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa) main-  tained that consideration of the problem by a com-  mittee of only ten members would be even more  restrictive than its consideration by the Executive  Board, which consisted of eighteen members. He  proposed either that the matter should be left  entirely in the hands of the Board, or that a  special committee, representative of all dele-  gations, should be established.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) consi-  dered that the phraseology of the item on the  Agenda was too restrictive. The United States  delegation wanted consideration to be given to  the general question of conference preparation,  not merely to preparations for the Fourth Session  of the Conference. While opposing the suggestion  for a committee consisting solely of members of  delegations not represented on the Executive  Board, Mr. Allen nevertheless thought that such  delegations should be given first priority.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) maintained that  the creation of a small committee would not give     sufficient opportunity for a large number of dele-  gations to express their views, and proposed that  the recommendations of the Executive Board on  the whole question of conference preparation  should be submitted to a plenary meeting of the  Conference for a general discussion.  In answer to a question from Professor KABIR  (India), the DIRECTOR-GENERAL agreed that the  General Committee, at an earlier meeting, had  referred the problem under discussion to the Joint  Committee of Fifteen, but, on further reflection,  that Committee had not seemed the appropriate  body to consider the question. He proposed that  the problem should be studied by an informal  meeting of all heads of delegations, together with  members of the Executive Board.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) put the pro-  posal formally to the meeting but, with the sup-  port of Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil), insisted that  the meeting should be a formal, rather than  informal, one.  M. MONTAGNIER (Head of the Bureau of Con-  ference Planning) point out that complications  might ensue if a formal meeting were held and  passed resolutions contrary to the recommen-  dations of the Executive Board.  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) remarked that, under the Provisional Rules  of Procedure, it would be improper for a com-  mittee of the General Conference to consider the  place of the next session of the Conference until  the Executive Board had made its recommen-  dations.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) with-  drew his proposal for a special committee of ten,  in favour of the proposal of the delegate of the  United Kingdom.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) also sug-  gested that the special meeting should submit a  report on its discussions to a plenary meeting of  the Conference.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL endorsed that sug-  gestion.  In answer to a question from Mr. ALLEN  (United States of America), the DIRECTOR-  GENERAL stated that heads of delegations could, if  they desired, be assisted by a member of their  delegation.  The proposal of the delegate of the United King-  dom for an official meeting of the heads of dele-  gations, together with members of the Executive  Board, to report to a plenary meeting of the Gen-  eral Conference on its findings, was adopted.  It was agreed that the meeting should take place  during the morning of Saturday, 4 December.  Consideration of the report submitted by the  Procedure Committee on the question  referred to it by the General Committee.  Dr. NIELSEN (Chairman of the Procedure Com-  mittee) gave a brief summary of the discussions  of the Procedure Committee on the problem of the  election and re-eligibility of members of the Exe-  cutive Board. The question under discussion had  been whether a member of the Executive Board  was eligible for immediate re-election, even if he  were not a member of his country’s delegation to  the Conference at which the election took place,  and if he were not proposed by his country’s dele-  gation. As the meeting had found that it was  not necessary, under the Constitution, for a can-  didate to be proposed by his country’s delegation,  discussion was restricted to the eligibility of a  candidate who was not a member of his country’s  delegation to the Conference. Basing their view  on Article V. A, paragraph 3, of the Constitution,  some members thought that membership of a  delegation was immaterial. Other members,  basing their view on Article V. A, paragraph 1,  considered that membership of a delegation was  essential. The Secretariat's Legal Adviser inclined  to the latter view. Still other members thought  that the question should be referred to the Inter-  national Court of Justice, in accordance with  Article XIV, paragraph 2, of the Constitution.  After a lengthy discussion, it had been agreed, by  a majority of one, that membership of a delegation  was not an essential condition for candidature.  Since the solution of the problem was no longer  urgent, Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) proposed that the matter should be  removed from the agenda of the plenary meetings,  and that the General Committee should request  the Procedure Committee, in considering the draft  Rules of Procedure, to make provision for the  establishment of an arbitral tribunal to consider  the question and any others of a similar nature  which were not sufficiently important to be  referred to the International Court of Justice, as  provided for in Article XIV, paragraph 2, of the  Constitution.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa), in  seconding the proposal, suggested that the  Director-General should appoint a competent  draftsman to study the Constitution and pick out  the points which needed clarification in that con-                 fused document. They could then be considered  by the next session of the Conference.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL promised to trans-  mit that suggestion to his successor.  The proposition of the Chairman of the Exe-  cutive Board was adopted.  Other Business.  In answer to a question from Dr. MOLINARI  MONTAGNIER (Head of the Bu-  reau of Conference Planning) confirmed that  simultaneous interpretation from Spanish into  English and French would thenceforth be possible  in the Conference Hall. The verbatim records of  the meetings held in that room would not, how-  ever, contain the original text of any speech made  in Spanish.  (Argentina), M.  The meeting rose at 10.45 a.m.  ELEVENTH MEETING  Wednesday, I December 19468, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman: M. Victor DORE (Canada)  Programme of meetings for the day.  The programme of meetings for the day was  adopted.  The SECRETARY announced that the Joint  Committee of Fifteen wished to meet the following  day; the meeting was fixed for 10.30 a.m. on  Thursday, 2 December.  Membership of the Executive Board.  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) reported that the Nominations Committee  had chosen the six persons to be nominated to  the General Conference for election as members  of the Executive Board.  He then raised the question of how best to  amend the Rules of Procedure, to ensure that all  the members of the Board attended meetings.  Dr. B. Carrion was generally absent, and was not  even able to send a deputy, so that geographical  representation on the Board had been less good  than it should have been.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa) con-  sidered that the question involved a change in the  Constitution itself; the problem could not be  solved by a mere alteration in the Rules of Pro-  cedure.  M. SEYDOUX (France) suggested that it would  perhaps be possible to settle the matter unoffi-  cially, instead of going through a complicated
233  procedure in a simple case. A member of the  Board might write to Dr. Carrion, suggesting  that he should appoint a colleague to represent  him.  The CHAIRMAN agreed with that solution,  which, however, did not prohibit an amendment  of the Constitution by the normal procedure.  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) remarked that, in that case, an amend-  ment would have to be prepared immediately, as  it was proposed that the next session of the Con-  ference should be held in May. Either the Pro-  cedure Committee or the Administrative Com-  mission might be asked to help.  Dr. NIELSEN (Denmark) (Chairman cof the  Procedure Committee) saw no objection to the  submission of the matter to the Procedure Com-  mittee.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL pointed out that it  would be desirable to consult a competent legal  expert on the matter.  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) thought that it would possibly also be  wise to consider any precedents which might  exist.  It was agreed that the question of the length  of the term of office of members of the Executive  Board should be considered, in due course, by the  Procedure Committee.  The meeting rose at 10.15 a.m.
