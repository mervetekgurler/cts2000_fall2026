12.6 According to the famous second law of thermodynamics, every closed natural system,  and in all probability the universe as a whole, is condemned to increase its entropy, totend,  that is, to disorganization and chaos. Living beings, as we all know, are not exempt from  this rule. Many living beings, man among them, extend their organizational  activities to their environment in order to transform it, as far as possible, into an hospit-  able vessel for life.
12.7 During the thousands of years covered by historical times, the rate of technological  progress was extremely slow. Man, of course, created admirable civilizations, developed  art, religions and theories about the world and about life, and organized the patterns of his  behaviour, creating social and political organizational systems. One must not however for-  get that progress centred primarily around man's concepts about the universe in which he  lived and very little around the methods by which he could exploit the natural resources of  his environmert. What effort man could spare from his efforts to survive, was directedat  conquering his world conceptually and symbolically. And the languages, the myths and works  of art that he created are the fruits of exactly such an endeavour and the expression of a  stochastic confrontation of the problems of survival and progress. These expressions and  attitudes were formed by the prevailing social, political and economic forces in the early  societies.
12.8 The situation changed dramatically 150-200 years ago, spurred by the rapid develop-  ment of the natural sciences and by favourable socio-economic and ideclogical concepts.  We were thus led into the period of the industrial revolution, rapidly followed by the con-  struction of more and more technological means to harness the forces of nature and offer  more and more conveniences to society and to man.

He who has lost his innocence becomes un-  certain in his thinking. Uncertainty of thought cannot lead totrue knowledge". These words  were spoken about 2,500 years ago by an old Chinese farmer. The machine he was so re-  luctant to accept was a simple fulcrum designed to assist him in lifting water from his well.  Even our ancestors, who gave human form to the Olympian Gods that personified the various  forces that ruled the ancient world, conceived of Hephaestus, the God of Technology, as an  ugly and lame old man.
