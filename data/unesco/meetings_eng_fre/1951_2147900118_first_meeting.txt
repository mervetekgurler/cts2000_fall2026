CHAPTER XII COMMITTEE ON REPORTS FIRST OF MEMBER STATES MEETING Saturday, 23 June 1951, at 10.30 a.m. Chairman: Mrs. GERÓNIMA Pecson (Philippines) Opening of the Session: Election of Officers. [1] The CHAIRMAN opened the session and asked the Committee to proceed to the business of electing its officers. She suggested that the Committee should confirm the officers designated by the General Conference in accordance with the relevant Rules of Procedure. A motion to that effect was proposed by Mr. SMITH (United States of America) and adopted unanimously. Adoption of the Agenda. [2] Mr. DARD (Head of the Division of Relations with Governments and National Commissions) drew the Committee's attention to the important items on the provisional agenda. With regard to item 4, the Committee would have to decide whether it was going to consider only the 20 reports sent in by the date fixed, or whether it would also consider the other 20 that had arrived late. Underitem 8, the Committee, in accordance with the terms of reference laid down, by resolution 30.214 of the Florence Con- ference, had to formulate its observations “concerning the co-operation of Member States in Unesco's work”. [3] As no objections were raised, the agenda was adopted. Question of the Committee’s Method of Work. [4] The CHAIRMAN reminded the Committee that it had been set up in virtue of a resolution of the Fifth Session of the General Conference (5C/Reso- lution 30.214). She drew attention to the fact mentioned in the observations of the Director-General on the reports submitted for 1950 (document 6C/4 Introduction) that whereas the deadline fixed for thereception of reports was 15 March, only 20 reports had been received by that date. As a result, it had been possible to translate and circulate only 20 reports before the Conference met. Those reports were con- tained in a printed volumeentitled Reports of Member States presented to the Sixth Session of the General Conference (document 6C/4). Some 20 reports from other Member States had been received after the deadline, and were consequently too late to allow of their being translated and circulated. They had, however, been multigraphed in their original lan- guage and were available in that form for the mem- bers of the Committee. The question was whether the Committee wished to consider all the reports, including those received late, or only the 20 con- tained in the printed volume. [5] Mr. BRANDT (Switzerland) thought it would be only fair to consider all the reports, including those received after the deadline. He proposed moreover that, rather than embark on the laborious task of examining all the reports in detail, the Committee should limit the scope of its investigations, and con- sider how Member States’ reports could be presented in more standardized form. [6] Mr. EUSTATHIADES (Greece) thought that the small Committee, meeting for the first time that day, ought perhaps to begin by defining its field of com- petence; it was not, he felt, its business to criticize the activities of the various Governments. The Com- mittee’s task was to report on the form and pre- sentation of thé reports; as for their content, he thought that neither the Committee nor even the General Conference was in a position to pass an opinion on the accuracy or the shortcomings of Member States’ reports. That was a delicate point, which needed clearing up. [7] Enche OTHMAN (United Kingdom) thought that it would be a long and arduous task for the Committee to examine all the reports in detail and present a report to the General Conference. He    REPORTS OF therefore suggested that a working party should be formed to go carefully into the reports and submit draft observations and recommendations to the Com- mittee. He proposed that the working party should consists of the representatives of Australia, the United States of America and Switzerland. [8] Mr. DARD pointed out that, though the special Committee in Florence had proposed that reports received late should not be distributed before the end of the Conference, the resolution adopted by the Florence Conference actually contained no such rigorous provision. That was why the Director- General had thought that the reports might at least be distributed, even if not translated. The Com- mittee was free to decide whether or not it wished to examine those reports. [9] Moreover, resolution 30.214 made it clear that the Committee had to take a decision, not only on the form of the reports submitted, but also on the activities of the various Member States, i.e., on the substance of the reports. Nevertheless, it was clear that what the Conference expected of the Committee was a general survey, which did not imply criticism of the activities of specific States. [10] Mr. McNICOL (Australia) thought it would be impossible for a small working party to study all the reports. That was a task for the Committee as a whole. [11] The CHAIRMAN agreed with the Australian delegate’s remarks. The Committee’s task was to produce a report which would show the relationship between Unesco and the Member States, and also to stimulate Member States to carry out the programme of Unesco with maximum efficiency. In her view, not only would it be more practical to decide to con- sider only those reports which had been received in good time and which had been translated and published, but in that way a certain amount of moral pressure would also be brought to bear upon States which had not sent in their reports in time. [12] Mr. TUNCEL (Turkey) thought that, before starting to examine the reports of Member States, the Committee should discuss the general plan drawn up by the Secretariat. What he was asking the Com- mittee to consider was a question of method. [13] Mr. SMITH (United States of America) said that, as his country was the only one represented on the Committee which had not submitted its report in time, he would be the last to insist that all
40 reports should be examined. He thought that the question of the time-limit should be considered in relation to the complexity of the form of the reports, and also that additional criteria should be established for the preparation of future reports. [14] Mr. MASSAQUOI (Liberia) said that the MEMBER SEXES XII-1 reports submitted by Member States constituted the main criteria with regard to the degree of co-operation of Member States in the Unesco pro- gramme. On the technical question whether the Committee should take into consideration all the reports, or only those which had been submitted in time to be translated and published, he was of opinion that all the reports should be examined. There was muchto be learnt from the reports, and if a complete picture was to be drawn in the sense of the resolution adopted at the Fifth Session, it would be a mistake to leave aside 50 per cent of the information available. [15] The CHAIRMAN suggested that the Committee might proceed to examine the reports contained in the printed volume, and later set up a working party to examine the others. [16] Mr. LUCET (France) thought that the Com- mittee should settle the interlocutory question of whether the substance or only the form of the reports sent in had to be studied. If it was only a question of the form, members of the Committee could obtain a general idea of those reports which had been received late and had not been translated; if, on the other hand, the Committee had to examine the sub- stance, it would doubtless have to confine itself to the first 20 reports, which had been published in the three working languages of Unesco. [17] Mr. TUNCEL (Turkey) asked the Committee to decide on the question of method which he had raised. He thought it very important that the Com- mittee should discuss first of all the plan drawn up by the Secretariat, before starting to study the reports. [18] Mr. EUSTATHIADES (Greece) thought that, if the Committee had to examine the reports in detail, it would be better, in view of the importance ofits task, if more Member States were represented on it. [19] Mr. DARD pointed out that the wording of resolution 30.214 left no doubt as to the scope and validity of the Committee’s terms of reference; more- over, the members of the Committee had been regu- larly appointed at the eighth plenary meeting of the General Conference. [20] Mr. ATASSI (Syria) suggested that the Com- mittee might set up a small sub-committee to study, not each report separately, but MemberStates’ reports as a whole, from the point of view of their conformity with resolution 30.214; and then present to the Com- mittee a review which should be, to some extent, statistical but should not pass judgment on the contents of each individual report. [21] Mr. FAYAT (Belgium) did not think there could be any question of the validity of the Com-        REPORTS OF mittee’s mandate. He read out Article VIII of the Unesco Constitution, which laid down that each Member State should furnish a periodical report to the Organization. Paragraph 6 of Article IV further provided that the General Conference should receive and consider the reports submitted periodically by Member States as provided by Article VIII. There could be no doubt that questions of both substance and of form were envisaged under Article VIII. [22] Mr. Fayat thought that the point raised by the Turkish delegate might be considered later under item 5 or 7 of the Agenda. He was glad to see that the Turkish delegate signified assent to that sug- gestion. [23] Mr. BRANDT (Switzerland) said the Com- mittee should examine all the reports received, for, to ignore reports sent in late would imply some censure of the States concerned; but he thoughtit would be a waste of time for the Committee to make too detailed a study of the substance of the reports. In his opinion, the Committee's task should be to record the reports and to concentrate chiefly on con- sideration of items 6 and 7 of the Agenda, for- mulating recommendations on the form to be adopted for future reports. In conclusion, the representative of Switzerland asked the Committee to reconsider Turkey's proposal, and to decide to make a general survey of the reports before it. [24] Miss MERCER (United Kingdom) thought it was undoubtedly the task of the Committee to examine the reports from the point of view of both form and content. One of the matters on which the Committee had to take a decision was whether the desiderata formulated by Unesco were realizable and reasonable. Failure to report or lateness in reporting MEMBER STATES might not be due solely to the shortcomings of Member States. [25] Mr. EUSTATHIADES (Greece) explained that he had not wished to question the validity of the Committee's terms of reference; his remark referred to the future, and his purpose had been to recom- mend the Conference to enlarge the Committee, in view of the importance of its work. [26] Mr. Eustathiades agreed with the views expressed by the representative of Switzerland; he, too, thought that the Committee should make a general survey of the reports and, in particular, submit observations regarding the future. [27] Mr. FAYAT (Belgium)stated that, as his coun- try had been one of the first to send in its report, he would not like to be too hard on the others. He pro- posed that a rider should be added to any resolution passed, pointing out that it was important for coun- tries to submit their reports by the deadline, in order to permit of their being translated and circulated to delegations well before the Conference. [28] A vote was taken, and it was decided, by eight votes in favour, none against and no abstentions, to examine all the reports received. [29] It was also decided that the attention of MemberStates should be directed to the importance of submitting their reports in good time. [30] It was further agreed that the decision just taken should not be regarded as constituting a pre- cedent, but that, in the future, reports received after the deadline might be disregarded.
