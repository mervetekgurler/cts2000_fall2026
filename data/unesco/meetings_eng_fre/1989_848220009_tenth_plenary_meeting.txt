1.1 The PRESIDENT:  Your Excellency, Mr Mario Soares, President of the Portuguese Republic,  Excellencies, Mr Chairman of the Executive Board, Mr Director-General,  distinguished delegates, ladies and gentlemen, it is indeed a great privilege  for me, in my capacity as President of the twenty-fifth session of the General  Conference of Unesco, to welcome here today His Excellency Mr Mario Soares,  President of the Portuguese Republic.
1.2 Mr President, your presence here is undoubtedly a manifestation of the  profound commitment of your country, Portugal, to the cause of international  co-operation and to the ideals that Unesco stands for; and your participation in  our deliberations here testifies to the importance you have personally attached  to the role that your Government and people must play, together with the rest of  the world community, in fostering the global, intellectual and moral solidarity  of mankind upon which rest the defences of lasting peace for this generation and  for generations to corne. It is, therefore, with great appreciation and interest  that we look forward to your message to the General Conference.
1.3 Your Excellency, the current session of the General Conference is  considering, among other documents, the Draft Medium-Term Plan for 1990-1995  which addresses the challenges confronting mankind the world over, which, as the  present Century is drawing to a close, Unesco must respond to, meaningfully and  effectively, through planned actions in its established fields of competence:  education, science and culture. These actions, needless to say, place the  highest demands on the international community's resources of intelligence and  creativity.
1.4 May 1 take this opportunity to inform Your Excellency that, from the  Chair, 1 could not help but see that, in their deliberations, the delegations to  this Conference from a11 Member States have demonstrated the highest degree of  determination and conviction regarding what they believe to be the duty of the  international community - to correct inequalities and imbalances within and  between nations SO that universality is achieved and human indignity erased from  the face of the earth: through the eradication of the illiteracy that hampers  the progress of the developing countries; through the elimination of  technological gaps between the rich and the poor nations; and through the  maintenance of a free two-way flow of information and communication between  nations. This determination and conviction, 1 believe, augurs well for the  future of mankind. And your presence at this session of the General Conference,  Sir, Will undoubtedly contribiite to the further strengthening of that conviction  and determination.
1.5 Mr President, the Portuguese people, as history records it, has  contributed in no small measure to the wealth of the world's heritage of  intellectual and creative expression. Portugal has charted a course of history  for itself in the past. Now Portugal is known as the land of great discoverers,  such as Vasco da Gama and Pedro de Marcorena. It is also the land of great  poets such as Luis de Camoes and Fernando Pessoa. In the South-East Asian  region, where 1 corne from, Alfonso de Albuquerque left his footprint in Goa and  in Malacca, where a Portuguese community is still to be found and where a great  gate of the famous Portuguese fort, A Famosa, still stands.
1.6 SO much for the past, Mr President. Now, in contemporary times, we are  greatly encouraged to see that Unesco's authentic commitment to the universal  endeavour to enhance human dignity the world over is well matched by the  characteristic role played by Portugal in the affairs of our Organization since
1965, when it rejoined us. It was with great joy and emotion that Portugal was  welcomed back to our fold on that occasion; and it is with the same enthusiasm  that we are happy to welcome your Excellency to our meeting today.
1.7 I now give the floor to Mr Vargas, Chairman of the Executive Board.

The Board is composed of
51 individuals acting both in their persona1 capacity and as the representatives  of their countries. Originating in six regions of four continents, the Board  represents a wide plurality of culture and political options - a microcosm of  our world. Among the countries representing the General Conference of Unesco in  the Executive Board there are 12, from a11 the continents, where the Portuguese  cultural presence was a marked one and in some cases continues to be important  to this day. This testifies to the universal vocation of your country, a true  bridge-builder among nations and cultures.
2.2 In the past, the Portuguese caravels took to the remotest corners of  the world, across the oceans, the new visions forged in Europe by the  Renaissance, which emerged from that most original blend of the  Greco-Judaeo-Christian-Arabie vision that had evolved over thousands of years in  the Mediterranean basin. Portugal itself being a bridge between the  Mediterranean and the wide ocean, it was natural that geography would generate a  people especially inclined to become the vehicle of cultural interchange, first  with the African civilizations in the Atlantic and later with those beyond the  seven seas in a11 continents.
2.3 It was through Portugal mainly that Europe absorbed the new ways of  thinking and living characteristic of novel cultural diversities. This entailed  a new world vision which later gradually inspired in large parts of the world a  spirit of mutual tolerance which hopefully continues to assert itself despite  the pangs and clashes of human passion.
2.4 The visit of a Head of State is always a very special event for our  Organization. In welcoming you, the most distinguished representative of the  people of Portugal, to Unesco, a meeting place of the world cultures, 1 am  reminded that our House symbolizes also the solidarity of a11 the peoples of the  world.
2.5 Your visit is therefore a source of great encouragement and support to  our activities. In fact, your presence among us coincides with the important  juncture in our institutional history when this session of the General  Conference Will chart the direction of Unesco's endeavours for the next six  years, which Will be based on the promotion of peace, development, human rights  and the protection of the environment. 1 am sure, Mr President Soares, that  these aims meet with your enthusiastic approval.
2.6 For, Mr President, you are a lawyer, historian and a statesman of wide  renown whose career has been built around these very ideals. However, it is  your action as a fighter for freedom which should be mainly stressed here and  now. Having suffered yourself exile and prison for freedom's sake, not only did  you fight courageously from your very youthful days for the liberation of  Portugal from obscurantism and oppression; you also played a seminal role in the  liberation of Guinea-Bissau, Mozambique, Angola, Sâo Tomé and Cape Verde - a11  represented here today as sovereign States - when YOU were charged with  negotiating their independence on behalf of the newly established democratic  government in your country. As a leader of the Socialist International, you  have pursued the same ideals, heading the Committee for Solidarity with Latin  America, and later with the Middle-East peoples, victims of oppressions which in  many cases unfortunately prevail to this day.
2.7 In Europe, as Prime Minister of your country, YOU initiated the  negotiations leading to Portugal's accession to the European Community in 1985,  thus strengthening further the ties with Europe without losing the identity  built up over eight hundred years.

4.2 Epris, par nature, de paix et de liberté, M. Mario SOARES, président de la République portugaise :  (Discours prononcé en portugais, texte français fourni  par la délégation)  Monsieur le Président de la Conférence générale, Monsieur le Directeur  général, Messieurs les ambassadeurs et membres des délégations, Mesdames et Mes-  sieurs, c'est pour moi un grand honneur que de pouvoir prendre la parole à cette  vingt-cinquième session de la Conférence générale de l'Organisation des Nations  Unies pour l'éducation, la science et la culture, institution à laquelle incombe,  selon moi, une responsabilité spéciale dans le monde contemporain.
6.2 J'étais bien sûr très jeune quand, le 4 novembre 1946, cette organisa-  tion spécialisée de 1'ONU a été créée dans le but de contribuer au maintien de  la paix et de la sécurité, en resserrant, par l'éducation, la science et la  culture, la collaboration entre les nations, afin d'assurer le respect des prin-  cipes de la Charte des Nations Unies. J'étais très jeune mais j'ai bien compris  - engagé comme je l'étais déjà dans la lutte pour la défense des libertés au  Portugal - l'importance d'une telle organisation et le saut qualitatif que sa  création représentait dans l'évolution des conceptions universalistes quant à  l'unité, a la solidarité et à l'égalité fondamentales du genre humain, par-delà  les considérations de race, de sexe ou de condition sociale.
6.3 C'est pourquoi j'ai ressenti comme une humiliation pour le pays le fait  que le Portugal ne fasse pas partie des 44 pays fondateurs de 1'Unesco. Lorsqu'il  y entra plus tard, en 1965, bien qu'encore soumis à un régime dictatorial, je  n'ai éprouvé aucune satisfaction particulière. Bien au contraire, je dois vous  l'avouer. Déjà engagé comme il l'était dans les guerres coloniales, dépourvues  de sens et d'avenir, le Portugal a dû abandonner l'Organisation en 1971, pour  n'y revenir - mais alors, véritablement! de plein droit - qu'en 1974, après la  "Révolution des oeillets", lorsque j'etais ministre des affaires étrangères.  Imaginez donc l'émotion - je dirais même l'orgueil justifié - avec laquelle je  vous parle ici aujourd'hui comme représentant d'un pays libre, tolérant, pleine-  ment démocratique et ouvert, fidèle à ses honorables traditions humanistes et en  harmonie avec les idéaux et les grandes causes universelles que 1'Unesco a pour  mission de défendre.
6.4 Nous vivons aujourd'hui une époque de changement accéléré, où tout se  transforme à un rythme auquel on ne pouvait s'attendre et où les signes du temps  nouveau confirment nos vieux motifs d'espoir qui ont subsisté même pendant la  dictature, les guerres froide et chaude et le cauchemar de la "diplomatie au bord  de l'abîme". La détente internationale, les mutations qui se produisent à l'Est,  l'aspiration universelle à la liberté qui jaillit de partout irrépressible, les

Il n'y a jamais eu, depuis la dernière guerre, autant de raisons si bien  fondées de croire à la paix et en une véritable politique de détente internatio-  nale. 13.1 Mr GONGAR (Liberia):  Mr President of the General Conference, Mr Director-General of Unesco,  Mr Chairman of the Executive Board, fellow delegates, ladies and gentlemen, 1  bring you greetings and warm felications on behalf of the President, Government  and people of the Republic of Liberia. 1 salute and congratulate Mr Anwar  Ibrahim, on his election to steer the affairs of this twenty-fifth session of  the General Conference, and 1 reaffirm the Liberian Government's and people's  faith in, and commitment to, the ideals and principles of the Organization.
13.2 Mr Director-General, we note with satisfaction the efforts aimed at  restructuring this our Organization SO that it becomes more responsive to the  needs of suffering humanity. Your role as one of the major organizers of the  World Conference on Education for Al1 is an attestation to the fact that Unesco  is still committed to the provision of a literate citizenry for our one world.  It is our hope that those nations blessed with the wealth of the world Will see  wisdom in coming to the aid of such a worthwhile venture, SO that mankind Will  burst the chains of illiteracy. My delegation congratulates you on two years Of  diligent stewardship. We look forward to more progress under your leadership.
13.3 1 am pleased to inform the Conference that the trend of educational  development in Liberia has taken a dramatic and realistic turn as we close the  decade of the 80s and prepare for entry into the decade of the 90s: a new
10  ten-year cycle that is shortly to commence with a reassessment of the impact of  sectoral and dimensional strategies in relation to our national human resources  development needs.
13.4 At this terminal point of the decade, our educational system has been  vigorously reviewed through a major sector assessment followed by our latest  quadrennial educational policy conference held in February of this year. The  conference was attended by leading Liberian educators along with representatives  of this august body as well as other multilateral organizations and agencies  involved in the activities of the knowledge industry. The conference convened  under the theme: Agenda for improving educational efficiency in the 1990s; it  reviewed the implementation of existing educational policies and derived new  ones in accordance with ouf present manpower development requirements. These  policies and regulations are now being crystallized to help raise the standard  of education at the primary, secondary and tertiary levels and to stimulate  sectoral efficiency and the effective use of available facilities, including  human, financial and other resources. Moreover, the harmonization of the  conference recommendations with those of the 1988 sector assessment has been  concluded as the basis for the formulation and development of our next Five-Year  Education Plan, which is presently being drafted. As designed, the plan  incorporates a11 sectors of our educational system, focusing mainly on policy  directions, objectives for the achievement of policy and strategies for its  implementation.
13.5 At the pre-primary level, effort is concentrated on the entry point.  Over the years, this sector of the primary level has had -the single largest  enrolment of pupils with a concurrent high drop-out rate before reaching grade  one (first grade). TO deter this trend, which would ultimately lead to a high  percentage of illiterate adults, a considerable portion of the five-year plan is  being devoted to strengthening this entry stage of our educational system. The  outlook is for training more female teachers, whose gentle hands in nurturing  Young school-age children in pre-grades is considered indispensable. Our primary  education subsector is based on the use of both programmed institutional  techniques as well as the traditional system. A major project aimed at  establishing a firm foundation for primary education is now in its third year.  We are convinced that at the end of this project a solid foundation for primary  education Will be established to improve the quality of the education received  by our youths.
13.6 The Five-Year Education Plan calls for a mother-tongue reading and  writing component that Will encourage students to appreciate their language and  culture, enrich their perception and equip them to be able to read and Write  effectively in the mother tongue. Accordingly, plans are being finalized to  begin instruction during the next academic year in four of our local languages.  At the secondary level, the plan, based on present needs, is for concentration  on science, maths, language and arts instruction that Will provide the necessary  basis for further education. Training of science teachers as well as the  provision of additional laboratory facilities for new and existing high schools  are part of the national imperative at this level.
13.7 1 shall now turn to adult and non-forma1 education. Since 14 February
1950, when the Government of Liberia officially launched and proclaimed a  literacy campaign, successive administrations have continued to make substantial  financial and human resources commitments for the development of a literate  society. The goal of a literate society is still high on our national agenda  today. This explains why our Government strongly embraces the Unesco Regional  Programme for the Eradication of Illiteracy in Africa by the year 2000 and  remains committed to active participation in a wide range of Unesco activities.
13.8 On this note, we quote a relevant portion of the message our President,  His Excellency, Dr Samuel Kanyon Doe, issued on the occasion of International  Literacy Day only a few weeks ago: 'As we observe this memorable day, we are  concerned about the high illiteracy rate which continues to plaque our world as  a result of rapid population growth in the face of global economic constraints.  Liberians must, therefore, remain committed to providing the type of adult  education facilities that Will enable us effectively to achieve our objective of  eradicating illiteracy by the close of this Century. This Will require the  pooling of our limited resources, time and effort to fight this common enemy.  Our literate youths must organize themselves into corps of volunteers during  their annual vacation sessions and go out into the hamlets ar,a villages  throughout the length and breadth of this country to teach their unfortunate  brothers and sisters to read and Write. By SO doing, you Will be accelerating  our national development process'. The challenge contained in this message has  been accepted by the Ministry of Education and efforts are being made to  organize a corps of youths to facilitate implementation.
13.9 With regard to higher education, our Government has passed the  necessary legislation for the creation of a National Commission on Higher  Education - a body of national decision-makers and technicians to accredit,  regulate and monitor the creation of a11 colleges and universities within our  territorial limits. The operation of this Commission is shortly to be set in  motion in an effort to de-escalate the proliferation of substandard institutions  of higher learning.
13.10 At the tertiary level, the University of Liberia has embarked on a  massive campaign to involve working adults through a continuing education scheme  in training courses for positions of responsibility in government, business and  the professions. Massive efforts are being planned to involve institutions in  the production of high-quality research with a view to formulating appropriate  policies and programmes to enable Liberia to achieve self-sufficiency in food  production, which under the name of the Green Revolution has today become a  household word in Liberia.
13.11 For too long universities in Africa have been perceived as ivory  towers, but today our National University has become more sensitive to the  realities of an illiterate population and to the dearth of highly trained  technical personnel in our nation. Accordingly, a collaborative venture is in  process under which the University of Liberia could train literacy teachers in  the community.
13.12 For the effective implementation of our goals of training high-quality  personnel to meet our manpower needs, to produce top-quality research and to  establish monitoring points for the elimination of toxic wastes from our  environment, we wholly support, among other things, the sections of the budget  relating to science and technology and to environmental and natural resource  management.
13.13 Mr President, our belief in the free flow of information and the  freedom of the press is evidenced by at least five daily newspapers, private  radio stations and the establishment of communication links with the entire  world. We in Liberia are committed to the belief that a11 people, regardless of  their social, religious or financial status, are entitled to equal protection  under the law and must be provided with the basic necessities for a meaningful  life.
13.14 Our support for the Unesco programmes aimed at meeting the three  challenges of our time - peace, development and the protection of the  environment - is demonstrated by the programmes and new directions being charted  for our educational system briefly described above under the heading 'Agenda for  improving educational efficiency in the 1990s'. As the conscience of the world,  Unesco must continue to serve as the Organization which seeks to crystallize the  divergent views and differing interpretations of human rights, press freedom,  social justice, environmental pollution, and SO on, in a single constructive  approach aimed at providing for succeeding generations a better world than the  one in which we find ourselves today.
13.15 Historically, my country has been a strong opponent of any and a11  forms of racial discrimination. Our stand against apartheid is internationally  known. In consonance, therefore, with this policy, we wish to express our  endorsement of the special project dealing with apartheid.
13.16 Mr President, it is the view of my delegation that the consciences of  those nations endowed with the wealth of the world must be awakened to the  importance of contributing to the eradication of a11 barriers that inhibit the  acquisition of basic knowledge and skills in order to facilitate development.
13.17 The pollution of the environment, the destruction of the ozone layer,  the production of such things as toxic waste are a11 by-products of the-  achievement of industrialized society. Unfortunately, it is we in the  developing world who must now exert efforts to fight against these by-products.  If collective effort is required to combat adverse effects, it is but just that  the gains from these achievements should also be shared collectively.
13.18 On this note, Mr President, we renew the cal1 we made from this podium  in 1987, by joining our voice to the voices of our colleagues who appealed to  those former members of the Organization which withdrew voluntarily from it to  reassess their action in the light of prevailing circumstances. The task of  making the world free from illiteracy, which tends to hamper social mobility and  progress, is a collective responsibility of a11 nations. Unesco, as the world  body established for this purpose and entrusted with this responsibility, must  in the interests of a11 nations be made a global organization.
