The meeting rose at 5.35 p.m.  MEETING  Monday, 29 November 1948, at 10.30 a.m.  Chairman: Mr. D. R. HARDMAN (United Kingdom)  Item 8 of the Agenda : Relations with the  United Nations.  The DEPUTY DIRECTOR-GENERAL made a  few introductory remarks on the development of  co-operation and co-ordination between the United  Nations and the Specialized Agencies. The chief  obstacle to effective working relations was the  fact that each Agency had its own charter, mem-  bership and form of organization; every effort  was being made to overcome that obstacle. It was  essential that Member States should ensure proper  co-ordination among their own government  departments, so that their representatives at  meetings of the various Agencies would pursue  the same aims and policies; progress was already  apparent in that respect. The Economic and  Social Council was increasingly showing its aware-  ness of the special responsibility which it bore,  under the terms of the United Nations Charter,  for ensuring co-ordination between the Agencies  on the policy level. The Secretary-General of the  United Nations had formed an Administrative  Committee on Co-ordination (consisting of the  Secretary-General himself and the Directors-  General of the various Agencies) which was trying  to develop procedure and administrative machi-  nery common to all the Agencies. Progress had  already been made on such questions as the time-  table of meetings, presentation of budgets, staff  regulations, allocation of regional offices, etc.  Little co-ordination had, however, been achieved  so far in one very important field—that of advance  planning of programmes, which would make pos-  sible concurrent consideration of similar proposals  at conferences of more than one Agency—though  the need for such planning and the need for the  Economic and Social Council's leadership in the  matter had been recognized at the Council's  meeting in Geneva in the summer. Much closer  co-ordination was needed, though there had been  instances of co-operation during the past year (as  in the Unesco Pilot Project for Fundamental Edu-  cation in Haiti, which had developed into a joint  UN-WHO-ILO-FAO-Unesco undertaking, and in  the question of teaching about the United Nations,  on which Unesco had held a seminar at Lake  Success during the summer.  The Deputy Director-General informed the  meeting that a memorandum was being drawn  up and would be circulated as soon as available,  giving answers to all the questions concerning  Unesco’s relations with the United Nations and  the Specialized Agencies which had been raised  by the delegate of the United States of America  at the second meeting of the Commission.
