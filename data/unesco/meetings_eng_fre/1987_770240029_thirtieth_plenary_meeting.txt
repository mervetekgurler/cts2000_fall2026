6.1 Mr WHITLAM (Australia) :  Thank you, Mr President. I wish to support the amendment moved by the  delegate of Jamaica and seconded by the delegate of Canada. I am not concerned  with the text of the original proposal by Canada and Australia to amend the  Financial Regulations, which I argued before the Legal Committee. I am not  concerned with the substitute text recommended by the Legal Committee, which was  drafted by members of a working group in which I was kindly invited .to par-  ticipate. I am not criticizing the text. My sole point in intervening is this:  that we should postpone this matter to the twenty-fifth session of the General  Conference. There are ten Legal Committee reports on a single group of proposals  to amend the Constitution and the Financial Regulations made by Canada and  Australia. Halt of them are being recommended by the Legal Committee to be held  over to the twenty-fifth session of the General Conference, and my contention -  like that of Jamaica and Canada - is that they should all be put over to that  session. The fact of the matter is that by no fault of the Administrative  Commission and by no fault of the Legal Committee - if it was anybody's fault, it  might have been that of the General Committee - there has been no opportunity for  them to confer together.
6.2 Be that as it may, the Administrative Commission has to make a recom-  mendation to plenary about the appointment of a new External Auditor, a position  which has fallen vacant because the Auditor last appointed by the General  Conference belonged to a Member State which has now withdrawn, and has  accordingly lost his qualification for the post. This Conference must thus elect  a successor. It can do so under the Financial Regulations as they stand, which  state that it is for the General Conference to decide on the period of  appointment.
6.3 Mr President, as we have been told by the distinguished Chairman of the  Administrative Commission, the discussions in that body up to now have centred on  the proposition that the External Auditor to be elected next week should be  elected for two years, which means that there is no urgency about it. There are  four candidates for the two-year appointment, and none of the Member States of  which they are the Auditors-General are contemplating withdrawing from the  Organization, so we have this very simple timetable: next week the General  Conference must elect an External Auditor; the Administrative Commiscion will  probably recommend - and the four Candidates themselves are happy about this -  that the appointment be for a duration of two years; none of the countries  concerned are going to withdraw from the Organization, so there can be no trouble  whatever in electing an External Auditor for two years under the Financial  Regulations as they stand.
6.4 I do not wish to answer any of the other arguments which have been  brought up ostensibly as clarifications, because they are, in fact, obfuscations.  I ani not criticizing the text; I am criticizing the timing. I support what  Jamaica and Canada have said. That will solve the matter in accordance with our  regulations as they stand; it will accord with our timetable; it will accord with  our membership. It means we will have an External Auditor with no further fuss.  Thank you very much, Mr President.

En ma qualité de représentant de Madagascar, je me suis abstenu lors du  premier vote pour une double raison, qui est fort simple : d'une part, je par-  tage l'avis exprimé par le Président de la Commission administrative .dans cette  salle même ce matin et, d'autre part, Madagascar propose un candidat aux Eonc-  tions de Commissaire aux comptes.
35.2 En ce qui concerne la deuxième proposition mise aux voix, Monsieur le  Président, nous avons vote pour. En effet, Monsieur le Président, pour mon pays  et pour moi, il est impossible de ne pas prendre en compte le travail considé-  rable qui a été accompli par le Comité juridique, même si je ne suis pas entie-  rement d'accord avec ses résultats. En tout cas, Monsieur le Président, j'ai  participé souvent 5 des réunions de travail et je sais combien la tâche est dif-  ficile et nécessite de concessions de la part de chacun. Je vous remercie.
36. Le PRESIDENT :  Je vous remercie. La parole est au délégué du Nigeria.
37. Mr INKO-TARIAH (Nigeria) :  Thank you, Mr President. I supported the proposal by the Legal Committee  because I thought it was a job well done, and it does afford Unesco a good basis  for appointing an External Auditor. I also supported it because it is not good  for Unesco to appoint ad hoc or temporary auditors: it is unwholesome for Unesco  to indulge in that. I would also say that, as regards the third Medium-Term Plan,  I strongly believe that the job of the External Auditor is to look at the  accounts of a preceding biennium and not to concern himself with the future. SO I  think there would not be much relevance in talking about the third Medium-Term  Plan now, when we are trying to appoint an External Auditor for Unesco. And  Unesco must have an External Auditor. For these reasons, Nigeria voted for the  amendment proposed by the Legal Committee. Thank you, Mr President.
38. Le PRESIDENT :  Je vous remercie. La parole est au délégué du Japon.
39. Mr NISHIZAKI (Japan) :  Thank you, Mr President. We abstained on the proposal by the  distinguished representative of Jamaica because, although we saw merit in it, we  did not see that it would definitely solve the problem of an External Auditor  whose country might withdraw from the Organization during his term of appoint-  ment. We supported the proposal by the Legal Committee for the amendment of  Article 12.1 of the Financial Regulations because, on the basis of our experience  in the debate in the Administative Commission during this session on the  financial report of the Organization, we consider that the External Auditor must  be in a position to give proper advice in the discussions on that item. That is  why we supported the Legal Committee's proposal. Thank you, Mr President.
40. Le PRESIDENT :  Merci. La parole est au délégué de la Jamaïque.
41. Mr WYNTER (Jamaica) :  Mr President, I simply wish to place on record that it is not correct to  say that Unesco has for the last 40 years been appointing ad hoc External  Auditors, nor temporary External Auditors; and Article 12.1, which has served US  faithsully for 40 years, has not been improved by the excellent work done by the  Legal Committee, which has simply added the fact that the External Auditor should  be on probation for two years, or more precisely - the word 'probation' was not  used - should be appointed for two years in the first instance and if, at the end  of two years, there is no objection raised by the General Conference the  appointment should automatically be extended for four years. This is simply a  slight gloss on the present position of appointing an External Auditor for six  years. It has not answered the question of what happens if, after appointing him  or her, the country from which he or she comes withdraws in the middle of a  biennium. And that was the major question put to the Legal Committee through the  amendment of Australia and Canada. And, Mr President, I simply want it to be  recorded that we are disappointed that that question has not been dealt with, has  not been answered, together with our plea that the matter should be looked at -  and very carefully - in the next two years. What happens also if a man resigns  and his successor refuses to accept the post? What does Unesco do? We felt that  all these things should be looked at again and despite the vote, Mr President, it  is my hope that the Executive Board will look at these matters in the next two  years so that we can clean the whole thing up at the twenty-fifth session of the  Conference. Thank you, Mr President.
