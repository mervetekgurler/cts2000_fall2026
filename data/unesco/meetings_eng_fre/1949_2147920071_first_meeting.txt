SUMMARY RECORDS OF THE MEETINGS OF THE PROCEDURE COMMITTEE FIRST MEETING Monday, 19 September 1949, at 4 p.m. Chairman: Dr. J. C. Beacrenore (New Zealand) Election of Chairman. Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America), sec- onded by Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France), pro- posed the delegate of new Zealand, Dr. Beaglehole, as Chairman. Adopted by acclamation. Dr. J. C. Beaglehole took the Chair. Election of Vice-Chairman. On the proposal of Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France), Dr. Ludwig Steiner (Austria) was unani- mously elected Vice-Chairman. Election of Rapporteur. Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America) pro- posed the delegate of Czechoslovakia as Rappor- teur, but Dr. BOOR (Czechoslovakia) declined. On the proposal of Mr. PILE (United Kingdom), Mr. Mulliken (United States of America) was unanimously elected Rapporteur. Adoption of the Provisional Agenda (doc- ument 4C/PRO/1). Mr. RODRIGUEZ (Mexico) asked whether he could have an additional item included in the Agenda, otherwise he would be obliged to make certain reservations concerning the Agenda or even to vote against it. Dr. SABA (Secretariat) pointed out the diffi- culties of including a new item in the Agenda. Items had to be presented at least thirty days in advance in order to be included in the Agenda (Rule 11 of the Rules of Procedure). During a session, such new items had to be approved by a majority of two-thirds of the General Conference (Rule 14 of the Rules of Procedure). The Com-
454 mittee was therefore unable, on its own account, to include additional items in its Agenda. He also said that the present session of the Conference was a very short Working Session and could not there- fore in principle examine new items. Mr. RODRIGUEZ (Mexico) asked whether he could submit a written statement. Dr. SABA (Secretariat) replied that any state- ment could be received by the Committee under the heading “Other Business”, but without pre- judice to the Agenda of the General Conference. The Agenda was then adopted. Draft Amendment to Article IV of the Con- stitution presented by the Director-General (document 4C/PRO/2). Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America) sup- ported the proposed amendment. Mr. AUREGLIA (Monaco) said that the text sub- mitted to the Committee filled in a gap not only in the Rules of Procedure but also in the Con- stitution, for neither Article IV nor Article IX of the Constitution made any reference to sanctions against a State failing in its financial obligations. The Rules of Procedure also made no mention of sanctions. An amendment was therefore neces- sary. The proposed text did not, however, en- tirely fill in the gap, for it merely provided for a limited sanction (suspension of the right to vote), and made no provision for exclusion. Was that intentional? What would be the position of a State which did not fulfil its financial obligations even after sanctions had been taken against it? He thought that the question might be discussed later. Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America) sup- ported the amendment on the understanding that the provision that a Member State whose contribu-            tions were in arrears should have no vote in the General Conference, should apply to the Com- mittees and Commissions of the Conference. Mr. NICHOLAS (Australia) said that his del- egation was under the impression that the second paragraph of the proposed new paragraph 9 might be difficult of interpretation, and proposed a new second paragraph as follows: . provided that it is satisfied that a Member State is in arrears as a result of conditions beyond the control of the Member State, the General Conference may, at any Session, permit the Member State to vote, provided also that such permission shall apply only to the Session at which the decision is made. The Australian delegation would propose that paragraph as a substantive amendment at the next session of the General Conference, but wished to have it noted immediately in the report. On a strict reading of the Rules, Mr. Nicholas assumed it was not possible to introduce that amendment at the present session. Mr. PILE (United Kingdom), referring to the point raised by the delegate of Monaco, thought it was quite clear that the proposed new para- graph 9 was an intentional move on the part of the Conference, as it had realized that there were no provisions, either in the Constitution or any- where else, to cover States who had not paid their financial contributions. It therefore took the step of introducing the same provisions which existed in other Specialized Agencies and in the United Nations. Mr. Pile thought the Committee should accept the amendment as it stood. With regard to the Australian amendment, he did not consider there need be any objection to that amendment being discussed at the present meeting. Mr. OGRODZINSKI (Poland) pointed out that the Third Session of the General Conference had anticipated an improvement in the economic situation, but that had not occurred; even Great Powers were confronted with financial difficulties and the proposed amendment would establish a discrimination against poor countries or soft- currency countries. He therefore proposed that the amendmentshould be postponed until the next session of the General Conference. Dr. BOOR (Czechoslovakia) and Dr. BARBA- GELATA (Uruguay) seconded that proposal. Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France) thought that the remarks of the Polish delegate were sound, but that the text of the proposed amendment itself met the case mentioned by him, since the General Conference may, nevertheless, permit such a Member State to vote, if it is satisfied that the failure to pay is due to conditions beyond the control of the Member State.
455 Professor NIELSEN (Denmark) thought it only fair that the right of disposing of benefits which might accrue from being a Member State of Unesco should be restricted to those Member States who bore the burden of contributions, though a State not in a position to pay should keep the right of taking part in discussions. He therefore opposed the Polish amendment. Mr. OGRODZINSKI (Poland) said that it would be a generous gesture towards a country that was short of foreign currency. He therefore main- tained his proposal. Mr. ZUTTER (Switzerland), explaining his del- egation’s views, said that, as regards the remarks of the delegate of Monaco, the fact that the sanc- tions mentioned by the amendment were limited to the suppression of the right to vote was certainly intentional. As regards the Australian suggestion, he thought that it was already covered by the second paragraph. In connexion with the Polish proposal, Switzerland feared that the absence of any provision for sanctions would deprive Unesco of financial resources. The Swiss delegation would therefore vote for the amendment proposed by the Director-General. Mr. OGRODZINSKI (Poland) pointed out that his proposal merely aimed at postponing the amendment until the next session of the Confer- ence. A discussion took place as to whether the Com- mittee should not vote first on the postponing of the amendment until the Fifth Session, then on the amendment presented by the Australian del- egation, and finally on the amendment proposed by the General Conference at its Third Session. Dr. SABA (Secretariat) said that the amendment to Article IV of the Constitution had been drafted after the last session and submitted to Member States six months in advance. The new text was that of Article 19 of the United Nations Charter adopted in San Francisco. The interpretation given to the latter was that, if the delay in pay- ment was due to exceptional circumstances, the defaulting State could be permitted to vote, but for one session only. The Rapporteur could make it clear that the privilege of voting would be only for the current session. That would remove all the anxieties of the Australian delegate. Mr. HACHEM (Syria) emphasized that, even if a defaulting State were deprived of the right to vote, it should at least enjoy its other privileges as a Member of Unesco. Dr. SABA (Secretariat) said that the amendment concerned merely the right to vote. The CHAIRMAN put to the vote the postponing   of the proposed amendment until the next session. The postponement was rejected. Mr. OGRODZINSKI (Poland) proposed an amendment to the text under discussion (doc- ument 4C/PRO/2, page 2). He asked that the words “and if the Conference so desires” should be inserted after the words “for the preceding two full years”. That would obviate the need of the following paragraph. The amendment was put to the vote and it was rejected. After some discussion, Mr. NICHOLAS (Austra- lia) withdrew his amendment, but wished it to be understood that the Australian delegation re- served the right to move it on a subsequent occa- sion and that this be mentioned in the report. After an exchange of views between the CHAIR- MAN, Mr. OGRODZINSKI (Poland) and Mr. PILE (United Kingdom) on points of procedure, Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France) said that it was obvious that the Committee could adopt amendments to the text proposed. As regards the substance of the question, he did not agree with the Polish delegate, for, if sanctions were not taken, the budget might well be voted by States who did not contribute to Unesco’s finances. The CHAIRMAN stressed that any alteration in the amendment already sent to Member States would require the text to be circulated again at least six months in advance (Article XIII of the Constitution). The Draft Amendment to Article IV of the Con- stitution (document 4C/PRO/2, page 2) was put to the vote and it was adopted. BEAGLEHoLE (New Zealand) Measures proposed to secure concurrencebe- tween the terms of office of members of the Executive Board and the Director-General and ordinary sessions of the General Con- ference (documents 4C/PRO/5 and 4C/PRO/5- Corr. ). The SECRETARYsaid that, owing to the fact that the Fifth Session of the General Conference (to be held in May 1950) would be almost six months earlier than usual, there would be a certain altera- tion in the rhythm of the work of the General Conference and particularly in the terms of office of the Director-General and of the members of the Executive Board. He pointed out that doc- ument 4C/PRO/5 proposed three alternative pro- cedures. Alternative I, which was the simplest, consisted in completely adhering to, and strictly applying the existing texts. But it had the disadvantage of preventing concurrence between the term of office of members of the Executive Board and sessions of the General Conference. Members of the Board were responsible to the General Conference for carrying out the programme adopted by the latter. If that alternative were adopted, however, they might, therefore, have to be carried out by other relatively irresponsible members. That disad- vantage was so serious that two other alternatives had been proposed to make the term of office of members of the Board coincide with the sessions of the Conference; either by lengthening it so that members of the Board would serve for three years and six months (Alternative II), or by shortening it to two years and six months (Al- ternative III). Alternative IT was fairly simple from the legal point of view. It would require an amendment to the Constitution providing that “elected mem- bers of the Executive Board shall serve for three working years from the close of the session of the General Conference at which they are elected until the close of the third ordinary session of the General Conference following that election...”. The text of such an amendment could be sub- mitted to Member States so that the Conference in Florence could vote an amendment to the Con- stitution.
