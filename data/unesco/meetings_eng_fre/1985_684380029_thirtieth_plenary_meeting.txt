1.1 Le PRESIDENT :  La séance est ouverte. Mesdames, Messieurs, à l'ordre du jour de nos  travaux, pour la séance de cet après-midi, figure l'examen du point 3.3 (Adoption  du plafond budgétaire provisoire pour 1986-1987). Les documents concernant ce  point sont : le document 23 Cl5 (Projet de programe et de budget pour 1986-
1987), les documents 23 C/5 Rev.1 et Rev.2 (Révision des prévisions budgétaires  pour 1986-1987 proposée par le Directeur général), le document 23 CI6 et Add.1 et
2 (Recommandations du Conseil exécutif sur le Projet de programe et de budget  pour 1986-1987) et le document 23 C/106, Partie I, ainsi que le document 23 CI104  qui contient la recommandation du Bureau à la Conférence générale en ce qui  concerne l'adoption du plafond budgétaire provisoire pour 1986-1987.
1.2 Pour l'examen de ce point, je donnerai la parole successivement au  Président du Conseil exécutif et au Président de la Commission administrative. 2.1 Mr SEDDOH (Ghana) , (Chairman of the Executive Board) :  Mr President, Mr Director-General, distinguished delegates, ladies and  gentlemen, Article V.B, paragraph 5 (a) , of Unesco's Constitution provides that  the Executive Board 'shall examine the programme of work for the Organization and  corresponding budget estimates submitted to it by the Director-General ... and  shall submit them with such recommendations as it considers desirable to the  General Conference'.
2.2 I am happy to report that the Executive Board has faithfully discharged  this responsibility assigned to it under the Constitution; it is now my duty to  submit the Board's recommendations concerning the provisional budget ceiling for  the financial period 1386-1987.
2.3 Mr President, in the presentation I made to the General Conference on
9 October by virtue of Article V.B, paragraph 9, dealing with the report of the  Executive Board on its own activities in 1984-1985, which is now reproduced in  document 23 C/INF.18, I submitted the Executive Board's recommendations on the  Draft Programme and Budget for 1986-1987 as contained in document 23 C/5. As.wil1  be recalled, these recommendations were formulated by the Board after detailed  and careful examination at its 121st and 122nd sessions. Document 23 C/6 contains  the recommendations made at the 121st session and I draw the attention of the  General Conference once again to paragraph 198 thereof which refers to the budget  in particular as follows:  ['The Executive Board,]  Submits for examination by the General Conference, with a favourable  recommendation, the Draft Programme and Budget for 1986-1987 (23/5),  bearing in mind [the Board's] observations and recommendations concern-  ing the adjustments that should be made to this Draft, it being under-  stood that the Board's final recommendations on the budget proposed will  be made at its 122nd session'.
2.4 These final recommendations were indeed made during the first part of  the 122nd session of the Executive Board. In this connection I would like to draw  your attention to Part I of 122 EX/Decision 4.1 reproduced in document 23 C/6  Add. which reads as follows:  'The Executive Board,
1. Having examined 23 C/5 Rev.1,
2. Recommends to the General Conference to adopt a provisional budget  ceiling of $382,645,000 for Parts I to VI11 of the budget, which  would provide for a total appropriation of $295,354,000 after
1078    transfer of the difference to Part IX of the budget and srbject to  the provisions of the Appropriation Resolution for 1986-1987'.
2.5 Mr President, as document 23 C/104 now before the General Conference  points out in paragraph 2, the figures I have just quoted were based on the  United Nations operational rates of exchange for the United States dollar in  force as at 1 August 1985, i.e. 8.75 French francs and 2.30 Swiss francs to the  United States dollar. The figures contained in document 23 C/5 Rev.1 were sub-  mitted to the Board during its 122nd session. They were arrived at by the appli-  cation of budgeting techniques in drawing up the Draft Programme and Budget for
1986-1987 in conformity with resolution 44 adopted by the General Conference at  its twenty-second session on the instructions of the Executive Board contained in
120 EX/Decision 4.1 inviting the Director-General inter alia to 'compute the pro-  vision (negative) under Part VIII (Currency Fluctuation) of the budget on the  basis of the United Nations operational rate of exchange prevailing in the month  preceding the month when the 23 C/5 document is finalized: the provision will be  revised on the basis of the rate prevailing in the month preceding the month when  the Executive Board makes its final recommendation and further revised on the  basis of the rate prevailing in the month preceding the month when the General  Conference adopts the total budget'.
2.6 Mr President, it was on the basis of this directive that the Director-  General subsequently submitted in document 23 C/5 Rev.2 estimates based on the  rates of exchange for October 1985 fixed at 8.10 French francs and 2.19 Swiss  francs to the United States dollar. The budget ceiling he therefore proposes is  identical to that which the Executive Board would have recommended if it had used  the October rate of exchange. This amount is $398,468,000 as stated in document
23 C/104. Document 23 C/104 also makes it clear that on the same basis, the  Executive Board's recommendation concerning Part IX of the Budget would have been  to place in reserve the sum of $91,245,000, leaving a total appropriation or  Operating Budget of $307,223,000. The Director-General's proposal was discussed  two days ago by the General Committee which has submitted for the consideration  of the General Conference the draft resolution in paragraph 4 of document
2.7 Mr President, it will be recalled that in his introduction to the  general policy debate, the Director-General spoke about the concern expressed by  several members of the Board during its 122nd session with regard to the Board's  earlier recommendation to reduce subventions to international non-governmental  organizations, and in order to avoid reopening the consensus reached in the  matter, he offered to make a suggestion to the General Conference on how the  situation could be remedied when the provisional budget ceiling would be  discussed.
2.8 The Director-General's suggestion consisted in increasing the total  amount of appropriations for Parts I to VI11 by the sum needed to maintain all  the subventions to the NGOs as first priority. The sum concerned would be added  to the actual budget while the budget would be maintained at the proposal level.  This suggestion was fully discussed by the General Committee, but as the  Director-General explained in his reply to the general policy debate this morning  it was not retained by the General Committee. The position was that several  delegations, especially the very ones which had wanted the subventions restored  to NGOs, found themselves unable to agree to any solution which would imply an  increase in the assessed contributions of Member States.
2.9 As a result, the provisional budget ceiling now before the General Con-  ference is $398,468,000 for Parts I to VIII, providing for an appropriation of  $307,223,000 after transfer of the difference to Part IX of the Budget. These  figures are wholly identical to those which would have been recommended by the  Board at the October 1985 rates of exchange.
2.10 Mr President, the occasion offered by the adoption of the provisional  budget ceiling is a moment of truth and mere profession of faith in the noble  ideals of the Organization is not enough. We must stand up and be counted and  this august body can do so in no uncertain terms by approving the provisional  budget ceiling which I strongly recommend to it for adoption. Thank you, Sir.

8.1 Mr SINGH (India) :  Mr President, distinguished delegates, it augurs well for our Organiz-  ation that on this important question of the provisional budget ceiling for 1986-
1987 there is unanimity between the Director-General's proposals of $398,468,000  and the Executive Board's recommendation. However, as explained in document
23 C/104, this will mean an effective budget of $307,223,000 for the biennium. My  delegation fully supports this figure and hopes that all Member States will also  endorse this amount unanimously.
8.2 I am fully aware that the amount proposed unfortunately provides for a  zero-programme growth, a concept to which India does not subscribe. Zero-growth  is a negative concept. The needs of developing countries are large and growing  and a zero-growth concept hinders development. I wish to emphasize that I hope  that the zero-growth idea will not prove to be a precedent but only a temporary  measure caused by financial stringency, and that in future biennia, Unesco's pro-  gramme and budget will allow for a reasonable and positive programme growth in  real terms.
8.3 The world is becoming increasingly interdependent, and multilateral co-  operation should grow through an increase in funds placed at the disposal of  international organizations such as Unesco. Both the developed and the developing  countries need such multilateral co-operation to survive and to progress. Soon  Unesco will be celebrating its fortieth anniversary: let us on this occasion  rally to support our Organization so that it can fruitfully carry out the task  assigned to it and promote the ideas enshrined in its Constitution. Let us  remember that the effective budget proposed is only equivalent to only some  $150,000,0@0 per year, a small sum for an organization which has more than 150  Member States.
8.4 It would be relevant, distinguished delegates, when one discusses the  budget ceiling, to recall the loss of some $43,000,000 in 1985 due to the with-  drawal of a major contributor to Unesco's budget, that is to say, the United  States of America. This withdrawal has seriously curtailed the programme voted by  the General Conference at its twenty-second session. The Director-General has  used all ingenuity, and together with the Executive Board has tried to maintain  the overall integrity of the programme for 1984-1985. Several Member States,  including India, have come to render a helping hand and provided voluntary con-  tributions to the amount of $9,000,000.
8.5 I wish to commend the efforts of the Director-General to introduce  reforms and streamline the functioning of the Secretariat. I congratulate the  Executive Board which through its Temporary Committee has made steps towards  improving the management of the Organization. All this has proved that Unesco is  a resilient organization, and has withstood the financial shock of a loss of
25 per cent of its budget in 1985. But, distinguished delegates, looking to the  future I wish to appeal to the United States of America to rejoin the Organiz-  ation as soon as possible, thus bringing back to it not only its budget contri-  butions, but also the rich contribution that this country can bring to Unesco  through its large reservoir of talent in the fields of education, science,  culture and communication.
8.6 In this context, distinguished delegates, may I also appeal to the  United Kingdom and Singapore to reconsider their intent of withdrawal and remain  within the Organization, thus restoring the universality of Unesco. Mr President,  it is in this hope and spirit that my delegation strongly supports the proposal  put by the Director-General and recommended by the Executive Board, and I hope  all delegates to this General Conference will support this unanimously and by  acclamation, thus reaffirming our faith in our Organization. Thank you,  Mr President.

The seminar also dealt with the  budgets of international organizations. It was agreed that there were three principles  to be observed. The first was that there should be a basis of comparison and that a  stable currency should be adopted in preparing those budgets. That principle has been  applied in drawing up the budget before us, since it is based on the constant dollar  value. The second principle was that under the present circumstances it was necessary  to take account of the rate of inflation so as to ensure that the figures had a basis  in reality. This principle has also been observed in the budget before us, since pro-  vision for a rate of inflation of 5 per cent has been made in Part VI1 so as to  reflect the actual situation. The third principle, which has also been followed in the  budget before us, was the need to make allowance for the lapse factor. This is like-  wise a sound principle conducive to budget transparency and to a more faithful reflec-  tion of reality.

25 per cent, in funds, leading to the creation of a new Part IX in the budget for what  are figuratively described as 'blocked funds' but which actually represent a reduction  in the total appropriations available for this budget.
