1.1 The PRESIDENT:  Ladies and gentlemen, I declare open the twentieth plenary meeting. We have just one item this  afternoon: item 3.3, 'Adoption of the provisional budget ceiling for 1992-1993". The following documents relate  to this item: 26 C/5, 26 C/5 Rev. 1 and 26 C/5 Rev. 2; 26 C/6 and 26 C/6 Add. which contain the Executive  Board's recommendations on the Director-General's proposal, adopted at its 136th session; and 26 C/135 which  contains the General Committee's recommendation which, in turn, is based on the recommendation made by  the Administrative Commission.
1.2 Before we proceed to the adoption of the provisional budget ceiling for 1992-1993, I shall first give the  floor to the Chairman of the Executive Board; then to the Chairman of the Administrative Commission; and  finally to the Director-General.
1.3 The Chairman of the Executive Board has the floor.
2.1 Mr ALIYU (Nigeria), Chairman of the Executive Board:  Thank you, Mr President. Mr President please allow me to introduce, in a few words, the  recommendations of the Executive Board on the Draft Programme and Budget for 1992-1993. As is customary,  Mr President, the Executive Board formulated its recommendations in two distinct stages: firstly, at its
136th session, it examined in detail the proposals made by the Director-General in document 26 C/5 from a  programme point of view and with respect to their budgetary implications. At the close of this very intensive  review, the Executive Board decided, in 136 EX/Decision 4.1, to submit the Draft Programme and Budget for
1992-1993 for examination by the General Conference, with its favourable recommendation, subject to the final  recommendations to be made at its 137th session. Secondly, at its 137th session, after having examined the  revised figures proposed by the Director-General in document 26 C/5 Rev. 1, the Executive Board adopted
137 EX/Decision 4.1, which is quoted in extenso in document 26 C/6 Add. before you and which is self-  explanatory.
2.2 I should finally stress, Mr President, that the recommendations of the Board are based on a thorough  examination by its Finance and Administrative Commission, under the able chairmanship of Mr Guruge, of the  Director-General's proposals in document 26 C/5. Now, of course, you are going to place before us the  recommendations of the Administrative Commission of the General Conference itself. Thank you,  Mr President.
3. The PRESIDENT:  I thank the Chairman of the Executive Board, and I now give the floor to the Chairman of the  Administrative Commission, who will present the relevant recommendations.
4.1 Mr GURUGE (Sri Lanka), Chairman of the Administrative Commission:  Thank you, Mr President. Ladies and gentlemen, I have the pleasure to present to you the  recommendations of the Administrative Commission relating to the provisional budget ceiling for 1992-1993.
4.2 The Administrative Commission, under item 3.2, examined the techniques and methods applied in the  preparation of the budget and budget estimates for 1992-1993 with a view to ascertaining their conformity with  the decisions of the General Conference and the Executive Board, bearing in mind the affirmative conclusions  of the 136th and 137th sessions of the Executive Board.
4.3 After a detailed examination, the Commission was satisfied that these techniques have been applied in  document 26 C/5 in conformity with the directives of the General Conference and the Executive Board. Many  speakers considered that the techniques currently practised were among the most advanced in the United  Nations system. Nevertheless, it was the unanimous view of the Commission that the budgeting techniques used  so far should be subjected to a very thorough review in the light of the changing international economic  situation and the budgeting practice of other United Nations agencies. It was therefore agreed ‘that the
680   Executive Board be authorized to specify the techniques to be used in the preparation of the Draft Programme  and Budget for 1994-1995. The Executive Board was also specifically called upon to utilize for this purpose the  group of experts on financial and administrative matters which is due to be constituted at the 139th session of  the Executive Board.
4.4 As regards the Draft Programme and Budget for 1992-1993 and in particular the budget ceiling, the  Administrative Commission recommended to the General Conference the adoption of a resolution in which,  inter alia, it: noted with satisfaction that the budget proposed by the Director-General for 1992-1993 has been  prepared in accordance with 25 C/Resolution 46, 135 EX/Decision 4.1 (Section III, para. 28(a)) and
136 EX/Decision 4.1 (Section IV, para. 107) concerning the Organization's budgeting techniques; recognized  the efforts made by the Director-General to achieve a reduction of $11 million in nominal growth, thereby  entailing a negative growth in real terms; recalled the obligatory savings of $7,506,000 to reimburse  miscellaneous income and the Working Capital Fund and $1,500,000 for the re-establishment of the Reserve to  finance draft resolutions submitted by Member States; and further recalled that, in accordance with decision 4.1  adopted by the Executive Board at its 137th session, the provisional budget ceiling was established at  $415,143,000 for Parts 1 to VII of the budget, to which will be added the figure in Part VIII which, according to  document 26 C/5 Rev. 2, amounted to $29,561,000.
4.5 Mr President, the budget ceiling of $444,704,000 proposed in the draft resolution before you  corresponds to the figure noted by the Administrative Commission. The final budget ceiling will, as usual, be  established at the joint meeting of the Administrative Commission with the five programme commissions and  will be duly reported to you. The Appropriation Resolution for 1992-1993 will finally be adopted by the  plenary. Thank you.
5. The PRESIDENT:  I thank the Chairman of the Administrative Commission for his presentation. I now give the floor to  the Director-General.
6.1 The DIRECTOR-GENERAL:  Thank you, Mr President. Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, the decision on the provisional budget  ceiling which the General Conference will have to take this afternoon is of particular importance. It concludes  as it were the general policy debate of the last ten days and paves the way for the vote on a definitive budget for
1992-1993. The views expressed during the general policy debate were generally positive although many  speakers regretted that negative growth in real terms occurs in the budget of UNESCO at a point in time when  the need for the Organization's programmes and presence is more urgent than ever before.
6.2 The Chairman of the Executive Board, Mr Aliyu, has just underlined that the Board submits document
26 C/5 with its favourable recommendation to the General Conference. The Executive Board has also  recognized the considerable efforts made by the Secretariat to reduce the total budget through planned austerity  measures and programme concentration, which have entailed cuts amounting to $11 million in programmes  and in services to Member States. The Chairman of the Administrative Commission, Mr Guruge, when  presenting his Commission's recommendation, stressed the very thorough technical examination given to my  proposal. It is important to recall that the Commission noted with satisfaction that the budgeting techniques in  document 26 C/5 had been applied as decided by the General Conference and the Executive Board. This has  been the case ever since these techniques were introduced by UNESCO.
6.3 Obviously, Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, modern techniques for budget calculations dealing  with the phenomena of economic inflation and currency fluctuation in all countries where UNESCO executes  its programmes and activities are by definition extremely complex. Their constant review and reform will help  Member States to understand and follow more closely the implementation of the Organization's budget. But the  overall level of the budget entrusted to UNESCO for execution, Mr President, is a political decision based on  the quality of the programme and on Member States’ conviction that UNESCO's constitutional task remains  relevant. It is with this remark that I submit the decision on the provisional budget ceiling for the approval of  the General Conference. Thank you, Mr President,
7.1 The PRESIDENT:  I thank the Director-General. Ladies and gentlemen, we have just heard that the Chairman of the  Executive Board, the Chairman of the Administrative Commission and the Director-General are unanimous in
20   recommending that the General Conference adopt the resolution in document 26 C/135. May I take it that such
1s your unanimous wish?
7.2 I see no objections. It is so decided. Thank you for your rapid decision.
