Mr. CARTER (Head of the Department of  Exchange of Persons) explained the reason for  the inclusion of the directive among the pro-  gramme resolutions. The Department of Ex-  change of Persons was committed not only to  the general stimulation of fellowships, but also to  the stimulation of fellowships specifically con-  cerned with certain aspects of Unesco's pro-  gramme. The problem of the social implications  of science figured largely in the Natural Sciences  programme, and it was hoped that the creation  of fellowships for research on that subject would  help towards the implementation of Unesco’s pro-  gramme in that field.  Mr. HOLCROFT (New Zealand) read a motion  which had been adopted at the conference held  at Royaumont in October 1948, on mental atti-  tudes and tensions :  The delegates express their support of Unesco acti-  vities relating to exchange of persons, and urge the  Jol  expansion of the programme of fellowships, visiting  professorsuips, etc. as one means of replacing  national stereotyped conceptions by more objective  experiences; at the same time, they suggest that such  a programme be combined with a scientific investiga-  tion of the conditions under which exchange of  persons favours international friendship, and the  conditions under which such contacts have the  opposite effect. The same considerations apply in the  case of exchange of information regarding national  characteristics.  Mr. CARTER (Head of the Department of  Exchange of Persons) pointed out that it would  have to be decided whether the Secretariat could,  as part of its normal work, take action in the  manner indicated by the resolution, or whether  an additional resolution was required before any  action could be taken.  Mr. GOULD (United Kingdom) moved the  adoption of programme resolution 2.1242.  In seconding the motion, Dr. BAHL (India)  stressed the great need for scientists to emerge           from their laboratories to study the problem of  human improvement in the light of scientific  research. He hoped that it would be possible to  establish more than three fellowships of the kind  indicated in the programme resolution in question.  Programme resolution 2.1242 (document 3C/5,  page 19) was adopted.  Resolution 2.1291.  Mr. GOULD (United Kingdom) moved the  adoption of the resolution, on condition that the  list of types of beneficiaries therein should not be  taken as exclusive.  Dr. PELLISSIER (Union of South Africa)  seconded the motion. Speaking from his own per-  sonal experience, he stressed the fact that mature  persons benefited more widely from fellowships  than young persons.  He was supported in that view by Dr. BAHL  (India).  In answer to a question from the CHAIRMAN,  Professor KABIR (India) informed the meeting  that, while supporting the resolution for the pro-  motion of fellowships for mature persons, the In-  dian delegation still wished the resolution, which  it had submitted at the first meeting in favour of  the encouragement of fellowships for young stu-  dents, to stand. It was considered, however, that  responsibility for the promotion of fellowships for  students should fall primarily on national govern-  ments. The Government of India had launched  many schemes for student exchange.  Mr. DESROCHERS (Canada) also supported the  resolution.  With a view to ensuring that people at all levels  should benefit from the encouragement of fellow-  ship programmes, Mr. LITTLE (Australia) pro-  posed the following resolution :  That it be arranged for qualified lecturers to bring  to the mass of the people the purposes and pro-  gramme of Unesco and to secure a diffusion of inter-  national and inter-cultural knowledge at the level of  the ordinary man. The interchange of such personnel  might be best attained by reciprocal action between  Member States.  Mr. CARTER (Head of the Department of  Exchange of Persons) drew attention to the un-  desirability of introducing into the Exchange of  Persons programme activities which really be-  longed to the sphere of action of other Depart-  ments. The diffusion of international and inter-  cultural knowledge at the level of the ordinary
362  man was more directly within the field of com-  petence of the Departments of Education and Mass  Communication. Mr. HOLCROFT (New Zealand) and Mr. DES-  ROCHERS (Canada) spoke in favour of the  adoption of programme resolution 2.1291 in its  original form. Resolution 2.125.  On the motion of Mr. GOULD (United King-  dom), seconded by Mr. HOLLAND (United States  of America), programme resolution 2.125 was  adopted without amendment.  Mr. GOULD (United Kingdom) proposed an  additional resolution :  That a small committee of experts from donor and  receiving countries be called to discuss the admini-  stration of fellowships and related problems.  While not wishing to submit a formal reso-  lution on the point, Mr. Gould hoped that the  Secretariat would also move gradually towards  the goal of sharing expenses equally between inter-  national bodies, such as Unesco, which granted  fellowships, and the governments of receiving  countries.  In answer to M. MAST (Belgium), Mr. GOULD  (United Kingdom) said that the phrase “related  problems” could be taken to cover the exchange  of civil servants.  Mr. CARTER (Head of the Department of  Exchange of Persons) considered that the problem  of finance involved in the calling of such a com-  mittee of experts could be most easily solved if  the meeting were summoned to meet on the day  preceding the next session of the General Confer-  ence. That had already proved the best solution  in the case of the meeting of representatives of  National Commissions, which had been held at  the commencement of the current session.  While supporting the proposal of the United  Kingdom delegate, M. ABRAHAM (France)  pointed out that it would be impracticable to  establish a single system for the administration  of fellowships, as different countries, of necessity,  had their own individual methods. He considered  the phrase “from donor and receiving countries”  should be deleted.  The motion put forward by the delegate of the  United Kingdom was adopted, as amended by the  delegate of France. Budgetary aspects of the Exchange of  Persons programme.  Mr. CARTER (Head of the Department of  Exchange of Persons) mentioned that it was not  for the Sub-Commission to pass resolutions  affecting the budget but merely to formulate  directives to guide the Budget Sub-Commission in  its work.  Mr. HOLLAND (United States of America) stres-  sed the threefold task of the Department of  Exchange of Persons : to collect and disseminate  information on fellowships; to strive to break down  barriers to the exchange of persons; and to  administer existing fellowships. His delegation  considered that the first two parts of that task  were of prime importance. In view of the re-  duction in the total budget figure approved by the  Conference, Mr. Holland suggested that any  reduction in the budget of the Department of  Exchange of Persons should fall upon the item  for the grant of Unesco fellowships. He proposed  that the item :  (twenty-five  $80,000” (document  “Scholarships and Fellowships  Fellowships at $3,200)
3C/6, page 60).         should be altered to :  “Scholarships and Fellowships (ten Fellowships  at $3,200) ... $32,000.”  Professor KABIR (India) thought it would be  highly undesirable to reduce the number of Unesco  fellowships. If a cut in the Exchange of Persons  budget was absolutely unavoidable, he proposed  that the item in question should be amended to  read :  “ten Fellowships and fifteen Associate Fellow-  ships, totalling $56,000”.  With regard to the selection of fellows, he made  a plea for the inclusion of more persons from  Africa and Asia.  Mr. LITTLE (Australia) was in favour of the  adoption of the earlier United Kingdom proposal  that the governments of receiving countries should  pay half the cost of Unesco fellowships. The  figure in question could thus be reduced to  $40,000 without reducing the number of fellow-  ships.  That suggestion was opposed by Mr. HOLLAND  (United States of America) and Professor KABIR  (India).  M. ABRAHAM (France) considered that, even  if a cut in the Exchange of Persons budget were  unavoidable, it would give a bad impression if the  Sub-Commission suggested that the cut should fall  on an item concerned with the implementation  of the programme, in view of the Conference's  concern regarding the general administrative costs  of the Organization.  Dr. PELLISSIER (Union of South Africa) was  opposed to forwarding any directive to the Budget    Sub-Commission for the reduction of the Exchange  of Persons budget.  Dr. ROBBINS (Canada) proposed forwarding a  recommendation to the Budget Sub-Commission  to the effect that, if a reduction in the Exchange  of Persons budget were found necessary, it should  be made in the item proposed by the delegate of  the United States of America.  Mr. HOLLAND (United States of America) sug-  gested the addition of a proviso that the cut should  not reduce the total figure to less than $32,000.  He also recommended that as many fellowships as  possible should be financed out of the total figure  approved.  Professor KABIR (India) was opposed to the  reduction of the figure to less than $60,000.  Mr. GOULD (United Kingdom) opposed the  Canadian delegate’s proposal, which might lead to  a cut far greater than the Sub-Commission anti-  cipated; he personally was in favour of the figure  suggested by the delegate of India, rather than  that proposed by the delegate of the United States  of America.  The CHAIRMAN put Dr. Robbin’s proposal to  the vote in the following form :  If it is found necessary to reduce the budget for  the Exchange of Persons, the Sub-Commission recom-  mends that the reduction be made in the appropriation  for Scholarships and Fellowship, but that the reduc-  tion in any case be not below $50,000.  The motion was adopted.  The meeting rose at 1.40 p.m.  , …   —   —   _ — _   ’
