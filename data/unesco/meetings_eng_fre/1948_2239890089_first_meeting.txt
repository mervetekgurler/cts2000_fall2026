SUMMARY RECORDS OF THE MEETINGS  OF THE  JOINT PROGRAMME AND OFFICIAL  AND EXTERNAL RELATIONS COMMISSION  FIRST MEETING  Wednesday, 24 November 1948, at 5 p.m.  Chairman: Mr. D. R. HARDMAN (United Kingdom)  The CHAIRMAN informed the meeting that,  after consultation with the Chairman of the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission and with the  Secretariat, it had been agreed that the following  items of the Agenda of the Official and External  Relations Commission (document 3C/OXR/1  (rev.)) should be considered by the Joint Com-  mission :
1. Item 5: Relations with Trust and Non-Self-  Governing Territories;
2. Item 6(a): Relations with Germany,  Item 6(b): Relations with Japan; Co
4. Ttem 4(a): Report on the representation of  Unesco in the main cultural areas of the  world;
5. Item 10 (b)(i): Relations with international  non-governmental organizations—Report on  criteria adopted for the granting of contracts  and grants-in-aid.  In accordance with the provisions of document
3C/BUR/1 (rev.), four joint meetings of the Pro-  gramme and Official and External Relations Com-  missions would be held. The results of the  meetings would appear in the Report of the Offi-  cial and External Relations Commission.  Relations with Trust and Non-Self-Govern-  ing Territories (document 3C/OXR/5).  The CHAIRMAN called upon the Director-  General to present document 3C/OXR/5.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL recalled the fact  that pages 101 to 103 of his Report dealt with  relations with Trust and Non-Self-Governing Ter-  ritories, and showed how the General Conference  resolution on the subject had been implemented  in 1948.  The question was an important one for two  reasons : (1) because such territories occupied  a large area and contained a section of the world s  population which needed Unesco’s help, since the  major part of the inhabitants were under-privi-  leged and backward; (2) because the govern-  ment departments responsible for administering  such territories were usually not those in closest  touch with Unesco, with the result that such terri-  tories tended to be neglected in Unesco's plans.  The following branches of Unesco’s work were  of particular interest to them : primitive arts,  folk arts, libraries, radio programmes, funda-  mental education, all grades of general education.  human rights and field science offices; it might  be found possible to set up a field science office  in tropical Africa.  Document 3C/OXR/5 showed the measures  taken in 1948 to implement the General Confer-  ence’s resolution, and ended with a resolution  similar to that which had been passed in Mexico,  except for the fourth and fifth paragraphs, which  were new.  Mr. WARD (United Kingdom) observed that  the Commission was faced with a challenge, a  hope and an invitation. The challenge was that  Unesco ought to do what it could to ensure that  Member States responsible for Trust and Non-  Self-Governing Territories did not neglect their  needs; Great Britain would gladly take up that  challenge. The hope was contained in the words  at the top of page 2 of the document : “It 1s  hoped that Member States will co-operate with  Unesco in promoting, through all appropriate  means, the development of Trust and Non-Self-
412             Governing Territories in the fields of education,  science and culture...”’; Great Britain shared that  hope. The invitation was contained in the fourth  paragraph of the resolution “The General  Conference invites Member States which are res-  ponsible... etc.”; Great Britain, with her heavy  colonial responsibilities, would be most happy to  accept the invitation. From over twenty years’  experience as a teacher and administrator in  Africa, Mr. Ward was deeply conscious of Bri-  tain’s need for Unesco’s help in promoting the  educational, scientific and cultural development  of such territories. On behalf of the British dele-  gation, he therefore warmly supported the pro-  posal.  M. FLORKIN (Belgium) gave a brief account  of the educational and scientific progress achieved  in the past sixty years in Belgium's African terri-  tories. He assured the Commission of Belgium's  desire to collaborate with Unesco in that field,  on condition that Unesco took into account the  different stages of development reached by the  various races, and their varying mentalities and  degrees of receptivity.  Husni Bey FAREEZ (Observer for Transjordan)  wished to know how much of the budget of admi-  nistering countries was spent on the education  of children in Trust and Non-Self-Governing terri-  tories.  Mr. SOLARU (United Kingdom) was very happy  to note that Unesco had included in its pro-  gramme a real concern for Trust and Non-Self-  Governing Territories and wished to enable them  to make use of the Organization's vast resources;  he was sure that the governments of such terri-  tories (he himself came from Nigeria) would also  welcome that step. Unesco'‘s activities in mass  communication, fundamental education, primi-  tive arts, language and literature, as well as its  function as a clearing-house and exchange market  for ideas, men and material, provided such terri-  tories with a wonderful opportunity which they  should not miss. He pointed out, however, that  Trust and Non-Self-Governing Territories had  already embarked on plans for development in  education, social welfare, health, mass education,  etc. and, much as he welcomed Unesco’s desire  to make its resources available to them, he  regretted that there was no reference in the reso-  lution to the governments of Trust and Non-Self-  Governing Territories being consulted and asso-  ciated more closely with Unesco's work. To  effect a cure, the patient's co-operation had to be  won, and many of the territories in question were  far from being prostrate patients and would like  to take a hand in the prescription of their cure.
413  Mr. Solaru therefore wished to move two minor  amendments to the resolution : in the third line  of the second paragraph, he wished the words  “responsible for” to be replaced by “concerned  with”. In the last line of the last paragraph, he  wished the words “the Governments of the Trust  and Non-Self-Governing Territories themselves,  the Administering Powers and” to be inserted  between “co-operate fully with” and “the United  Nations in this field".  Mr. THOMPSON (United States of America)  seconded the British motion in support of the  Director-General’s proposal. While agreeing that  Unesco had much to give to Trust and Non-Self-  Governing Territories, he thought that the latter  had also something to offer Unesco, which could  gain valuable experience from the plans already  being developed there. He also welcomed the  interest shown by administering governments.  The United States delegation was anxious, how-  ever, that the programme should be developed in  the closest co-operation with the Trusteeship  Council and the Economic and Social Council of  the United Nations, rather than directly with the  countries concerned. With regard to the remarks  made by the delegate of the United Kingdom, the  United States delegation would later present a  proposal for associate membership for Trust and  Non-Self-Governing Territories.  Mr. THERON (Union of South Africa) men-  tioned that his country was closely associated  with Trust and Non-Self-Governing Territories,  from which thousands of people came to work in  the mines and industries of the Union of South  Africa. The Union had eight to ten million Afri-  can subjects, and, in January 1949, it was to  launch a great literacy campaign. The Govern-  ment had appointed a committee, with wide  powers to investigate the question of the education  of the African, and its report would be available  in the next year or two. He asked all govern-  ments concerned to work, therefore, in close  co-operation with his country, which had much  the same prcblems and which could give them  valuable assistance.  M. SEYDOUX (France) suggested that, in the  annual reports made by Member States, a special  chapter should be devoted to progress made in the  development of Trust and Non-Self-Governing  Territories. As a result of the resolution passed  on the subject in Mexico, the French Government  had decided to convene a seminar in Paris in 1949  for young teachers from overseas; at that seminar  they would study the problems which they  would encounter upon their return to their own         countries. France would keep Unesco informed  of the progress of the scheme.  The French delegation would support the United  Kingdom's motion to amend the resolution in  document 3C/OXR/5, provided that the text was  in accord with Articles IX and X of the Agreement  between Unesco and the United Nations. He  asked the Director-General to make a statement  on that point. He also asked to be given the text  of the proposed amendments, in writing, In  French.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL presumed that the  delegate of France was referring to Articles IX  and X of the agreement with the United Nations,  stating that Unesco would co-operate with the  Trusteeship Council and render any assistance  requested by the Council, and that it would  co-operate with the United Nations in accordance  with Chapter XI of the Charter. He saw no  obstacle or limitation to Unesco’s right to colla-  borate with any Member State willing to co-  operate in the matter; in many cases Unesco  worked in co-operation with Governments, non-  governmental bodies, and other Specialized Agen-  cies of the United Nations on the same projects.  The Director-General was delighted with the  general reception of the resolution, as it was a  project very dear to his heart and he had often  pressed, in the past, for its inclusion in Unesco 's  programme.  With regard to the suggestion made by the  delegate of France concerning the devotion of a  special chapter to the subject in the annual reports  of Member States, he reminded members that the  question was covered by the Report on Trust and  Non-Self-Governing Territories issued by the  United Nations, tabulated State by State. The  information given was partly analysed by Unesco,  and he therefore suggested that the Secretariat  should be asked to provide cross references be-  tween that document and the Reports of Member  States, so that there should be no overlapping.    In answer to the delegate of Transjordan, the  Director-General said that the information he  required was contained in the same document.  M. SEYDOUX (France) was satisfied with the  explanation.  M. FAROUKH (Lebanon) was anxious that, in  helping Trust and Non-Self-Governing Territories,  Unesco should ensure that regional traditions  were preserved, and that the local authorities  should collaborate in the work. He therefore  moved the following resolution :  The General Conference recommends that the Pro-  gramme for 1949 be carried out in conformity with  the needs and traditions of the populations of Trust  and Non-Self-Governing Territories and in collabo-  ration with their local inhabitants.  The resolution was adopted, with the reser-  vation that small drafting changes should be made  by the Secretariat, in conjunction with the dele-  gate of Lebanon.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL suggested the follow-  ing wording for the second amendment proposed  by the delegate of the United Kingdom :  . co-operate fully with the United Nations in this  field and, through the Administering Powers con-  cerned, with the Governments of the Trust and Non-  Self-Governing Territories themselves.  The first amendment proposed by the United  Kingdom delegate was adopted.  The second amendment, as re-worded by the  Director-General, was also adopted.  The whole resolution, as amended, was adop-  ted. (185)  The meeting rose at 6.20 p.m.
