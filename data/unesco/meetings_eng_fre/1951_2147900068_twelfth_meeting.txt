9. LocAL SALARY AND ALLOWANCE SYSTEM. [14] Mr. GIELGUD (Head, Bureau of Personnel and Management) read a statement by the Staff Association, in order that the Commission might be aware of the staff’s attitude towards the matter      VII-12 dealt with in the Administrative Commission’s reso- lution 21.4 (Cost-of-living adjustments). The Staff Association wishes to draw the attention of delegations to the system adopted for cost-of-living adjustments corresponding to rises in the cost of living. According to the Administrative Commission’s report, salaries of locally recruited staff engaged since the Florence Conference would be reviewed every six months for the purpose of determining what, if any, cost of living adjustments were necessary as a result, not of changes in the cost-of-living index, but of changes in the best prevailing rates in the Paris area. This would of course be a retrogressive step compared with Unesco’s practice since 1947, which has been to apply cost-of-living adjustments automatically to coincide with rises in the cost-of-living index; and that has been the practice during a period when there was no thought of reducing salaries. Moreover,it is generally recognized that salaries in France are always below the level of the cost-of-living index, and are increased only as a result of action taken by the workers, often of a drastic kind. In conclusion, therefore, the Staff Association considers that the only equitable procedure for cost-of-living adjustments would be simply to maintain the sliding salary scale, adjustable to rises in the cost-of-living index, in accordance with existing practice. [15] On behalf of the Administrative Commission, the CHAIRMAN took note of the statement. [16] Paragraph 9 was approved.

[23] Mr. SPIRIDAKIS (Greece) wished to comment on the decision taken, in his opinion too hastily, by the Administrative Commission on 5 July on the disposal of the credit balance of the Provident Fund. He did not think that the Commission had had enough information on all the aspects of the prob- lem. It should allow the Chairman of the Committee for the administration of the Fund to give it further information, and it could then express its carefully considered opinion. [24] The CHAIRMAN reminded delegates that they should think before taking a decision and not after- wards, which led to complicated procedure. He pointed out that many members of the Commission were absent and that, after the meeting had been adjourned for 10 minutes, the Commission had been . obliged to proceed with the consideration of its report to the Conference without a quorum. Recall- ing the long discussion which had preceded the vote, the Chairman remarked that, while he could not say whether or not the decision was right, no one could dispute its validity and he therefore felt it his duty to oppose the suggestion that the Com- mission, with so many of its members absent, should go back on an earlier decision. He asked if any member of the Commission wished to appeal against his ruling. [25] Miss LAISE (United States of America) agreed with the Chairman, but thought that the problem of payment of funds upon termination required further study. She therefore proposed that the matter should be reconsidered during the next year and be brought up again at the Seventh Session of the Con- ference. [26] The CHAIRMAN observed that the United States delegate’s suggestion could not be followed unless the debate were re-opened, and that would involve reversing a previous decision of the Commis- sion.
