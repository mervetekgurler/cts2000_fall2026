Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) emphasized that  the delegate of Norway had very rightly pointed  out that originally the Executive Board had estab-  lished a sort of balance between the powers of  Member States and the powers of the General  Conference as a whole. At the time of his election,  a member of the Board was the representative  of a State; once elected, he became only the  representative of the General Conference on  Unesco’s second body; he ceased to belong to his  country, representing instead all the Member States  of the Organization. As a representative of the  General Conference and of all the Member States  composing it, the member of the Executive Board  could be re-elected unconditionally. It had been  noticed that many countries avoided nominating  a member of the Executive Board to membership  of their delegation at the General Conference,  holding that the same person could not at the  same time represent the Executive Board and the  government of the country to which he belonged.  That meant that, in the case of a member of the  Executive Board, the function of representative of  the Conference took first place. That function  was attended by certain risks and, if the Confer-  ence left the member of the Executive Board with-  out protection and did not consider him as a direct  representative of itself, every member of the Board  would be in a difficult position for the first three  vears of his term, and the position of the whole  Executive Board would be weakened by the fact  that its members might be influenced by political  considerations. The question which the Com-  mittee was required to consider was not an isolated  instance; it was a very serious matter. A member  of the Executive Board was accountable to nobody  but the General Conference and for that reason  could be re-elected even if he were no longer a  member of a delegation to the General Conference.  Dr. PARRA-PEREZ (Venezuela) also thought  that the Committee should answer the question in  the affirmative, and fully shared the view ex-  pressed by the delegates of Norway and Brazil. He  added that the members of the Executive Board  were chosen from among the members of the  delegations, but that they were not necessarily  proposed by their delegation. It was for the  Conference to choose at its discretion. A member  of the Executive Board could be re-elected, no  longer as the representative of a State, but by  reason of his personal qualifications; it was the  man and not the State that the Conference re-  elected. The delegate of Venezuela further pointed  to the precedent established by the League of
449  Nations, which had created a certain number of  what were called “semi-permanent” members of  the Council, whose re-election assumed the aspect  of a political vote, since it was a country and not  an individual who was re-elected. In Unesco, on  the other hand, members of the Board were chosen  for their personal qualifications, and their re-  election depended not upon their nationality but  upon the qualities they had displayed.  Dr. WALKER (Australia) fully endorsed the  views of the previous speakers. He pointed out  that nothing in the Constitution or in the Rules  of Procedure indicated how candidates for the  Executive Board should be proposed. They could  be proposed by their own delegation or by any  other delegation, whether the candidature was  for first election or for re-election. Members of  the Board were elected as individuals and, once  elected, they acted in the name of the whole  General Conference and under its authority (Ar-  ticle V, paragraphs 5 and 11). On the specific  question asked by the General Committee of the  Conference, Article V.3. was quite clear; it con-  tained no restrictive clause and, as the Constitution  had been very carefully drafted, it could be con-  cluded that, if the authors had wished to place  conditions upon a member's re-election, they  would certainly have done so. From that. the  delegate of Australia concluded that a member  of the Board could be re-elected, whether or not  he was a member of a delegation to the Confer-  ence, and whether or not he attended the session  of that Conference.
