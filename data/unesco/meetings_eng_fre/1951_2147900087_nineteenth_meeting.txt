Benber (Netherlands) Examination of the Financial Consequences of New Resolutions submitted by Member States. [1] The CHAIRMAN explained that the Committee had next to examine the new resolutions submitted by Member States. Those resolutions had been dis- tributed by the Secretariat, together with an estimate of the budgetary increases which their adoption would entail. It was the responsibility of the Programme Commission to decide whether or not the new resolu- tions should be adopted, the Budget Committee having merely to decide whether the estimated costs were reasonable and accurate. The Executive Board had discussed all resolutions before the meeting, and its representative was present to give the Board’s opinion. [2] Mr. HODSON (Head, Programme and Budget, Office of the Director-General) explained that only resolutions submitted before the opening of the Gen- eral Conference (39 in number) had been costed. Resolutions submitted after that date had been ac- cepted only if they had no budgetary implication. He suggested that the Committee should synchronize its work with that of the Programme Commission. Bureau of Conference Planning and General Services. Document 6/DR/1. ProposEp ESTABLISHMENT OF A RECEPTION AND INFORMATION OFFICE AT Unesco House. [3] Sir Ronald ADAM (Representative of the Ex- ecutive Board) said that the Board did not feel that the Secretariat was at the moment in a position to establish such a service. In any case, it was felt that it was a matter for which the Cultural Activities Department of the French Government should be responsible. [4] Mr. BRULL (Cuba) said that the proposal for the establishment of a Reception and Information Office at Unesco House had been put forward by the Cuban delegation. Without entering into details, he would like to explain to the Committee the spirit In which the project had been conceived. [5] It was not a new project, but the answer to various criticisms which had been made to the Cuban delegation by all the American countries, which could not understand why such an important Organization as Unesco did not possess even a rudimentary re- ception service. Such a service would establish con- tacts with intellectuals, journalists, students and all other persons interested in the Organization’s activities. [6] It also seemed necessary to have at Unesco’s headquarters an Information Office which would give people visiting Paris the information they needed on cultural matters. Such an office would be of great value and would help to dispel the erroneous idea which journalistic circles had of Unesco; it would be able to establish direct contacts and thus be an extremely valuable instrument of liaison. The establishment of such an office would silence the criticism to which he had already referred. [7] With regard to the budgetary implications of the project, the Cuban delegation had made enquiries and estimated that such a service would cost $16,000 but the important thing was that a real want should be supplied and Mr. Brull hoped that the necessary credits would be granted. [8] The CHAIRMAN asked the representative of the Department of Mass Communication to outline the reception and information facilities already exist- ing within the Organization, as he felt sure that some kind of service for visitors was provided. [9] Mr. EL DIWANY (Egypt) said that people want- ing information about Unesco were always received by one of the Department’s officers and given all the information they asked for. [10] Mr. FARR (Deputy Director, Department of Mass Communication) explained that, for over a year, his Department had had one staff member with an office on the ground floor to assist visitors. The present incumbent spoke both French and English and possessed useful experience and contacts in Paris, particularly in governmental circles. If able to do so, she provided information directly to visitors, or, if not, introduced them to the appropriate departmental head. It was even possible to arrange interviews with French educational and cultural bodies if that was the type of service desired. Mr. Farr agreed that Resolution 6C/DR/1 required an expansion of the service, but pointed out that, although small, it was already quite efficient. [1] The CHAIRMAN said that the Committee had still to consider the costing of the remaining new resolutions submitted by Member States, and would then examine the amendments, including reductions, to the Programme adopted by the Programme Com- mission. He explained that the Programme Com- mission had been reducing the estimates for each Department by nine per cent, to bring the total budget within the ceiling voted by the General Con- ference. It had since become necessary to make a further overall reduction of 2.38 per cent, and the Commission proposed to do so on a second reading. The revised estimates before the Budget Committee were, therefore, not final. The Budget Committee was, therefore, requested to study all projects to indicate where possible further reductions might be made. Distribu- tion of the additional appropriation of $105,000 throughoutthe entire budget would require an overall reduction of 1.2 per cent, while distribution through- out the programme Departments only would require a general reduction of 1.7 per cent.
