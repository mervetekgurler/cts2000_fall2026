2.3 The Drafting Committee held 6 meetings from 23 to 26 November, It was agreed,  on my suggestion, to use document 14 C/DR, 187, the draft resolution submitted by India,  Mali and the United Arab Republic, as a basis for our deliberations, I am happy to be in  a position to report that, in spite of many difficulties, we came to a complete agreement  on the text that is now before you, It is for me both a duty and a pleasure to pay tribute to  the remarkable spirit of co-operation shown by all delegations, and to the efforts towards  compromise made by all, which have allowed us to achieve this result. 2.4 It is headed Draft Resolution Prepared by the Drafting Committee on Unesco's Contri-  bution to Peace:  The General Conference,
1, ‘Taking into consideration that the United Nations bears the primary responsibility  for the maintenance of international peace and security, and that all organizations  within the United Nations system discharging responsibilities within their own  spheres of activities should contribute to the creation and maintenance of the condi-  tions of peace and international co-operation,
2. Bearing in mind the principles of the Unesco Constitution proclaiming that the basic  purpose of the Organization is "to contribute to peace and security by promoting col-  laboration among nations through education, science and culture",
3. Desiring to co-ordinate Unesco's work with related activities being curried out by  other agencies of the United Nations system,
4. Noting with satisfaction that certain activities undertaken by Unesco during the last  twenty years in its field of competence have, taken as a whole, helped to build up  and to strengthen the foundations of peace.
5. Attaching great importance to the implementation of previous decisions of the General  Conference and the Executive Board direcied towards the strengthening of peaceand  in particular the resolution 8,1, adopted by the General Conference at its eleventh  session, concerning "peaceful and neighbourly relations", the resolution 8.3 adopted  by the Executive Board at its 66th session concerning ""Unesco's tasks in helping to  achieve general and complete disarmament in connexion with the signing of the treaty  banning nuclear weapon tests in the atmosphere, in outer space and under water",  and resolution 8,21 adopted by the General Conference at ita thirteenth session, con-  cerning "Unenzco's tasks in contributing to peace, peaceful co-operation and living  peacefully together, among States with different economic and social systema",
6. Considering that the Secretary-General of the United Nations in his message to the  General Conference referred to Unesco's "efforts to disseminate the truth that war
18 no longer a possible solution to man's problems" ; expressed his bolief that "in  the past twenty years, the very progress of science and technology has brought new,  terrible and ever-present dangers to mankind as a whole, the dangers inherent in the  new weapons of masa destruction”, that "Unesco can effectively supplement the ef-  forts of the United Nations to contain and reduce these grave dangers" and expressed  his "profound hope that it will do so"; stated his desire that the Organization "bring  home, at all times, to all peoples and governments in all parts of the world, what  war means today" and, lastly, that it "remind them of the solemn obligation to re-  nounce war as an instrument of national policy, assumed under the Charter by all  members of Unesco who are also members of the United Nations",  Taking note of the report of the Director-General on the views communicated to him  by Member States on the occasion of Unesco's Twentieth Anniversary concerning the  Organization's contribution to peace,  Noting also withappreciation the work of the Bellagio Meeting and of the Round-Table  Conference on Unesco's Contribution to Peace and thanking the eminent persons who  participated in these meetings for their co-operative efforts,  Convinced that in the light of the United Nations Charter, Unesco's Constitution, the  appropriate decisions of the governing bodies of these organizations, and the results  of the work referred to In paragraph 8, all Member States should:  (a) reject war once and for all as an instrument of their national policy and con-  demn all forms of direct or indirect aggression and of interference in the domestic  affairs of States;  (b) renounce all recourse to violence in the settlement of their differences;  (c) respect the right of all nations to self-determination and independence, free-  dom to choose their political, economic, social and cultural systems ;  (d) take all necessary action to contribute to the agreement on general and com-  plete disarmament under international control;  (e) associate themselves more closely by all possible means with the constructive  work for peace through education, science, culture and mass communications with  which Unesco is directly charged ;  Convinced that the full effectiveness of the Organization depends above all upon its  universality and upon the active and loyal participation of all countries willing to  respect and implement the principles of its Constitution,  Mindful that the United Nations General Aagsembly Resolution 2105 (XX) affirms that  the continuation of colonial rule and the practice of apartheid as well as all forms of  racial discrimination threaten international peace and security and constitute a crime  against humanity”, and expressing the view that the continuation of all these is in  contradiction with Unesco's Constitution,  Recognizing the importance of the contribution that the Member States could make  towards the implementation of the ideals of peace and the relevant programmes of  Unesco, the necessity of gaining still greater support in this direction and consider-  ing as desirable to undertake with this aim a thorough evaluation of the past experience;  Emphasizing that greater efforts need to be made to implement the decisions men-  tioned in paragraph 5 and other decisions of the governing bodies of Unesco, including  resolution 5,202 adopted by the General Conference at its eleventh session, concern-  ing the utilization of information media for the purpose of strengthening peace and  mutual understanding and decisions on the same subject adopted at subsequent ses-  sions ;  Invites the Director-General, in executing the Organization's programme to take full  account of the decisions adopted by the governing bodies of Unesco providing for the  maximum contribution by the Organization to peace, living peacefully together and  peaceful co-operation, among States with different economic and social systems ;  Requests the Director-General to submit to the 77th or the 78th session of the Exe-  cutive Board, after consultation with the governments of Member States and with the  Secretary-General of the United Nations and taking into account the suggestions of  the Member States, of the Bellagio Meeting and of the Round-Table on Peace, pro-  posals concerning a concrete plan of activity for the next one or two budgetary pe-  riods which Unesco could successfully undertake alone or in co-operation with other

17. 2.5 I should like to add three points which I was instructed to stress in my oral report  concerning this resolution,
2.6 First, the Drafting Committee agreed to call on the Director-General to give, in the  letter to be gent by him to Member States for purposes of consultation on the preparation  of the next programme and budget, special emphasis to the preparation of a plan of action  described in the resolution, It was also agreed that the Director-General should stress in  his letter the responsibility of Member States in this respect.

124,4 Il me semble, Monsieur le Président, qu'il y avait, à l'ordre du jour de la Confé-  rence générale, outre les questions de programme et de budget et les questions de politique  générale sur lesquelles la Conférence va être saisie d'un projet de résolution demain matin,  trois grandes questions qui dominalent l'horizon de cette session et qui sont solidaires :  celle du colonialisme et du racisme, celle du développement et celle de Ja paix. Les cir-  constances ont fait que vous les avez tranchées le même jour et c'est peut-être pour cela  que nous nous sentons un peu fatigués ce soir, au terme de ce qui a été une journée histo-  rique pour l'Unesco,
124,5 Si l'on voulait résumer les choses, sans nuances, on pourrait dire que, sur la ques-  tion du colonialisme et du racisme, vous avez vu, Mesdames, Messieurs, le monde tel  qu'il est : c'est-à-dire, hélàs, un monde divisé, où il y a des frontières entre les hommes,  entre les peuples, parfois même à l'intérieur des nations. Quelque douloureux qu'ait été  le débat, il faut avoir le courage de reconnaître que c'est bien ainsi qu'est le monde et  qu'il est fatal, lorsqu'on aborde de telles questions - dont certaines sont l'héritage des  siècles et dont d'autres proviennent des tréfonds de l'instinct le plus irrationnel (et qui  dira qu'il n'y a pas de l'irrationnel dans l'homme ?) - que se manifestent des oppositions  extrêmement violentes et pénibles. Je pense que, peu à peu, l'Histoire atténuera ces dif-  férences et que l'Unesco contribuera à cette évolution, mais, aujourd'hui, elles existent  et ce serait nafveté ou hypocrisie que de le nier,

Que serait l'Unesco, en effet, si elle  n'était pas d'accord A l'intérieur d'elle-même sur la manière d'envisager le monde qu'elle  souhaite aider à construire ? Il faut néanmoins reconnaître que la résolution adoptée se  situe à ce que j'appelais à l'instant l'horizon, c'est-à-dire à la limite de notre pensée et  de nos efforts présents et qu'elle ne s'insère pas d'une manière pratique au coeur de notre  action,
124,7 C'est pourquoi, entre le monde tel qu'il est et le monde tel qu'il devrait être, la ré-  solution que vous venez d'adopter sans oppogition sur le développement revêt une importance  particulière dans la mesure où, précisément, elle reflète le monde tel que nous essayons  de le faire, C'est un témoignage de la vérité de ce qui a été dit si souvent et à quoi je me  suis permis de faire écho, à savoir que le développement est l'un des moyens les plus propres  À traduire de façon concrète, dans ses aspects essentiels, cette finalité qu'est la paix, Que  vous n'ayez pas réalisé encore une unanimité complète sur le développement, cela aussi,  Mesdames, Messieurs, est compréhensible, Car, comment passer de la division actuelle  dont je parlais tout à l'heure à l'unanimité idéale, si ce n'est progressivement ?
