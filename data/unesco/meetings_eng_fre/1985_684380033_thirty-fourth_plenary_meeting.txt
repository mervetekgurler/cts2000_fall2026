11.2 Having said that, Mr President, I am feeling that, perhaps because  Mr Rahal and Mr Wilson speak French fluently, and Canadians are well known for  their bilingualism, the French language is perhaps better able to bring about a  consensus than the English language. I'm saying this with due deference to my  English friends; but I do have a-minor point to make, Mr President, without wish-  ing to start a debate. On page 2 of the English version of 23 C/PLEN/DR.13 Rev.,  in paragraph l(c) (i), the word 'vocation' in the original French has been trans-  lated into English as 'calling'. My limited knowledge of and acquaintanceship  with the English language-for over 50 years makes me feel that 'calling' is not  the right word. But, since the original text is in French and there is no differ-  ence there, I would reserve the right to call it either 'vocation' or 'character'  or 'objectives' or 'ideals' or 'appeal' or 'mission', or whatever phrase our  British friends may like. The word 'calling' does convey a different sort of  meaning. I am saying this for the purposes of the record.
11.3 Mr President, I learned in school during my mathematics lessons that  parallel lines meet only at infinity. But I think the working group which I had  the honour to chair did narrow down the differences considerably, and the two  parallel lines converqed more and more and more until in the Valley of Roses they  met. And I think parallel lines meet not only at infinity but also in Unesco,  which has both a universal and an infinite character. It is in this spirit and  with this hope that I support the draft resolution before us in its French  version; although I don't understand French very well, I am given to understand  that it is perfect French. In the English version, I would for the record say  that the word 'calling' should be changed either to 'vocation' or 'objectives' or  'character' or 'ideals' or 'appeal' or 'mission', whichever phrase my distin-  guished friend Mr Gordon likes to have. If he does not agree to any, well my  views may be placed on record.
11.4 I should like to pay a tribute to the British delegation, who I think  were genuine in their desire to come to a consensus and therefore asked for more  time. Although I know feelings were running very hard and many of the co-sponsors  of DR.10 felt that time was running out, I think we were wise - as you said  Mr President - under your guidance to give a little more time; and I am very  happy that we have achieved this consensus, which will be a lesson and an example  to us in the future that parallel lines and parallel ideas and thoughts can meet  in the forum of Unesco, because of its infinite and universal character.
11.5 To conclude, Mr President, there was one point that I should like with  your permission to stress again. There is a part III to this document, and that  is document 23 C/102 Add. and Corr. It has already been adopted by the General  Conference, but it should be mentioned in this resolution also. Thank you,  Mr President.
