The SECRETARY announced that since the re-  port had been drafted, a letter had been trans-  mitted to the Chairman of the Commission from  the Chairman of the Budget Committee on the  subject of paragraph E (2) on page 12 of Annex Il  of the Report. The letter was as follows:  Your letter of 7 June has been considered by the  Budget Committee. The Committee has concluded  that the paragraph to which you refer should read as  follows:
(2) The General Conference shall give special  attention to the total sum to be paid from the Budget  of Unesco in the form of subventions to the inter-  national non-governmental organizations or prepara-  tory committees.  The Budget Committee is of the opinion that there  need not be a special annex to the Budget Document  for three reasons:  (a) The Budget is a project budget by previous  decisions of the General Conference. Grants-in-aid  therefore are voted in each individual project and are  not therefore approval in total.  (b) As a corollary to the first, the Committee  agrees that more information on grants-in-aid is  desirable in the Budget presentation. Accordingly,  the Budget Committee has requested additional  information to be included in the Budget for 1952,  on a project basis.  (¢) Until such time as the grants-in-aid increase  substantially in number, the Budget Committee  believes that there is no need for an over-all table in  the Budget. Over-all tables in the Budget generally  have been reserved for those activities which, because  of their complexity and number, cannot be considered  except in total. This is not yet true of subventions,  however.  The Budget Committee, in its final report, will  include a statement, with the agreement of the  Official and External Relations Commission, that,  should subventions increase in number substantially,  an over-all table should be included with all informa-  tion necessary to ensure a clear presentation of the  Budget. His  delegation had assumed that the new Directives  would become effective upon adoption by the Gen-  eral Conference. On a previous occasion, the del-  egate of Switzerland had asked how the new  Directives would affect existing grants-in-aid or  subventions, and the Director-General’s represen-  tative had replied that the Directives would be-  come effective at the beginning of the next  calendar year: in the subsequent discussion, it had  been agreed that the new Directives would not  affect existing or pending subventions, but the  United States delegation had assumed that it would  be appropriate for the new Directives to cover new  applications for subventions and for consultative  arrangements, that is to say, subventions which  had not yet been considered by the Executive  Board; those which had come before the Board  would be covered by the old Directives.  Mr. Mulliken therefore moved the deletion of  the last phrase of the resolution: “to become  effective as of 1 January 1951”, which would mean  that the new Directives would become effective  upon adoption by the General Conference. [11] Assuming that the whole report would be  adopted, Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of Amer-  ica) announced that, if the matter were put to  the vote, his Government would feel compelled  to abstain. The United States delegation had been  glad to participate in the work of the Commission  and of its Sub-Committees, and had appreciated  the patience with which those bodies had listened  to the United States point of view; but his del-  egation was disappointed with the results achieved  as far as the new Directives were concerned.  It considered that closer relations should have  been established with international non-gov-  ernmental organizations, especially with regard  to oral and written communications. It was also  concerned about the clause that non-governmental  organizations, in order to be eligible for con-  sultative status, had to be “primarily” concerned  with matters within the competence of Unesco;  it feared the exclusion of certain organizations,  such as Youth and Church groups, that were not  primarily concerned with Unesco’s work.  The United States delegation was also disturbed  by the fact that the conditions for the granting of  subventions, as set forth in the new Directives,  did not protect the expenditure of Unesco’s funds  sufficiently explicitly.  The Report of the Commission was adopted by

33.51, 33.52, 33.521, 33.522 and 33.523, as a complement  to Article VII of Unesco’s Constitution regarding  liaison between Unesco and workers’ organizations.  The aim of these resolutions is to ensure that the  masses shall share in the benefits of culture and  in an understanding of scientific progress. In the  belief that the most fundamental way of achieving this  object is to have worker’s representatives on the  National Commissions, the Federations request the  Member States to associate workers’ organizations in  the work of the National Commissions and to consider  the possibility of including representatives from these  organizations in their delegations to the General  Conference.  We must, however, point out that  organizations are not now represented on the National  Commissions nor in the delegations, except in the  case of two or three States which, in forming their  National Commissions and delegations, have taken  into account the invitation contained in the resolution  quoted above.  We request the General Conference to remind  Member States to act in accordance with the resolu-  tion adopted at the Fourth Session of Unesco's General  Conference, so that workers’ organizations shall in  future be represented in Unesco in accordance with  their importance from a national and a world point  of view.  workers’  [13] Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France) observed  that this letter brought up two very different  questions at one and the same time: that of work-  ers’ representation on the National Commissions,  and that of their representation on delegations.  While he entirely agreed with the signatories of  the letter on the importance of workers sharing

[16] Dr. MINNUCCI (Head of the Italian Junior  Red Cross) stated that the League of Red Cross  Societies, a world federation of the sixty-seven  national Red Cross and Red Crescent Societies now  in existence, uniting more than a hundred million  men, women and children in the same human-  ilarian ideal, had been happy to take part as an  observer in the work of Unesco’s Fifth Annual  Session. The League had already taken part in  the work of successive annual sessions of Unesco’s  General Conference, and it has also been happy  lo be a member of the Temporary International  Council for Educational Reconstruction. During  the past year, representatives of the League had  also shared in the work of the Conference of  Directors of Children’s Communities, of working  parties on exchange of persons problems, and of  the meeting of representatives of non-governmental  student and youth organizations, as well as in  the work of other organizations supported by  Unesco.  This showed that Unesco could rely upon the  cordial co-operation of the League of Red Cross  Societies in every field that touched Red Cross  Interests or where Red Cross activities might be  called for. This co-operation, based on the com-  plete independence and freedom of action of the  two Organizations, would be continued at all time  and in all places, whenever and wherever there  was human suffering to be eased and where the  level of man’s physical, moral or intellectual life  could accordingly be raised; it would be con-
609  tinued, above all, through the activities of the  Junior Red Cross, a movement which—regardless  of politics, religion or race—today extended to  sixty countries and had forty million members,  in a cause which was that of Unesco, namely  international understanding.  Dr. Minnucci added that he was particularly  happy to give this assurance, not only as delegate  of the League of Red Cross Societies, but also as  Head of the Italian Junior Red Cross, of the nation  which had had the great privilege of extending its  hospitality to this important session of Unesco's  General Conference.  If the League’s co-operation with Unesco was  thus an accomplished fact, the League, for its  part, was grateful for Unesco’s help in drawing up  an educational programme for the Palestine re-  fugees. Dr. Minnucci recalled that the League,  together with the International Red Cross Com-  mittee and the Quakers, had been entrusted by  the United Nations with the distribution of relief  to those refugees. The League had had to deal  with some 330,000 refugees living on Jordanian,  Lebanese or Syrian territory. Its activities, which  had lasted sixteen months, had come to an end on
30 April 1950, when a new United Nations organ-  ization took over the work. In the name of the  League, Dr. Minnucci wanted to take this op-  portunity of expressing the hope that Unesco  would be able to continue bringing to these un-  fortunate refugees the educational aid that had  helped them so much in the past.  The Red Cross, the national Red Cross and  Red Crescent Societies in every country, as well  as their world federation, had all shown by prac-  tical action, like that on behalf of the Palestine  refugees, that they were always prepared, wher-  ever it was a question of relieving human suffering  and improving men’s living conditions. But they  had also shown by their practical work that they  regarded it as one of their essential tasks to make  an effective contribution to peace and international  understanding, within the spheres of Red Cross  activity.  In this spirit, and in the name of the League ol  Red Cross Societies, Dr. Minnucci was very pleased  to note the work so successfully accomplished by  the present Conference, and he hoped that Unesco's  mission throughout the world would go from  strength to strength.  The CHAIRMAN thanked Dr. Minnucci for this  statement.
