4.1 Mr SEDDOH (Ghana), Chairman of the Executive Board:  Mr President of the General Conference, Mr Director-General, dis-  tinguished delegates, ladies and gentlemen, it is my privilege and duty to intro-  duce this general policy debate by reporting on the Executive Board's activities  in 1984-1985, by presenting the Director-General's reports on the work of the  Organization and by submitting to this General Conference the Draft Programme and  Budget for 1986-1987 together with the Board's recommendations thereon.
4.2 It has become the established practice that this general presentation  which is described in the agenda of the present session of the General Conference  as 'Report by the Executive Board on its own activities in 1984-1985 including  the reform process', should be made orally by the Chairman of the Board. In  decision 6.4 taken at its 121st session, the Board once more confirmed this  practice for the purposes of the present General Conference, it being understood  that my report. would be made available to you very shortly after its presentation  as a document in the 'INF' series. I will therefore be reporting on the results  achieved during the five ordinary sessions (the 118th to the 122nd) and one  special session of the Executive Board (its fourth).
4.3 Most of my predecessors on similar occasions have no doubt been able to  report on a relatively smooth flow of events in the life of the Organization and  the Executive Board. My task today assumes quite a different dimension. For, as I  stated at the opening of the current 122nd session, 'no Executive Board has been  confronted with circumstances so rare and difficult as ours has. None of the pre-  ceding Boards has had to maintain so delicate a balance and reconcile so many  conflicting imperatives'.
4.4 In the last two years my fellow Board members and myself have had to  deal with a crisis in the life of this Organization. While it has been a crisis  in today's accepted meaning it has also been, in a more positive sense, a vital  or decisive stage in the life of the Organization, a turning-point, or if we go  back to the original Greek usage, a time of decision or judgement. Unesco has  faced such trials before at different stages in its evolution; I am sure that  this time again, as in the past, it will emerge renewed and strengthened. In the  last two years it has been through a process of introspection and self-  evaluation. This has required both courage and imagination and seems to me a fit-  ting way for the Organization to close its first 40 years of existence and lay  the ground for its fifth decade.
4.5 We had to face a major challenge right from the second session of the  present Board, the 119th, held in the spring of 1984. The twenty-second session  of the General Conference had once again proved that Unesco was based on a truly  universal spirit, assuring each and every Member State a forum for the expression  of its identity and values. It was an eminently successful Conference. And so,  when we took leave of each other after the 118th session which immediately  followed, little did any of us expect that the United States of America would  avail itself of its constitutional right to give notice of withdrawal from the  Organization. Further bad news was to come before the year 1984 ran out. On
5 December 1984 the Government of the United Kingdom informed the Director-  General of its intention to withdraw from Unesco at the end of this year unless  substantial reforms were carried out. Also,'on 10 December 1984 the Government of  Singapore gave notice of withdrawal which, although motivated by quite different  reasons, constituted yet another threat to the universality of the Organization.
4.6 For the Executive Board, the week-long special session, held in February
1985, was a most difficult moment. On that occasion the Board had to grapple with
53    a web of complex problems resulting from the withdrawal of the United States of  America. The financial shortfall was foremost among these problems. The Board  approved the measures already taken by the Director-General with respect to the  execution of the 1984-1985 Programme and Budget. It was able to give the  Director-General guidance as to the economy measures required as far as the  future programme was concerned and adopted certain criteria with a view to  improving programme concentration. The Board asked the Director-General to report  to it at its next ordinary session so that appropriate adjustments might be  recommended. The Board also considered that the difficult financial situation of  the Organization could be improved if Member States would voluntarily agree to  forego the surpluses accruing to them in Part Vi11 of the budget for 1981-1983 or  by making other voluntary contributions.
4.7 In this connection, it is heartening to note that on the eve of this  Conference the special account created for this purpose had reached a total of  $5,562,830 based on contributions received from 53 Member States and other  sources. The financial shortfall, however, was not the only problem the Board had  to grapple with. Thus, at its fourth special session the Board also considered  the delicate questions of a diplomatic, administrative and legal nature that  inevitably arise in the event of the withdrawal of a Member State. it will come  as no surprise to you that these questions were most difficult to deal with.  Although some pertinent decisions were taken at the time, doubts continued to  linger in some members' minds and it was in fact not until the first part of the  current session that the Board came to feel that it was possible as well as  opportune to put certain questions to the General Conference. In the decision it  took just a few days ago, the Board decided to draw the attention of the Confer-  ence to the gravity of the situation created by the withdrawal of a Member State,  as well as by the notification of withdrawal given by certain other Member  States. The Board requested me in this report to emphasize its concern about this  matter and to inform you of the measures it has taken or envisaged.
4.8 Mr President, I intend to carry out this task faithfully but to be able  to do so I will have to take a bit of your time.
4.9 All the information required by the Board in order to discuss this  matter was provided to it by the Director-General in a comprehensive report he  prepared for the fourth special session. Apart from the budgetary and financial  consequences of withdrawal, the document covered the constitutional provisions  relating to withdrawal and precedents; it examined the implications of withdrawal  for the various organs of Unesco and the possible relations between the Organiz-  ation and States withdrawing; finally, it studied the impact of withdrawal on the  activities of the Organization.
4.10 The first two questions discussed by the Board concern the rights and  obligations of a Member State which withdraws from the Organization.
4.11 With respect to the payment of contributions, the Director-General sub-  mitted to the Board the legal opinion of a working group o€ jurists which con-  cluded that 'under the terms of Article IT, paragraph 6, of the Constitution, a  Member State of Unesco whose withdrawal becomes effective on 31 December 1984  will be legally bound to discharge all financial obligations and, in particular,  to make its full financial contribution to the Organization's regular budget for
1984-1985 as determined by the General Conference in resolution 16 and resol-  ution 29.1 adopted at its twenty-second session'. One member of the working  group, however, held the contrary opinion. This matter gave rise to a long debate  in which opinions were divided, particularly as to the interpretation of the  relevant articles of the Constitution.
4.12 At the fourth special session, many members expressed the view that the  Executive Board should have this problem of international law elucidated and  that, in accordance with Article V.B, paragraph 11, it should request an  'advisory opinion' from the International Court of Justice. Several other members  felt, however, that this would be inadvisable, if not an unnecessary exercise,  and yet others considered that this was a matter on which the General Conference  should pronounce a decision. The upshot was that no reference to this matter was  made in the decisions of the fourth special session, which in fact invited the  Government of the United States of America to resume its active participation in  the Organization as soon as possible. It was also understood that the Director-  General would negotiate with the government concerned with respect to the legal  opinion already given on this and other matters. At its 121st session the Board,  having discussed the problem once again, requested the Director-General to pursue  these negotiations with a view to reaching a satisfactory settlement.
4.13 At its fourth special cession the Board had also considered that 'the  establishment of a United States observer mission at Unesco would be a valuable  means of ensuring contact between the United States of America and the Organiz-
54    ation'. It therefore authorized the Director-General 'to provide facilities, as  far as possible, to the observer mission of the United States of America'. This  matter also involved a good deal of discussion prior to and after the decision.  Some members pointed out that, since observer status was not provided for in the  case of Member States having left the Organization, their rights and obligations  under such status would not be easy to define.
4.14 Another set of problematical questions concerns the impact on recruit-  ment, management and composition of staff in the Unesco Secretariat. In his  report to the fourth special session, the -Director-General had clearly set out  the relevant provisions of the Constitution and the Staff Rules and Regulations.  He also described the problem of reimbursement of the income tax levied on the  salaries of United States staff members in the context of an agreement between  the government of that country and Unesco, unilaterally denounced by the Govern-  ment of the United States in 1981. This problem has also arisen in the other  agencies of the United Nations system.
4.15 At the fourth special session there were some who felt that inter-  national civil servants should in no way be affected by the withdrawal of their  country while others thought that the unprecedented circumstances had created an  anomalous situation in this respect. The Board for its part was of the opinion  that 'a State that withdraws from the Organization loses all the privileges of  membership in Unesco in particular the quota established for it in implementation  of resolutions of the General Conference and taking into account the principle of  the equitable geographical distribution of posts within the Secretariat'.
4.16 During the first part of the current session of the Board the Director-  General made it abundantly clear in his oral report that difficult financial and  administrative decisions would be necessary in order to ensure the future of the  Organization, among them a drastic reduction in staff. In this connection, I  should like to mention that the Director-General also reported to the Board on  the establishment, operation and financing of an account for end-of-service  grants and indemnities. The Board's recommendation to the General Conference that  it authorize the financing of a special account for this purpose will be dis-  cussed by the Administrative Commission, as the Conference has just agreed when  it accepted the recommendations of the General Committee.
4.17 It was in the context of a large number of inevitable staff separations  that the Executive Board, through a draft decision submitted by 17 members, at  its 122nd session returned to the question of the staff members of States having  withdrawn from the Organization. This draft in fact covered the questions I  have so far mentioned. After another long debate the Board decided it would be  eluding its responsibility if it were not to put the relevant questions clearly  to the General Conference. The Board realized that these questions would not only  have to he asked in the context of the withdrawal of one Member State but that it  was also necessary to lay down general guidelines for possible future with-  drawals. In view of the sensitive nature of this matter, I crave your indulgence  to read out the decision in its entirety, so that the Conference may clearly per-  ceive the Executive Board's intentions in drawing to its attention a series of  fundamental and interrelated questions. This decision is being reproduced as  General Conference document 23 C/9. The text of the decision reads as follows:  The Executive Board,  Concerned by the situation created within the Organization by the with-  drawal of a Member State and by the notification of withdrawal of two  other Member States,  Recalling the measures which it has taken to deal with this situation,  in particular its decision to accord observer facilities to the State  which has withdrawn from the Organization,  Having, at its 121st session, requested the nirector-General to pursue  negotiations with the government of the State which has withdrawn from  the Organization, with a view to reaching a satisfactory settlement of  the questions pending between that State and the Organization,  Having taken note of the Director-General's report on the results of  those negotiations,  Taking into account the wish expressed by the Director-General to be  given specific instructions with regard to the measures to be taken to  ensure the future of the Organization,  Reaffirming the importance that it attaches to the universal character  of Unesco and its permanent readiness to facilitate and encourage the  restoration of that universality within the Organization,

4.18 I now come to the various reforms the Organization has been engaged upon  for the last two years. The origins of this reform process are to be found at the  Executive Board's 119th session when it decided to set up a Temporary Commi.ttee  to review the functioning of the Organization. This Committee was asked to  examine all the ideas which had emerged from the Board's debates on various  items, which included specific suggestions concerning the methods of work of the  Organization and questions regarding personnel recruitment and management. The  Board asked the Committee to present to it 'recommendations and concrete measures  designed to improve the functioning of the Organization, on which either the  Executive Board could take decisions or which could form the basis of recommen-  dations to be presented by the Executive Board to the General Conference at its  twenty-third session'.
4.19 The Temporary Committee began its work in the latter part of May 1984  and thus began a long and hard labour of reflection and concertation. Presenting  its first results at the 120th session, Mr Margan, the Chairman of the Temporary  Committee, told us unequivocally that 'a very important moment in the life of  this Organization is being experienced, and the complexity of the problems at  hand required adequate treatment, which, in clear conscience, we can call "the  reform of Unesco"'. In concrete terms, the Temporary Committee's first report  brought us a series of important recommendations concerning the General Confer-  ence itself, the Executive Board, Unesco's decision-making procedures, decentral-  ization, programme elaboration, programme implementation, publications and docu-  mentation, operational activities, programme evaluation, personnel, budgetary and  financial matters, public information, outside advice, the role of the Permanent  Delegations to Unesco and finally, the Medium-Term Plan.
4.20 Parallel to this comprehensive review, the Director-General on his own  initiative had launched a series of far-reaching measures concerning the func-
56    tioning of the Secretariat. He thus set up working groups which proceeded to  examine rapidly and thoroughly: recruitment procedures and staff management  methods, budgeting techniques and budget presentation, evaluation methods and  techniques and public information. An internal working group was charged with  making a critical analysis of the programme. The efforts of these five working  groups greatly facilitated the work of the Temporary Committee and the Director-  General's follow-up to their recommendations came to be closely interlinked with  the further progress of the Temporary Committee.
4.21 The latter's proposal's were endorsed by the Board at its 121st session  which also decided to extend the life of the Temporary Committee until the 122nd  session. One specific element of its mandate deserves mention. The Temporary Com-  mittee was 'expected to ensure the implementation of its recommendations endorsed  by the Executive Board, in accordance with an appropriate timetable'. On the  basis of its first report the Board in anticipation of the eventual withdrawal of  the United States of America, requested the Director-General to report to it,  before its 121st session if possible, on all the likely consequences of such  withdrawal.
4.22 The Temporary Committee's second report, submitted to the 121st session  of the Board, was based on the deliberations of two sessions held during April  and May 1985. In the report the Committee noted with satisfaction that its pre-  vious recommendations had indeed been implemented with respect to decentraliz-  ation, programme evaluation, publications and documentation, personnel and public  information. The Committee's recommendation also indicated specific points to  which the Director-General should pay special attention. Even more significant  was the fact that the report contained a table showing the measures adopted or  planned in order to give effect to its recommendations and also contained a time-  table for their implementation.
4.23 There were nine recommendations of the Temporary Committee whose imple-  mentation was the Board's own responsibility. Seven of these concerned the func-  tioning of the Executive Board itself, the eighth concerned programme elaboration  and the ninth programme implementation. The Temporary Committee suggested that  the Bureau of the Executive Board examine these proposals. This was done at the
121st session on the basis of an analytical note prepared by me as well as my own  observations concerning each recommendation. The Bureau noted that for two out of  seven recommendations concerning its own work the necessary adjustments or  improvements had been provided for already. Four other recommendations will be  dealt with at the 123rd session of the Board. As to the remaining recommendation,  which touches on an extremely important point, namely 'that the Board's recommen-  dations on the draft programme and budget are utilized more effectively by the  General Conference', an excellent initiative, which the Board warmly commended,  was taken by the Director-General himself. On 14 August 1985, the Director-  General addressed a personal letter to all the ministers responsible for  relations with Unesco in Member States by which he sent to them a copy of docu-  ment 23 C/6.
4.24 In his letter the Director-General underlined the special character of  these recommendations and specified the kinds of decision that the General Con-  ference would have to take. I will return to this point later. Suffice it to  recall here that the Executive Board has indeed long been concerned by what has  appeared as insufficient attention paid to its carefully considered recommen-  dations. This is an unfortunate shortcoming inherent in the existing relationship  between the Conference and the Board, and I hope that at this twenty-third  session, thanks to the Director-General's initiative, we can begin to eliminate  it.
4.25 Two recommendations of the Temporary Committee concerned programme  matters. One states that when making its recommendations on the preparation of  the draft programme and budget, the Board should clearly identify the degree of  priority of subprogrammes or programme components. As we shall see in more detail  when we come to the 23 C/5, the Director-General himself has developed the means  of identifying such priority, in purely technical terms, through the system of  asterisks proposed in the document. Another recommendation aimed at fuller moni-  toring of programme implementation by the Board, on a selective basis and without  adding to the volume of documentation. The Board also took the initiative, at the
119th session, of recommending that question-and-answer sessions with the respec-  tive Assistant Directors-General be included in the agenda of the Programme and  External Relations Commission. These question-and-answer sessions have become  established practice since the 120th session.
4.26 The third report of the Temporary Committee, which was discussed during  the first part of our current session, maintained the momentum achieved earlier.  This report reflected the work of the sixth and seventh meetings of the Com-
57    mittee; like its predecessors, its volume indicated the breadth of its coverage  and the wealth of useful information it contains. On the basis of the report the  Board saw fit to express its satisfaction at the rate of successful implemen-  tation by the Director-General of the reforms proposed to and accepted by the  Executive Board. It welcomed the overall progress achieved in the functioning of  the Organization as well as the timetable designed to ensure further successful  implementation.
4.27 Having decided to end the mandate of the Temporary Committee, the Board  took note of the Committee's conclusions as to future arrangements for monitoring  the process of reform. The Committee itself considered three possible options  concerning such arrangements, envisaged the possibility of yet other solutions  and pointed out that the composition, functions and financial implications of  whatever solution would have to be defined. The Board endorsed this view and also  asked the Director-General to report to it on the financial, statutory and other  implications of the three proposa.ls so that an appropriate decision could be  taken at its 123rd session.
4.28 I have chosen to dwell at some length on certain details of the action  taken to improve the functioning of the Organization because this undertaking has  dominated the work of the Board and set the tone for all its sessions in 1984 and
1985. In engaging ourselves in this process we have laboured for the future of  Unesco in both the medium and long term. As I stated at the opening of the fourth  special session of the Board 'so much effort had been put into the improvement of  the functioning of our Organization in 1984, which justified ... the hope that  succeeding weeks would bring wise counsel to all those who believe in the common  understanding of mankind and who share in the purposes of our Organization'. Un-  fortunately, such wise counsel did not prevail and the Government of the United  States of America was unable to reverse its decision to withdraw.
4.29 Regardless of this sad reality, however, it is my own personal. view, and  I believe history will bear me out, that the movement for reform led to a period  of intense creative concertation. My fellow members were well aware of this when,  in various ways, and on various occasions, they described the situation of our  Organization today as one of 'revitalization', 'a search for development and  renewal', 'a dynamic of reforms', 'an ongoing process of healing' in a mood of  'new fervour'. It is now for the General Conference itself to build upon our  work, It will have ample opportunity to do so when it considers the draft pro-  gramme and budget together with the Board's recommendations thereon as well as  the Director-General's reports on the activities of the Organization in 1984-

4.31 Item 4.3 on the agenda of the General Conference - Jerusalem and the  implementation of resolution 22 C/11.8 - was considered carefully by the Board at  its 120th and ltlst sessions. On both occasions it had before it reports prepared  by the Director-General in pursuance of the above-mentioned resolution. These  reports were based on the results achieved by Professor Raymond Lemaire, personal  representative of the Director-General. While it warmly thanked the Director-  General for his efforts, the Executive Board felt that the results did not  entirely measure up to the expectations of the international community and par-  ticularly regretted the fact that the occupying authorities had not seen it fit  to provide the detailed information on the effects of the excavations being  undertaken. It deplored the policies and practices of the occupying authorities  which appear to deny these rights and called upon them to comply with the pro-  visions of the Geneva and the Hague Conventions.

At its 119th session the  Board invited the Director-General to continue his efforts to assist the Lebanese  Authorities in carrying out the projects already identified by the Organization  for which extra-budgetary financing is necessary. It also invited Member States  to increase their voluntary assistance for the reconstruction of Lebanon, men-  tioning in particular, at its 120th session, the participation of Unesco in the  safeguarding of the Lebanese cultural property which is part of the World  Cultural Heritage, in accordance with the relevant provisions of the Hague Con-  vention of 1954.

The Board reiterated its earlier decisions and decided further that pro-  vision should be made in the Draft Programme and Budget for 1986-1987 for a  voluntary fund for assistance to Lebanon. It invited the Director-General to  launch an appeal to Member States, international governmental and non-  governmental organizations, foundations and all possible public and private  sources with a view to building up the fund thus created.

29.121 Les discussions ont porté, pour l'essentiel, sur deux grands thèmes. Le  premier, qui présente un caractère quelque peu théorique dans les circonstances  présentes, a trait à la question d'une révision des contingents de répartition  géographique, du fait de la perte par les Etats-Unis du quota de postes dont ils  étaient détenteurs. Le second revêt une actualité plus pressante ; il touche à la  situation des ressortissants américains ayant la qualité de membres du personnel  du Secrétariat.
29.122 Le premier problème - celui des quotas - a été exposé dans le document
4 XIEX12 présenté à la quatrième session extraordinaire du Conseil exécutif, puis  longuement traité dans un document plus récent (122 EXl37) consacré à l'évolution  de la répartition géographique des postes au sein du Secrétariat et qui est  soumis à la Conférence générale sous la cote 23 C/53. I1 peut se résumer dans les  termes suivants : le départ des Etats-Unis rend théoriquement disponible le quota  de postes (114 maximum - 85 minimum) dont ce pays bénéficiait jusqu'ici, en tant  que plus grand contributeur au budget de 1 Organisation. La solution la plus  logique serait donc de procéder à une révision d'ensemble des quotas des 160  Etats membres demeurés au sein de l'organisation et à cet effet, de recalculer,  sur la base de l'assiette actuelle globale de 1100 postes géographiques répartis  entre ces pays, les quotas susceptibles d'être alloués respectivement à chacun de  ces pays.
29.123 Toutefois, dans les circonstances actuelles, une telle révision risque  de rester un exercice théorique. D'une part, en effet, il est certain que le  nombre relativement élevé de fonctionnaires de nationalité américaine occupant  des postes géographiques (76 en juillet dernier et 72 à la fin de 1985) fausse  les calculs de répartition. Cette donnée de fait ne peut être ignorée. D'autre  part et surtout, les compressions de postes et d'effectifs appelées à se déve-  lopper au cours des prochains mois risquent de réduire considérablement, en 1986,  le nombre de postes établis soumis au principe de la répartition géographique.  S'il n'est pas encore possible de connaître avec précision le nombre exact de ces  postes, on peut estimer, d'ores et déjà, que l'assiette actuelle de 1100 postes,  qui sert de base de calcul aux quotas de distribution géographique, se révélera  fortement surévaluée et que celle-ci devra probablement être ramenée à une base  plus modeste de 1.000 ou même de 900 postes. Ce n'est donc que sur la base des  données du nouveau Programme et budget, tel que la Conférence générale  l'approuvera, qu'il sera possible de déterminer cette base de façon précise. Au  stade actuel, mieux vaut peut-être s'en tenir aux quotas actuellement dévolus aux  Etats membres, étant bien entendu que tout Etat qui a ou qui aura quitté  l'organisation ne pourra se prévaloir d'un quota quelconque. Par sa décision
122 EXl8.9, le Conseil exécutif a approuvé cette approche du problème, et m'a  invité à lui présenter, à sa 125e session, une étude de la question avec des  propositions appropriées. Je ne manquerai pas, bien entendu, d'élaborer cette  étude.
29.124 Le second thème de discussion qu'il convient de rappeler ici a trait à  la situation administrative des fonctionnaires américains au sein du Secrétariat,  pris dans leur ensemble (137 lors du départ des Etats Unis, 130 à l'heure ac-  tuelle, toutes catégories confondues, c'est-à-dire occupant des postes soumis ou  non à répartition géographique). Le Conseil exécutif s'est longuement penché sur  cette question. Selon un premier courant d'opinions, il est difficile d'ignorer
4  les conséquences du retrait des Etats-Unis sur la situation des fonctionnaires  américains en service au sein du Secrétariat. I1 serait quelque peu anormal, du  point de vue de l'équité, que l'organisation soit amenée, du fait des réductions  d'effectifs qui prendront effet d'ici la fin 1985, à se priver des services d'un  nombre important de membres du personnel ressortissants de pays qui continuent  d'apporter leur contribution à l'Unesco, alors que les fonctionnaires d'un pays  qui s'en est retiré seraient, dans le même temps, maintenus au sein du Secréta-  riat. Ces fonctionnaires devraient être les premiers à libérer leur poste dans le  cadre des réductions en vue. En outre, et afin de faciliter ce processus, les  contrats des ressortissants américains parvenus à expiration ne devraient pas,  d'ores et déjà, être renouvelés, les intéressés étant informés de cette décision  le plus tôt possible. En un mot, et pour reprendre l'expresion de l'un des  tenants de cette thèse, la présence au sein du Secrétariat de fonctionnaires  ressortissants des Etats-Unis, à un moment où le personnel de l'organisation est  exposé à de graves compressions d'effectifs, du fait du retrait de ce même pays,  serait une situation irrégulière qui aboutirait à faire de cet Etat non membre un  pays " indûment représenté" .
29.125 Selon un autre courant d'opinion, les fonctionnaires américains sont des  membres du personnel comme les autres. Leur cas devrait être traité, de ce fait,  de façon semblable, sans que leur situation administrative ne se ressente de  l'attitude que le Gouvernement de leur pays a cru bon d'adopter vis-à-vis de  l'organisation. En particulier, ils devraient être maintenus en service ou  licenciés, en cas de réduction générale des effectifs, selon les mêmes critères  de compétence et de loyauté envers l'organisation, sans que leur nationalité  entre en ligne de compte.

29.127 En ce qui concerne le recrutement des ressortissants d'un pays ayant  quitté l'Organisation, la situation est simple. La Disposition 104.2 du Règlement  du personnel stipule en effet que "sauf impossibilité de recruter quelqu'un  d'également qualifié, aucun engagement ne peut être offer;' à un candidat qui  n'est pas ressortissant d'un Etat membre de l'organisation. Ces consignes ont été précisées tout récemment en ce qui concerne certaines  catégories de personnels auxiliaires n'ayant pas la qualité de membres du  personnel proprement dit, comme les surnuméraires ou les titulaires de contrats  d'honoraires.

29.181 Monsieur le Président, Mesdames et Messieurs, il y a quelques jours, des  chercheurs de l'université de Chicago annonçaient la découverte des preuves d'un  incendie qui aurait ravagé la terre entière, il y a 65 millions d'années. I1  s'agit d'une épaisse couche de suie, retrouvée sur tous les continents, qui  aurait été constituée par les retombées d'un nuage de flames et de fumées,  probablement produit par le choc avec notre planète d'un astéroïde géant. Ce  nuage, qui se serait répandu tout autour de la terre, aurait provoqué une  obscurité et un froid mortels. Plus de la moitié des espèces animales et végé-  tales existantes aurait alors disparu. Pour les chercheurs en question, cette  scène d'apocalypse suggère assez bien ce que seraient les suites d'une guerre  nucléaire généralisée. Leur découverte a de quoi faire réfléchir. La longue nuit  de mort qu ils évoquent, conséquence inévitable d'une catastrophe naturelle, aura  au moins épargné quelques-unes des espèces vivant à cette époque - dont nous  serions les très lointains descendants. Tandis qu'un embrasement nucléaire, qui  serait le résultat parfaitement évitable de la folie des hommes, pourrait bien  mettre fin à l'odyssée de la vie sur terre.
29.182 C'est pour éviter une aussi tragique éventualité, et pour permettre aux  nations du monde de se consacrer, de concert, à leur prospérité commune, que  l'organisation des Nations Unies a été créée il y a quarante ans - alors que la  deuxième guerre mondiale touchait à sa fin et à la veille de l'explosion des deux  premières bombes atomiques de Hiroshima et de Nagasaki. Le Préambule de sa Charte  commence ainsi :  "NOUS, peuples des Nations Unies, résolus à unir nos forces pour main-  tenir la paix et la sécurité internationales, à recourir aux institu-  tions internationales pour favoriser le progrès économique et social de  tous les peuples.. ."  La Charte prévoyait la création d'un certain nombre d'organisations et d'agences  spécialisées formant, autour de l'organisation des Nations Unies, un système  cohérent de coopération internationale - système qui a été porteur, à sa  naissance, d'immenses espérances. Peu à peu, dans tous les pays et dans tous les  domaines d'activité, des femmes et des hommes y ont vu poindre l'aube d'un  nouveau matin du monde - le début d'une aventure collective qui devait permettre,  progressivement, de mettre fin aux injustices et aux conflits entre les nations.
29.183 Au cours des quatre décennies écoulées, le système n'a certes pas  répondu à toutes les attentes placées en lui. Mais il n'en a pas moins repre-  senté, tout au long de cette période, l'ultime rempart dont disposait l'humanité  pour empêcher qu'éclate un conflit planétaire, ainsi que l'unique fondement d'un  effort concerté destiné à mettre pacifiquement fin au système de domination  établi à l'époque coloniale et à oeuvrer solidairement pour la prospérité et la  sécurité de tous les Etats.
29.184 Ainsi a-t-il non seulement servi de cadre à la réconciliation progres-  sive entre certains groupes de pays, héritiers d'animosités parfois sécu-  laires, mais il a aussi permis de contenir nombre de conflits de caractère local  ou régional, souvent nourris par des antagonismes dont certains ont leurs sources    ailleurs, en les empêchant de dégénérer en affrontement généralisé ; il a enfin  permis de développer une infinité de réseaux de communication, d'échange, de  coopération et d'action, touchant aux domaines les plus divers, qui ont suscité  un immense progrès. des savoirs et des savoir-faire, en même temps qu'ils favo-  risaient le rapprochement entre les peuples et entre les cultures du monde  entier.
29.185 Maurice Bertrand, Inspecteur du Corps commun d'inspection des Nations  Unies, conclut ainsi sa "Contribution à une réflexion sur la réforme des Nations  Unies" :  "L'auteur espère avoir montré que l'ONU et son système ont réussi, en  construisant pour la première fois dans l'histoire un début "d'espace  politique mondial", à démontrer qu'une organisation mondiale était  indispensable. I1 n'est pas contradictoire d'affirmer en même temps -  qu'il faut: moderniser l'institution actuelle en la réformant en pro-  fondeur, pour mieux répondre aux espoirs que continuent de mettre en  elle une grande partie des peuples des Nations Unies."
29.186 Comment, en effet, ne pas voir le rôle que joue le système des Nations  Unies dans un monde où les destins nationaux rejoignent de plus en plus la  trajectoire d'un devenir planétaire, mais où, parallèlement, s'aggravent de  nombreuses disparités et s ' exacerbent trop souvent les méfiances et les préjugés  réciproques ? Et comment ne pas voir, à l'heure actuelle, que toute remise en  cause de ce système porterait une atteinte mortelle à la seule tentative visant à  transcender les égoïsmes nationaux et à concevoir un avenir commun à l'humanité,  à l'unique filtre protecteur conçu par la sagesse des hommes contre les radia-  tions mortelles de l'égoïsme, du chauvinisme et du désespoir ?
29.187 Or, il se passe aujourd'hui ceci qu'en recherchant des avantages  nationaux à court terme, au détriment des perspectives internationales  nécessaires, qu'en exagérant l'importance des failles ou des défauts du système - comme s'il pouvait y avoir oeuvre humaine parfaite ! - plutôt que de faire  ressortir ses irremplaçables vertus et de s'efforcer d'en améliorer le fonc-  tionnement, qu'en jetant, plus généralement, le doute sur tout ce qui touche à la  coopération multilatérale, on est en train de saper les fondements de la première  aventure universelle de l'histoire, alors qu'il n'existe aucune autre alternative  à cette quête que le chaos et la guerre. On est en train de créer dans l'orga-  nisme de la communauté internationale l'équivalent d'un virus du syndrome  immuno-déficitaire acquis - ou SIDA.
29.188 Et ce n'est sans doute pas un hasard si l'Unesco a été la première cible  de cette entreprise de déstabilisation. Dans le cadre du système des Nations  Unies, notre Organisation occupe en effet une place particulière. Elle est le  lieu de rencontre des sensibilités, des pensées, des impulsions créatrices les  plus diverses, le champ ouvert d'une permanente floraison d'idées et d'initia-  tives, qui font l'objet de débats, de divergences, voire de confrontations. Cette  effervescence comporte quelque chose d'éminemment équitable dans un monde où  règnent, sur tant d'autres plans, disparités et frustrations : sur le territoire  de l'esprit et de l'art, en effet, les rapports de force n'ont pas cours - et les  peuples se parlent à dignité égale, au nom de leur irréductible a ort à l'his-  toire de la conscience universelle. Enfin, l'effervescence dont 1 Unesco est le  siège a cette vertu, sans doute décisive pour le devenir de l'homme, qu'en  déplaçant les affrontements du terrain de la politique ou de l'économie vers le  terrain de la culture, au sens le plus général du terme, elle leur offre la voie  d'un dépassement possible par fécondation mutuelle.
29.189 Car la culture est, par excellence, le mode d'affirmation de soi qui  permet à chaque peuple de dire son originalité et, dans un même temps, de saisir  celle des autres. En elle résident, dès lors, certaines des solutions virtuelles  aux diverses impasses d'aujourd'hui, certaines des clés d'un devenir où chacun  peut se reconnaître et connaître le reste du monde. Ainsi la vocation de l'Unesco  est-elle de réaliser, à travers la culture, l'empathie de soi et de l'autre, la  respiration de l'un dans le multiple. A l'Unesco, la diversité peut ne plus mener  au conflit, mais au rapprochement et à l'enrichissement mutuel. A l'Unesco peut  naître, du concert des identités assumées et respectées, une nouvelle symphonie  du monde. C'est dire qu'en frappant l'Unesco, on frappe le système des Nations  Unies à la place du coeur.  PP
29.190 Et c'est pourquoi, m'adressant à vous de cette tribune, je tiens à  souligner que nous approchons, désormais, d'un moment décisif. L'Unesco, et à  travers elle l'ensemble du système, doit être préservée coûte que coûte. Ses  idéaux et ses principes doivent être défendus avec la plus grande détermination -  parce que ce sont les premiers qu'aient assumé en commun les hommes de partout.  Et qu'il n'y en a pas d'autres.
29.191 La défense de ces idéaux et de ces principes ne se confond évidement  pas avec le maintien figé des dispositions qui, depuis quarante ans, ont été peu  à peu mises en place pour leur donner vie - et qui, elles, peuvent et doivent  être repensées, en fonction de l'évolution du monde et des volontés conjuguées de  la communauté internationale. Peut-être même les tensions qui se font jour,  depuis quelques années, offrent-elles une occasion unique de réexaminer certains  des mécanismes du système, dans une perspective de démocratisation qui leur  permette d'épouser plus fidèlement la diversité des aspirations et des possi-  bilités de la société internationale et qui mette les organisations à l'abri de  pressions financières possibles.
29.192 Mais il nous faut être parfaitement lucides. Au-delà des changements  nécessaires se profilent parfois des doutes sur les finalités mêmes du système.  Et c'est pourquoi certains termes du débat en cours sont faussés : on parle de  tel ou tel aménagement à faire et d'aucuns entendent déjà qu'il faut abattre  l'édifice tout entier. Dès lors apparaissent des impasses qui tiennent moins à  des mécanismes institutionnels ou financiers dépassés qu'à des malentendus entre  les Etats membres. Les failles du système relèvent alors de l'état des relations  internationales qui le sous-tendent ; elles relèvent surtout d'un affrontement  entre des visions différentes de l'avenir du monde.
29.193 Ce n'est pas la première fois que les Nations Unies connaissent une  telle impasse. A 1 exemple de Dag Hammarskjoeld, il appartient dans ce cas aux  chefs des organisations du système d'assumer pleinement leurs responsabilités.  Guidés par leur conscience, dans le respect des principes de la Charte des  Nations Unies et des Actes constitutifs des institutions spécialisées, allant  parfois à contre-courant de certaines idées largement répandues, c'est à eux de  maintenir le cap de l'avenir, alors même que certains se trouvent désorientés  dans les brumes passagères du présent.
29.194 Permettez-moi d'ajouter, s'agissant de la situation actuelle, que  l'Unesco est un espace de communication humaine complexe, subtil, et par là-même  vulnérable. Mais cette vulnérabilité, qu'elle tient de son ouverture au monde,  fait sa force à long terme. C'est celle même de la plante qui plie sous le vent - mais sans rompre - et qui, une fois passée la bourrasque, se retrouve forti-  fiée, enracinée plus profondément dans le sol OU elle vit. Et la bourrasque est  bien souvent l'annonciatrice de pluies nouvelles, de fécondations à venir.
29.195 Mesdames et Messieurs, il est arrivé à chacun d'entre nous, un jour ou  l'autre, à la lecture de certains grands événements de l'histoire de l'humanité,  d'éprouver soudain un sentiment d'intense frustration. Avec le recul, il sem-  blerait que les hommes eussent pu, dans bien des cas, en faisant preuve d'un  minimum de sagesse et d'ouverture, éviter tant de conflits, tant de désastres,  tant de désillusions I Je vous invite à travailler ensemble, dès aujourd'hui,  afin que nos descendants ne soient jamais saisis de ce même sentiment de frus-  tration, en pensant aux occasions que nous aurions manquées. Je vous invite à  faire de cette vingt-troisième session de la Conférence générale de l'Unesco un  grand moment de vérité, de courage et d'imagination - où la communauté inter-  nationale retrouve de nouvelles raisons de croire en elle-même et d'espérer en  son avenir.
