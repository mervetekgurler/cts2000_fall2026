Turning to the text of the proposed resolution (document 4C/OXR/20), Mr. Mulliken said it had two purposes. The first was to secure an imme- diate change in the directives governing relations with international non-governmental organizations to the end that, instead of the Executive Board determining what organizations should be admit- ted to consultative status, the General Conference should have that task. He emphasized that only one definitive step was asked of the present Gen- eral Conference. The rest of the resolution related to information which it was thought could be pre- sented to the General Conference at its Fifth Ses- sion. With regard to paragraph 1 of the proposed re- solution, Mr. Mulliken pointed out that the Ex- ecutive Board would continue to receive the ap- plications for consultative status; to screen them; possibly to eliminate some of them; to pass on others, which it tentatively approved, to the Gen- eral Conference, where a further screening would take place. The Executive Board would still per- form a very important function in that connexion, but the final decision would rest with the General Conference. Paragraph 2 did not mean that the United States delegation in any way pre-judged the matter in regard to any organization on the list at the present time, but that a periodical review would be desir- able. With reference to paragraph 3, the United States delegation’s belief was that Unesco had possibly been unduly zealous, and that some organizations had been established where there was not a real or demonstrated need. He hoped that the Secre- tariat would comment on the list in paragraph 3.
