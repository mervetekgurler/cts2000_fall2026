[19] Sir Ben THOMAS (United Kingdom), Chair- man of the Working Committee, drew attention to the Working Committee's proposal, in the first paragraph of section IL.1 of the report, to change from 1 January 1951 to 1 January 1952 the date upon which the classification plan for international staff should become effective. It was quite clear that the many administrative changes involved could not be fully implemented until 1952. [20] Referring to sub-paragraph (b) of the Working Committee’s recommendation (document 6C/ADM/
21 Rev., page 5), Mr. TAYLOR (Deputy Director- General) observed that the whole point of intro- ducing the international salary scale was to bring Unesco into line with the United Nations, whereas the Working Committee’s recommendation was not in accordance with United Nations practice. The Director-General was opposed to it, on the ground that Directors of Departments in Unesco should not be treated differently from those in the United Nations. [21] Mr. PHOTIADES (Greece) recalled that the Finance Committee of the Executive Board had been of the same opinion as the Director-General: it would be unsuitable and undignified for high officials of an international organization to be obliged to produce restaurant or pastry-cooks’ bills to justify their representation expenses. [22' Mr. ILLUECA (Panama) asked the Commis- sion to strike out item (b) in section 1 dealing with the international system of salaries and allowances. In his view, it wouldbe better to continue the system prevailing in respect of the representation allowances of Department Directors. They ought not to be ADMINTS TRAT EVE COMMISSION 490 asked to produce supporting documents in respect of the representation expenses they were obliged to incur in any instance. [23] Mr. Illueca's own experienceof the practice in a numberof Latin American countries warranted the assertion that the normal arrangements governing such expenditure were seemly and appropriate to the status of the officials concerned. Moreover, the allowance of $1,000 a year for such representation expenses, far from being excessive, was very modest. In conclusion, Mr. Illueca associated himself with those delegates who hadexpressed similar views and asked the Commission to vote against clause (b). [24] Miss LAISE (United States of America) explained that the Working Committee had inserted the clause because it had been found both in Unesco and in the United Nations that the representation allowance constituted a supplementto salary. United Nations salaries had not been increased as recom- mended by the Committee of Experts, and the representation allowance had therefore beenretained; but, as Unesco salaries were being increased, the representation allowance should be abolished. [25] Mr. BENDER (Netherlands) supported the United States delegate's remarks and the recom- mendation of the Working Committee: with increased salaries, extra expenses could be covered by a voucher system. He agreed with the Greek delegate that the voucher system might involve certain administrative difficulties, but he was sure that the Director-General would be able to overcome them. [26] Mr. TAYLOR (Deputy Director-General) reminded the Commission that the five Directors concerned would be subject to the conditions mentioned in clause (c); a transfer to the inter- national scale would mean an increase of $1,000 for four of them, and an increase of $1,500 for the fifth. If, therefore, the representation allowance of $1,000 was abolished, four of them would have no increase in salary at all, and the fifth would have an increase of only $500. [27] Miss LAISE (United States of America) explained that she had meant that, if salaries were increased, a representation allowance should not be given in addition. [28] Mr. BENDER (Netherlands) observed that the representation allowance would not be withdrawn, but merely given in a different form. [66] Mr. TAYLOR (Deputy Director-General) observed that it would upset the budget if the resolu- tion were adopted. The sum involved was approxi- mately $6,000. There were still many permanent delegations in Paris who did not have office space in Unesco House; if the resolution were passed, not only would fresh requests for offices be received, but the larger delegations would ask for more space. In view of the need for economy, the proposal did not seem expedient, but he suggested that it should be referred to the Budget Committee.
