[5] Mr. GUIGNOLET (France) pointed out that the French proposal, which merely recommended that the question should be considered, went much less far than the United Kingdom draft resolution, proposing that a special budgetary session of the Conference should be held before the end of the first twelve months of a two-year period. [6] The French delegation could not accept the proposal of the delegate of the Union of South Africa that the limited session of the Conference should be replaced by a meeting of the Budget Com- mittee, upon which only a few Member States were represented. The special budgetary session of the General Conference would be attended by represen- tatives of the 64 Member States, but each delegation would be small and the agenda limited. The purpose of the restricted session would be to study the Auditor’s report on the previous financial year, the distribution of surpluses, or measures to be taken in order to meet a possible deficit, any necessary changes in the scale of contributions, and especially the administration of the Working Capital Fund. [7] Although it might be possible to prepare a programme for two years, it would be much more difficult to prepare accurate budget estimates for a period of the same length. Many unexpected events might take place in two years, such as a revaluation of currencies. Mr. Guignolet doubted if it would be possible to wait two years before examining the financial consequences of such events. A special budgetary session of the Conference would enable the Parliaments in the different countries, which were obliged to vote an annual budget, to exercise the necessary supervision. [8] Moreover, as each Member State would send only one or two delegates to a limited session of the Conference, the latter could probably be drawn from Embassies in Paris, thus reducing expenses far below those of an ordinary session; a rough calculation showed that the cost would amount to the relatively small sum of $7,000. That would certainly not be a high price to pay for an assurance of future financial security. The French delegation was therefore strongly in favour of maintaining item 5 in section 1 of the report (document 6C/ PRO/12, page 2) as it stood.
