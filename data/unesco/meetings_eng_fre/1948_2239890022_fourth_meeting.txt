FOURTH     MEETING  Tuesday, 23 November 1948, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman: Hamid Bey FRANGIE (Lebanon)  Date for the discussion of the total budget  figure for 1949.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL suggested that the  total budget figure should be taken at the after-  noon’s plenary meeting.  Professor RIVET (France) was in favour of  hearing all the speeches on the Director-General’s  Report before the budget question was con-  sidered.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL emphasized that the  procedure he had suggested was essential for the  efficient working of the Programme and Budget  Commission and its Sub-Commissions.  Professor RIVET (France) accepted the Direc-  tor-General’s statement.  The suggested procedure was approved.  Organization of the work of the Session  (continuation) (document 8C/BUR/1 (rev.)).  The Committee continued its consideration of  certain matters raised at the first meeting by the  delegate of the United States of America.  Organization of future sessions of the General  Conference.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) sub-  mitted the following resolution :  The General Committee recommends :  That the General Conference establish an ad hoc  committee of the whole, to consider the organization,  scope and content of the work of future Sessions of  the General Conference;  That the ad hoc committee make its report to the  plenary meeting of the General Conference in time for  consideration under item 15 of the Agenda.  The proposal was warmly supported by  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa), who  thought that a 25 per cent increase in efficiency  could be achieved at sessions of the General Con-  ference if they were better organized.  The draft resolution was adopted with two pro-  visos : (1) in order to avoid over-burdening small
220  delegations with numerous committees, and in  order to limit the number of members necessary,  the composition of the ad hoc committee should  be the same as that of the committee which had  been proposed for the study of Unesco’s future  information activities; (2) because of the heavy  work-load up to 1 December, the committee should  meet after that date.  Consideration of the draft Agreement for  facilitating the international circulation of  visual and auditory materials of an edu-  cational, scientific and cultural character.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) sub-  mitted the following draft resolution :  The General Committee remits to the Sub-Com-  mission on Communication the consideration of a  draft convention on auditory and visual materials  (item 18 of the Agenda), for report to a plenary  meeting of the General Conference.  The draft resolution was adopted.  Drafting Committee of the Programme and  Budget Commission.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) sub-  mitted the following resolution as an amended  form of the third paragraph of section 1.2.A.5.2.  of document 3C/BUR/1 (rev.):  The Commission should appoint a drafting com-  mittee to edit the texts of resolutions to be submitted  to the Conference, to place such new resolutions and  such amendments to existing resolutions as are  approved in appropriate parts of the Programme and  to make necessary revisions in the Introduction to the  Programme.  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) suggested the insertion of the words “to  make any necessary re-arrangements of the Pro-  gramme” immediately after “‘parts of the Pro-  gramme”.  The draft resolution, as amended, was adopted.  It was agreed that the Director-General should  make the resolution known to the Chairman of  the Programme and Budget Commission, who  would notify the plenary meeting of the Con-  ference.                  Request of the delegation of Cuba for the  inclusion of a new item on the agenda  (document 3C/13).  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) pointed out an error in the wording, since  the item in question was not a “new” one. The  resolution submitted by the delegation of Cuba  proposed that, in accordance with a resolution  adopted at Mexico City, the General Conference  should  “decide on the geographic areas in which re-  gional centres should be established, particularly  as regards the continent of America.”  The SECRETARY pointed out that the proposed  item could fittingly be studied under the item,  already on the Agenda of the Official and External  Relations Commission, concerning the represen-  tation of Unesco in the main cultural areas of the  world.  Professor RIVET (France) considered that, if  the Cuban resolution were adopted, it would give  the impression of a pre-judgment of the question  of where Unesco’s regional centres were to be set  up, if the decision on the general problem was  finally in favour of decentralization.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) reminded the  meeting that the question of regional centres for  Unesco had been raised at the Mexico City Con-  ference and that the Director-General had been  instructed to consult with the United Nations  Committee on Co-ordination and report back to  the Third Session of the Conference. The ques-  tion was already on the Agenda of the Conference,  and the Cuban resolution merely aimed at en-  suring full discussion of the subject.  M. DORE (Canada) suggested the addition of  the words : “following a favourable decision on  the question of principle” to the proposed reso-  lution.  On the proposal of Dr. WALKER (Chairman of  the Executive Board), the draft resolution sub-  mitted by the Cuban delegation was adopted,  with the recommendation that the matter should  be referred to the appropriate Commission as soon  as the General Committee thought fit.  Terms of reference of the Programme Sub-  Commissions.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) sub-  mitted a proposal, arising out of discussions at  the Reconstruction Sub-Commission on 20 No-  vember, that all Sub-Commissions should con-  fine their resolutions to programme matters,
2 ~  omitting any mention of the budget or detailed  work plans for the implementation of the pro-  gramme. The Budget Sub-Commission would  then be left to introduce resolutions on the  budget.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL stressed the need for  adequate correlation between programme reso-  lutions and proposed expenditure.  Dr. WALKER (Chairman of the Executive  Board) emphasized the same point and pointed  out that it was, in any case, for the Executive  Board and the Director-General to take decisions  upon the plan of work required for the imple-  mentation of the Programme.  The General Committee decided to define the  scope of the work of Programme Sub-Commissions  as follows : Programme Sub-Commissions should  not pass formal resolutions on any but pro-  gramme matters; they should merely formulate  general directions concerning the budget for the  guidance of the Budget Sub-Commission, which  would then pass the formal resolutions on the  subject; and they should not finally approve work  plans, but should merely give general directions  for the guidance of the Executive Board and the  Director-General in making their decisions on the  methods to be used for the implementation of  the programme.  International Committee of Experts on the  preservation of historical monuments and  sites.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) read a proposal  suggesting that, during the coming year, there  should be created, under Unesco’s auspices, an  international committee of experts on the pre-  servation of historical sites and monuments, and  that the Director-General should be asked to  report on the possibilities of creating an inter-  national fund to help in the preservation of such  monuments.  On the proposal of the CHAIRMAN, it was  agreed that the delegate of Brazil should submit  his proposal in writing to the next meeting of the  Committee.  Candidates for election to the Executive  Board.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) proposed  that a meeting of the Nominations Committee  should be held between the date of the election  of the new Director-General and 3 December, to  discuss names proposed for election to the Exe-  cutive Board.
1        After some discussion, it was agreed to defer  the proposal until the next meeting, by which  time the Secretariat would have been able to study  the question of the most convenient date for such  a meeting.  Study of Eastern history.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) submitted to the  FIFTH     meeting a letter, which the head of the delegation  of Afghanistan had asked him to transmit, and  which contained a request that the study of  Eastern history should be more widely included  in the history textbooks of the Western world.  It was decided that the request should be sub-  mitted to the Sub-Commission on Education.
