2.1 Miss YOUNG (United Kingdom): ;  Thank you, Mr. President , be Jnodtd 14 like to support very warmly the. analysls in document
18 C/103 of the rôle Unéadosholld play "in Felatioh to the Declaration” ‘and Programme of  Action. The Director-General has rightly eliminated a large part of these resolutions as being  appropriate to other United Nations fora. We can support his suggestions for Unesco action in  paragraph 9 of document 18 C/103, but we should like to commend particularly strongly the  Director-General's emphasis on the missing socio-cultural dimension of development. This is,  indeed, an essential part ot the development process and is an area in which Unesco can take  the lead‘and dah play 4° unique tôle. - We share’ equally his views on tHe total application of jus-  tice and the distribution of a country's resources among its people.
2.2 With regard to the Special Programme, I think the document is a little over-pessimistic,  particularly in paragraph 35. There is, perhaps, some confusion between the two prongs of the  Special Programme. The first, the'e ergency operation,’ is already under way and a total of
2, 700 million dollars has already been pledged,’ of which. 223 million will be:paid into the  Secretary-General's special account. More is needed, of course, and it'is to be hoped that it  will be forthcoming, fn particular from those countries which have aé yet made no commitment.  But some immediate relief to the countries most seriously affected by the econcmic crisis is  already flowing. The second prong, the Spectal Fund, was only intended to be established from
1 January 1975, and to deal not only with immediate emergency aid but also with longer-term  development assistance. "There is clearly no rôle for Unesco in the first prong, the emergency  operation, which is simply concerned with the quick supply of funds, food and fertilizers, but it  should be able to play an important part in the second stage.
2.3 As regards strengthening the United Nations system in the field of international economic  co-operation and action for development, 1 would urge very strongly that we avoid recommending  the establishmerit of new bodies, The machinery {4 thére already. ‘It only needs to bé used properly,  ef(iciently, and above all with goôd will, and this applies to both sécretariats and member gov-  ernments. As the distinguished delegate of the Federal Republic of Germahy sald yesterday in  another context, member governments should co-ordinate the activities of their delegations in  the different fora. If members of the governing council of a Specialized Agency think alterations  need to be made to UNDP's procedures, they should propose these in the UNDP Governing Coun-  cil, and vice versa. This will be far more effective than passing critical resolutions in the  complaining organization's meetings, ’
2.4 Now, if 1 may comment briefly on the draft resolutions before us; we found document
18 C/PLEN/DR.15 immensely interesting and thought- provoking, containing a wealth of mate-  rial for reflection. It does seem, however, to lend support to the notion of creating new mech-  anisms of which, as I have already said, my Government doubts the necessity. The Belgian pro-  posal has, nevertheless, the merit of being more modest than some others that have been put  forward.
2.5 With regard to 18 C/PLEN/DR. 13, I would only remind delegations that the United Kingdom  made a number of reservations on resolutions 3201 and 3202 at the sixth special session of the  General Assembly, and that we maintain those reservations. Thank you, Mr. President.
