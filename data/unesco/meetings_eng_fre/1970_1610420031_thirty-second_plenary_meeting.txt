22.1 Mr, HELA (Finland) Chairman of the Administrative Commission:  Mr. President, in introducing document 16 C/46 concerning geographical distribution  of Secretariat staff, the Assistant Director-General for Administration recognized that  although some progress had been achieved in recent years in improving the geographical  distribution of posts in the Secretariat, the present situation was not satisfactory. He re-  ferred to the measures already taken by the Director-General in order to achieve a more  balanced representation of nationalities and emphasized that substantial improvement  could only be achieved if Member States gave their full co-operation to the Director-  General, inter alia by submitting candidates with the required training and professional  experience,
22, 2 The delegate of the USSR stressed his conviction that a better geographical distribu-  tion would increase the efficiency and effectiveness of the Secretariat, Although he wel-  comed the steps taken by the Director-General, more had to be done to achieve a full im-  plementation of the principle of geographical distribution,
22, 3 The Director-General expressed his conviction that it would be preferable not to lay  down rigid rules for the appointment of staff, because in some cases exceptions were in  the interests of good administration, He referred specifically to cases of exceptionally-  qualified candidates and to the desirability of making a limited amount of P- 1/P-2 posts
32  accessible to General Service Staff whose advancement to the Professional ranks was a  matter of justice,
22,4 During the ensuing debate several members of the Commission expreseed their con-  cern that too many Member States and regions were still insufficiently represented in the  Secretariat. Some members referred specifically to the under-representation of the Asian  region, Some felt that the present criteria governing geographical distribution, which are  based upon contributions to the budget of the Organization, were not entirely satisfactory  and that they could be supplemented by others, such as population, level of posts and re-  presentation on a regional basis,
22,5 The debate of the United Kingdom had pointed out that although the first table in the  Annex to document 16 C/46 appeared to indicate that 20 new appointments had gone to na-  tionals of his own country since July 1968, 18 of these posts arose from the inclusion in
1969 of the International Institute of Educational Planning and of UNDP posts within the sys-  tem of geographical distribution, The delegate of Trinidad and Tobago referred to the in-  adequate geographical distribution of posts within certain departments of the Secretariat,
22,6 The Director-General observed that although the present criteria were numerical and  not weighted, in fact he took into consideration many other factors whenever deciding on  appointments to vacant posts, He noted that no formal proposal had heen made to change  the present system and stated that the Executive Board would have every opportunity to  study the matter in the light of the reports submitted to it at its future sessions, He em-  phasized that whereas the Secretariat could present the data on geographical distribution  in any form that the General Conference requested, including the breakdown of nationali-  ties by departments, it was clear that the principle of equitable geographical distribution  had to be applied to the Secretariat as a whole, He ha! heen particularly impressed with  the statement made by some delegates of developing countries not represented in the Secre-  tariat, that their governments had not submitted candidates for appointment to the Secre-  tariat because they needed all qualified persons in their own countries, This was the reason  why he had proposed in the Draft Programme and Budget for 1971-1972 the establishment  of several posts reserved for officials seconded by developing Member States for periods  not exceeding three years, thus contributing to their overall training,

26,1 Mr. HELA (Finland) Chairman of the Administrative Commission:  Mr. President, item 46 refers to staff policy with particular reference to the grant-  ing of indeterminate appointments,
26, 2 The Commission held a lengthy discussion on this item. In introducing document
16 C/50 the Director-General stressed the necessity of instituting a system for the grant-  ing of indeterminate appointmente based on objective criteria, He pointed out that while  the principle of geographical distribution must be taken into account in determining the  overall proportion of indeterminate appointments, it should not serve as an element of  appreciation in individual cases, since the granting of an indeterminate appointment to a  staff member must be based exclusively on merit and suitability, He also said that the  system envisaged in document 16 C/50 would be applied to field staff as well as to Head-  quarters staff,
26.3 Two members of the Commission expressed the view that the granting of permanent  contracts should be the exception rather than the rule, They emphasiczed the importance  of the principle of equitable geographical distribution of posts, which did not accord with  the system of permanent contracts, Moreover, a constant renewal of the Unesco Secre-  tariat should be one of the basic criteria of staff policy, Other members of the Commis-  sion felt that in certain posts of the Secretariat, continuity was an advantage, Another  member of the Commit ter thought that the number of indeterminate appointments granted  to date was insufficient and should be substantially increased,
26, 4 In commenting on the proposed draft resolution the Director-General inquired as to  the exact meaning of paragraph 7 concerning the principle of "equitable geographical dis-  tribution", He repeated that the question of geographical distribution was an important  consideration in choosing between candidates for a given post in the Secretariat, He was  convinced, however, that once a candidate had been appointed and become a staff member  of the Organization, it would be unjust and inequitable to let his nationality affect the  course of his career. Consequently the question whether indeterminate contracts should  or should not be offered to an individual staff member must, in his opinion, be determined  by sole reference to his competence and suitability in the light of the criteria enumerated  in document 16 C/50,
26.5 The sponsors of the draft resolution pointed out that they proposed to omit sub-  paragraph (ii) of paragraph 5 (f) of document 16 C/50, because they felt that in any case,
