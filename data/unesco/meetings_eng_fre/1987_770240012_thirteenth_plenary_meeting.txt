(4) The PRESIDENT (Translation from the Spanish):  We shall continue the general policy ,debate. I give the floor to His  Excellency Ambassador Luis SimÓn Figueroa del Rio, Permanent Delegate of Chile to  Unesco and head of his country's delegation.
(5.1) Mr FIGUEROA DEL RIO (Chile) (Translation from the Spanish):  Mr President, Mr Director-General, Distinguished Delegates, this Conference  is being attended by high-ranking government officials and persons who are eminent in
442    the fields of education and science, clear proof of Member States' interest in the  management and functioning of Unesco. The atmosphere at this twenty-fourth session of  the General Conference is one of anxiety, and this is inevitable: the election of the  Director-General, the need to establish guidelines for determining our policy, and  pressing financial problems suffice to make it so.
(5.2) The problems relating to the Organization's future that have to be settled  are the focal point of attitudes and are surrounded by concrete facts, myths and  beliefs. We no longer feel that profound desolation experienced by so many peoples  that were destroyed or ruined by the great and terrible war which induced leaders and  thinkers in a number of countries to light a common torch of hope through an  international agreement that gave birth to Unesco.
(5.3) The enthusiasm for creating the foundations on which our hopes could be built  has been transformed in only 40 years into pride and satisfaction in the achievements  in the various branches of knowledge and in the conquest of space; but it has also  been transformed into fear and terror of the existing machinery of war; humanity has  always used the instruments of destruction that it has itself produced.
(5.4) Societies that have been relatively static and settled in their pattern of  life are today experiencing and observing this new phenomenon of the dynamic growth of  economies, which has enabled peoples and nations to produce in less than one  generation goods and resources that reach the entire world, bringing even the remotest  corners of the earth into the sphere of their creativity and inventiveness.
(5.5) Today any nation that has clear objectives, determination and appropriate  policies can overcome its traditional limitations and actively join in this  extraordinary adventure of humanity. Achievements of this kind have, in 40 years,  altered the scale of events; there are now only two superpowers, and the chronicle of  momentous events has assumed continental dimensions. Nations are being increasingly  linked by trade and financial systems and this intense activity is fostering the  exchange of knowledge. Purely official relations between States are becoming much less  influencia1 than the force of natural and necessary contacts between nations. This  sustained and bewildering progress has had the effect of confusing men's minds and, if  this were not all, misleading words and phrases repeated as though they were accepted  truths add to the confusion and obstruct clear, sound reasoning. A case in point is  the concept of the 'Third World'. What does it really mean? Does it perhaps refer to  cultures that are less developed? By no means, because societies are identified and  become known and respected through their cultures, which develop at their own pace:  there are ancient cultures with a wealth of tradition and harmony, and there are new  and active cultures, full of determination to transform what already exists. Each  culture makes its own contribution to the individuals within it.
(5.6) Inclusion in the 'Third World' category would imply having similar  characteristics. What are those characteristics? The quest for harmony, the conception  of matter, the idea of transcendence, the taste for luxury, the way food is cooked?  This line of reasoning will get us nowhere. Nor is industrialization a criterion of  classification: there are prosperous and contented agricultural societies, just as  there are others that obtain their wealth or comfort from the extraction of minerals.  And so far as poverty is concerned we know that once the need for shelter, a roof over  one's head, food, health and access to knowledge is met, the difference lies in  quality, in which is linked climate, customs, tastes, fashion and so on.
(5.7) It is pointless to continue along these lines: 'Third World' is an expression  that is misleading in an international organization that seeks to establish  understanding among peoples and is required to bring clarity to the 'minds of men', as  set out in the Constitution. What we have is a planet organized in the form of nations - some different, some similar - with their own development characeristiks, linked  through trade, ideas and law, jealous of their sovereignty and eager for genuine  solidarity.
(5.8) What do we want of Unesco? We do not need to think long about it: it suffices  to peruse the objectives set forth in its Constitution; they are plain and  crystal-clear and might easily be thought of as a childish dream. I think that it is  here that the heart of the crisis lies. Arrogance, pride, fear, material wealth, the  new geographical reality and myths have perhaps led to a loss of faith in Unesco's  objectives: such lofty ideals cannot be attained without faith. We can never repeat  sufficiently often our endorsement of these objectives.
(5.9) Nevertheless, the situation that we have described means that a different  method of work is called for. Here are a few ideas on this subject. The premise on  which Unesco's logic is based is that 'wars begin in the minds of men'. It goes  without saying that the message of understanding is not addressed to leaders or  scientists and educators, because it is they who brought Unesco into being and have  supported it. It is, then, directed at the people at large, but the general public is  ignorant of the Organization, let alone its objectives.
(5.10) If we wish to establish appropriate policies for Unesco, if we really believe  in its objectives, if we want Unesco to be recognized without doubts or reservations,  it is vital that we acknowledge one immense truth that imposes upon us a series of  practical tasks. Not only is Unesco unknown to most people in the world, but even as  we are pronouncing these lofty speeches the world is creating quite naturally, without  our participation, millions of contacts and links, contributing more than we can  possibly imagine to knowledge and to understanding among peoples.
(5.11) Ladies and Gentlemen, one important initial task would be to set aside part  of the budget for the dissemination of information, using modern means of advertising  and communication, in order to explain what Unesco aspires to, not what it does, but  what it seeks to achieve. If we believe in its ideals, if we have faith in them, all  men and women in the world should know that the politicians and intellectuals of our  planet, at the risk of being considered ingenuous, seek balanced international  relations based on the integrity of people's intentions rather than on power  relationships.
(5.12) There can be no doubt that this vast publicity campaign will be instrumental  in fostering valuable and selfless work by men and women to promote Unesco's  objectives. What is more, it will help to guarantee., once and for all, respect for the  right of nations unconditionally to determine their own forms of social organization  and progress without foreign interference and ensure that outside attempts to  destabilize governments in power are held to be unacceptable. Such a campaign will  debar certain experts from giving their views on matters of which they know nothing  and from arrogantly and simplistically laying down the law on how a foreign country  should be governed. If the message of concord set forth in Unesco's Constitution can  reach the minds of the great majority, there can be no doubt that its beneficial  effects will be incalculable.  *
(5.13) The second major task is the process of reflection that is being proposed and  about which so much has been said. Clearly Unesco cannot stand-zside from analysis of  the changes occurring in present-day societies if it is to alert all who endorse its  message of concord to the pitfalls and possibilities that lie ahead. Nevertheless,  this process of reflection must be restricted to the context of the Organization's  specialized fields. Discipline and intellectual rigour must, here, be a model of  effectiveness and a source of confidence. But that is not sufficient. The results of  the discussions and procedures should, if possible, be widely disseminated. It is my  firm conviction that Unesco's action must find its strength in the interest it can  arouse among the people at large as well as among a specialized public.
(5.14) As far as the third task is concerned, that is co-operation as such, it would  be as well to bear in mind the increasingly obvious phenomenon of growing links  between firms, experts, universities, foundations, corporations and research  institutions throughout the. world. The co-operation schemes being promoted by  international organizations should take advantage of the efficiency, speed and  reasonable cost of the services provided by those individuals and bodies. The  international organizations cannot compete with them, because the prestige of  multilateral co-operation would be put at risk. Firstly, there would be criticism in  regard to feasibility, timing and the period required to launch the scheme; next, its  cost would be criticized; and, lastly, its effectiveness. The most suitable procedure  at the present time would be for organizatins to avail themselves of the services of  these experts and bodies which, subject to the free choice of the beneficiary country,  would be contracted out at the market price under the supervision of Unesco.
(5.15) Mr President, provided that we realize that the public at large must be  involved in our action; that we recognize the changes that 'have taken place in  people's minds between the time of the founding of Unesco and the present day; that we  consider economic, technical, political, social, geographical and other world factors  not as something to be contemplated but as experiences that Unesco must absorb and  that, consequently, we must modernize the co-operation exercise; provided that  analyses and reflection focus on the Organization's undisputed specialized fields, and  the concepts employed and disseminated are clear-cut and precise, making a genuine  contribution to our capacity for discernment - which means discarding forthwith those  whose meaning is ambiguous - then, and only then, can we be certain that Unesco will  possess the strength of all that emerges as necessary and indispensable.
(5.16) Mr President, I cannot end without congratulating you on your election and  expressing the immense satisfaction I feel on hearing the Spanish language spoken in  the high office that is yours. I should also like to take this opportunity to express  my cordial greetings to that educationist from Senegal, Mr Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow, whose  term of office as Director-General of our Organization is coming to an end. His  unswerving determination to fight for the ideas in which he has placed so much faith  fully deserves our admiration. Thank you very much, Mr President.

7.1 M. HONWANA (Mozambique) :  Monsieur le Président de la Conférence générale, Monsieur le Président  du Conseil exécutif, Monsieur le Directeur général, Messieurs les ministres et  délégués, Mesdames et Messieurs, permettez-moi, Monsieur le Président, de com-  mencer par vous féliciter pour votre election unanime au poste de président de  cette vingt-quatrième session de la Conference générale de l'Unesco. Aux compli-  ments protocolaires je souhaite ajouter l'appréciation de ma délégation pour la  façon sûre et efficace dont vous conduisez les travaux, aussi bien au cours des  séances plénières qu'au Bureau de la Conférence. Sous votre direction, Monsieur  le Président, nous sommes assurés du succès de cette vingt-quatrième session,  qui est appelée à se prononcer sur des questions de grande importance pour la  réalisation des objectifs de l'organisation.
7.2 Cette reunion a pour toile de fond l'extrême perturbation des relations  politiques internationales. Alors que le fossé qui sépare les pays riches des  pays pauvres se creuse, on assiste à la dégradation rapide des valeurs morales  et éthiques sous-tendant les conceptions politiques fondamentales qui, 6 la fin  de la deuxième guerre mondiale, semblaient constituer un point de consensus uni-  versel. De nouveau, on retrouve dans les relations entre Etats et dans la solu-  tion des conflits internationaux l'arrogance et le chantage, l'absence de respect  d'autrui, voire le recours à la violence, à la subversion, à l'agression armée ;  a la fin de ce XXe siècle coexistent les plus grandes réalisations du génie  humain dans les domaines des sciences et de la technique et les manifestations  les plus avilissantes de la discrimination et du racisme. En Afrique, notamment
12 où subsistent des situations coloniales et des ambitions hégémoniques carac-  térisées par la pratique et l'encouragement des prétendus conflits localisés,  s'accroissent de façon dramatique le nombre de réfugiés et de personnes déplacées  ainsi que la mortalité infantile. Dans de nombreux pays de notre continent, le  rêve de paix, de justice et de prospérité, associé à l'idée d'indépendance, est  déjà bien éloigné d'un quotidien de guerre, de misère et de famine, causé par  l'intervention d'intérêts qui ne trouvent pas leur place dans une Afrique auto-  déterminée et souveraine. La conscience des inégalités profondes qui séparent  les sociétés humaines et, plus particulièrement, la conscience de l'injustice  des causes profondes des déséquilibres, ne se reflètent pas naturellement dans  les positions des Etats et des forces politiques émergeant de ce qu'il est don-  venu d'appeler le tiers monde. Pour nous la correction des facteurs de déséqui-  libre et l'éradication des causes de déstabilisation doivent constituer la  préoccupation commune de la communauté internationale tout entière.
7.3 Dans le système des Nations Unies, l'Unesco est appelée & mener un  effort de création et de maintien de la paix dans les domaines ou elle garantit  la solidarité intellectuelle et morale, dans un cadre d'égalité, de dignité et  de respect de la personne humaine. La participation de la République populaire  du Mozambique à la vie et aux activités de l'Unesco est le témoignage de notre  adhésion renouvelée aux objectifs énoncés dans l'Acte constitutif de l'organisa-  tion et de notre conviction que ce forum a un rôle irremplaçable à jouer, dans  le cadre de la coopération multilatérale, pour la solution des grandes questions  liées à l'éducation, à la science, à la culture et à la communication. Depuis  notre entree 6 l'Organisation, nous avons trouvé au niveau des différents dépar-  tements et services de l'Unesco le meilleur climat de compréhension et d'ouver-  ture. Le nombre d'actions que nous avons pu réaliser dans notre pays avec l'appui  de l'Unesco, tant au titre du Programme ordinaire que du Programme de participa-  tion, est appréciable. Nous espérons faire encore davantage, grâce & la création  du Bureau régional d'Harare, qui permettra, nous en Sommes sûrs, de mieux faire  connaître la situation tres particulière que vit l'Afrique australe.
7.4 Pour l'impact qu'elles ont eu dans mon pays, j'aimerais citer, parmi  tant d'autres réalisations de l'Unesco : la préparation du Programme régional  d'élimination de l'analphabétisme en Afrique, à la suite des recommandations  formulées par la Conférence MINEDAF V ; la tenue de la Conférence CASTAFRICA II ;  les célébrations de l'Année internationale de la paix et du 40e anniversaire de  l'Unesco, et le traitement très particulier que l'Unesco a accordé à la Confé-  rence internationale contre l'apartheid. S'agissant de la réalisation de ces  activités, et de bien d'autres encore, et, plus généralement, de la conduite des  travaux de notre Organisation durant ces treize dernières années, il est juste
445    de mettre l'accent sur l'intervention dynamique du Directeur général, M. Amadou-  Mahtar M'Bow. Je me joins, en mon nom propre et au nom de mon pays, aux homages  qui ont été rendus au cours de cette Conférence à M. M'Bow, avec Peque1 je garde  une relation de sympathie et. de considération, et dont j'admire le dévouement  exemplaire à la cause de l'Unesco et de la solidarité humaine.
7.5 Monsieur le Président, l'Unesco, comme d'autres institutions multilaté-  rales, connaît une situation de crise. La proposition formulée par certains pays - et pas assez discutée en public - selon laquelle les voix seraient pondérées  proportionnellement 5 la contribution financière des Etats dans les institutions  multilatérales, est à l'origine de cette crise. Cette prérogative, qui équivau-  drait à generaliser le droit de veto, est contraire au principe de l'égalité  stricte entre les Etats, qui est énoncé dans les statuts et règlements internes  d'institutions comme l'Unesco : il s'agit donc d'essayer de remplacer, dans la  pratique internationale, la coopération multilatérale par la coopération bilaté-  rale, laquelle, contrairement à la première, établit un lien politique évident  entre le pays bénéficiaire et le pays donateur, s'agissant de nations ayant  atteint des niveaux de développement différents.
7.6 C'est dans ce contexte qu'est avancé, en faveur de la coopération bila-  térale, l'argument de l'excessive lourdeur administrative dont souffrent parfois  les organismes de coopération multilatérale. L'Unesco, comme d'autres institu-  tions de dimension comparable, est confrontée à la nécessité d'adapter ses  infrastructures et ses mécanismes de fonctionnement à de nouvelles exigences  sociales et à de nouvelles techniques de gestion. On attend de notre Organisation  une décentralisation plus grande, propre à assurer une présence plus efficace  sur le terrain, et un meilleur équilibre entre les activités de réflexion et  l'action ; on attend de l'Unesco une plus grande ouverture intellectuelle et un  plus grand souci de l'innovation avec, pour conséquence, un allègement de l'in-  tervention bureaucratique. Bien que notre Organisation ait joué, au sein des  Nations Unies, un rôle de pionnier dans la réalisation de réformes internes,  elle est devenue la cible de critiques sévères, qui atteignent ses propres fon-  dements institutionnels.
7.7 La personnalisation déplorable de cette crise nous a conduit au jeu  stérile de la confrontation entre les blocs, faussant ainsi les règles démocra-  tiques qui doivent présider à la concertation d'intérêts entre Etats souverains,  provoquant le retrait de certains Etats de l'Organisation et concourant à  l'aggravation de la situation, ce qui s'est traduit concrètement par la réduc-  tion, voire la suppression, de certaines activités. Ce précédent dangereux a  déstabilisé notre Organisation, 5 tel point qu'il devient fréquent aujourd'hui  d'entendre proférer la menace de nouveaux retraits dès qu'on examine tel ou tel  sujet.
7.8 C'est avec une profonde inquiétude que nous avons constaté ces atti-  tudes, qui ne facilitent pas le maintien du climat de sérénité et de confiance  si nécessaire 6 la solution des graves problèmes auxquels l'Unesco est confron-  tée. Nous avons suivi avec la plus grande attention les travaux du Comité spécial  du Conseil exécutif, et nous avons encourage le processus de réformes, dans les  limites imposées par les statuts de notre Organisation. Pour la République popu-  laire du Mozambique, il importe de rendre son universalité à l'Unesco sans rien  sacrifier pour autant de l'essentiel des objectifs statutaires de l'Organisation.
7.9 L'élection du Directeur général de l'organisation, inscrite 6 l'ordre  du jour de cette vingt-quatrième session de la Conférence générale, constitue un  élément majeur de l'effort de relance de l'Unesco, qui a connu des moments im-  portants lors de la vingt-troisième session de la Conférence générale, à Sofia.  Nous formons l'espoir que le climat dramatique qui a marqué la session du Conseil  exécutif au cours de laquelle a été désigné le candidat au poste de Directeur  général s'apaisera lors du processus de consultations et de négociations entre  les différents groupes électoraux, afin que la Conférence générale puisse se  prononcer dans le sens de l'unification, du renforcement et du progrès de
7.10 Monsieur le Président, la République populaire du Mozambique félicite  le Secrétariat de l'organisation pour la qualité des documents présentés, en  particulier le Projet de programme et de budget pour 1988-1989, ainsi que pour  l'effort de concentration du programme qu'il a déployé en vue d'accroître l'ef-  ficacité et de garantir une meilleure utilisation des ressources disponibles.
7.11 Cependant, nous sommes convaincus qu'il est impératif de développer la  collaboration avec d'autres organismes du système des Nations Unies. C'est ainsi  et ainsi seulement que nous pourrons garantir la coordination des efforts néces-  saires pour l'accomplissement des tâches spécifiques à chaque organisation tout  en évitant les chevauchements de fonction et la dispersion des ressources
13  humaines et financières qui en résulte. La complémentarité de l'action des orga-  nisations internationales, intergouvernementales et non gouvernementales devra  constituer un autre point de repère dans l'exécution du programme du prochain  exercice financier.
7.12 La délégation mozambicaine aura l'occasion d'exposer son point de vue  en détail lors des débats dans les commissions ; aussi me contenterai-je ici de  quelques brèves remarques sur le document 24 c/5. Nous avons analysé de façon  approfondie les grands programmes I, VIII, XII, XII1 et XIV qui à notre avis  fournissent le cadre conceptuel de l'action de l'Unesco et ouvrent des Perspec-  tives 6 l'action future de l'organisation. Je dois dire cependant que, dans les  conditions particulières de l'Afrique australe, la réalisation de ces grands  programmes, notamment le grand programme XII et le grand programme XIII, est  conditionnée par la situation à laquelle nos Etats sont confrontés quotidienne-  ment. Permettez-moi donc d'attirer votre attention sur l'importance et l'oppor-  tunité du programme XII.3, intitulé "Lutte contre l'apartheid". A la suite de la  réunion d'experts tenue à Beijing en septembre 1986, le programme se ProPoSe,  parmi d'autres objectifs, de développer une activité de recherche en sciences  sociales et humaines qui puisse conduire à une meilleure compréhension de l'his-  toire du mouvement nationaliste en Afrique australe et des relations entre le  Système de l'apartheid et l'ensemble de la région sur le plan politique et éco-  nomique. Nous pensons que ces travaux feront apparaître la réalité de l'action  dégradante, criminelle et intolérable du régime de l'apartheid en Afrique du Sud  elle-même et dans les Etats indépendants de la région.
7.13 Sans prétendre être exhaustif et en me situant seulement dans le cadre  des objectifs concernant le grand programme II - Education pour tous, je vou-  drais rappeler que, voilà quelques semaines, en prononçant son discours devant  l'Assemblée générale des Nations Unies, le président Joaquim Alberto Chissano a  dénoncé la responsabilité de Pretoria dans les actions terroristes qui ont gra-  vement endommagé 2.000 écoles primaires au Mozambique, interdisant ainsi à plus  d'un demi million d'écoliers de mener une vie scolaire normale. L'action directe  et indirecte engagée contre mon pays atteint également les moyens de transport,  les voies de communication, les infrastructures civiles et les unités économiques  et maintient un climat de trouble et de déstabilisation qui, tout en accentuant  l'effet des sécheresses prolongées que nous avons subies ces dernières années,  affecte environ quatre millions de Mozambicains. Malgré la situation d'urgence  que nous vivons au Mozambique, mon pays a le plus grand intérêt à ce que soient  mises en oeuvre les activités prévues au titre des grands programmes II, III,  IV, V I  VIII, XI et XIV.
7.14 Permettez-moi, en ma qualité de Ministre de la culture, de faire une  observation spéciale à propos du grand programme XI et des préparatifs en cours  dans mon pays en vue de l'annonce de la constitution de la Commission nationale  pour la Décennie du développement culturel. En vue de la réalisation des actions  prévues au titre du Programme national et régional de la Décennie, nous mainte-  nons une étroite coordination avec les pays de la SADCC et avec les autres pays  africains ayant comme langue officielle le portugais ; nous avons aussi des con-  tacts avec des pays d'autres régions. Parmi les actions que nous envisageons de  mener dans le contexte de la Décennie et en association avec l'Unesco, nous  citerons l'Exposition sur l'art makonde et les projets relatifs à 1'Ile du  Mozambique, aux archives du patrimoine culturel et au fonds bibliographique de  la langue portugaise, ainsi que le projet de centre d'études des langues  mozambicaines.
7.15 Monsieur le Président, la République populaire du Mozambique croit  résolument en l'optimisme. Les résultats, bien qu'encore timides, du dialogue  Sud-Sud donnent corps à l'espoir d'un nouvel équilibre dans les relations inter-  nationales, qui permette aux pays en développement une défense conjointe et le  rassemblement sur leurs intérêts communs. La preuve en est que les moyens de  cooperation régionale en Asie, en Afrique et en Amérique latine vont en se  développant.
7.16 Les progrès récents dans l'effort de dialogue entre l'Est et l'Ouest,  notamment en ce qui concerne les conditions d'un désarmement progressif et la  réduction des arsenaux nucléaires est pour nous un motif d'espoir fondé. La  signature récente dans votre pays, Monsieur le Président, de l'accord sur les  procedures à mettre en oeuvre pour établir une paix ferme et durable en Amérique  centrale constitue, nous en sommes certains, un pas important vers la stabilisa-  tion de l'Amérique centrale. Nous espérons que les efforts de médiation réussi-  ront à rétablir les conditions du dialogue propre à permettre la réduction des  tensions au Moyen-Orient, au Maghreb et dans d'autres zones oh la paix 3 été  troublée.
7.17 En Afrique australe, nous espérons que la pression de la communauté  internationale parviendra à détruire la cause unique de l'humiliation, de la
447    violence et du deuil dans notre zone. Le régime de l'apartheid constitue la sur-  vivance aberrante des temps sombres où l'homme n'avait pas encore appris à assu-  mer son essence plurielle et la nécessité absolue du respect de l'égalité dans  les relations humaines.
7.18 Pour terminer, j'aimerais réaffirmer l'appui résolu que le peuple et le  gouvernement de mon pays apportent aux objectifs de cette Organisation, lesquels  participent à la promotion de la paix, du développement et du consensus. Merci,  Monsieur le Président.

(9.1) Mr MALEVITCH (Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic) (Translation from the  Russian) :  Mr President, distinguished delegates, I should like first of all to join in  the congratulations addressed to you, Mr President, and the other members of the  General Committee on your election to such high office; and to wish you success in the  execution of your responsibilities.
(9.2) The delegation of the Byelorussian SSR is fully aware of the importance of  this session of the General Conference, and is prepared to do everything in its power  to help to strengthen Unesco and enhance its role and authority in the matter of  international Co-operation in education, science, culture and information, and in the  collective resolution of the global problems facing mankind.
(9.3) The history of civilization has seen numerous attempts to find forms of human  existence that might lead to harmonious relations between man and his fellows, between  the individual and the species, between mankind and nature. Today, the quest for a new  international order in the domains of economics, information, culture and human  relations has assumed global dimensions, in which Unesco by its very nature is called  upon to play a stimulating role.
(9.4) Needless to say, a just world order and a new political philosophy will not  come into being from one day to the next. But unless progress is made in that  direction, the sole alternative will be rivalry and confrontation, the consequences of  which could be catastrophic for the entire world.
(9.5) Unesco is an organization endowed by its Constitution with a creative,  constructive prerogative. It is the centrepiece of a unique network of iriternational,  intellectual, collaborative relations. As we formulate its programme of activities for  the coming period, we cannot but take into account the 40 years of experience of such  collaboration that our Organization has accumulated. Unesco has much that is positive  and useful to its credit. Not without reason has it been termed ‘a laboratory of  ideas’ and praised in other terms that bear witness to the authority which it enjoys.  Its future programme activities must be more comprehensively, consequentially and  clearly designed to increase its contribution to the development of States and peoples  and the creation of conditions for fruitful collaboration in the intellectual and  operational fields alike.
(9.6) We believe that the Draft Programme and Budget (document 24 C/5) submitted  for approval at this session constitutes a good general basis for acceptable and  concerted decisions. But at the same time, we consider that the Draft suffers from a  lack of balance where certain programme matters are concerned. At certain points, it  is not fully concordant with the guidelines and principles set out in the Medium-Term  Plan for 1984-1989, or with general resolutions adopted at previous sessions of the  General Conference.
(9.7) We have grounds for noting with regret that the past two or three years have  beea marked by a somewhat timid approach to the implementation of measures related to  Unesco's contribution - within its spheres of competence - to the prevention of  nuclear catastrophe, to peace and disarmament and to the creation of a climate of  absolute confidence and mutual understanding among peoples.
(9.8) In this connection, it is apposite to underline the particular significance  of the programmes on the 'maintenance of peace and international understanding' and  'education for peace and respect for human rights and the rights of peoples'. We  approve their orientation towards the promotion of increased familiarity with and  understanding of disarmament issues, and the investigation of the interrelationship  between peace, security and disarmament on the one hand and educational, scientific  and cultural development on the other. We believe that these programmes and their  underlying principles must be transformed from statements of intent into specific  act ions.
(9.9) We also believe that particular attention should be paid in Unesco's  activities to tangible improvements in such programmes as those dealing with: the  analysis of world problems and future-oriented studies; communication in the service  of man; the protection of the environment; the celebration of a World Decade for  Cultural Development; and the eradication of illiteracy. Special attention should be  drawn to the educational and scientific problems that are posed by environmental  amelioration and conservation.
(9.10) It is extremely important that Unesco should work out a programme of measures  in implementation of the recommendations adopted at the International Congress on  Environmental Education and Training held in Moscow in August 1987. Needless to say,  the environment must be protected both globally and regionally on the basis of  international collaboration. In our opinion, Unesco can and must make a meaningful  intellectual contribution to the prevention of ecological disasters.
(9.11) More than 200 draft resolutions have been submitted for consideration by the  twenty-fourth session of the General Conference. A single criterion will govern our  delegation's attitude in examining them: will they contribute to substantially  increased international intellectual activity, or will their adoption merely lead to  an unjustified increase in the Organization's expenditures? It is quite obvious that  the enhancement of Unesco's effectiveness will not be achieved through the piecemeal  distribution of its modest resources, but rather through the intensification and  extension of its intellectual activities and the co-ordination of its functions.
(9.12) To be sure, a great many obstacles still lie in the path of development. It  is impossible to talk seriously about the equality of opportunities or 'the forward  match of democracy' in the developing countries at a time when colonialism in a  variety of new guises, notably technological, is active. Racism pursues its violent  course in southern Africa, finding expression in pseudo-theories according to which  widespread hunger, poverty, sickness and illiteracy may be explained by the incapacity  of certain peoples to take decisions for themselves and to manage and organize their  own lives accordingly. As an organization for intellectual collaboration, Unesco could  do significantly more than in the past in its undeniably positive contribution to the  exposure and extermination of racism and racist policies in southern Africa and  elsewhere. Accordingly, Major Programme XII 'The elimination of prejudice,  intolerance, racism and apartheid' should be strengthened.
(9.13) As far as the formulation of Unesco's new Medium-Term Plan for the 1990s is  cmceriied, its conception must obviously and above all be based on the provisions of  the 0rg.cization's Constitution and the imperatives that stem from the realities of a  nuclear and cosmic age.
(9.14) The underlying principle should be that Unesco, within the framework of its  competence, can and must with the tools of education, science, culture and information  contribute to the creation of a comprehensive system of international security, to the  implantation in people's minds of new forms of political thinking, including ideas  about the preservation of peace, humanism and mutual respect between individuals and  nations, and the protection of the environment. It seems apposite here to recall with  some emphasis that the signatories to Unesco's Constitution expressed thereby the firm  resolve 'to develop and to increase the means of communication between their peoples  ... for the purposes of mutual understanding and a more perfect knowledge of each  other's lives'.
(9.15) In our opinion, Unesco's cultural activities should be broadened and  deepened: a most favcurable oczasion for this is furnished by the proclamation of the  World Decade for Cultural Development. Participating in the Decade and in the  implementation of the corresponding Programme of Action, Unesco will be making a  simultaneous contribution to the formulation of the International Development Strategy  for the Fourth United Nations Development Decade.
(9.16) Unesco's authority would be enhanced by its active involvement in  transforming into reality the idea that a worldwide consultative committee might be  set up under the aegis of the United Nations, to be composed of a global intellectual  élite, representing the domains of culture, education, literature and art, and capable  of substantially enriching the spiritual and humanistic potential of modern political  relations.
(9.17) Mr President, the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic will do its utmost  to ensure that peace on earth is preserved and that fruitful relations of  international collaboration are developed and deepened in the interests of all  mankind. On the basis of its achievements and experience in the educational,  scientific and cultural fields, the Byelorussian SSR is ready to continue to play its  part in the implementation of Unesco's programmes in those domains. Before the Great  October Revolution, the 70th anniversary of which is to be celebrated in a few day's  time, Byelorussia did not have a single establishment of higher education. Today,  students, graduates and trainees from 102 countries are to be found in the BSSR, which  is generously sharing with other countries its experience in the development of  education, science and culture. We believe that much greater use might be made of the  capacities of our specialists in carrying out Unesco's activities.
(9.18) We shall not conceal our preoccupation with certain administrative and  financial matters affecting the Organization, not least because we are not the only  ones to be deeply concerned with a whole series of unresolved problems that are  brought up at one session after the other.
(9.19) We have to observe, unfortunately, that a number of the Organization's items  of expenditure, which aggravate its financial position, sometimes lack objective  justification. And the constant tendency of administrative costs to outstrip outlay on  programmes cannot be brushed aside.
(9.20) To overcome the Organization's present financial difficulties will require  the efforts of the Member States and the Secretariat alike. Savings can be made  through further reforms in the structure of the Secretariat, a reduction in the number  of its central services, the elimination of duplication of functions and expenditures  among sectors, the suppression of a number of senior posts, cutbacks in general  management costs and wider use of - for example - the facilities of computerization.
(9.21) Taking into account not only the Organization's complicated situation, but  also the serious problems confronting a number of countries as a result of the massive  dimensions of their external debts, we must look together for effective ways and means  of breaking out of the impasse which do not inflict further harm on States that  already have grave financial issues to cope with.
(9.22) We believe that a correct long-term strategy for Unesco's activity should be  based on an understanding of the main trends in modern reality. It is obvious that  social and economic progress will refashion the perspectives of social development.  New socio-economic, political, scientific and technical factors are making their  appearance, both nationally and internationally. The interdependence of States and  peoples is increasing. All of which places new challenges before every State and every  international organization - particularly such a prestigious organization as Unesco.
(9.23) Whatever the strategy and the objectives selected by Unesco for the years to  come, whether or not it will contribute to the strengthening of peace and  international security as it is enjoined to do by the Constitution depends on all of  us, on our collective determination to contribute by all possible means to the  attainment of Unesco's noble goals. Thank you.

(11.1) Mr ORBEGOZO RODRIGUEZ (Peru) (Translation from the Spanish):  Mr President of the General Conference, Mr Chairman of the Executive Board,  Mr Director-General, first of all, Mr President, as a Latin American, I congratulate  you on on the high office conferred on you by this assembly. I am sure your experience  and wisdom will enable the work of this Conference to be brought to a successful  conclusion. I belong to a people that, like yours, a cradle of Mayan civilization, is  proud to have been the source of prosperous cultures in the past and now upholds  aspirations for peace, justice and freedom. Peru joins Central America in reaffirming  the principles of non-intervention and self-determination that are vital for putting  an end to aggression and establishing democratic stability in the region.
(11.2) The Government of President Alan Garcia, brought to power by the will of the  people, is facing the historic challenge of changing a society on which domination has  placed its mark of poverty and violence. To this end we are strengthening democracy,  extending it to all social strata by means of far-reaching changes in the economic  structure; by fully exercising our sovereignty so as to obtain independent development
13  with social justice; by encouraging decentralization and social participation so that  our people can live together in peace. Finally, we seek to strengthen our national  identity by applying the principle of solidarity.
(11.3) This is no easy task. Peru's present structure still reflects the  contradictions created by the superimposition of the European element on its  traditional culture. although the colonial system made valuable cultural contributions  it displaced the Andean system and subsequently gave rise to the task of building a  new society based on miscegenation and the integration of cultures.
(11.4) We are doing this in Peru by trying to achieve cultural development within  the framework of ethnic and linguistic diversity. The character of the coastal region  is strongly marked by European, Asian and African influence; the Amazon forest is home  to various ethnic groups whose identity must be respected and preserved; but the  Andes, which form the backbone of our country, express our national character more  strongly. To come to terms with this diversity in a creative manner, with absolute  freedom of expression and flow of ideas, is the task to which we are committed, with a  view to strengthening our national identity and projecting it towards the future.
(11.5) The task of education is naturally the most important factor of this social  transformation of Peru. President Alan Garcia said 'Let those who know teach; let  those who do not know learn', encapsulating in those words the motivation by which the  educational process should be propelled. The new education plan for Peru is now being  prepared, and I feel that to describe it in any detail would be premature. I shall  merely tell you that its broad outlines fully coincide with Unesco's objectives and  recommendations: the eradication of illiteracy; the democratization of education; the  incorporation of education into the production process; respect for local  characteristics, particularly as regards bilingual education; and the all-round  education that enables people to participate in political life and exercise their  democratic rights fully.
(11.6) In the field of science, we are trying to incorporate advarices that will fit  our own circumstances. At the same time, we are striving to develop appropriate  technologies that will help us to reduce our traditional dependence in this field. The  international community must vigorously pursue policies that will enable the  developing countries to progress in a manner befitting their own scientific and  technological needs, and reduce the gap separating North and South. Peru, like other  countries in the area, is a member of the Andean Council for Science and Technology.  The Council will enable subregional co-operation to take place - with a view to  South-South exchange, which also needs to be promoted in the Third World.
(11.7) Mr President, our policy is in perfect accord with this Organization's  manifest concerns in education, science and culture. Peru and Unesco have a long  tradition of co-operation and understanding - as the Director-General was able to see  for himself a few months ago during his official visit to Peru to launch the  international campaign for the temple of San Francisco de Lima, a handsome  architectural complex of the Latin American baroque period.
(11.8) A study of the major programmes has shown us that in future this co-operation  between Peru and Unesco can develop in many fields. We are interested in educational  research with a regional bias, we wish to modernize the teaching of science, to  attempt, with a view to post-literacy work, to promote Associated Schools and to  improve the administration of the education services. It is also vital to learn to set  greater store by the cultural testimonies of the past and to establish international  co-operation so that the heritage of humanity may be conserved as well as possible.
(11.9) We should like Unesco, moreover, to establish a support programme for the  Regional Centre for Peace, Disarmament and Development, which was set up in Lima by a  decision of the General Assembly of the United Nations. This twenty-fourth session of  the General Conference will be examining a draft resolution supported by various  countries, submitted to that effect. Unesco has an opportunity to act in various ways  through this Centre supported by the goodwill of my government. We believe that the  benefits of instituting peace education will have quite a significant regional impact  for the consolidation of peace, thereby fulfilling the mandate contained in Article I  of the Constitution of Unesco.
(11.10) Peru's co-operation with Unesco is also apparent through the bodies on whose  councils it sits, such as the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission, the  International Programme for the Development of Communication and the Intergovernmental  Committee for Promoting the Return of Cultural Property to its Countries of Origin or  its Restitution in Case of Illicit Appropriation. My country will continue to  participate actively in these bodies and in those we shall be joining during the  twenty-fourth session. I should also like to inform this Assembly that my government  attaches special importance to the World Decade for Cultural Development and will at  this session be supporting the establishment of an Intergovernmental Committee, on  which we hope to sit.
(11.11) We also accord special importance to the preparation of the third Medium-Term  Plan. This guideline document must be very carefully prepared so* that the biennial  programmes may be, followed through in a well-ordered manner. Its structure should be  sufficiently flexible, but it must be firm in seeking to attain thq objectives set  forth in Unesco's Constitution. It is vital for us to prepare a document that will not  be easily accessible to specialists alone. Brevity and clarity of expression are  assets that will encourage extensive dissemination, which is in any event desirable if  our aims are to be shared and our project successful.
(11.12) Mr President, my country has faith in the future of Unesco. The process for  the nomination of a candidate for Director-General by the Executive Board - has  culminated with the proposal of the name of Federico Mayor. Peru supported his  candidacy from the outset, not only because of his personal merits but because it felt  that his election would provide the necessary guarantees for re-establishing our  Organization's unity and strength and change within continuity. We do not think that  this choice of ours in any way jeopardizes our solidarity with the Third World or our  policy of noz-alignment. Such solidarity has always been present in our struggle  against colonialism and is still there iq our condemnation of the racist regime in  Pretoria.
(11.13) A Director-General, in the dignity of his office, carries out the charge  entrusted to him by Unesco's organs. Our solidarity must be such as to enable this  Organization to look with confidence towards the future. Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow fulfilled  our charge doggedly and courageously and left the imprint of his forceful personality  on our Organization. During his term of office the Third World showed that it was a  force to be reckoned with and that its problems affected the entire international  community. And for this he deserves our sincere gratitude.
(11.14) This Organization enjoys a universality that must, be preserved. We must  reflect on and remember the fact that if Unesco fails in its mission to obtain  international co-operation in the fields of education, science and culture we shall  not be able to appeal democratically to any other forum. This seat of dialogue and  co-operation must be kept alive for us to move towards the attainment of a juster  world by the year 2000. The imbalances between North and South constitute a serious  affront to human civilization. This twenty-fourth session of the General Conference  must deploy efforts to achieve conciliation and consensus so that we may wisely  accomplish a task that affects the dignity of the individual and the development of  mankind. Thank you very much, Mr President.

17.1 M. COUDREAU (Bureau international catholique de l'enfance) :  Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Directeur général, Mesdames,  Messieurs, parlant au nom des enfants, de leurs besoins et de leurs attentes,  mon organisation, le Bureau international Catholique de l'enfance, est particu-  lièrement attentive au programme de l'Unesco : l'éducation, la science et la  culture ne sont-elles pas d'abord au service des enfants pour la préparation  d'un avenir dont ils seront, demain, les bénéficiaires et les acteurs ?
17.2 Certes, la vingt-quatrième session de la Conférence générale ne les a  pas oubliés. Le grand programme II aborde l'enseignement primaire et la lutte  contre l'analphabétisme, tout comme la promotion de l'éducation de la première  enfance. Toutefois, dans l'approche des problèmes étudiés,' je voudrais exprimer  deux regrets. D'une part, dans l'ensemble du programme, on parle trop souvent de  la jeunesse dans sa globalité, sans se référer aux enfants qui se trouvent dans  la période prépubertaire, pourtant si fondamentale et si décisive. Notre Bureau  souhaite que cette dimension de l'enfance, de la petite enfance, soit retenue  dans la programmation du troisième plan. D'autre part, les apports - progrès ou  questionnements - de la psychologie génétique éclairant ce premier âge ne sont  pas suffisamment pris en compte. Notre Bureau souhaite que l'on porte davantage  attention aux données de la science et à ses actuels développements.
17.3 Cette remarque d'ordre général, très importante à nos yeux, étant faite,  mon intervention portera essentiellement sur un point du programme, sans doute  l'un des plus importants, à savoir la Décennie mondiale du développement cultu-  rel. Mon organisation - c'est son rôle et son devoir - s'interroge sur la signi-  fication et la portée du développement culturel des enfants. Cette interrogation  la conduit à présenter à la Conférence générale deux réflexions, l'une plus fon-  damentale, l'autre plus ponctuelle.
17.4 La première réflexion est d'ordre anthropologique. Dès qu'il y a  culture, se pose, bien sûr, la question du "comment" i il s'agit alors de
13  rechercher et de découvrir les "composantes" de la culture et les "modalités" de  leur développement. Mais se pose aussi - et peut-être d'abord - la question du  "quoi" : qu'est-ce que l'on cultive ? En effet on ne peut parler de développe-  ment culturel sans répondre d'abord à cette question impérative et radicale :  que faut-il cultiver ? I1 s'agit alors de rechercher et de découvrir le germe,  la semence d'où jaillira toute culture, qui en sera la source dynamique, qui en  définira la nature, disons mieux, qui en révélera le "secret". Le jardinier ne  peut programmer la culture d'une plante que dans le respect de la graine semée.
17.5 Quand il s'agit de l'homme, le développement culturel "s'origine" dans  ce que l'espèce humaine a de différent, mieux, de spécifique, par rapport à  l'animalité, ce qui a surgi en elle de "nouveauté" lors de la mutation brusque  d'où elle a jailli. Or, ce qui est "spécifique" de la personne humaine n'est-ce  pas l'émergence de l'esprit, fondement de sa dignité et source de ses droits ?  Cultiver l'homme, c'est donc d'abord libérer en l'enfant le dynamisme de l'esprit  pour lui permettre de devenir apte à exercer ses droits. En effet, la défense  des droits de l'homme commence par l'aptitude de l'homme à les exercer. Or, cet  aptitude s'acquiert et se développe par l'éducation et par la culture.
17.6 Dès lors, la toute première enfance ne serait-elle pas l'espace et le  temps de ce "comnencement" du développement culturel ? Ne faudrait-il pas cher-  cher les lois, les rythmes et les chemins d'une véritable initiation à la décou-  verte progressive que l'enfant doit faire du secret de sa propre existence  humaine ? Ainsi s'opère la lente gestation de la personne ; ainsi jaillit et se  développe le dynamisme du mystère de l'être humain, qui a provoque l'admiration  et le questionnement de tant de philosophes et de savants.
17.7 C'est dire le sens et la portée de la période prépubertaire pour que  soient jetés les fondements et construit le tremplin du développement culturel.  A cette tâche, mon organisation a décidé de contribuer par sa participation au  travail entrepris dans le cadre de l'Unesco. Cette premiere étape du développe-  ment culturel doit dégager les valeurs universelles de base qui fonderont en  l'enfant la fierté d'être un homme, le gardant ainsi de tous les racismes et de  toutes les discriminations, et permettant à "la" culture d'être principe de  communion sous-jacent à la riche pluralité "des" cultures.
17.8 La difficulté apparaîtra sans doute quand on voudra préciser ces valeurs  universelles. Mais n'approcherait-on pas d'un consensus si elles se présentaient  comme les trois rayons d'une même source lumineuse : le don de l'amour, le dis-  cernement de l'intelligence et le choix de la liberté ? Ne serait-ce pas  l'ébauche d'un programme du développement culturel de l'enfant ? Telle est ma  première réflexion concernant le germe dynamique de la culture.
17.9 La seconde est d'ordre sociologique. Si la culture exige du jardinier  le respect de la semence, elle requiert aussi une terre végétale de qualité,  adaptée aux besoins de la plante. De même, le développement culturel de l'enfant,  avant l'action des facteurs et des techniques éducatives, exige un humus riche  pour nourrir l'émergence de l'esprit. Préciser la nature de ce terrain favorable  au devenir culturel, mieux encore de cette matrice porteuse du jaillissement  culturel, c'est faire apparaître l'importance décisive de la famille comme milieu  nourricier de la culture.
17.10 Mon organisation a noté avec le plus grand intérêt la place donnée à la  famille dans le projet de la Décennie, dans le cadre des grands programmes VI et  VIII. Sans doute la nécessité de ce terrain favorable au développernent culturel  chez l'enfant pose-t-elle le problème de l'institution familiale, de la stabi-  lité de ses structures et de sa place dans la société. Mais, au-delà de l'insti-  tution, pour s'épanouir comme personne humaine, l'enfant a besoin de la fervente  chaleur d'un amour et, en particulier, de cet amour spécifique dans l'espèce  humaine qui unit les êtres dans une relation harmonieuse et stable de communion.
17.11 Comme une plante qui a besoin de terre pour s'épanouir, la personne  humaine, surtout en cette première étape de son éclosion culturelle, a besoin  d'amour. Dès lors, la question se pose, avec plus d'acuité peut-être aujourd'hui  qu'hier : les modes de vie des hommes dans les sociétés modernes permettent-ils  à l'enfant le bénéfice de cette plus-value culturelle de l'amour ? Telle est  notre seconde interrogation. C'est pourquoi mon organisation, étant un bureau  d'études, souhaite pouvoir coopérer à la recherche prévue dans le grand pro-  gramme I "Réflexion sur les problèmes mondiaux et études prospectives".
17.12 Elle souhaite également que s'intensifie le travail de réflexion et de  recherche sur le thème des relations vitales entre la famille et la culture. Sur  ce point précis, véritable enjeu de la Décennie mondiale du développement cultu-  rel, mon organisation veut être ici l'écho vibrant de l'appel, plus encore, du  cri d'alarme des enfants. Mais aussi et surtout, elle se veut porteuse de toute  l'espérance qui envahit le coeur des enfants de partout. En leur nom, Monsieur  le Président, Mesdames et Messieurs, je vous salue et je vous remercie.
