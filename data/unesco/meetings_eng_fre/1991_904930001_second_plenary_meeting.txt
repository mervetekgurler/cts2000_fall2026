5.1 The PRESIDENT:  We shall move on to the next matter for consideration, namely the membership of Lithuania, Estonia  and Latvia. These three countries, which we welcome here, have just become members of the United Nations  organization and as stated in Article II, paragraph 1, of the Constitution, this automatically gives them the right  to become members of UNESCO. The instruments of acceptance have been duly completed, submitted for  consideration and received.
5.2 I therefore have the pleasure of announcing acceptance of the membership of Lithuania; and the  honour and pleasure of inviting H.E. Mr Darius Kuolys, Minister of Culture and Education of Lithuania to  come to the rostrum to place the flag of his country and to address the General Conference.
6.1 M. KUOLYS (Lituanie) :  Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Directeur général, Excellences, Mesdames, Messieurs, au nom du  gouvernement de la République de Lituanie, au nom de mes compatriotes, je vous remercie de la confiance que  vous nous témoignez en accueillant la Lituanie au sein de la communauté des membres de l'UNESCO, branche  maîtresse des Nations Unies dans les domaines de la culture, de l'enseignement, des sciences et de la  communication.
6.2 Le principe fondateur de l'UNESCO, appelé à assurer la coexistence des peuples en partant de leur  identité culturelle, est particulièrement cher à la Lituanie du fait de la tradition millénaire de culture balte dont  est porteur notre pays, situé au confluent culturel des Europes : celle de l'Ouest et celle de l'Est, celle du Nord et  celle du Centre. Au Moyen Age, le Grand-Duché de Lituanie donna un exemple de tolérance face à la diversité  religieuse et ethnique de ses communautés, de même que l'Université de Vilnius s'employa pendant quatre  siècles à défendre le respect des principes de l'humanisme.
6.3 Lors des occupations successives dont fut victime la Lituanie, elle eut sa culture pour seul refuge, pour  seul garant de la survie de la nation.
6.4 Plus récemment, c'est l'identité culturelle qui a dicté la stratégie du rétablissement de l'Etat lituanien.  C'est notre culture, et elle seule, qui nous a permis de croire obstinément que nous pourrions résister à la force  et à l'injustice historique grâce à une éthique politique.
6.5 La liberté de création, le maintien et l'affirmation de son identité culturelle, le développement des  échanges entre des hommes et des peuples d'appartenance différente, sont aujourd'hui essentiels pour la  Lituanie.
6.6 Si la Lituanie a tant voulu l'indépendance, c'est pour pouvoir exister par elle-même et vivre selon son  entendement. La Lituanie voulait être libre afin de se donner pour unique principe une créativité sans entrave.  Aujourd'hui, le retour de la Lituanie au sein du concert culturel des nations, son entrée dans cette continuité,  cette communauté d'hommes et de nations chaque jour plus nombreux éclaire d'une lumière nouvelle notre  présence ici - celle de chacun d'entre nous et celle de nous tous, réunis.
6.7 Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Directeur général, Mesdames et Messieurs, nous sommes  convaincus que la Lituanie ne vient pas vers vous les mains vides : située aux confins de l'Europe occidentale,  elle vous apporte l'expérience d'une continuité, d'une spiritualité uniques, trempées à l'épreuve de la création  interdite.
6.8 Les hommes et les femmes de Lituanie ont la plus haute estime pour les acquis de l'UNESCO en  matière de coopération dans tous les domaines de la culture, de l'éducation, des sciences et de la  communication, ciments de la solidarité entre les peuples.
6.9 En rassemblant dans une même famille universelle des cultures de pays et de traditions on ne peut plus  différents, l'UNESCO illustre, mieux que quiconque, cet ordre mondial de demain où les priorités culturelles  seront décisives, car porteuses de paix et de vie meilleure.
6.10 Une fois encore, je tiens à vous remercier Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Directeur général,  Excellences, Mesdames et Messieurs, je tiens à vous dire combien nous tous Lituaniens sommes heureux d'être  des vôtres. Je remercie tous ceux qui ont montré pour notre peuple tant de bonne volonté.

8.1 Mme MALLE TALVET (Estonie) :  Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Directeur général, Mesdames et Messieurs, c'est pour moi un très  grand bonneur et une grande joie de pouvoir exprimer aujourd'hui, au nom de l'Estonie, de son gouvernement  et de son peuple, la gratitude et la satisfaction profondes que nous éprouvons en devenant membre de  l'UNESCO.
8.2 L'Estonie, comme les autres pays baltes, vit en ce moment son retour en Europe et avec ce retour en  Europe, c'est tout un processus de décolonisation que nous vivons, que nous sommes obligés de vivre, tant au  niveau social qu'au niveau, par exemple, industriel. C'est le cas même en ce moment solennel ; permettez-moi  de vous en donner un petit exemple : dans notre pays qui est petit mais d'une culture ancienne, plutôt paysanne  et agricole, nous avons une région de mines de schistes bitumineux assez rares dans le monde. Avec ce schiste  bitumineux, nous produisons de l'électricité. Cette électricité, nous la vendons à nos voisins de l'Est dix fois  moins cher que ne nous coûte la production. Ce voisin la vend, à son tour, aux autres pays contre des devises en  faisant un profit. Ainsi, c'est à lui que revient le profit, tandis qu'à nous, il ne reste que les cendres. Des  cendres, cette région du pays en produit une tonne par an par habitant et pour cette raison, l'espérance de vie  des habitants y est sept ans moindre qu'ailleurs.
8.3 Voilà donc un petit exemple qui est un symptôme de la maladie dont le nom est la colonisation  totalitaire. Seul un processus de décolonisation peut guérir cette maladie. L'adhésion aux organisations  démocratiques en est un moyen, mais la guérison n'est pourtant pas simple. C'est pourquoi l'Estonie est très  heureuse de faire maintenant partie de l'UNESCO et de pouvoir tenter de résoudre ses problèmes sociaux,  écologiques et industriels si complexes, tout en s'efforçant de contribuer à la tâche universelle qui est celle de  l'UNESCO. Merci de nous avoir admis. Merci de votre attention.

Après  une éclipse d'un demi-siècle, elle entend reprendre sa vocation de carrefour commercial et culturel.
10.3 La Lettonie fut d'ailleurs un participant actif à la vie internationale pendant sa période d'indépendance,  entre 1918 et 1940. Membre de la Société des Nations, elle eut l'honneur de voir un de ses ministres des affaires  étrangères présider cette auguste assemblée. Aujourd'hui,  malgré les incertitudes économiques de la période, les différentes activités de la vie culturelle reprennent avec  une vigueur renouvelée, Nos scientifiques, qui ont poursuivi leurs traditions séculaires, quoique de façon plus  restreinte, pourront désormais s'épanouir de nouveau. Le monde et les possibilités de rencontre sont de nouveau  ouverts pour eux.
10.4  Ainsi, en vous remerciant de nous recevoir en votre sein, je puis vous assurer d'une loyale coopération  du peuple letton et des grands efforts qu'il fera pour figurer honorablement parmi vous. Merci.

I regard this as a fitting climax to my involvement with UNESCO since 1964 as a  participant in all the General Conferences, as a consultant, as Chairman for about seven years of the  International Scientific Committee for the drafting of the General History of Africa and more recently as a  member of the Executive Board. 36.2 Your Excellencies, it has been said that great minds meet at the summit. Whatever the circumstances  and irrespective of ideological, racial and gender barriers, men and women of science, education and culture  will always find it possible to communicate meaningfully. UNESCO was intended by its founders to be the  intellectual summit of the United Nations system. The prehistory of UNESCO illustrates this clearly. In war-  torn London, when ordinary mortals were concerned with questions of survival, a few people of vision -  ministers of education, scientists, writers, philosophers - were seriously engaged in discussions and speculative  thinking, between 1942 and 1945, which finally resulted in an organization that was to become UNESCO in
1946. We see them arguing fervently about the field of competence of the future organization; we can almost  hear them arguing about the name: whether it should be UNEO, UNECO or UNESCO? We see the top British  scientists, led by the biochemist Joseph Needham and the biologist Julian Huxley, campaigning vigorously for  the inclusion of science in UNESCO. And when in April-June 1945, the United Nations Conference on  International Organizations was convened in San Francisco to discuss, among other matters, the organization  of post-war security, we see George Bidault, the French Minister of Foreign Affairs and leader of the French  delegation, insisting that 'In addition to the economic and social corner-stones, the international edifice needs a  third mainstay’. He therefore recommended the establishment of an international organization on ‘intellectual  co-operation’. But what kind of intellectual co-operation and towards what objectives?
36.3 When the first session of the General Conference was convened here in Paris in November-December
1946, and opened at the Sorbonne under the presidency of Léon Blum of France, a major ideological debate  ensued between those who believed, with Julian Huxley, that UNESCO must have its own philosophy and  others, led by France, who contended that UNESCO, as an intergovernmental organization, could not have its
29   own doctrines to preach. No conclusion on this debate was reached at the first General Conference. A year  later, at the second session of the General Conference, held in Mexico City, the ideological debate continued  and was joined by other intellectual heavyweights such as Dr Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan of India. The last word,  however, was spoken by Jacques Maritain, leader of the French delegation, who disassociated himself from  Huxley's desire (Huxley had been elected Director-General of UNESCO at the first session of the Executive  Board in 1946) to provide UNESCO with a specific doctrine. Maritain contended that a look at contemporary  thought revealed a wide diversity. 'Dig as we may’, he continued, ‘there are no common foundations for  speculative thought ... Because the goal of UNESCO is a practical goal, agreement between minds can be  reached spontaneously, not on the basis of common speculative ideas, but on common practical ideas; not on  the affirmation of one and the same conception of the world ... but upon the affirmation of a single body of  beliefs for guidance in action. No doubt this is little enough ... It is, nevertheless, enough to enable a great task  to be undertaken’. In other words, it was agreed in Mexico that UNESCO was not to be an international  ideological organization but an international intellectual organization that would bring together eminent  scholars, thinkers and statesmen from diverse cultural, political and intellectual backgrounds, for specific  practical purposes in the fields of education, science, culture and communication.
36.4 Your Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen, this brief historical digression has been included here  because I believe that as we meet in Paris during the twenty-sixth session of the General Conference to review  the work of our Organization and to discuss its future role, it is essential that we go back to the ideals and hopes  of the founding fathers of UNESCO. We should examine whether these objectives, which were relevant in 1946  when a new world order was ushered in following the Second World War, are still relevant today when that  very world has come tumbling down.
36.5 Your Excellencies, momentous changes have taken place in the world since our last General  Conference in 1989. Among these, perhaps the mot dramatic have been the reunification of Germany, the  international movements towards greater democracy, greater espousal of market economy policies, greater  assertion of cultural identities and remarkable progress towards the elimination of apartheid in South Africa.  But these very successes, it is regrettable to say, have helped to produce a swelling tide of ethnic and racial  bitterness, prejudices and xenophobia. These conflicts are resulting in national boundary disputes or even wars;  and general attacks on ethnic or racial minorities, especially migrant minorities. In South Africa, although all  the legal underpinnings for apartheid have been removed, a constitution to inaugurate a new democratic South  Africa is yet to be drawn up. In fact, as we speak here today, Nelson Mandela has no vote in South Africa. The  drawing up of the constitution will therefore require tolerance and compromise from all groups. A new political  culture of accommodation has to be fostered. In this connection, we note with pleasure that Mr Nelson Mandela  and Mr Fredrick de Klerk are the winners of the first Félix Houphouét-Boigny Peace Prize ‘for their  contribution to international peace’. It is also a good omen that Nadine Gordimer, who has spent her whole life  in South Africa attacking apartheid through her novels and short stories, won the Nobel Prize for Literature  this year.
36.6 But globally speaking, what are the possibilities of a new future? What are the new visions of the  future? Currently, there is much talk about a new world order. What are its ingredients? Market economy?  Pluralist democracy? Sustainable economic development? All these are laudable aims. But each concept raises  problems that cry out loudly for solution. For the principles of market economy to operate for the benefit of all  in the new world order, agreement must be reached on the GATT trade negotiations, if the Cold War is not to  be replaced by fierce international economic competition among major trade blocs.
36.7 For any new world order to work, it must be based on the very principle of pluralist democracy its  champions are espousing, that is, it must be based on an international consensus democratically arrived at. But  are we ready and willing to apply the cherished principles of pluralist democracy to the United Nations system?
36.8 As the world prepares for the 1992 United Nations Conference on Environment and Development to  be held in Brazil, we have to think of ways and means of redirecting the global economy towards  environmental sustainability. We are living in an age which combines a promise of a new world order and a  threat of no world. For the first time in world history our species, Homo sapiens, is threatened with extinction,  like our ancestors Homo erectus and other early hominids, thanks to our activities. It is ironic that we should be  busy raising funds for endangered species such as the African elephant when man himself should be on that  list. True, we all recognize the fact that the globe has reached its limits. But what are we to do? Curtail further  development in industrialized countries? And what are the developing countries supposed to do during the  transition to sustainability?
36.9 Your Excellencies, we are at the dawn of a new age in world history. Perhaps the time has come when  we should seriously attempt to involve the new Huxleys and Radhakrishnans in the formulation of new  objectives for international intellectual co-operation, for a new UNESCO. The topics that were hotly debated  during the first three years of UNESCO's existence, such as objectives, fields of competence and programmes,  are currently under scrutiny. There are those who would like UNESCO to go back to its policy of doing less in  order to achieve more. There are others who wish to transform UNESCO into a kind of development agency, an  intellectual version of UNDP. Whatever form is eventually adopted, one thing is certain: we cannot have  intellectual co-operation without intellectuals. The scientists, the educators, the painters and sculptors, the  actors, the musicians, the writers, the philosophers, the journalists - all these groups of intellectuals have to be  involved in the work of UNESCO. They have to be encouraged to feel that UNESCO is their Organization.
36.10 In conclusion, Your Excellencies, the ancient Greek philosopher Aristotle said that people get the  government they deserve. May I paraphrase that remark by asking the question: Is UNESCO, as presently  conceived and organized, what the Member States deserve? Thank you.
