The Convention  defines discrimination, including under this term any distinction, exclusion, limitation or prefer-  ence which, being hased on race, colour, sex, language, religion, political or other opinion, na-  tional or social origin, economic condition or birth, has the effect of nullifying or impairing equa-  lity of treatment in education and in particular of depriving any person or group of persons of  these advantages on any of these pretexts. Under Article 4, States  undertake to formulate, develop and apply a national policy which, by methods appropriate to the  circumstances and to national usage, will tend to promote equality of opportunity and treatment  in education and, In particular - and here we find the whole Unesco programme - to make primary  education free and compulsory, to make secondary educationinits different forms generallyavail-  able and accessible to all, to make higher education equally accessible on the basis of individual  capacity, to ensure that all comply with the obligation to attend school prescribed by law, to en-  sure that the standards and quality of education are equivalent inall public educational institutions  of the same level, to take effective measures to encourage and advance the educationof those who  have not received any primary education, or who have not completed the entire primary course,  and enable them to continue their education on the basis of individual capacity, and so on.
