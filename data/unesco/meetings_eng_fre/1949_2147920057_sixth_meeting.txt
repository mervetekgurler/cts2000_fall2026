SIXTH MEETING Saturday, 1 October 1949, at 4 p.m. Chairman: Mr. Antonio Castro LEAL (Mexico) The CHAIRMAN observed that there was not a quorum; in accordance with Rule 69, paragraph 2, of the Rules of Procedure, he decided that the meeting should be adjourned for ten minutes. On resumption of the meeting, the CHAIRMAN proposed to the delegates present that Rule 69 should be temporarily waived. Agreed. International Federation of Children’s Com- munities (continuation). The CHAIRMAN made a statement on the question raised in connexion with the International Federa- tion of Children’s Communities. The Commission had referred the matter to the Executive Board and, in reply, the Board had explained that, as there were certain preliminary conditions for the grant of consultative status, it was necessary to wait until the international status of the organ- ization concerned was finally determined and it was known how wide its influence was in the world. The Executive Board would consider the question at its next session, but had authorized the Director-General to assist FICE while its position was under consideration. Item 6 B (v) of the Agenda: Liaison with labour organizations (continuation) (doc- ument 4C/OXR/13). The CHAIRMAN emphasized the importance of liaison between Unesco and labour organizations and drew the Commission’s attention to the two draft resolutions on pages 3 and 4 of document
4C/OXR/13. In the fourth paragraph of the first resolution, there was a reference to associating labour organ- izations with the work of National Commissions and including representatives of those organiza- tions in delegations to the General Conference. Mr. VERNIERS (Belgium) entirely agreed with the resolution but wished to make a few very slight amendments to the French text: in resolu- tion I, (second paragraph, second line), to replace the words “au progres de la science et aux avan- tages...” by “la compréhension des progres de la science et des avantages...”; in resolution II (sub- paragraph 2, second line) to replace the words “a Végard de certains projets de l'Unesco et de les associer” by “à l’égard de certains projets de l’Unesco et d’associer les dits milieux...”. Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom), referring to resolution I, suggested that the last part of the last sentence (“... and to include representatives of those organizations in their delegations to the Gen- eral Conference”) should be deleted, as it did not seem that the Conference should recommend the composition of national delegations and as, in certain cases, it was redundant: the United Kingdom, for instance, had already included representatives of those organizations in their del- egations. With regard to resolution II, Mr. Cowell felt that, in order to cover all eventualities, the words “and others” should be inserted in the second line of sub-paragraph 3 after the words * trade union leaders”. Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America) said that his Governmentattached great importance to the relations of Unesco with the various labour organizations; it was very satisfied at the progress which had so far been made and very impressed with the new steps under consideration. He agreed with both the resolutions now before the Commission. With regard to the modifications suggested by Mr. Cowell, his Government had had the same con- siderations in mind, but it had not intended to make a recommendation as to how delegations should be composed. His Government had intended to meet the problem in a simpler manner, namely, by modifying the fourth paragraph of resolution I to read: “Invites Member States to consider...” thereby making the language used less mandatory. Turning to resolution II, Mr. Mulliken suggested that the words “and implement” be inserted after the words “to draw up...” in the first line of sub- paragraph 1. Sub-paragraph 2, as he understood it, did not imply that Unesco’s work should over- lap with the work of the national labour organ- izations concerned. With regard to sub- paragraph 3, he found the suggestion by the del- egate of the United Kingdom acceptable, but pointed out that the last part of the paragraph (“certain activities having a direct bearing on the needs and interests of labour”) might imply and overlap with the work of the International Labour

Professor EUSTHATIADES (Greece) endorsed the proposals made by the delegate of the United States of America. The CHAIRMAN emphasized that, in resolu- tion JI, the United Kingdom's proposal did not mention labour organizations, but trade-union leaders and others concerned. Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) agreed to the proposal made by the delegate of the United States, with the exception that the words “and imple- ment” (sub-paragraph 1 of resolution II) appeared to place the whole responsibility on the Director- General. Dr. ZEISSL (Austria) agreed to the suggestion made by the delegate of the United Kingdom to delete the last phrase of resolution I, for he felt that the initial wording might give rise to considerable difficulties in the case of small countries which were unable to send large del- egations to Unesco: it might be impossible for those countries to include representatives of national labour organizations in their delegations. M. VERNIERS (Belgium) proposed that the United States delegation’s amendment to resolu- tion II, sub-paragraph 3, suggesting the addition of the words “educational, scientific and cultural” to the word “activities”, should be modified. He thought that the word “scientific” should be omitted, as it seemed to him Utopian to speak of the scientific activities of a labour organization. Dr. ANAS Khan (Afganistan) agreed with the views of the delegates of Belgium and Austria. There was a great difference between industrial and non-industrial countries. In his own country, workers” organizatons were very little developed and it would be extremely difficult to include representatives of them in the delegation to the General Conference of Unesco. Moreover, travelling expenses were very high and might seem exorbitant in a small budget. Afghanistan was an agricultural country whose industry was little developed. It was impossible to take countries such as the United States of America and the United Kingdom as models. That gave rise to other budgetary complications. For instance, the International Labour Organization might also, in its turn, suggest that a member of the National Commission should be included in delegations attending its conferences. In that way, the number of members of each delegation to inter- national conferences would gradually increase. The CHAIRMAN announced that twenty-eight reports from Member States had been received, and that the Commission had before it a draft resolution proposed bythe United States delegation (document 4C/OXR/22) requesting that, at the Fifth Session of the General Conference, a sub- committee should consider the reports of Member States and report on them to the General Con- ference. Member States would not be asked to submit new reports to the Fifth Session of the General Conference; but those Member States which had not yet sent in their reports would naturally be at liberty to do so; the sub- committee would be able to consider those reports also. Professor EUSTATHIADES (Greece) approved the United States proposal and suggested that it should not be limited to 1949. If the procedure produced good results, is might be used regularly at all future sessions. The reports often did not reach Unesco in time, and would therefore be desirable to specify that a sub-committee was to examine the reports during the session of the Gen- eral Conference. Professor Eustathiades made some reservations, however, with regard to para- graph (2) of the draft resolution proposed bythe United States delegation. Dr. ANAS Khan (Afganistan) said he would be grateful if the United States delegate would tell him what he had in mind. With regard to the reports from the Member States, he suggested that, instead of completing these, the Commission should consider the practical application of the Director-General’s report. Further, the reports by Member States should, in his opinion, contain an item dealing with the advice received from the National Commissions or Co-operating Bodies of the Member States collaborating with Unesco. Mr. VERNIERS (Belgium) asked whether the Commission was discussing document 4C/OXR/22 or the amended text. He also wished to know the views of the United States delegation regarding the composition and work of the proposed sub- committee. Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America) sub- mitted for consideration by the Commission a redraft of document 4C/OXR/22 which, he requested, should be used as a basis for the present discussion. The reason for the United States suggestion that a special sub-committee should be convened at the Fifth Session of the General Conference to examine in detail the reports submitted by Member States was that such an examination by the present Com- mission would necessarily be superficial owing to the very short time at its disposal. In view of the very great importance of these reports to the work of Unesco, the United States felt that their sug- gested solution was the only one possible. In reply to the delegate of Afghanistan, Mr. Mul- liken pointed out that the reports submitted did not cover the whole year 1949, and he would propose that an additional report, to cover the remaining period of 1949, should be submitted for consideration by the General Conference at Florence; conversations with the Secretariat and other delegations had shown the desirability of having a full year’s report. Dr. ANAS Khan (Afghanistan) thanked the     United States delegate for his explanation. He pointed out, however, that many countries would certainly meet with great difficulties in preparing a further report in the short time remaining before the next session of the General Conference. In addition, he was not quite clear whether a report was intended to cover a full calendar year or the period between two sessions of the General Conference. The CHAIRMAN remarked that the reports of Member States had reached Unesco in July or August 1949. States would therefore be able to send in supplementary reports for the Florence Conference, dealing with the rest of 1949, about February 1950; that would enable the reports to be circulated in time for the Fifth Session on the General Conference. Mr. NICHOLAS (Australia) stated that his Government could not agree to the United States suggestion that a sub-committee should be con- vened to consider the reports from Member States. The information contained in these reports was of such major importance that, in the view of his Government, they should be studied in detail by every member of the Commission. He would even go so far as to suggest that at least one plenary meeting of the General Conference should be devoted entirely to reports from Member States. For this reason, he formally opposed the United States proposal. M. de LACHARRIERE (France) considered the United States draft resolution quite acceptable, but wished to have some further details. It would be necessary to ask Member States for additional information about the composition of their National Commissions and the method of financing the latter. It was particularly important to obtain information regarding the question of finance. He therefore proposed an amendment to the revised draft resolution. At the end of the penultimate paragraph, he wished to insert the words “in particular, by providing information about the composition and method of financing their National Commission and its secretariat”. There was a second point to which the French delegation wished to draw the Commission’s attention, without, however, submitting any amendment: it would be advisable to ask Member States for information about the structure of the liaison services between their National Commis- sions and Unesco.
