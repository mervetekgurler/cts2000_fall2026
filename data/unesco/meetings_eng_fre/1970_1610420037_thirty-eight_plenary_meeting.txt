4.1 Mr. BRENNAN (United States of America):  Mr. President, I shall intervene only once on the general subject of regional centres,  but what I have to say now under sub-section 1, 33 will apply equally to sub-section 1.42.
4.2 At the outset, Mr. President, 1 should like to make it quite clear, as our delegation  has attempted to do throughout the debate on this subject both in the Education Sub-  Commission and the Programme Commission, that my government is in no manner what -  ever opposed to continuation of Unesco's support for the three regional educational centres  which we shall be considering. Quite the contrary, my government has for many years  been in the forefront of the nations which have supported such centres and we are still. In  fact, at this very Conference, several days ago, the United States proposed a formulation  for continuing Regular progr.mme budgetary assistance to the centres beyond 1972.
4.3 We are, however, very much concerned, Mr, President, over the amended wording  that now appears in paragraphs 1.33(d} and 1.42(b) respectively of the proposed resolu-  tions, on the ground that it risks contravening the established operating procedures of this  Organization and establishes a highly dangerous precedent. We should all keep well in
38  mind, Mr. President, that should the language of these texts be adopted without clarifica-  tion and precision by this General Conference, the Organization may be irrevocably com-  mitted to the support of these centres, whether they fare well or poorly, whether they can  enlist adequate external support or not, for the next eight and not only for the next six  years, For these four biennia, Mr, President, the cost of their support will be close to  $5 inillion, a sum which this Organization can ill afford to spend unwisely.
4.4 Now, as we know, Mr. President, some of these regional centres have fared well and  some have been disappointments. As the Director-General himself put it, in paragraph 68  of document 16 C/4, "they have had chequered fortunes''. Each of the centres we are now  considering is in a current process of reorientation or reorganization or both, Again the  Director-General notes this situation in paragraph 183 of 16 C/4 when he proposes that  continuation of Unescu assistance to one centre ve accorded upon the condition, and again  I quote, "that the caanges now being implemented prove effective",
4.5 The Director-General has lauded, in 16 C/4, the wisdom of this Organization's deci-  sion in 1962 to limit Unesco assistance to such centres to a period of ten years. He ex-  pressed the belief that, by that time, the centres would have proved their usefulness, or  their uselessness, and their value to those Member States that might bz expected hence-  forth to support them. Pointing out that the decision had been reaffirmed by every subse-  quent General Conference, the Director-General urged adherence to this policy as a test  of the consistency and the constancy of the General Conference itself.
4.6 Our attention is then invited to the decision of the General Conference, taken four years  ago, that before terminating financial aid to these regional centres, their situation should  be objectively examined with reference to the new relations which Unesco might establish  with them. Now we agree, Mr, President, that this is indeed a wise and prudent measure  to assure the future of organizations in which Unesco presumably has a strong interest and  with which it has been actively associated. But this is not to assert that continued agsis-  tance to such organizations, currently in a state of reshaping or redirection, should be ir-  revocably guaranteed for a further period of eigk« ycars. This wouid be neither prudent  nor responsible; it would tend to ignore changes in tte centres which, {or better or worse,  might make further continuation of such assistance either unnecessary or unwise.
4.7 My dclegation cannot therefore believe, Mr. President, that this is the sense or the  intent of this General Conference in the resolutions before us. Accordingly, and in keeping  with the assurances given by the Deputy Director-General during the discussions at the  Sub-Commission on Education and the Programme Commission, my delegation wishes to  record its understanding that these resolutions, in their present wording, will in no way  limit the freedom of action of any future General Conference in the matter of the work of  and the continuation of assistance to these regional centres. On that understanding my  delegation is prepared to vote in favour of resolutions 1. 33 and 1.42. Thank you,  Mr. President.

24.1 Mr. ROMAIN (Trinidad and Tobago):  Mr. President, fellow delegates, I have asked for the floor to speak on paragraph 57,  and on the proposal for the elevation of the division for the protection and development of  the cultural heritage into a department, On the face of it, this is a harmless proposal, but  in substance it is not as harmless as it appears, particularly in regard to the future of cul-  ture and in regard to the finances of the Organization.
24,2 The more we questioned this proposal, in the Sub-Commission or in the Programme  Commission, the less convincing the explanations became, The blue book gives three rea-  sons, in paragraph 848 - the increase of activities, the need for dynamic direction and the  need for a more systematic conception of aims. The Director-General and his represen-  tatives gave other explanations such as that without this, the Organization could not do the  work that it was called upon to do, and that this was an administrative matter. Now, the  Secretariat has given us the additional cost of this operation as $5,300 and says that this  merely involves the upgrading of a post of D-1 to a post of D-2. Mr. President, this is a  very serious matter. We have already disposed of the reasons given in the blue book. An  answer to the increase in activities and the need for direction and goals cannot come from  upgrading a D-1 into a D-2, or changing a name from division to department.
24,3 So we are left to look for other reasons for this proposal. We believe that this is not  the time to fragment the department of culture, especially in the wake of the Venice Con-  ference. We believe that culture should have been a sector long ago, long before things  like social sciences, information, administration and so on became sectors - these things  which are re lly just service operations within the house. But it has been lumped with  social scienc 's and human sciences, for better or for worse, and now instead of being ele-  vated, it is being fragmented, Now, is this for the good of culture, Mr. President, or be-  cause operations and studies ought to be separated in order to flourish? No, thisisbecause,  as the Director-General himself explained, he needs to promote a person who has given  devoted service. Now | am not ridiculing anything, I am giving just his explanation, Is it  because the staff is inadequate to do the job that we have given it to do? This cannot be the  explanation because a modest increase is proposed for this division, as indeed for most of  the divisions in the house. The present division has a Director's Office, a Monuments and  Museums section headed by a P-5 with four Professionals and three General Service staff,  and a section for operations and the safeguarding and devélopment of the cultural heritage  headed by another P-5 with eight Professionals and four General Service staff, making a  total of 22 staff members - and this is to be the nucleus of a whole new department. By the  way, I am using the figures given in the list of members of the Secretariat because this is  more reliable than the figures given in the blue book.
24,4 If we create a department out of this little body we will not merely be obliged to up-  grade a D-1 to D-2. This is merely the icing on the top of the cake. We would have to  create at least two D-1 posts to head two divisions, the minimum number of divisions in  any department in the house, and of course upgrade a few additional sections as well to give  validity to the divisions, otherwise there would be no point in creating a department. And  this all-round upgrading of staff is going to cost something, so that instead of just $5,300  we would have about 15 or 20 or more promotions and a few other overheads which will  push the cost of this thing to somewhere in the region of $100,000, even without any increase  in costs. And this excludes the $40,000 odd that would be involved in building up the office  of the Assistant Director-General who is now going to need some additional help in co-  ordinating, Now, the social sciences, human sciences and culture sector has two depart-  ments, the social sciences and culture - social sciences has three divisions and culture has  four and there is also a separate little division called philosophy. When culture is frag-  mented who will do the co-ordination? Is itthe Assistant Director-General, as the Director-  General told the Executive Board? No, cultural co-ordination has to be achieved within  culture, otherwise it is not cultural co-ordination, it is pure politics,
24.5 Now, do we have a case on the grounds of numbers for upgrading this division? The  Department of Culture now has 74 staff including 37 Professionals, but the Department of  School and Higher Education has 84 staff including 46 Professionals, the Department for  the Advancement of Education has 78 staff including 33 Professionals, the Department of  Science Teaching has 71 including 36 Professionals, and the Office of Public Information,  which is like a department, has 120 staff including 54 Professionals. So what is the case  for fragmenting the Department of Culture? If we look at the division itself we see no case  on grounds of numbers, The division has 22 staff. The Teacher-Training Division has 38;  the Training Abroad Division has 37. The Division of Educational Financing 37; Literacy
24; Technological Education and Research 36; Studies and Research related to Natural Re-  sources 34; Press Division 31; Radio and Visual Information 43. These are all divisions  that I am talking about, but here we have a proposal to upgrade a division nf 22 into a de-  partment, It has not even yet grown into a full-fledged division.
24.6 Now, Mr. President, we do not believe that we have been given the reasons for the  upgrading of this division. Whatever they are it is a costly proposal, and it is a backward  step for culture. I will not go so far as to say that this has been put forward as a test of  our gullibility, but I will go so far as to say I will oppose it. It will consume money which  can be used in many other fruitful ways such as for peace, human rights and culture. I  thank you very much, Mr. President.

26,1 Le DIRECTEUR GENERAL, -  Monsieur le Président, Mesdames, Messieurs, jusqu'au dernier jour et presque jus-  qu'au dernier moment, il me faudra intervenir sur ce point particulier pour défendre une  idée qui me paraît répondre aux intérêts d'une bonne administration, J'aurais souhaité que  l'acharnement que certaines délégations ont mis à combattre ma proposition, d'abord en  sous-commission, puis à la Commission du programme et maintenant en séance plénière,  eût été mis au service d'une meilleure vause,

26.3 Voici d'abord les raisons de ma proposition. Premièrement, je considère, en tant  qu'administrateur responsable qui a une connaissance quotidienne des activités du Secréta-  riat, que la charge de travail que représentent les sections 3, 3 et 3.4 du programme est  devenue quantitativement trop grande pcur un seul département et pour un seul directeur,  quelle que puisse être la personnalité de ce directeur, M, le délégué de la Trinité et Tobago  a voulu, en se référant À certains chiffres touchant le nombre des postes, procéder à des  comparaisons avec d'aatres unités du S:crétariat, Je voudrais donc lui faire observer que  le nombre des pustes n'est pas le seul indice, ni même l'indice principal, sur lequel il con-  vienne de se fonder pour juger de l'importance du travail, car les activités de l'Unesco  présentent toujours des aspects qualiialifs en fonction desquels varie la charge qu'elles im-  posent au Secrétariat, Il est en particulier bien évident que, lorsqu'on a affaire à des acti-  vités hétérogènes, la charge de travail s'en trouve augmentée dans des proportions plus  grandes que ne peut l'indiquer, par exemple, le nombre de postes ou le nombre de dollars,  Voilà donc ma première raison et je vous donne là mon jugement d'administrateur ; si vous  n'avez pas confiance en votre administrateur, c'est vraiment le moment de le dire,
26,4 Deuxièmement, si vous considérez les trois résolutions qui figurent sous la rubrique
3, 4 (à savoir : une résolution de caractère normatif, une résolution concernant l'aide à ap-  porter aux Etats membres pour le développement des musées et une résolution de carac-  tère purement opérationnel), vous constaterez que, malgré les différences de modalités,  il y a entre elles une certaine unité et qu'elles forment un ensemble différent de celui da la  section 3. 3 intitulée : "Etudes, développement et diffusion des cultures”, L'existence d'une  différence entre les deux ensembles correspondant aux sections 3.3 et3, 4 trouve d'ailleurs  sa preuve dans le fait que le rythme d'expansion des crédits, qui dépend naturellement du  rythme d'expansion des activités, est jort différent, Pour ne parler que du budget ordinaire,  l'augmentation est de 4 % pour la section 3,3, alors qu'elle est de 17 % pourla section 3, 4,  Donc, dans la réalité - pas seulement dans les concepts, qui peuvent donner matière à dis-  cussion, mais dans la réalité même - des différences peuvent être objectivement perçues  dans l'évolution de ces deux ensembles ; et cela me confirme dans l'opinion que ces deux  ensembles ont chacun leurs caractéris:iques propres, bien que ressortissant l'un et l'autre  au dornaine de la culture, Mesdames, Messieurs, voilà les raisons de ma proposition ; et  il n'y en a pas d'autre,
