A little money would be needed,  however, for a small Secretariat, and to pay modest  salaries to a few younger men; they should not  be paid too much, for they would have to learn  the idea of scientific abnegation.  The project was of the greatest importance to  Unesco, since it aimed at the unification of  cultures and civilizations. The old nations which  had served civilization with great merit in the  eighteenth century should not keep the monopoly;  new and jealous forces were arising in the world,  and some of them had not yet shown their full  strength. A balance-sheet should be drawn up  to show the contribution to civilization which each  people had made and could make. Humanity was  a dream, an aspiration and an ideal of the future,  but one day it would become a reality, through  the collaboration of all the peoples of the world.  M. 1. that each chapter concerning a given coun-  try should be drafted within the country itself  and then forwarded to the central body set up by  Unesco; or
2. that the chapters should be drafted by  the central Unesco body, but that, after drafting  and before publication, they should be submitted  for comments to each country concerned.  By either method it would be possible to obtain  a history accepted by all Unesco’s Member States,  which would, at the same time, be a Unesco  work.  The discussion being closed, the CHAIRMAN  submitted the Egyptian proposal to the vote.  It was adopted by thirteen votes to seven, with  one abstention. Now, in the light of the  progress that science had made, he had a better  understanding of the meaning of the tensions  project. Nevertheless, as far as he himself was  concerned, he looked at it from quite a special  point of view. Dr. AWAD (Egypt) thanked Mr. Klineberg for  his reply.
