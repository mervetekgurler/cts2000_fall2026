SUMMARY RECORDS OF THE MEETINGS OF THE PROGRAMME AND BUDGET COMMISSION FIRST MEETING Tuesday, 20 September 1949, at 4 p.m. Chairman: Professor PAULO DE BERREDO CARNEIRO (Brazil) Election of the Chairman. Following a suggestion from the Nominations Committee, the Programme and Budget Commis- sion unanimously elected Professor Paulo de Ber- redo Carneiro (Brazil) as its Chairman. Mr. Carneiro took the Chair. Election of Vice-Chairmen and Rapporteur. The CHAIRMAN proposed the election of two Vice- Chairmen to help him in the long and complex work of the Commission. Mr. BILLIÈRE (France) proposed Mr. Przemys- law Ogrodzinski, Vice-President of the Polish del- egation, and Professor Jean Piaget, President of the Swiss delegation, as Vice-Chairmen. Adopted. Mr. PHOTIADES (Greece) emphasized that the Rapporteur of the Commission should have had wide experience of the work of the Organization in general, and of the duties of Rapporteur in particular. He proposed Mr. W. D. Pile, of the United Kingdom delegation. Adopted. Adoption of the Agenda (document 4C/PRG/1). The CHAIRMAN stated that, in accordance with a decision of the Beirut Conference, the present session was to be a short business section. Con- sequently, no radical amendments should be made to the programme as a whole, and he asked the Commission to regard that principle as a gentle- man's agreement which should be respected. After giving some practical information about the documents relating more particularly to the programme and budget, he said that the assur- ances given at the plenary meeting, following on a speech by the British delegate, with regard to the progress of the Commission's work, would be carefully borne in mind. Wednesday, 21 September, had been fixed as the last day for receiving any new draft amend- ment or new proposal involving an amendment of substance to the programme or the budget. Up to the present two new proposals had been received, from the delegations of the Netherlands and the United States of America respectively. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) wished to make certain suggestions regarding the Agenda, in the light of the financial events of the past forty-eight hours. His delegation feared that the Agenda would not be a business-like one if Ttem 9 remained so far down, as it would mean that the budget ceiling would be discussed rather too late. He would, however, be prepared to leave the Agenda as it was, with the warning that in 1946 the late dis- cussion of the Budget ceiling had led to difficulties which had been avoided in Mexico City and Beirut by placing the discussion of the ceiling earlier in the Agenda. His most important suggestion concerned Item 4: the United Kingdom delegation considered it very important to have a real Budget Sub-Committee like those which had proved so successful in Mexico City andBeirut. It was therefore impor- tant to decide, as soon as possible, on the terms of reference of the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee for the Programme and Budget. Professor SOMMERFELT (Norway) seconded the British proposal for a Budget Sub-Committee re- commendation that the ceiling of the Budget should be discussed earlier in the Agenda. If the latest financial events had not taken place, it would be less necessary to discuss the ceiling at an early stage. But it had already been very difficult for some delegations to obtain from their Governments an acceptance of the high Budget figure proposed by the Director-General; now, as a result of the revaluation of the dollar, the con- tributions of certain Member States would be increased by 30 per cent, they would therefore be              forced to vote for smaller sums than those in the existing budget estimates. Professor Sommerfelt concluded by stressing the necessity of drawing up terms of reference for a Budget Sub-Committee, as advocated by Mr. Hardman, considering that a good Budget Sub-Committee was of great importance to the Conference's work. Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of America) considered it important to examine each item of the programme with its financial implications before deciding on the final amount to be appro- priated by the Conference. With regard to Item 4, the United States delegation would prefer the Sub- Committee to be a Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee, as stated in the Agenda, rather than a Budget Sub-Committee, to discuss the ceiling of the budget and other important financial questions, and to make recommendations back to the Programme and Budget Commission. His delegation would also prefer items 6 and 10 to be combined in one item, which would mean that each chapter of the programme, with its financial implications, would be thoroughly discussed with the help of information from the Director-General and his staff. If such a policy were followed, the total arrived at might be too high or too low, but that would not preclude any delegation from pro- posing a different budget total. Dr. MARTINEZ BAEZ (Mexico) pointed out that the method advocated by the United Kingdom delegation had been followed during the Second and Third Sessions of the General Conference; but, at that time, the Secretariat had not the necessary experience to apply that business-like method satisfactorily. Nevertheless, Unesco must not be too business-like, for the Organization should, above all, concern itself with the fulfil- ment of its duties. He therefore thought that it was necessary first to determine the Organization’s tasks and then to consider the budgetary implica- tions of each of the items of the programme. There would be sufficient time to shelve certain items at a later stage or limit their budgetary implications, if real economic difficulties were encountered. He therefore seconded the proposal of the United States delegation. Professor SCHAFF (Poland), in the name of his Government, supported the Agenda in the form presented by the Secretariat. If the ceiling were discussed first, as suggested by the British delega- tion, there would be insufficient time left for the important detailed discussion of programme items with their budgetary implications, mentioned by the United States delegation. He therefore moved the adoption of the Agenda as given in docu- ment 4C/PRG/1.
253 Mr. PIAGET (Switzerland) quite understood how the recent currency devaluations had induced certain delegations to propose the fixing of a budget ceiling before examining each item of the budget. But he did not share that view, for in his opinion the present uncertainty made the fixing of such a ceiling extremely difficult. He observed that the fact of examining, to begin with, each item of the programme would not necessarily lead to a higher budget figure, and that it would always be possible after having determined programme needs to adjust them, if necessary, to a ceiling. The Swiss delegation was therefore in favour of the Agenda proposed by the Secretariat. Mr. SEYDOUX (France) supported the view of the Polish and Swiss delegations. The devalua- tion of certain currencies undoubtedly gave rise to a problem as regards both the contributions of States and the budget of the Organization; but it seemeddifficult for the moment to foresee the incidence of that devaluation on the budget, for expenditure in the sterling area and in the franc area would not automatically increase in the pro- portion. Experts of the Secretariat were at present examining the question, and Mr. Seydoux thought that the Commission could meanwhile consider the different items of the programme as proposed in the Agenda. Mr. SHARIF (Pakistan) agreed with the British delegation that the ceiling of the budget should be fixed before discussing each of the programme items. He considered this to be a practical and realistic approach, and therefore strongly sup- ported the United Kingdom delegation. Sir James SHELLEY (New Zealand) had been instructed by his Government to support the British proposal that the ceiling should be dis- cussed first. It was important to know what percentage of the total budget figure each project would take, before discussing the projects. If the procedure advocated by the United States delega- tion were followed, higher and higher amounts might be required for the projects first discussed and, if the ceiling was reached, later items would receive poor consideration. Thus the weak parts of the programme would suffer, whereas the Con- ference might wish to sustain and develop them. Professor PHOTIADES (Greece) said that the Executive Board had thought that, as present circumstances might upset the balance of the budget, it would be better to defer a decision concerning the programme and the allocation of funds. He emphasized that the latter should take into account the priority factor, which implied that credits provided for certain neeeds should be maintained intact.   Mr. ANDREWS (Union of South Africa) thought that, in the light of the last two days’ events, the only logical procedure was to start by fixing a budget ceiling: he therefore seconded the United Kingdom proposal. He also supported the British motion in favour of a Budget Sub-Committee, as the system had worked so well at the two preced- ing sessions of the Conference. He concluded by pointing out that the Programme and Budget Commission held the future of Unesco in its hands and, if it were not realistic regarding expenditure, it would make Member States apprehensive about the future budget of the Organization. Dr. EL-HASHIMI (Iraq) thought that there was a discrepancy between the hopes expressed in connexion with the Director-General’s Report and the proposal to fix a rigid ceiling for the budget. Such rigidity might jeopardize the activities of Unesco altogether, and prevent the Secretariat from fulfilling the aspiration voiced at the Conference. He therefore supported the United States proposal to examine the value of each project in terms of its importance in furthering the aims of Unesco, and to apportion funds to each item in accordance with that evaluation. It would be unfair for Unesco to be the first victim of the devaluation of the pound and Unesco’s programme would be jeopardized if it were discussed on that basis. Mr. RUEFF (Monaco) was not in favour of an inflationary policy as far as Unesco’s projects were concerned. He thought that the problem was whether receipts should be adapted to expenditure or expenditure to receipts, and whether expendi- ture was desirable in spite of the added burdensit would involve. He said that one of the recognized merits of the British financial system wasits elasti- city, which, thanksto variations in rates, permitted receipts to be kept on a level with expenditure. He thought that it would be illogical to abandon that great tradition of English financial law and to have recourse to an arbitrary method which prevent them for considering each projects on its merits. In his opinion, the fixing of a target figure would rob the examination of the budget by the Commission of all real importance. More- over, there was nothing to prevent them from examining the different items of the budget so strictly that the total amount of the budget might be lower than the target which would have been directly imposed. Mr. PORTUONDO (Cuba) stated that he had been Chairman of the Budget Commission of the Cuban Parliament, where it was the custom to examine the needs of the country first and then to vote the corresponding items of the budget. He thought that they should first draw up a pro- gramme of the needs of the Organization and then establish the budget. He did not think that such a procedure would involve greater expense, and, even if it did, he felt sure that no Government would refuse to incur slight additional expenditure in order to contribute to the cause of peace, cul- ture and education. Dr. KRUYT (Netherlands) thought that monetary difficulties made it almost impossible to discuss the ceiling of the budget, and suggested that the Secretariat should make a report on the implica- tions of the devaluation for each programme item. On the other hand, he supported the United King- dom delegation in so far as he considered it essen- tial to have a Budget Sub-Committee. Dr. NAVRATIL (Czechoslovakia) was against fixing a budget ceiling first, as he thought that Unesco’s duty was first to discover needs and examine ways of meeting them, and then to find the necessary funds. While a good business man might have money and then plan to buy goods, a good man first considered the needs to be filled and then how to find the necessary money. Dr. WALKER (Australia) recalled that Australia had hitherto supported an early discussion of the ceiling on the Agenda; but, in the light of the last few days’ events, it was clearly impossible to fix a ceiling at once. The most urgent task of the Programme and Budget Commission was to examine changes made in the programme since Beirut, the financial implications and the budget estimates. He supported the United States pro- posal to take Items 6 and 10 together, provided that Item 10 was moved up to the place of Item 6. He recalled that the Fourth Session of the General Conference was not invited to debate the pro- gramme in detail, but only minor adjustments in the Programme approved at Beirut. Dr. Walker also supported the proposal of the Netherlands delegation that the Secretariat should be requested to report on the implications of the recent monetary changes for the existing budget estimates. It was impossible to discuss the budget until it was known how much money the Director- General thought would now be required for the programme. It would be valuable for the Pro- gramme and Budget Commission to ask its Draft- ing and Co-ordination Sub-Committee to make a detailed examination of the Secretariat’s report on the implications of the recent monetary changes: that Sub-Committee should therefore be empow- ered to look into detailed points arising from the new budget estimates. Until such time as the Secretariat’s report was ready, the Programme and Budget Commission should deal with those items on the Agenda which had no financial implica- tions.            The CHAIRMAN summed up the three proposals made: (1) the United Kingdom proposal to con- sider Item 9 of the Agenda before Item 6; (2) the proposal concerning the nature and functions of the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee;
(3) the United States proposal to consider Items 6 and 10 simultaneously. Mr. SEYDOUX (France) remarked that there was also another proposal, namely, the outright adop- tion of the draft Agenda. The CHAIRMAN said that he had taken into account the amendments proposed to the draft Agenda, which was naturally the basic document. He asked the Head of the United Kingdom delega- tion to state whether his proposal was indeed to examine Item 9 of the Agenda before Item 6. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) had wished to point out that it might be advisable to take Item 9 at an earlier stage; but his most important proposal had been to decide upon the terms of reference of the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub- Committee mentioned in Item 4. The CHAIRMAN inferred from the speeches of the United Kingdom and Norwegian delegates that the British proposal was to establish a Budget Sub- Committee. He feared that the discussion which had taken place concerning the order of Items 9 and 6 did not coincide exactly with the proposal which had just been made by the delegate of the United Kingdom. Professor SOMMERFELT (Norway) stated that his point of view was exactly the same as that of the British delegation. Dr. MARTINEZ BAEZ (Mexico) said that a new proposal was thus before the meeting, for the dis- cussion had merely concerned the ceiling of the budget. He was in favour of the Agenda proposed by the Secretariat and therefore also in favour of the establishment of a Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee. He thought that a Sub-Com- mittee could do excellent work by dealing simul- taneously with each item of the programme and with its budgetary implications. He hoped that the Sub-Committee would duly be informed of the conclusions of the experts concerning the budgetary implications of the devaluation. The CHAIRMAN asked the delegate of the United Kingdom whether he desired to change the order of the various items of the Agenda or only to alter Item 4. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) was anxious for an assurance that the Sub-Committee in Item4 would have the same terms of reference as the Budget Sub-Commissions of the two previous
255 sessions: he was not concerned with the order of items on the Agenda. The CHAIRMAN said that Item 4 of the Pro- visional Agenda proposed the establishment of a Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee, but not a Budget Sub-Committee such as had been formally proposed by the delegate of the United Kingdom. Mr. RUEFF (Monaco) said he was prepared to support the latter proposal; it was unimportant whether the work in question was done by the Sub-Committee proposed by Mr. Hardman or by the Secretariat, since the organization of the Com- mission’s work would not be changed as a result. He asked for an immediate vote on the British proposal. The CHAIRMAN asked the delegate of the United Kingdom to indicate the composition and powers of the Sub-Committee proposed by him. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) repeated that he was asking for an assurance that the Sub- Committee mentioned in Item 4 would in fact be the Budget Sub-Commission which had been so successful in Mexico City and in Beirut. There had been no budget Sub-Commission in 1946, and the result had been a hurried consideration of later items on the Agenda and the approval of so wide a programme that many delegations had expressed the fear that it could never be fulfilled. The DIRECTOR-GENERAL pointed out that the proposal under Item 4 of the Agenda had not been made by the Secretariat but by the Executive Board. He said that the proposed Sub-Committee would not be exactly the same as the Sub-Com- mission that had functioned in Mexico City and Beirut. If the Commission desired the establish- ment of a Budget Sub-Committee similar to the Sub-Commission set up at the two previous ses- sions, a formal proposal to that effect should be made. Dr. KRUYT (Netherlands) seconded the British proposal. Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of Amer- ica) recalled that the had previously spoken in opposition to the British proposal for a Budget Sub-Committee; but there had since been an Australian proposal which he was prepared to support: that the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub- Committee mentioned under Item 4 should be authorized to handle details of the budget. The CHAIRMAN asked the delegate of the United Kingdom whether he agreed with the proposal of the United States delegation.   Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) said that he would withdraw his own proposal and support that of the Australian and United States delega- tions. The latter proposal was adopted. The CHAIRMAN then put to the vote the ques- tion of the simultaneous consideration of Items 6 and 10 of the Agenda and said that the United States proposal was to place the corresponding budget estimate opposite each project. Adopted. The Agenda was adopted, subject to the two above-mentioned amendments. Establishment of a Drafting and Co-ordina- tion Sub-Committee for the Programme and Budget. The CHAIRMAN said that a Committee of seven members had been proposed, and that it had been suggested that the delegations of Belgium, the United States of America, France, Iran, Mexico, the United Kingdom and Czechoslovakia should appoint one of their members to serve on the Sub-Committee, provided, of course, that they had persons capable of dealing with the technical questions involved. Adopted. Examination of the Draft Code of Policies (document 4C/5 rev., Part One). The CHAIRMAN regretted the absence of the Chairman of the Executive Board, who was best qualified to present the Draft Code prepared by the Board. He asked Professor Sommerfelt to say a few introductory words. Professor SOMMERFELT (Norway) recalled that the General Conference at its Third Session had adopted the following Resolution:
8.6. Cone or POLICIES FOR 1950 DRAFT PROGRAMME. The Director-General and the Executive Board are re- quested, in preparing the draft programme for 1950, to preface this programme with a code of policies to include those approved by previous sessions of the General Conference and those recommended for adoption in 1950. In implementation of that Resolution, the Ex- ecutive Board had drawn up a Draft Code of eighteen directives which, in the spirit of Unesco’s Constitution, defined the chief aims and general policy to be followed in planning and implement- ing the programme of the Organization. The Executive Board had not considered it appropriate at the current session to introduce new matters into the directives, as the programme was intended to be a continuation of the Beirut programme. At the Florence Session, however, important changes and new projects might be considered; it would then be necessary to draw up new directives. Mr. EVANS (United States of America), on behalf of his delegation, congratulated the Execu- tive Board on having prepared the Draft Code. His delegation would, however, like to see the programme carried somewhat further, and certain of the directives expanded to contain more descrip- tion of Unesco’s programme items. In consider- ation of the Draft Code, his delegation was pre- pared to submit a resolution changing the proposal made by the United States at Beirut. Mr. Evans then read the new resolution, announcing that the text of it would be distributed the next day. The resolution was an attempt to overcome deficiencies of past procedure, when resolutions passed in one year had been forgotten the next, revived the year after that and finally omitted by error in the programme of the succeeding year. The United States delegation felt that there should be a basic programme, which would only be altered by amendments from year to year; there should also be administrative directives to guide the policies and procedures of the Secretariat. In reply to a question from the CHAIRMAN as to whether the United States delegation dis- approved of the Draft Code of Policies, Mr. EVANS (United States of America) said that his delega- tion considered it an inadequate resolution as an amendment. The CHAIRMAN proposed that the discussion of the United States amendment should be post- poned until the next meeting. The text would meanwhile be circulated in the working languages. Agreed. Subject to the above, the Code of Policies (document 4C/5 (rev.), Part I) was provisionally approved 2. Dr. PARRA PEREZ (Venezuela), Vice-Chairman of the Executive Board, submitted the documents containing the amendments to the programme of the Organization considered advisable by the Executive Board after a thorough examination of the question.
