6.1 M. BODEN (Luxembourg) :  Monsieur le Président, je voudrais tout d'abord me joindre aux orateurs  qui m'ont précédé pour vous féliciter de votre élection à la présidence de cette  Conférence générale et pour remercier le pays hôte de nous avoir accueillis dans  sa capitale pour cette vingt-troisième session.
6.2 Monsieur le Président, Mesdames, Messieurs, comme le Luxembourg assure  actuellement la présidence des Etats membres de la Communauté européenne, j'ai  l'honneur de prendre la parole au nom des dix Etats membres ainsi que de  l'Espagne et du Portugal qui en seront dans quelques mois membres à part entière.
6.3 En chargeant la présidence en exercice de prendre la parole à cette  Conférence au nom des Etats membres de la Communauté, les Dix aimeraient réaf-  firmer leur attachement indéfectible à la coopération multilatérale et aux  objectifs et principes de l'Unesco tels qu'ils sont énoncés dans son Acte cons-  titutif. Une organisation qui s'efforce de promouvoir la coopération dans les  domaines de l'éducation, de la science, de la culture et de la communication, de  combattre l'analphabétisme, de sauvegarder le patrimoine de l'humanité et de  défendre les droits de l'homme dans tous les domaines de sa compétence, reste  aujourd'hui aussi indispensable qu'au moment de sa création.
6.4 C'est dans cette optique que, dans une déclaration commune, les mi-  nistres des affaires étrangères de la Communauté ont exprimé le 29 avril leur  inquiétude face à l'actuelle crise qui touche l'Unesco ; ils ont souligné à cette  occasion la nécessité d'obtenir cette année des réformes effectives en ce qui  concerne à la fois la gestion administrative et le Programe et budget relatif au  prochain exercice biennal. A cet égard, les ministres ont insisté sur l'impor-  tance d'aboutir, lors de la 121e session du Conseil exécutif, à un accord sur :  des recommandations concernant un Projet de programme et de budget pour 1986-1987  qui recueille l'appui de tous et qui reflète pleinement la diminution des res-  sources de l'organisation ; un plan et un calendrier général pour la mise en  oeuvre de réformes administratives ; l'indispensable réduction sélective des  dépenses au cours de la présente année afin de tenir compte du déficit résultant  du retrait d'un Etat membre.  J'invite S. Exc. M. Fernand Boden, ministre de l'éducation nationale et
6.5 Les Dix reconnaissent que des discussions approfondies ont eu lieu  depuis lors et que le document 23 C/6 contient des conclusions importantes. Les  Dix marquent leur plein appui au consensus que les membres du Conseil exécutif  ont pu réaliser à sa 121e session. ils expriment l'espoir que la Conférence  générale entérine prochainement ce consensus afin que la mise en oeuvre des  décisions soit rapide et effective et que, dans les questions laissées ouvertes  par le Conseil exécutif, surtout dans le grand programme XIII, la Conférence  générale se mette également d'accord par consensus.
6.6 Durant cette session où la Conférence générale est appelée 2 prendre des  décisions importantes pour les activités futures de l'Unesco, les Dix sont  disposés à coopérer avec toutes les délégations dans le but de rendre opéra-  tionnel ce consensus et de trouver un accord sur toutes les questions non encore  résolues. A cet effet, les Dix envisagent de formuler des propositions concrètes.  Ils s'attendent également à ce que ,soit établi un mécanisme chargé d'assurer le  suivi des décisions du Conseil exécutif pendant le prochain exercice biennal et,  notament, la mise en oeuvre des réformes ayant fait l'objet d'un accord au terme  de nos travaux.
6.7 Les Dix sont convaincus que si les Etats membres, le Directeur général  et le Secrétariat unissent leurs efforts en vue d'améliorer davantage le fonc-  tionnement de l'Unesco et d'assurer une exécution efficace et rationnelle de ses  programmes, nous surmonterons les difficultés que connaît actuellement l'Orga-  nisation. Si l'esprit et l'enthousiasme des origines ainsi que le respect des  droits de chacun nous animent, nous réussirons à réaliser les grands desseins que  l'humanité tout entière attend de cette Organisation. Nous sommes sûrs que cette
239    voie est la 'seule qui puisse rétablir l'universalité de l'Unesco et ainsi la  faire bénéficier de l'appui et du concours de tous les Etats.
6.8 Après avóir fait part des intentions et des espoirs des dix Etats  membres de la Communauté européenne ainsi que de l'Espagne et du Portugal,  permettez-moi de faire quelques remarques en qualité de représentant de mon pays.
6.9 Depuis un peu plus d'un an, lors de la constitution du nouveau Gouver-  nement luxembourgeois, les relations avec l'Unesco ont été transférées du Mi-  nistère des affaires culturelles au Ministère de l'éducation nationale et de la  jeunesse. Décision d'autant plus logique que sont réunis désormais sous une même  responsabilité ministérielle trois secteurs qui recouvrent directement plusieurs  grands domaines de l'Unesco.
6.10 I1 s'agit en premier lieu de l'éducation, qui reste une des grandes  priorités mondiales et la première condition du progrès économique et social,  tant dans les pays en voie de développement que dans les pays industrialisés.  Elle est également le meilleur fer de lance contre les préjugés de tous ordres et  partant, la base de la compréhension et de la paix internationales. I1 va de soi  qu'un petit pays comme le Luxembourg s'intéresse à toutes les formes de coopé-  ration régionale et internationale sur le plan de l'éducation. A cet égard, je ne  puis que souscrire aux' observations formulées par mon homologue français à cette  tribune, à savoir que l'éducation et la formation doivent plus que jamais demeu-  rer au coeur du programme de l'Unesco et constituer l'axe autour duquel s'orien-  tent et se développent les autres activités.
6.11 Dans le domaine de la science et de la recherche, les apports de notre  pays ne peuvent évidemment être que très modestes. Mais l'Unesco nous fournit  précisément un cadre idéal pour des échanges fructueux dont nos chercheurs,  placés dans des conditions souvent difficiles, sont les premiers bénéficiaires.  Dans le même esprit, nous saluons tous les efforts de l'Unesco pour réaliser des  programmes de recherche communs et nous tâcherons de nous y associer dans la  mesure du possible. Dans un monde où la recherche scientifique s'accélère à un  rythme vertigineux, le savoir tend de plus en plus à se concentrer aux mains de  quelques-uns. L'Unesco fait contrepoids à cette évolution dangereuse, en tant  qu'elle garantit l'accès des petites nations aux grands courants de recherche.  Aussi avons-nous constaté avec satisfaction que le grand programme VI accorde une  grande importance à la recherche, à la formation et à la coopération interna-  tionale dans les domaines des sciences exactes, des sciences naturelles et des  sciences humaines ainsi que de la technologie.
6.12 Un troisième domaine - celui de la jeunesse - a été rattaché à mon  Ministère parce qu'il est en fait indissociable des grandes options d'une poli-  tique éducative. Le monde de demain sera façonné par la jeunesse d'aujourd'hui.  La décision des Nations Unies de proclamer 1985 Année internationale de la  jeunesse a été une initiative heureuse qui a contribué à mettre en relief les  problèmes et les angoisses, mais aussi les espoirs et le formidable potentiel  d'action et de création des jeunes générations. Je me réjouis donc de constater  que les programmes de l'Unesco, au-delà de leurs finalités spécifiques, mani-  festent une ouverture de plus en plus large vers les problèmes et les préoccu-  pations de la jeunesse.
6.13 En tant que représentant du gouvernement luxembourgeois, je m'en vou-  drais de ne pas profiter de l'occasion pour préciser l'attitude de notre pays à  l'égard de l'Unesco. Le Luxembourg a toujours fait preuve d'un attachement  particulier aux institutions internationales vouées à la sauvegarde de la com-  préhension entre les peuples et de la paix mondiale.
6.14 J'aimerais tout d'abord relever le climat de coopération qui s'est  instauré au sein de l'Unesco au cours des derniers mois et qui a déjà produit des  résultats que nous jugeons très positifs. Nous apprécions surtout la bonne  disposition des pays non alignés qui a permis d'atteindre des compromis honnêtes  et des progrès tangibles. Nous avons sans cesse défendu la nécessité d'établir le  Projet de programme et de budget pour 1986-1987 sur la base d'une croissance zéro  en termes réels et nous sommes satisfaits de voir que le Conseil exécutif a  soumis à la Conférence générale une recommandation à cet effet. Nous jugeons  également nécessaire que le déficit créé par le retrait d'un Etat membre ne soit  pas compensé par une augmentation des contributions, mais équilibré par des  ajustements et des économies budgétaires. Le moyen choisi pour la réalisation de  ces économies est l'établissement de priorités et la concentration des pro-  grammes, de manière à éviter tout chevauchement entre les programmes de l'Unesco  et ceux d'une autre organisation du système des Nations Unies. Cette concentra-  tion devra également permettre une affectation plus efficace du personnel. Des  priorités ont été fixées pour la presque totalité des grands programmes. Nous  espérons vivement que les délégations puissent également appliquer ce procédé au
240    grand programme XIII. Le budget-programme pour 1986-1987 en sortira mieux équi-  libré et bénéficiera d'un soutien encore plus marqué. Nous constatons aussi avec  satisfaction que la portion du budget réservée aux programmes est en progression  par rapport aux crédits correspondant aux dépenses administratives. Nous sommes  convaincus que cette tendance pourra encore être accentuée par une plus grande  décentralisation au sein du Secrétariat.
6.15 Je n'ai pas l'ambition d'entrer dans tous les détails du consensus  auquel ont abouti les membres du Conseil exécutif à la 121e session. Cependant,  je tiens à relever le chapitre de l'information et de la communication auquel le  Luxembourg attache une attention particulière. La nécessité de veiller à ce que  l'information circule librement et qu'elle connaisse une diffusion toujours plus  large et mieux équilibrée trouve notre plein accord. I1 en va de même de la  définition réaliste du nouvel ordre mondial de l'information et de la communi-  cation, compris comme "un processus évolutif et continu".
6.16 Le Luxembourg juge regrettable que les subventions aux organisations non  gouvernementales aient été réduites de quelque 900.000 dollars. La collaboration  entre l'Unesco et ces organisations doit être jugée très positivement. Elle  permet de nouer et de maintenir des contacts avec des organisations oeuvrant sur  le terrain et qui souvent représentent de larges portions de nos populations. Je  nourris l'espoir que cette conférence trouvera les moyens pour limiter au maximum  les effets négatifs de cette réduction et que nous pourrons continuer à associer  sur une large échelle les organisations non gouvernementales aux travaux de notre  Organisation.
6.17 Monsieur le Président, Mesdames, Messieurs, j'ai déjà à plusieurs  reprises relevé l'orientation plus positive de l'activite de notre organisation  au cours des derniers mois. J'espère que nous aurons le courage de trancher les  points encore en suspens et de consolider le mouvement de réformes qui s'est  instauré grâce à un climat de plus grande compréhension dans le but de renforcer  l'Unesco et de lui assurer un long avenir fructueux. Si nous continuons et  soutenons le processus de réformes engagé, je suis sûr que la construction de cet  avenir se fera avec la pleine participation de la famille des Nations Unies sur  la base des idéaux immuables de l'Acte constitutif.

8.1 Mr BIRMINGHAM (Ireland) :  Mr President, distinguished delegates, may I preface my remarks by echo-  ing other speakers in congratulating you, Mr President, on your election; let me  assure you of my full support and the full support of my entire delegation as you  carry out the onerous tasks that you have undertaken.
8.2 Mr President, this is the first opportunity that I have had to intervene  in the general policy debate at the General Conference. I am very happy to have  the opportunity of doing so in the beautiful surroundings of Sofia. And I would  like to thank our hosts for their hospitality and all the effort that has gone  into the arrangements. I, am also particularly pleased that my intervention should  come immediately after that of the delegate of Luxembourg who of course spoke not  just on behalf of his own country but on behalf of all the members of the Euro-  pean Community, including Ireland.  a. 3 With your permission, Mr President, I should like first of all to  address the general issue of my country's role within the Organization. Ireland  has been a member of Unesco for 23 years. It has witnessed the growth of the  Organization's membership from 45 States, back in 1962, to 160 States in 1985.  During all of that time we have consistently supported the aims and objectives of  the Organization. We consider that the existence of a body charged with the pro-  motion of international co-operation in education, science and culture, with  ensuring respect for justice, human rights and basic freedoms, and with the  eradication of illiteracy, is essential to our modern world. We shall continue to  lend our support to Unesco and to participate as fully as possible in its exten-  sive activities.
8.4 The past two years have not been easy for Unesco. Ireland has followed  with concern the developments which have led to the withdrawal from the Organiz-  ation of one Member State and the serving of notice by others that they too will  withdraw. We have always maintained that the principle of universality is funda-  mental to the integrity and effectiveness of the United Nations system as a  whole. That is why we feel that every effort must be made to enable all States to  participate fully in the United Nations system and to ensure that disagreements  are resblved on the basis of discussion and co-operation. Negotiation and compro-  mise are at the heart of any rational system of international co-operation. It
241    is my view that those qualities are particularly necessary in relation to Unesco,  whose Consti:tution commits it to promoting the objectives of the United Nations  through collaboration in education, science and culture.
8.5 My govereent has been greatly encouraged by the efforts towards reform  which have taken place within Unesco during the past year. There are definite  signs that the problems which have afflicted the Organization in the past are  beginning to be addressed in a realistic manner. The results of the most recent  meetings of the Executive Board demonstrate that trend. It is my government's  hope that the General Conference will seize the opportunity to endorse the  initiative of the Executive Board by approving the relevant decisions and recom-  mendations adopted by the Board in relation to the draft programme and budget.
8.6 That will be a significant first step, but the General Conference must  go even further. This session is likely to be decisive in determining the future  of Unesco. If it can build on the positive approach demonstrated by the Executive  Board, then there are grounds for confidence that the Organization will once  again be able to concentrate its full strength towards fulfilling the basic  objectives for which it was founded.
8.7 It is essential, Mr President, that this session reflect in its work the  purposes and functions of Unesco as laid down in its Constitution. The fields of  competence of the Organization are clearly spelled out in the founding documents.  Unesco has neither the mandate nor the resources to pursue programmes of activity  which in some cases duplicate and cut across the responsibility of other inter-  national agencies. HOW could that be justified when so much remains to be done in  order to fulfil the fundamental role of the Organization?
8.8 The principal theme which runs through the purpose and function of  Unesco, as defined in the Constitution, is collaboration. Unesco was founded to  promote collaboration among nations. But too often in recent years, the Organiz-  ation has been the focus of division rather than of collaboration. It is our  responsibility to ensure that it once again becomes a forum for co-operation. We  can do this by taking up the example of the Executive Board, and making greater  efforts to achieve consensus. We must also avoid the promotion of politically  slanted resolutions.
8.9 At the present session, we are faced with a draft budget which prompts  us to make a closer than usual examination of the administration of the Organiz-  ation. I have already expressed the hope that one major cause for retrenchment  may be a purely temporary phenomenon. My government welcomes the measures taken  by the Director-General to realign the proportion of Unesco funds spent on pro-  grammes, as compared with that spent on personnel and administration, and we very  much hope that that trend will be continued over the next few years. We see that  as being essential if Unesco is to become more relevant to the role for which it  was created.
8.10 Mr President, it seems to me that these steps - endorsement of the  Executive Board's decisions; agreement to concentrate the work of the Organiz-  ation on issues which properly fall within its mandate; a genuine effort to avoid  divisive political rhetoric; and a greater focus on programme goals and expendi-  ture - would help greatly to overcome the difficulties which the Organization has  faced in the past two years. It is my hope that this Conference will mark the end  of the divisions which have threatened the integrity of the Organization in the  recent past and the beginning of a new era when all countries - the large and the  small - will have sufficient confidence in Unesco to work together to achieve its  essential aims.
8.11 My other main purpose here this afternoon, Mr President, is to comment  on the draft programme and budget from the Irish point of view. My first comment  has to be that the improvement in the quality of presentation of the C/5 document  continues. I want to congratulate the Secretariat on a thoroughly competent piece  of work. The decision to present an overview of the programme in a separate  volume is a good one: I believe the General Conference will endorse this format  for the future. There is a clarity of presentation in Volume II of document
23 C/5 which reflects credit on its authors. I think that even people who might  be unfamiliar with Unesco's working methods will be impressed, for example, by  the orderly progression in the major programmes from targets to expected results  to proposed actions.
8.12 Two questions kept occurring to me in my necessarily brief perusal of  the document. One was: with what degree of accuracy could the outcome of pro-  gramme actions be predicted from the terms used to describe the results expected  from them? That is to say, how accurately can you predict what is intended to  happen from the verbal description set out in the document? The second concerned  the relationship between the level of funding of particular activities and the
242    expected outcomes. That is to say, is it reasonable to expect a certain level of  results from the amount of money provided?
8.13 In relation .to the first point, I believe that great progress has been.  made in setting out clear goals for programme action. For example, in Major Pro-  gramme II, one of the results expected from the subprogramme entitled 'Training  of literacy personnel' is the following: 'Production and use by Member States of  training and self-training materials for the continuing training of educational  personnel and of documents for conducting training workshops'. That is linked to  a series of specific (and adequately funded) activities. It should, therefore, be  possible to say with reasonable accuracy at the end of the biennium the extent to  which the expected result will have been achieved. The overall standard in this  respect is high although there are, predictably in a document of such length and  complexity, some points of programme construction which we will take up in the  relevant programme commissions.
8.14 My second point relates to the adequacy of funding for individual  activities. Again I think I can best illustrate this by reference to a specific  example. In Major Programme XI, Culture and the future, one of the expected  results of the subprogramme. entitled 'The cultural dimension of development' is,  and again I quote, 'Development of exchanges of information on current research  dealing with the interactions between culture, the economy and society'. My ques-  tion is whether the related activity and the proposed funding of US $30,000 could  represent significant progress towards the stated goal. I recognize the problems  involved in distributing the modest resources of the Organization in a way which  seems equitable to all the Member States. And I am not suggesting that this par-  ticular activity is not intrinsically important (indeed, on the contrary, I  believe that the overwhelming majority of the activities proposed for the next  biennium are intrinsically important and intrinsically interesting). No, the  point I am calling attention to here is the level of financial support at which a  particular activity becomes viable. Unless we can allocate realistic resources in  a specific case, the question must be asked whether the activity should be under-  taken at all. Now I accept that this is a matter which will have received atten-  tion from the Executive Board. I still feel, however, that the quality of pro-  gramme execution might be enhanced by even more rigorous concentration of our  activities, a suggestion which is likely to acquire even greater urgency in the  next biennium, if our worst fears about a drop in the Organization's income are  realized.
8.15 My general comment, then, on the issue of programme construction is that  our present approach is the right one in that it provides a framework within  which we are able to conduct a realistic evaluation of our activities. (In paren-  thesis I stress the need to continue to seek still greater clarity in stating the  results expected from some programme actions.) My feeling on the second issue  that I raised, that of programme concentration, is that we should take a further  look at those programme actions where the available funding is close to the  margin of viability and go on to establish some workable criterion to guide us in  that respect.
8.16 I would like now with your permission, Mr President, to comment briefly  on a few aspects of the draft programme which are of particular interest to  Ireland at the present time. One of those areas is that of adult education, which  is the subject of a recent report by a Commission of experts set up by the Irish  Government. Major developments are taking place in the field, including the  establishment of local education boards with the specific task of developing  adult and community education in their areas. We were therefore glad of the  opportunity to participate in the Fourth International Conference on Adult Edu-  cation which was held in Paris last March. I hope very much that it will be  possible to take full account of the recommendations of that conference in our  future programme of work. A variety of social and economic factors, too well  known to require enumeration here, contribute to the growing conviction that edu-  cation services will in future have to take account of the needs of adults as  well as young people, on the principle that education is a lifelong process.
8.17 Another programme which I am especially interested .in is the Pro-  gramme IV.2 entitled 'The educational sciences and their application to the  renewal of the educational process'. An independent curriculum and examinations  board has recently been established in Ireland to advise on all matters relating  to the curriculum in primary and secondary schools. The activities proposed in  the subprogramme entitled 'Improvement of the content of education' will I am  sure, provide an international framework to'which the Board will be able to refer  in developing initiatives at national level.
8.18 The last point of detail that I want to raise, Mr President, relates to  culture, and in particular to the activities under the heading of 'Cultural  identity and intercultural relations'. As you are aware, a number of activities
243    relating to Celtic studies were planned for this biennium. And I am happy to  report that all the targets have been met. In particular, a Central Co-ordinating  Committee has been established under the chairmanship of a distinguished Celtic  scholar from the D.ublin Institute of Advanced Studies in order to plan future  work. And as we indicated in Paris, this project has proved to be a valuable  instrument of international co-operation. It is appropriate, I think, that I  should acknowledge here the contribution which Celtic scholars from Eastern  Europe, including Bulgaria, have made to the development of this project, which  was launched in Belgrade in 1980.
8.19 In conclusion, Mr President, I have followed with interest the detailed  examination of Unesco's performance which has been carried out at various levels  of the 0rgani.zation over the past 18 months. I am aware that this examination  involved many months of concentrated endeavour by a large number of dedicated  people within and outside the Organization. I know that national delegations will  have reflected on the outcome of the efforts to enhance the capacity of the  Organization to live up to the high ideals enshrined in the preamble to its Con-  stitution. (28.13) To sum up, we hope to obtain Unesco's assistance for the National Plan for  Non-formal Literacy Training for Development, the Project for the Organization of  Adult Education Training in Schools and Centres, the Project for the Organization of  Local Educational Resource Centres, and the Project for Rural Public Libraries.
(28.14) On 2 September 1984, the Director-General of Unesco, Mr Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow,  made an appeal on behalf of the National Literacy Plan of Honduras, to which the  international community has not responded in the way that Honduras needs and hopes,  given current circumstances and the serious social, economic and political situation  it is experiencing today. For this reason I should like to repeat to the Director-  General, to this august General Conference and to the world community my call for  international solidarity and co-operation in carrying out the major task of bringing  literacy to my country, thereby contributing to education, science and ultimately to  economic and social development, as well as to the preservation of peace in our  country and the world.
(28.15) We took note with great satisfaction of Major Programe V, 'Education, train-  ing and society', particularly Programmes V.3 and V.4. With regard to Programme V.3,  'Education and the world of work', we consider that interaction between education and  productive work is an effective way of achieving the democratization of education and  of society as a whole. Honduras fully supports its implementation, with the intro-  duction at the various educational levels of activities enabling young people to be  trained, from a very early age, to enter the arena of the world of work.
(28.16) As regards Programme V.4 on the promotion of physical education and sport,  Honduras is gratified at having played an enthusiastic part in the 'First World Week  of Physical Education and Sport for All', which served to promote and popularize the  values of this important discipline, and at having involved the national community in  the performance of a number of activities connected with International Youth Year.
(28.17) Honduras attaches the highest importance to Major Programme XI, 'Culture and  the future', and endorses the work carried out by Unesco in this sphere, whose purpose  is to preserve and restore the cultural heritage, in particular through international  campaigns for the safeguarding of historic monuments and sites, regarded as an essen-  tial part of the common heritage of mankind. We should like to emphasize our interest  in Programme XI.3, 'Creation and creativity', which is meant to encourage creation and  stimulate creativity in the various artistic disciplines, such as music, dance, drama  and literature and to promote the development of handicrafts.
(28.18) With regard to Unesco's contribution to peace and international understand-  ing, we think that it can best contribute by promoting the teaching of international  law, to which small nations can turn for protection.
(28.19) The Delegation of Honduras calls for the closest possible co-operation from  the Unesco Regional Office for Latin America and the Caribbean, and more particularly  from the Subregional Office for Central America and Panama, with headquarters at San  José, Costa Rica, which should concentrate their efforts on the development of plans,  programmes and projects designed to achieve the goals and objectives of the Major  Project .
(28.20) In concluding this statement, we should like to renew our support for the  Draft Programme and Budget for 1986-1987, secure in the knowledge that its execution  will make an important contribution to the development of education, science, culture  and communication among our peoples, while at the same time expressing our gratitude  for the co-operation we have received and may receive in the future. Mr President,  delegates: it is the earnest desire of the Delegation of the Republic of Honduras that  your attendance at the twenty-third session of the General Conference will be crowned  with success.
