Telegram from the Committee for the  Liberation of Istria.  [7] The CHAIRMAN read the following telegram  received from Mr. Fragiocomo, Chairman of the  Committee for the Liberation of Istria:  At a time when the world’s leading representatives of  culture are seeking to make their contribution to the  consolidation of peace, by ensuring the dissemination  of the enduring values of civilization, the National  Committee for the Liberation of Istria would wish  to gain a hearing for the claim of the peoples of  Istria to that respect for human and national rights  which is an essential condition for peace and the  progress of civilization. The peoples of Istria ask  Unesco to adopt a resolution ensuring the supremacy  of human values wherever they are ignored or  disputed in diplomatic dealings. The Istrian peoples  who are the victims of these dealings appeal to Unesco  to prevail upon the Governments of the Great Powers to  recognize that right to free self-determination which  is the basic condition for modern civilization.  [Translated from the Italian. |
208  The Chairman asked the Committee what action  should be taken in the matter: should the tele-  gram be merely filed or should it be communicated  to the Conference? The simplest solution would  perhaps be to authorize the President to transmit  it to the Italian delegation, which might inform  the Conference of its contents, without comment.  [8] Taha HUSSEIN Bey (Egypt) thought that as  the message merely claimed the right to free self-  determination—a right that it was Unesco's duty  to defend—there was no reason for withholding it  from the Conference.  [9] While agreeing with that view, the CHAIR-  MAN cautioned the Committee against the danger  of initiating a discussion incompatible with the  spirit of Unesco.  [10] Taha HUSSEIN Bey (Egypt) felt that Unesco  should do its duty, regardless of the consequences.  [11] In view of the political implications of the  message, Dr. BARALT Y ZACHARIE (Chairman,  Official and External Relations Commission) sug-  gested reading it at a subsequent plenary meeting  of the General Conference, without calling for any  decision on the matter.  [12] Dr. WALKER (Chairman, Nominations  Committee) thought that, as it was an appeal from  a minority group, Unesco could not be expected to  take any action, its duty being to publicize the  Universal Declaration of Human Rights and not to  ensure its observance—a matter which was the  responsibility of the United Nations.  [13] In support of that view, Sir John MAUD  (Chairman, Executive Board) quoted a passage  from the Constitution of Unesco:  With a view to preserving the independence, integrity  and fruitful diversity of the cultures and educational  systems of the States Members of this Organization,  the Organization is prohibited from intervening in  matters which are essentially within their domestic  jurisdiction.  [14] The CHAIRMAN observed that the Com-  mittee had three practical proposals before it:  merely to file the message (Dr. Walker's proposal) :  to bring it to the notice of the Conference and even  to permit its discussion (Taha Hussein Bey's pro-  posal); and lastly to read it to the Conference  without discussion (Dr. Baralt y Zacharie's pro-  posal). This latter proposal coincided more or less  with his own suggestion, which was to transmit  the telegram to the Conference through the Italian  delegation.  [15] Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom), refer-  ring to the precedent of the free Poles with their  headquarters in Paris, thought it possible to settle    the matter merely by acknowledging receipt of the  telegram, for it would be unwise to allow a dis-  cussion to take place that might give rise to  political disputes; all could be notified of the  acknowledgement of the telegram through notice  in the Journal of the Conference.  [16] The CHAIRMAN mentioned another prece-  dent—the appeal of the Sudan-Egyptian Com-  mittee, which had been ignored by the Beirut  General Conference on 13 December 1948.  [17] Mr. DELBOS (France) approved the solution  advocated by Mr. Hardman; care must be taken  to avoid a political debate which might be a  proper proceeding for the United Nations but  certainly not for Unesco.  [18] Taha HUSSEIN Bey (Egypt) said he would  prefer the acknowledgement of receipt to indicate  that the message had been transmitted to the  United Nations as the competent authority, in  order at least to give the impression that Unesco  was defending human rights.  [19] Dr. Walker's proposal, to which the latter  requested that Mr. Hardman’s suggestion should  be added (acknowledgement of the message  without placing it on the agenda of the Confer-  ence), was adopted by 6 votes.  It was decided that the Chairman should draft  the letter of acknowledgement.
