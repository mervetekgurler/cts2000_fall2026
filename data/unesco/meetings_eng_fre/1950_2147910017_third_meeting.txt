THIRD MEETING  Friday, 26 May 1950, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman:  Inclusion of a new question in the Agenda  of the General Conference (continuation)  [document 5C/20].  [1] The CHAIRMAN recalled that it was for the  General Committee to prepare a recommendation  for the General Conference in accordance with  Rules 14 and 36 of the Rules of Procedure.  [2] Mr. DELBOS (France) emphasized the  importance of the question and associated himself  in principle with the project. But he drew the  attention of the General Committee to Article XIII  of Unesco’s Constitution relating to proposed  amendments to the Constitution. He suggested  that the question be referred to the Procedure Com-  mittee, so that the latter might give its opinion on  the application of Article XIII to the present case.  Taha HUSSEIN Bey (Egypt) seconded this  proposal.  [3] Mr. DELBOS (France) was glad to note that  the general feeling was in favour of referring the  question to the Procedure Committee. He pointed  out the uselessness, and even danger, of beginning  a detailed discussion of the matter before the  Procedure Committee had taken a decision and  before the delegates had consulted their Govern-  ments.  [4] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL raised the ques-  tion whether, during the discussions of the Plen-  ary Conference on the inclusion of the question in  the Agenda, the substance of the matter could be  discussed or whether it should be postponed.  [6] Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom), seconded  by Mr. DELBOS (France), thought that substantial  discussion would be inopportune at the moment,  as delegates lacked the necessary information.  [6] Taha HUSSEIN Bey (Egypt) remarked that,  before informing the Plenary Conference of the  British draft, it would be necessary to scrutinize  the legal aspects of the problem and the conse-  quences of the proposed discrimination between  two kinds of Members.  [7] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL proposed that the  General Conference be informed of the inclusion in  Count STEFANO Jacint (Italy)  its agenda of the proposal submitted by the British  delegation. The General Committee would recom-  mend the Conference to refrain from a detailed  discussion and to refer the matter to the Procedure  Committee for legal advice.  [8] Dr. WALKER (Chairman, Nominations Com-  mittee) supported the Director-General’s proposal.  He also suggested that the Procedure Committee  should be invited to take a general decision on the  interpretation to be given to Article XIII, and  particularly to paragraph 2 of that Article.  [9] The proposals by the Director-General and  Dr. Walker were adopted.  Draft Amendment to Item 19 of the Agenda  presented by the Yugoslav delegation.  [10] The CHAIRMAN informed the General Com-  mittee that the Yugoslav delegation would like to  see the names of Descartes and Giordano Bruno  added to the list of personalities to be honoured  this year by the General Conference. In accordance  with Rule 36, paragraph 3, of the Rules of Pro-  cedure, it was for the General Committee to  examine such an amendment to an item included  in the Agenda of the Conference. [11] Dr. WALKER (Chairman, Nominations  Committee) pointed to the complications which  would ensue from this type of request, and foresaw  that in the years to come such difficulties would be  sure to grow. The meeting rose at 11 a.m.  FOURTH MEETING  Saturday, 27 May 1950, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman:  Organization of the work of the Session  (continuation).  [1] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL informed the Com-  mittee of his misgivings: whilst it was unlikely  that the vote on the budget ceiling would involve  lengthy discussion (since the amount of the budget  had been more or less stabilized), there was  nevertheless some uncertainty with regard to the  actual budget of the Organization, for account had  to be taken of the arrears of certain States in the  payment of their contributions. Until that vital  information was available, it would be impossible  to carry out serious work. The Administrative  Commission would need to settle this financial  problem before the Programme and Budget Com-  mission fixed the budget ceiling, especially as  certain delegations would certainly suggest some  curtailment of the 1951 programme in order to  finance the fifty-odd new proposals which would  mean a clear expansion of the programme.  It was decided to recommend to the Admini-  strative Commission to place the question of  financing the deficits at the head of its agenda.  [2] In reply to Dr. BENDER (Chairman, Budget  Committee), the DIRECTOR-GENERAL expressed  the opinion that the Administrative Commission’s  recommendations regarding the financing of the  deficit should not go before the Budget Committee  Count STEFANO Jacint (Italy)  before submission to the Programme and Budget  Commission.  [3] In accordance with the DIRECTOR-  GENERAL'S suggestions, it was decided to recom-  mend to the Programme and Budget Commission  to conclude its consideration of the Basic Pro-  gramme and the Statement of Methods before  embarking on the general discussion of the 1951  programme and budget; however, the Commission  should defer consideration of the section relating  to international conventions until the Procedure  Committee, which was then engaged on its discus-  sion of the use of Spanish as a working language,  could express its opinion.  [4] Dr. BENDER (Chairman, Budget Committee)  described the work of the Budget Committee,  which was to submit a provisional report to the  Programme and Budget Commission by 29 May;  this would include general considerations, followed  by annexes dealing with each of the chapters of the  programme. Addenda would be prepared after the  consideration of any fresh proposal that might be  submitted to the Committee for its views.
