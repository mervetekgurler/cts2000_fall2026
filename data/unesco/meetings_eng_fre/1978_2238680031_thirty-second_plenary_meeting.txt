2.1 Mr. DAYAL (India), Chairman of Programme Commission II: Thank you, Mr. President. May I take this opportunity of thanking the General Conference for the honour it did me in electing me as Chairman of Commission II, whose report I now submit.
2.2 During the deliberations of the Commission, we held several extensive and intensive discussions on various aspects of Unesco's science programme. The Commission devoted 19 meetings to this end, and the debate on the 13 objectives of the item concerning the Science Sector (item 12.2) was organ- ized in four discussion units (Units 8, 9, 10 and 11). The last three sessions were devoted to a
963    general debate on the overall programme of the Science Sector, and to examination of the general resolution 2/0.1. I should like to point out in this connection that during this fairly extensive examination of Unesco's programme and policy for the natural sciences, a large number of decisions had to be taken; all without exception were reached by consensus. It is indeed a very happy result that throughout this period it was never once necessary for the Commission to take a vote; this, I believe, is a tribute to the excellent spirit of understanding and co-operation which all delegates demonstrated in the execution of our numerous and complicated tasks.
2.3 Mr. President, I will very briefly touch on some of the objectives which were discussed in connection with the science programme. The discussion on Unit 9 threw into relief Unesco's contri- bution to the United Nations Conference on Science and Technology for Development (UNCSTD), and the new proposals for the contribution of the Science Sector to Unesco's programme for integrated rural development. Various proposals concerning the future activities of the Organization were presented: in this connection the themes most frequently mentioned were those relating to energy, informatics and technology for rural development.
2.4 Discussion Unit 10 dealt with Objectives 7.1, 7.3 and 7.4 (mineral and energy resources; water resources; and ocean and coastal marine systems). Among the recommendations of the Commission concerning these objectives there is a proposal for amendment of certain Articles of the Statutes of the International Geological Correlation Programme (IGCP) Board. The Commission called for a feasibility study of new modalities of international co-operation in the field of natural hazards; and also examined proposals for the planning of the second phase of the International Hydrological Pro- gramme (IHP). Measures for the reinforcement of the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission and related activities in the marine sciences are also recommended.
2.5 Discussion Unit 11 dealt with four objectives covering different aspects of the Man and the Biosphere Programme (7.2: terrestrial and biological resources; 7.5: environment and human settle- ments; 7.6: preservation and presentation of the cultural and natural heritage; and 7.7: education and information relating to the environment). These subjects also attracted a great deal of attention.
2.6 The Commission's debate on the general orientation of the programme for the natural sciences was very extensive, and I believe, extremely important for the future development of this programme. Moreover, the debate was underscored by a very deep concern for the role of the sciences within Unesco's programme as a whole. Many delegates stressed that Unesco should accord adequate priority to its scientific and technological programme, in conformity with its leading role in the international community in the field of sciences. In this connection, several suggestions were made with a view to strengthening the science programme and the provision of additional resources in order to facilitate its implementation.
2.7 Mr. President, the extremely rich, lively and constructive discussions in Commission II have led to recommendations which will be of extreme importance in the discussion and finalization of the programmes of the Organization in the coming biennium, as well as in future biennia. These dis- cussions, as I have said, took place in an excellent spirit, and the scientific standing of the delegates, as well as the brilliance of their interventions, have been adequately demonstrated. I feel that the Commission's report, which has also been accepted in the same spirit of unanimity, may now be adopted by the plenary of the General Conference. You have already referred to the documents, Mr. President, and I now commend them to the Conference for its attention. Thank you, Mr. President

30.1 Mr. GARBO (Norway), Chairman of the Drafting and Negotiation Group: Thank you, Mr. President. In my capacity as Chairman of the Drafting and Negotiation Group, I have the honour to set before the General Conference draft resolution 20 C/PLEN/DR.17, submitted in connection with item 17 of the agenda (Cultural and scientific co-operation on the basis of mutual equality and interest as an important factor in strengthening peace, friendship and reciprocal under- standing among peoples).
30.2 You may recall, Mr. President, that item 17 was initially assigned to Programme Commission V, and that under that item the Commission received draft resolution 20 C/PRG.V/DR.5, submitted by the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics and several other Member States. The Commission decided, however, to close the debate on the matter before it had really started, and requested the General Committee of the Conference to transmit responsibility for item 17, together with the draft resolution to which I have referred, to the Drafting and Negotiation Group.
30.3 Mr. President, the Drafting and Negotiation Group felt somewhat uneasy in taking up a draft resolution which had not been the subject of thorough discussion in one of the major organs of the General Conference; and its work would have been greatly facilitated if such a debate had taken place. Nevertheless, we did our best to negotiate among ourselves, and a working group under the chairmanship of Mr. Gopal of India succeeded in preparing a draft which formed the basis for a broad agreement within the Drafting and Negotiation Group, the text of which is now before you.
30.4 Mr. President, careful comparison of draft resolutions 20 C/PRG.V/DR.5 and 20 C/PLEN/DR.17 will show that most of the elements contained in the former have been retained in the latter. Two matters which gave rise to lengthy debate were firstly the reaction of delegates of the developing countries, who considered that the first draft did not present clearly enough their views and specific problems, and that an effort should be made to broaden the draft in that direction; and secondly the statements by other delegates that some parts of the draft gave too much weight to possible intervention by governments in communication matters, and that the text should therefore be modified to take account of the specific situation of countries which do not have any machinery to control the information media, and whose law and public opinion are opposed to such control.
30.5 As it stands, the text before you is the result of a compromise between various tendencies. The Drafting and Negotiation Group as a whole did not, for instance, find it imperative to repeat the notion of free exchange which is already established in the Constitution of Unesco. Instead, we talk in the tenth preambular paragraph about "exchanging ideas, knowledge and other cultural achieve- ments in a spirit of open-mindedness and on a footing of equality, in the common interest of all". This seems to be an expression of the same basic notion, without using words which unfortunately in certain contexts have become politically controversial.
30.6 Paragraph 1 of part II of the draft resolution created some difficulty, as some members felt that the call for discouragement of the propagation of the notions of hatred, etc., might be used as a pretext for engaging governments in actions contrary to the freedom of expression. However, as it stands the paragraph is not adverse to governments. It stresses respect for human rights, and seeks to strike a balance implying that the propagation of inhuman notions should be counteracted with means which do not infringe upon the free expression of opinions and ideas.
30.7 I must add that one member of the Group specifically regretted that the word "détente" still appears in the text in the way in which it has been used in the last preambular paragraph.
30.8 Mr. President, the draft resolution before you has no title of its own. It is submitted under reference to item 17 of the agenda of the General Conference. The present text is, however, certainly wider than the original draft resolution sent to us from Commission V. It does not limit itself to cultural and scientific co-operation in a restrictive sense, but explicitly refers even to education, communication and social sciences. That is why we have emphasized that the preparation of a study on international cultural co-operation which is called for in paragraph 1 of part III should be carried out "in the light of this resolution". It should take into consideration the whole field of competence of Unesco. Let me also stress that we explictly invite the Director-General to "initiate" the prepara- tion of this international study. No additional financial expenses are therefore foreseen in the course of the coming biennium.
30.9 Some delegations may perhaps feel that the efforts of the Drafting and Negotiation Group have, in certain regards, weakened the original text. I do, however, think, that the present draft, which reiterates our commitment to the Declaration of the Principles of International Cultural Co-operation adopted by Unesco in 1966, constitutes a useful reminder and follow-up of that Declaration. The draft resolution is inspired by the conviction that international cultural co-operation in the broadest sense of the term contributes to the strengthening of peace, friendship and understanding among peoples. This conviction is indeed one of the main foundations upon which Unesco has been built.
30.10 Mr. President, the Drafting and Negotiation Group has, as it is bound to do, sought to reach consensus on the draft resolution which is before you. Given the controversy which was caused in Programme Commission V by the original draft, this seemed at the outset to be no easy task. I was informed today that unfortunately one Member State might not be able to vote in favour of the pro- posed text. However, Mr. President, I do hope that in its present form the draft resolution will meet with the broad agreement of all the interested parties, as it certainly did in the Drafting and Negotiation Group, and that it will therefore be adopted in the plenary by consensus. Thank you, Mr. President.
