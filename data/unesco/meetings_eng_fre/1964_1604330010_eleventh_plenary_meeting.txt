2.2 Voici le texte du rapport du Comité ;  "1. Le Comité a examiné les projets d'amendement figurant à l'Annexe Il du document
13 C/27 Addendum. Il a entendu, à cet égard, le Président de la Commission finan-  cière et administrative du Conseil exécutif, qui lui a fait part des débats qui se sont  déroulés sur cette question, tant au sein de cette Commission qu'au Conseil exécutif  lui-même.
2, Le Comité a constaté que le projet de résolution À tendait à ce que l'ensemble des  opérations relatives au vote et au dépouillement des bulletins soient effectuées dans  une salle distincte, pendant que la Conférence générale poursuivrait l'examen d'autres  questions inscrites à son ordre du jour. Le projet de résolution B, par contre, tendait  à ce que le scrutin même et la première vérification, par les scrutateurs, du nombre  d'enveloppes déposées dans l'urne (articles 1 à 10 du Règlement sur les élections au  scrutin secret), soient effectués en séance plénière et dans la salle de la Conférence  générale, alors que l'ouverture des enveloppes, la lecture des noms et le dénombre-  ment des voix (articles 11 à 14) auraient lieu dans une autre salle, de telle manière  que la séance plénière puisse poursuivre ses travaux pendant ses opérations.
3. Le Comité a estimé que l'une ou l'autre de ces deux procédures, si elle était 1ete-  nue, aurait pour effet de permettre une économie de temps appréciable dans les travaux  de la Conférence générale. La première solution (projet de résolution A), si elle devait  permettre une procédure plus expéditive que la seconde (projet de résolution B), a paru  néanmoins au Comité ne pas être entièrement exempte d'inconvénients, tout en étant  susceptible d'apparaftre à certains comme n'apportant pas toutes les garanties offertes  par la procédure suivie jusqu'ici,
4. Le Comité a, en conséquence, exprimé une préférence pour la solution proposée  dans le projet de résolution B, tout en lui apportant divers aménagements.
5. Le Comité a estimé, en particulier, que si cette solution devait être retenue, elle  devrait être incorporée dans le Règlement sur les élections au scrutin secret, de ma-  nière telle que la procédure proposée n'ait pas à faire l'objet d'une décision expresse  de la part de la Conlérence générale à chacune de ses sessions et qu'elle soit automa-  tiquement applicable.
6. Le Comité a estimé également que les nouvelles dispositions devraient indiquer  d'une manière expresse que les opérations qui se dérouleraient dans une salle autre  que celle des séances plénières de la Conférence générale pourraient être observées  par les délégations qui le désireraient.
7. Le Comité a considéré, enfin, que les nouvelles dispositions trouveraient mieux  leur place à la suite de l'article 10 du Règlement actuel plutôt qu'après l'article 11,  les opérations visées à l'article 11 devant être effectuées selon la nouvelle procédure.
8. Compte tenu de ces modifications, le Comité juridique, à l'unanimité, recommande  à la Conférence générale d'adopter le projet de résolution figurant en annexe au pré-  sent rapport.

(4) Le PRESIDENT. - (Traduit du russe. )  Nous reprenons la discussion générale. Je donne la parole à M. Lund, président de la Com-  mission nationale suédoise pour l'Unesco, chef de la délégation suédoise.
5.1 Mr, LUND (Sweden):  Mr. President, whom we are all very happy to see in the Chair at this General Con-  ference, like many other speakers at this rostrum, I should like to pay tribute to all those  who have composed the document now under debate - the Director-General, the Directors  of Departments - but also to those many hundreds who work here for Unesco and whom we  never see in this hall.
5.2 We are meeting at this General Conference at a time when Unesco 2nd the other Spe-  cialized Agencies of the United Nations are in a period of rapid increase of their activities.  This increase, of course, should be judged in the context of the importance which the inter-  national organizations are assuming in the field of assistance to the less developed coun-  tries: The Swedish Government and the Swedish people welcome this development and are  giving their support to the assistance activities carried on by the Specialized Agencies, our  prasent concern being with those of Unesco. There are, however, certain problems in-  herent in a rapid expansion, unless it is preceded by careful long-term planning and syste-  maiic efforts at co-ordination. With your permission, Mr. President, I shall deal with  some of these.
5,3 First, I should like to elaborate very briefly the background of the Swedish positive  attitude towards, and support of, an international organization like Unesco, and I refer now  both to its operational activities in assisting the less developed countries and to Unesco's  more “traditional tasks as an instrument for collaboration in the fields of education, science  and culture, both between developed countries and less developed countries, Both types of  activities are in reality different aspects of one and the same thing, because the ultimate  goal of all our endeavours in this field is that the less developed countries should take part  in intellectual co-operation with the developed countries on a truly equal footing,
5.4 We, inSweden, as you all well know are strongly in favour of multilateral projects. A number  of political and practical factors combine to motivate the primary importance which we have accor -  ded to the multilateral rather than the bilateral approach in our assistance policy. 1 need  not point out in detail how often bad feeling on the part of the recipient may be avoided by  approaching a country through an international organization. Such an approach is not met  with the suspicion that there is some more or less hidden motive or intention to achieve  something other than helping the country in question to solve its problems. A further prac-  tical factor is that a small country like Sweden has strictly limited financial, administrative  and manpower resources and therefore it seems quite natural for us to channel as much as  possible of our aid through international organizations. ‘Fhus, of the total Swedish assis-  tance, approximately one half is directed through multilateral channels, whereas a very  rough estimate of the global figure would show a proportion between multilateral -bilateral  programmes of 1 to 10. As you know, it is very easy to channel bilateral assistance through  multilateral organs. Sweden has given a sum to Unesco and, in agreement with Unesco, has  earmarked it for the education of young women in Africa; this machinery has worked very  well, Therefore, though we realize that the reasons for our emphasis on the multilateral  approach may not be valid for all countries, we strongly support an expansion of this work  of Unesco as a truly international organization.
5.5 That is why we are not in agreement with those who think that the rapid growth in pro-  grammes and activities that has taken place in the Specialized Agencies, among them Unesco,  is an alarming development. On the contrary, we regard this as positive. In the discussion  now going on concerning the possibility of introducing some form of “controlled expansion",  we want to stress the word "expansion", with due'regard to the need for control and co-  ordination. It does not seem advisable, as has been suggested, to establish a fixed percen-  tage as some sort of ceiling for an increase in activities. The pace of growth ought not to  be fixed by rules established in advance; this pace must be determined by the content and  aims of the activity, and these may vary from time to time. The guiding line for our posi-  tive positions in Unesco, as in the other Specialized Agencies, has been to accept and sup-  port projects which appear to be sound and well conceived. Rather than a fixed percentage,  sound and well conceived activities which seem most likely to lead to positive results for  the less developed countries, and for other tasks that Unesco has to fulfil, ought to set the  scope of the budget.
5.6 In this context, I should like to say only a word or two about the curious method we use  in Unesco when deciding our financial budget. We start our work by fixing a budget ceiling  before we go into detail and take a stand on what we want to accomplish through our Organi-  zation during the next two years. Once this budget ceiling has been decided upon, all tasks  have to be pressed into the economic framework thus available. Any new proposal having  budgetary implications can be carried out only at the cost of dropping some other project  that may be as well founded. The notion of a budget ceiling implies the possibility that,  when outlining the programme, the Programme Commission might decide upon a pro-  gramme that costs less than the budget ceiling agreed upon, although I must admit that this  is most uniikely.
5.7 I do not know whather it has occurredto y u that this is a very curious method, indeed,  which - as fav as I knew - is not used in any other parliament in the world, nor is it prac-  tised in any other United Nations Specialized Agency. On the contrary, budgetary problems  are approached in a more reasonable way in other organs. The method used by Unesco
301   XI Plenary meetings / Séances plénières  amounts, in effect, to a declaration of tutelage; you place yourself under a tutor who sets  an unjustified limit to the initiatives of the delegations, however brilliant and constructive  those initiatives may be.
5.8 I do not intend to go into the reasons why this has come to be as it is. The method we  use does not contribute to creating a sense of responsibility amongst the delegates. If we  believe in education and the possibilities of educating ourselves and others, we should not  expose ourselves to budgetary methods that do not foster responsibility and do not promote  education for respunsibility. In any case, my delegation cannot accept the point of view  that the delegates to Unesco’s General Conference should behave in a less responsible way  than do the delegates to the other Specialized Agencies. Indeed, in many cases they are  the same persons. There they are empowered to use their brains and judgement, but here  we must accept a budget without having taken a stand as to the content of the programme.
5.9 I do not intend to propose any immediate action on this point. My only proposal is that  we should all, including the Executive Board and the Secretariat, think over the problem.  To my mind, our thinking should be along the lines that a humiliating procedure, not  deemed necessary in the other Specialized Agencies nor in any parliament in the world,  should not be used in the world parliament for education, science and culture.
5.10 The positive approach we in Sweden take to the international activities of Unesco does  not mean that we deem it unnecessary to follow with searching and critical eyes the imple-  mentation of the different decisions. But, precisely because of this positive attitude we are  anxious to safeguard the efficiency of the work. Every endeavour to simplify, concentrate  and rationalize Unesco's activities must be applied and will be supported by us. I can be  brief on this point, by associating myself with the statement of the Head of the Finnish  delegation, H.E. Mr. Saukkonen, in this connexion in the general debate a few days ago.
5.11 Since we must foresee an expansion of Unesco's budget, as in that of the other inter-  national organizations, it is obvious that this question of control of implementation and  safeguarding of efficiency will receive greater attention in proportion to the expanding  budgets in all contributing countries on the part of their governments, their parliaments  and peoples. And rightly so. The question is whether the present machinery of control in  Unesco provides sufficient independent information, independent, that is to say, of the Sec-  retariat, the Director-General and the Executive Board. These are the institutions to be  controlled. * Public opinion in many countries feels the necessity of having the opinion of an  independent body as to the effectiveness of the work and this need is the more strongly felt  as such countries are prepared to invest more money in Unesco and other international  bodies. In many countries, parliaments have set up special controlling and supervising  agencies which, on behalf of the parliament, control the administrative and governmental  activities and report to the parliament. I can testify from my own experience that this sys-  tem has contributed very effectively to the efficiency of the work of the administrative  bodies in our country. Do we need something of the same kind in Unesco? The need is felt  in many circles. Suggestions have been made that the main contributor States should estab-  lish some control of their own. But it would seem more appropriate that the General Con-  ference set up a small body with its own independent secretariat for continuous control of  operational, as well as headquarters activities and the implementation of the budget, which  body should report on its findings to the General Conference. I do not intend to present a  formal proposal here. I offer’ this as a suggestion to be discussed in appropriate circles  and I recommend that a study be made of the work of the committee on budgetary matters  in the United Nations.
5.12 In many countries a system of long-range forward planning is being increasingly app-  lied. Something of that kind has been in force in Unesco but, nevertheless, a greater de-  gree of forward planning would seem to be desirable. The basis for this planning should be  the course of expenditure likely to result from the continuation of existing policies and pro-  grammes, subject to continuous review of the need for them. In drawing up the forward  plans, consideration should be given to the needs and expressed wishes of the developing
302   Sesiones plenurias. Hicxapuue saccdauus XI  countries, to the capacity of the Organization's staff to carry out the programme cffectively,  and to the financial resources,
5.13 I shall now comment on some of the chapters in the programme, taking up some points  that are, or rather are not to be found there. The Declaration of Human Rights states that  everyone has the right to education as a prerequisite for a dignified human life and in order  to enable him to make an effective contribution to society, We all agree that in the endea-  vour to reach this goal no nation and no group of people should be excluded. But there is  one group to which no attention, or very little, has been paid in Unesco's educational acti-  vities so far, I refer to those who are handicapped, deaf, blind, cercbro-palsied, mentally  retarded and similarly handicapped. They should not be excluded from enjoying the human  rights. Even in countries with good educational systems they ure, however, very often left  aside. Difficulties arise at the very beginning, in the transport of handicapped children to  and from school. For children who are not handicapped very good and effective transport  is provided in many countries, but those who are handicapped must very often stay at home  or are secluded in asylums where they are considered more as children in need of treat-  ment than of education. The schools in many countries are quite often so constructed as to  exclude handicapped pupils. This applies equally to furniture, teaching material and so on.  It would seem that our constructive imagination does not suffice to put all groups of school-  children into the picture. What I have said applies algo to teacher training. And most im-  portant of all: children, teachers, none of us, learn to live and get on with handicapped  persons in a natural way; we are apt rather to look upon them benevolently, condescendingly  and sentimentally.
5.14 We have learned a slogan nowadays: "education pays" both for the individual end for  the community. I do not wish to stress this point too much, for after all we do not live in  this the best of worlds only to be trained to fulfil some task in the process of production.  But if education pays for all others, it pays doubly for those who are handicapped. Instead  of being a burden to society, given a proper education and training, they could contribute  effectively to the common good. 1 agree that what I have just now hinted at is a new and far-  reaching idea and programme for Unesco, and I must say that so far the Director-General  and the Secretariat have taken a rather lukewarm attitude towards the whole problem. When  we were invited to present suggestions with regard to the forthcoming programme and bud-  get, Sweden, in due time, sent in a proposal for inclusion in the programme to be presented  to this Conference dealing with the question of the handicapped. But when we received a  copy of the proposed programme and budget we found not a single word about this problem.  The Director-General, obviously, does not find the question worth mentioning. Therefore  we have presented a draft resolution, co-sponsored by the Nordic countries, to the same  effect (document 13 C/8 Add.I). I am also happy to say that Madagascar has presented a  draft resolution pertaining to some of the problems dealt with in our draft. I shall not  comment in detail here on the resolution; we intend to fight for it in the Programme Com-  mission. But I wish to state here that the hesitant attitude taken by the Director-General  in his comments and the obstacles he has built up do not do him credit. Let us establish  here at this General Conference a Magna Carta for those who are handicapped and their  right to education. That would be the only rightful decision of an assembly composed of  delegates who are not handicapped, showing generosity to those who are not so fortunate.
5.15 I shall touch briefly on only two more points, Mr. President, both referring to pro-  gramme activities to which Sweden and the other Nordic countries have always attached  great importance. The question of the rights of women in the educational field and in other  fields is a common concern of all our countries. As I mentioned before, Swedish bilateral  aseistance to Africa is channelled through Unesco to women's education. The same applies  to some Danish projects in Nigeria and Kenya. But apart from this and some other projects,  it is our impression that, although we all agree that in the present stage emphasis should be  put on educational activities for women in the developing countries, little happens in the  practical implementation of this directive. We experience this in our country when we pro-  vide scholarships for students from less developed countries, for seldom do we get women
303   XI Plenary meetings / Séances plénières  students. This is a question for the Secretariat to think of and act upon, More initiatives  must be taken in this field and higher sums might be allocated, for example, for study tours  for women.
5.16 In the field of adult education we find the expansion rather slow; we are still far from  reaching the goals that were set at the Adult Education Conference in Montreal. 1 recom-  mend to my fellow delegates that they study the draft resolution on adult education presen-  ted by the five Nordic countries - Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Norway and Sweden, May I  mention that we are especially happy to have Iceland in our Nordic group at this General  Conference.
5.17 My criticisms of the adult education programme apply also to the programme for  workcrs' travel, now reduced to a minimum, which we in the Nordic countries find quite  unacceptable. One might speculate on the reasons for the negative stand taken by the Sec -  retariat and the General Conference on the participation of workers in Unesco activities.  Perhaps one of the former Directors-General, Mr. Torres Bodet, who took the initiative  in bringing workers into Unesco activities, was right in foreseeing a risk that one day  Unesco might deviate into becoming a mere international association of professors, not having  the broad masses in mind, and forgetting that after all it is the broad masses that provide the  money to Unesco, and that Unesco cannot survive if it fails to win confidence among the  rank and file of the different countries.
5.18 These, Mr. President, are some of the suggestions I wish to put forward for the con-  sideration of the Director-General, the Secretariat and the Programme Commission. You  will note, Mr. President, that even our critical observations stem from a positive approach  towards international collaboration and institutions. Sweden is, as you will have under-  stood, strongly in favour of peaceful co-operation between countries, an: we are fully pre-  pared to pay our part of the budget of Unesco as of other international organizations. As  long as we expand, deepen and broaden international organizations and co-operation, we  align ourselves against wars, and for every country wars entail the most dreadful conse-  quences, in comparison with which'budgetary implications are the very least of our consi-  derations.

Tandis que le fameux livre gris, que vous connaissez tous, Mesdames et Messieurs, avait  tendance, ces dernières années, à morceler les tâches de l'Unesco en une multitude de pro-  jets particuliers, le Projet de programme et de budget qui nous est soumis maintenant est  caractérisé par la concentration des divers points du Programme, l'accent étant mis sur leu  tâches prioritaires et sur le problème de l'équilibre du Programme.

7.3 Après ces remarques préliminaires, permettez-moi, Mesdames et Messieurs, d'en-  trer maintenant dans quelques détails du nouveau Programme, La République fédérale d'AI-  lemagne approuve le projet du Directeur général de transformer le Département de l'éduca-  tion en une sous-direction générale de l'éducation, qui comprendra désormais le Départe-  ment de l'enseignement scolaire et supérieur ainsi que le Département de l'éducation des  adultes et des actlvités de jeunesse, Nous nous félicitons, en particulier, de ce que cette  nouvelle structure du Département de l'éducation favorise la planification de l'éducation et  resserre les liens entre l'Institut international de planification de l'éducation et le Secré-  tariat de l'Unesco,

Nous nous demandons cependant si, en revanche, les études sur le contenu et le but de  l'éducation, ainsi que la promotion de la recherche pédagogique et psychopédagogique, n'ont  pas tendance à passer au second plan. Nous croyons en voir un indice dans le fait que les  efforts réclamés par le Conseil exécutif en vue de lutter contre la discrimination dans l'en-  seignement et en faveur de l'éducation pour la coopération internationale figurent au dernier  rang des directives générales du projet de la sous-direction de l'éducation. Nous formulons  aussi des réserves au sujet de l'opinion selon laquelle l'éducation ne serait qu'une introduc-  tion à la vie intellectuelle, dont les sciences exactes et naturelles seraient aujourd'hui le  moteur proprement dit. Ce point de vue nous paraît dangereux, en ce sens que l'éducation  considérée ainsi comme une affaire essentiellement technique est coupée de son contenu et  de son objectif.
7.5 11 est vrai que le Directeur général lui-même déplore les éventuelles conséquences né-  gatives de cette conception en un point du Programme où il signale la disproportion entre  les mesures opérationnelles de l'aide à l'éducation et Jes projets intellectuels de recherche  inscrits au programme de la sous-direction de l'éducation. À ce propos, nous exprimons  l'espoir que l'Institut de l'Unesco pour l'éducation, à Hembourg, enrichira sensiblement  le programme de l'Unesco dans le domaine des sciences pédagogiques comparées, Nous  espérons aussi qu'au cours de la présente session la Conférence générale approuvera les  propositions du Directeur géréral contenues dans le Rapport sur les Instituts de l'Unesco  pour l'éducation et pour la jeunesse, Je pense, à ce propos, que mon Gouvernement appor-  tera la contribution que l'on attend de lui pour le maintien de l'Institut pour l'éducation.
7.6 En ce qui concerne le programme de sciences exactes et naturelles, la République fé-  dérale d'Allemagne se rallie, en principe, à l'idée selon laquelle il convient de réserver  davantage de place aux sciences exactes et naturelles dans le Programme de l'Unesco, en  raison de l'importance croissante de la science et de la technique pour le relèvement du  niveau de vie de certains pays et de certaines régions, Cependant, on ne pourra parvenir  à ce but en faisant de la sous-direction des sciences exactes et naturelles, au Secrétariat  de l'Unesco, un appareil administratif de grande envergure, chargé non seulement de pour-  suivre les projets en cours depuis des années, mais aussi d'organigeer nombre de tâches  nouvelles, notamment l'aide aux pays en voie de développement, C'est pourquoi, au sein  des commissions et comités compétents, nous ferons des propositions relatives à l'arti-  culation de la nouvelle sous-direction des sciences exactes et naturelles. Je me contente-  rai de noter ici que seule une concentration du Programme, par la suppression des divers  projèts particuliers, accompagnée de la limitation des tâches administratives et d'une col-  laboration plus étroite avec les organisations internationales non gouvernementales, paraft  justifier une majoration de 57 % du budget de cette sous-direction. (9.1) M, MINKOVITCH (RSS de Biélorussie). - (Traduit du russe.)  Permettez-moi, Monsieur le Président, de vous féliciter de tout coeur de votre élection au  poste de président de la Conférence générale dé l'Unesco et de vous souhaiter le plus grand suc-  cès dans l'exercice de vos hautes fonctions.
(9.2) Mesdames, Messieurs, il y a deux ans que s'est tenue dans ce même batiment la douzième  session de la Conférence générale de l'Unesco. Que d'événements importants se sont produits  pendant cette courte période de deux ang ! La situation internationale s'est améliorée ; les forces  puissantes qui s'opposent à l'impérialisme et au colonialisme et contribuent à consolider la paix  et la sécurité générales ont intensifié leur action,
(9,3) Le Conssil exécutif de l'Unesco, exprimant la volonté de la majorité des Etats membrus  de l'Organisation, a adopté à sa 66e session une résolution rappelant, une fois da plus, que la  tâche fondamentale de l'Unesco, définie par son Acte constitutif, est "de contribuer au maintien  de la paix et de la sécurité en resserrant, par l'éducation, la science et la culture, la collabo-  ration entre nations'’. Plusieurs autres résolutions de la Conférence générale et du Conseil exé-  eutif soulignent la nécessité d'accroftre la contribution de l'Unesco À la cause de la paix, de la  coexistence pacifique et de la coopération entre les nations,
(9.4) Malgré l'oeuvre positive accomplie par le Secrétariat de l'Unesco, force est de constater  qu'entre ces résolutions et le programme de travail concret de l'Unesco pour 1965-1966 il sub-  siste le même fossé qu'autrefois. (9.6) Un autre problème essentiel de notre époque est celui de 1'éjimination rapide et définitive  du colonialisme et de ses séquelles.
