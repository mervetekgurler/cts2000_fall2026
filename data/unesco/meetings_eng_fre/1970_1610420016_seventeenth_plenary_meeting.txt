1,. The PRESIDENT:  The meeting is called to order. May I invite H,E. Dr, Escudero, Minister of Educa-  tion and leader of the delegation of Ecuador, to take the floor, .
(2,1) Mr, ESCUDERO (Ecuador) (Translation from the Spanish):  Mr. President, I should like to express my sincere congratulations and good wishes to .the  illustrious President of this sixteenth session of the General Conference of Unesco, whose high  prestige matches his moral and intellectual qualities which redound to thé credit of his homeland  of Argentina and hence to the great Latin American and Caribbean homeland, which is honoured  by his election.
(2.2) I also wish to congratulate all the high officers of the Conference whose concerted efforts  will assist us to achieve what we all desire for this great institution,
(2.3) In ancient Greece, whose wisdom has come down to us through the ages, the philosopher  Protagoras stated that man was the measure of all things, .of those which were because they were  and of those which were not because they were not. By that profound thought, he meant that man  was the great protagonist in the world, and at the same time he was referring to the universality  of the human condition,
(2.4) This thought gives us to reflect on the whole man, a being of flesh and blood at the apex of  creation, whose claim to supremacy resides essentially in his mind. Thus, all the civilizingand  creative labour of our time should be aimed at perfecting his mind through culture in the widest  meaning of the term and by all economic, educational, artistic, scientific and technical means,
(2.5) After the tragedy of a world-wide conflagration, the United Nations was created in 1945 to  build upon a firm foundation the indivisible pcacé and security of the nations, In the Preamble to  its Charter, the founder countries reaffirmed their faith in fundamental human rights, in the  dignity and worth of the human person and in the equal rights of men and nations, and undertook  to promote social progress and better standards of life in larger freedom, In Chapter IX, a dyna-  mic standard for international co-operation in cultural and educational activities was set and pro-  vision made for the creation of the Specialized Agencies, including an agency responsible for  cultural and educational activities.
(2.6) The United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization thus came into being  under the sheltering wing of the United Nations as one of its Specialized Agencies, Its Constitution was  adopted in London on 16 November 1945 and has been modified and improved upon to keep it in  line with changing needs.
(2.7) But its origins are to be found earlier in the Committee on Intellectual Co-operation of the  former League of Nations whose first tentative efforts foreshadowed the future development of an  immense effort to redeem men from their cultural poverty.  {2. 8) And thus through a quarter of a century the joint efforts of the United Nations, sustained by  a vigorous and increasingly resolute will in our international institution, have strengthered its struc-  ture, widened its horizons and perspectives and reflected more closely the philosophy which pre-  sided at its conception and creation, Looking back on the path of progress covered, we can see  that what has been done is greater than what has been left undone.
(2.9) It ehould, however, be noted that humanity is now living through one of the most convulsive  periods of its history: not only is the explosive rate of demographic growth outstripping the rate  of increase in output, particularly in the developing countries, but conflicts of political doctrine  are becoming increasingly acute and bitter, and local wars and local tensions, which could spark  off another world conflict continue; such a conflict this time would mean the extermination of hun  dreds of millions and the ruin of our civilization if nuclear or thermonuclear weapons were used,  for the splitting of the atom, discovered by man and applied to warlike ends, may be compared  to the fire which Prometheus stole from the gods and by which he himself perished.
(2.10) We should also note that all the various forms of imperialism are still alive in the world  today, from the imperialism which imposes political submission on weaker countries to the cul-  tural imperialism which seeks to impose the model of the developed nations on nations having  diverse and original cultures of their own, even going as far as linguistic imperialism - thus I  see with sadness how my own Spanish language is gradually losing its glorious purity through the  corrupting introduction of foreign terms, and this in spite of the wealth of synonymous Castilian  terms,
(2,11) To complete this tragic picture, the gap is steadily widening between the rich countries,  which are becoming richer, and the poor countries, which are becoming poorer because of the  flagrant injustice of international trade, in which the absurd rule prevails that exorbitant prices  are charged for industrial products exported by the big countries whilst miserable prices are  paid for basic products and raw materials exported by the medium and small countries,
17  {2.12) What ie Unesco's mission in this torn, divided and unjust world? In the firat plade, strong  links must be established between education, science and culture on the one hand and world peace  on the other, Under the auspices of the former Committee on Intellectual Co-opération a memo-  rable dialogué was héld by correspondence onthe theme "Why war?" between some of the most  distinguished representatives of world culture, and I recall In particular the letters exchanged  between two geniuses of the time, Albert Einstein and Sigmund Freud. In reply to the question  masterfully formulated by Einstein, Freud affirmed that wars would continue to break out so long  as the destructive instinct remained deep in the conscious and sub-conscious mind, and that only  through a long drawn out éducational process covering many generations would it be possible to  root out this instinct for cruelty, destruction and death,
(2.13) Our Organization has resolutely undertaken the task of investigating the sociological and  psychological aspects of aggressivity, and the enormous range of its activities is mainly directed,  through the agency of education, science and culture, to establishing the foundations of world peace.  It is not for nothing that the admirable axiom "since wars begin in the minds of men, it 18 in the  minds of men that the defences of peace must be constructed’ appears in the Preamble to its  Constitution.
(2. 14) 1 theréfore belleve that Unesco's rôle in the building of peace is more efféctive than all  the mechanisms and agreements for international disarmament, since it ls essential for this pur-  pose that the minds of men should first be disarmed, freed of their aggressive instincts,
(2,15) The establishment of peace does not, of course, depend on efforts made in the fields of  education, sclence and culture alone, for world peace depends on justice among nations just as  internal peace within nations depends on soclal justice and the recognition of the rights and obli-  gations of all citizens.
(2. 16) The triune influence of education, science and culture is of fundamental importance inpro-  moting economic and social development; conversely, development is a prerequisite for the ad-  vancement of education, science and culture, since these three fields and development are paral-  lel and related concepts.
(2.17) I have endeavoured to draw a rapid outline of Unésco's philosophy, but I should also like to  express certain hopes, for example that Unesco will simplify its methods and procedures with a  view to rendering its activities more direct and effective, for I think that many of them are so  complex as to be fully comprehensible only to experts with a complete mastery of this new and  esoteric science. '
(2.18) The delegation of Ecuador, together with the delegation of Venezuela, has submitted an  important draft resolution, contained in document 16 C/DR, 103, for the consideration of the Gen-  eral Conference, in which the need for the completest mutual knowledge among nationa, on which  depends their mutual understanding and indivisible solidarity which in turn form the basis of their  joint co-operation is indicated as a basic consideration, It is suggested in this draft resolution  that the General Conference recommend that the governments of those nations which are Member  States of Unesco and which have reached a high degree of development should revise and improve  the teaching of the geography and history of the developing countries, and that the governments of  “ the latter be urged to do their utmost to make known their history and geography as widely as pos-  sible and in general the specific conditions prevailing in each of them to as many countries of the  world as possible,
(2.19) In the same draft resolution, the governing bodies of the press, radio, television and other  mags communication media of the developed nations are invited to draw upon authentic sources of  information and render in their news and publications a faithful account of the developing countries
80 as to give a faithful picture of them and reveal the efforts made by them to overcome their under -  development, As to this last part of the proposal, I must point out that the news and information  transmitted by the mass communication media, which are controlled by business in the major  countries, contain their own form of imperialism - and often give a distorted account of events  and conditions in the medium and small countries, causing them irreparable harm and damaging  the understanding and spirit of harmony which should prevail between the former and the latter  countries,
(2.20) I wish to reaffirm my support for the plan drawn up by 16 Latin American countries, includ -  ing my own country, the Republic of Ecuador, concerning the progressive stages of cultural inte-  gration in Latin America, and including the sub-reglonal cultural activities of the Andean group  referred to in the "Andrés Bello Agreement" whose title further honours the renowned Venezuelan  Latin American humanist,
(2.21) I support in general the draft resolution submitted by the Group of 77 as its basic contri  bution to the United Nations Second Development Decade, Lo ;
(2.22) Regarding the Executive Board of Unesco, I would Venture to criticize ‘the infquitous dis-  tribution of Its 34 seats among the 5’electoral groups, for it violates the first of the principles  enshrir d fn Article 2 of tli¢ United Nations Charter, the principle of the sovereign equality of  all Stat: s, It would perhaps be advisable to increase the representation of Groups III (Latin ‘  America and the Caribbean), IV and V on the Board by one representative each in order tocomply  with the aim thus envisaged in the Charter and borne out by justice and equity.
(2.23) Coming back to my own country, the Republic of Ecuador, I have drawn up a draft resolu-  tion designed as a first preparatory step towards the establishment of the Latin American ‘fnsti-  tute of Plastid'Arts at Quito, our capital city, which fully deserves this singular privilege if view  of its splendid mission and artistic heritage,
(2.24) 1 wish to express my country's thanks to Unesco for its invaluable contribution to the  operation of the International Centre for Higher Studies in Journalism in Latin America which ,  during the 10 years of its existence has done an outstanding job of the highest educational stand-  ard and of the widest international impact, I also wish to thank Unesco for the assistance it has  provided to the pilot projeèt for adult Ifteracy in Ecuador and in the ‘Quito Polytechnic, Similar  assistance should be extended to thé ‘Charles DarwinExperimental Station inthe Côlon Archipélago,  better known as the Galapagos Archipelogo, which contains the rarest specimens of flora and  fauna in the world and provided the distinguished English scientist after whom the station was  named with the basis for study in working out his celebrated theory of the origin of species,
(2.25) Finally, Me, President, I wish to reaffirm my Government's faith and confidence in the  high destiny of Unesco, which by its very nature is identified with humanity itself, dignified by  justice and culture and fortified by peace.

14.16 Depuis la fin de la Seconde Guerre mondiale la vie internationale connaît des sou-  bresauts inquiétants, et l'action des organisations internationales n'a pas encore réussi à  élever les défenses de la paix dans l'esprit des hommes, Pour déplorable que soit cette  situation, notre conviction demeure qu'elle est normale et que l'hydre des égoïsmes sacrés  ne saurail accepter sa défaite sans avaler ses dernières victimes, Mais les dernières con-  vulsions de la bête montrent encore plus clairement à ceux qui n'en sont pas encore suffi-  samment convaineus que l'esprit de tolérance demande plus que jamais à être soutenu dans  la communaute humaine,
14,17 Pour nous, Camerounais, nous apprécions les sacrifices consentis par des gouver-  nements pour soulager les misères physiques de l'humanité, Mais nous souhaitons que ce  sacrifice soit complété par celui d'une parcelle de leur égoïsme spirituel, L'esprit de to-  lérance seul devrait conduire le monde vers une véritable régénération, En menant mieux  notre combat pour le développement de l'éducation au sein de l'humanité, nous devrions  réussir à vaincre la misère physique et spirituelle dés peuples du Tiers Monde, ce Tiers  Monde que l'on ne trouve plus uniquement en Afrique, en Asie et en Amérique latine, mais  aussi dans les bidonvilles des cités européennes et d'Amérique du Nord,
14,18 Si nous réussissons à chasser le spictre de la faim et de l'ignorance, nous aurons  contribué à conserver toutes les richesses spirituelles des peuples antiques, créant ainsi  des conditions favorables à l'élaboration d'une éthique nouvelle de nos relations, Et l'ac-  ceptation totale de la réalité culturelle diversifiée de la société humaine pourra faciliter  la tolérance agissante entre tous ceux qui oeuvrent pour le bonheur et la survie de l'huma-  nité dans un climat de paix véritable.
