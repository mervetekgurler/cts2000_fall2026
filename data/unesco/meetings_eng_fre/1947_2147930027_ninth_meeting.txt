DROHOJO WSKI (Poland) said that his dele- gation had re-examined the situation in the light of the previous day’s discussion and had realized that two problems were before them. Two days previously, at a meeting of the Programme and Budget Commis- sion, a draft resolution had been presented by the Polish delegation. The Chairman had deemed it necessary to refer the resolution to the General Com- mittee and, therefore, the Polish delegate, who was the Rector of Warsaw University, had not been per- mitted to speak on the resolution. He recalled that the University of Warsaw was very favourably dis- posed towards Unesco and regretted this incident. The second matter he wished to raise was that of the two Polish resolutions contained in document
2C/BUR/2. Although these were drafted in the same spirit, they were two distinct resolutions. During the previous day’s meeting of the General Committee, there had been an animated discussion regarding procedure. The delegate of the United States of America had proposed that the resolutions should be referred to the Programme and Budget Commission and, although the Polish delegation had wished them to be submitted direct to the Plenary Conference, it had compromised and agreed to this proposal. Before any decision had been taken, it had been necessary for the meeting to adjourn. Questions of substance could not be discussed by the Committee, as it had not sufficient time at its disposa; also Rule 27 of the Rules of Procedure excluded all discussion on substance. During the discussions, certain objections had been made to the resolutions : (1) that they were parallel to a resolution of the United Nations dated 3 Novem- ber and were therefore superfluous; (2) that they had political implications; and (3) that they advo- cated measures to be taken against those who in- cited to war. As regards the first objection, he pointed out that the Polish resolutions were mot parallel to that of the United Nations and, in any case, there was no reason why different bodies should not make reso- lutions on parallel lines. In the second place, he agreed that the resolutions had a political aspect but recalled that the Preamble and Article I of the Unesco Constitution also had political implications. In any dictionary, the meaning of the word «political » was «relating to public life» and Unesco’s work was inevitably connected with public life. As regards the third objection, raised by the British delegation, he felt that the British tradition of freedom of the press and literary expression was sometimes at fault, as some dangerous publications were widely read, whereas other harmless omes were banned. In the spirit of co-operation which the Polish delegation had shown the previous day, he announced that they did not consider the text of the resolutions as absolutely rigid and would welcome suggestions. They would not, however, agree to any play on words or substitutions which would mean that their sincere proposal became deformed or lost its original meaning. He stressed how anxious Poland was to preserve peace and recalled that, in proportion to population, she had probably suffered the heaviest losses during the war. In conclusion, the Polish delegation considered that the General Committee was not empowered to change the substance of the resolutions, although it could direct them to whieh- ever part of the Conference it desired. He added that, if a discussion of substance did take place at the present meeting, the Polish delegation would abstain from taking part in it. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) moved that the resolutions should be submitted direct to the Plenary Meeting. He was seconded by the delegates of POLAND and CANADA. Agreed. Mr. BENTON (United States of America) recall- ed that he had proposed the previous day that the Ecuadorian resolutions, which affected the Programme, should be referred to the Programme and Budget Commission. He suggested that this was a valid principle for all future matters concerning the Programme. He again put forward this motion and was seconded by the delegate of Poland. Agreed.
