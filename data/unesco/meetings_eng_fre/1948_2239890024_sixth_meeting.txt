The meeting continued in secret at 10 a.m.  MEETING  Thursday, 26 November 1948, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman: Hamid Bey FRANGIE (Lebanon)  Constitution of the Joint Committee of  Fifteen.  Mr. ROBERTS (Chairman of the Administra-  tive Commission) confirmed his approval of the  five names proposed by the Administrative Com-  mission.  The CHAIRMAN stated that the Joint Com-  mittee of Fifteen was therefore to be considered  established.  Additional meeting of the Sub-Commission  on Natural Sciences.  The General Committee had received a request  for an additional meeting of the Sub-Commission  on Natural Sciences, and the CHAIRMAN thought  it would be practicable for such a meeting to be  held on the morning of 2 December.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) feared  that a similar situation might arise with other  Sub-Commissions; there might be a certain  danger in making an exception in favour of the  Natural Sciences Sub-Commission.    Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) pointed out  that the Sub-Commission on Communication had  arranged a meeting for Friday, so that it might  not be as easy to agree to the proposed meeting  as it appeared.  Count Stefano JACINI (Italy) thought it quite  possible that other Sub-Commissions might be  faced by a similar necessity; but it would be inop-  portune for the Committee to withhold its assent,  particularly as most evenings were free.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL pointed out that  there were eight or ten times, between 1 and
6 December, at which the Sub-Commission could  meet, without holding an evening meeting. He  stressed that the Secretary-General of the Confer-  ence should know the date chosen as soon as  possible.  Dr. NIELSEN (Denmark) emphasized that the  holding of such a meeting did not imply any  obligation on the part of the Programme and  Budget Commission to defer its own decisions.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL said that that was  quite clear from the time-table of the Conference.
223         Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) proposed to  agree to the extra meeting of the Sub-Commission  on Natural Sciences, without, however, creating  a precedent for any later decisions of the same  nature.  M. SEYDOUX (France) agreed with the dele-  gate of Italy and the Director-General. He praised  the Secretariat for the smoothness and speed with  which the Conference was running, but that very  efficiency did not give experts much time for  discussions. He was, therefore, in favour of  approving the request.  With the reservation suggested by Mr. Hard-  man, the request for an extra meeting was  approved.  Appointment of a delegation by the Govern-  ment of Czechoslovakia and nominations  for the Executive Board.  The CHAIRMAN read the following cable which  had been received from Prague :  “I hereby beg to advise that the following dele-  gates were appointed for the Third General Confer-  ence Beirut. Delegation leader : Czechoslovak  Ambassador in Paris, Dr. Adolf Hoffmeister.  Deputy leader Czechoslovak Government dele-  gate to Unesco, Dr. Benjamin Jedlicka. Marie  Bartuskova of Ministry of Education. Dr. Vaclav  Navratil of Ministry of Information. Dr. Jean  Boor. Participation of the said Czechoslovak  delegates in Beirut Session rendered impossible  by matters outside the province of relations be-  tween Czechoslovakia and Unesco. For candidate  to Executive Board the Czechoslovak delegation  puts forward Czechoslovak Ambassador in Paris,  Dr. Adolf Hoffmeister,  Jedlicka Borex.”  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL suggested that the  cable should be transmitted to the Credentials  Committee. The most important point of the  communication—apart from the very regrettable  absence of the Czechoslovak delegation—was that  it might create a precedent, as a member of an  absent delegation was being nominated as a can-  didate for election to the Board. It had occurred  previously that an absent member of a delegation  which was itself present had been nominated and  elected.  Dr. MOLINARI (Argentina) agreed that the  legal point brought out by the Director-General  should be settled, but he wished to declare that  his delegation would vote for Dr. Hoffmeister,  since Czechoslovakia was a full Member State and  had paid its contribution to the Organization.  In reply to Count Stefano JACINI (Italy), the  DIRECTOR-GENERAL explained that no time-  limit existed for the nomination of a delegation.  Any time up to the end of the Conference was  constitutional.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) urged that  it should be made quite clear to the Credentials  Committee that it was being asked : (1) to exa-  mine whether the cable constituted regular cre-  dentials for the delegates mentioned; (2) to decide  whether the delegation could be considered duly  accredited to the Third Session of the General  Conference, although it did not, in fact, take part  in its proceedings.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) raised  the question of procedure in the Nominations  Committee. He thought that the Chairman of  that Committee would have to place Dr. Hoff-  meister’s name before the Committee. Mr. Allen  had no objection to that procedure, although it  seemed somewhat irregular.  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL agreed that the pro-  cedure suggested by Mr. Allen appeared to be  correct.  M. SEYDOUX (France) agreed with Mr Hard-  man; he was of the opinion that, if the Committee  could vote for an absent member, it followed that  a member could be absent de facto but present  de jure.  Sir Sarvepalli RADHAKRISHNAN (India) held  that Article V.A, paragraph 1, of the Constitution,  was quite clear and did not mention presence or  absence. The only point which remained to be  settled was whether that Article referred to mem-  bers appointed at the period of the election or on  some former occasion,  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL thought it meant  the delegates to the Conference.  Dr. MOLINARI (Argentina) supported Mr. Hard-  man's proposal but, failing another solution, he  was prepared to propose the election of the  Czechoslovak delegate.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) pointed out that  Article V.A, paragraph 3, said that members of  the Executive Board were “immediately eligible  for a second term” and mentioned no other  formality.  The Committee adopted the United Kingdom  delegates proposal.  It was also decided to ask the Procedure Com-
