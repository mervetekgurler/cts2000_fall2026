The answer to this question could  have serious implications for. all of us in the future. Whether my delegation abstains or  votes "no" on the resolution as a whole will depend on the intgrpretation which is given to  paragraph 5, and I believe that the sponsors should give us such an interpretation,

6.2 My delegation welcomes first of all the Director-General's report in document 16C/12  and his proposals for a long-term plan of integrated action. We agree with him that Unesco  has already taken a number of practical steps to this end, and it is now for us tomake fur-  ther practical proposals for the future. We are fully aware of the difficulty of this task, a  difficulty which is explained in detail in paragraph 10 of document 16 C/12.
6,3 We think that the analysis given in document 16 C/12 provides an excellent basis for  our activities and I would like to say a few words on this.
6.4 We recognize the importance of peace research, and are aware of the necessity of  thoroughly analysing man's aggressiveness. The Federal Republic has therefore established  an institute for peace research, which has recently started its work in Munich under the  direction of the well-known scientist and peace researcher, Professor von Weizsäcker. I  consider it a happy coincidence that precisely today a German Society for Peace Research  has been founded, of which the Federal President, Dr, Heinemann, is patron. Provided  with a reasonable budget, this society will give newimpetus to the important work of peace  research, Permit me, distinguished delegates, to give you some additional information  which I have just read this morning in a German newspaper. The task of this society, ac-  cording to its constitution, which has been unanimously adopted, is to promote peace and  conflict research and to disseminate the idea of peace, which is in perfect agreement with  document 16 C/12. During the opening session, the Federal President is reported as say-  ing: "Since time immemorial historians have been busy describing battles and wars, ‘ but  little energy has been spent on finding ways and means to avoid them"
6.5 To continue, the Federal Government is convinced that racialism and other forms of  discrimination and the suppression of human freedom, no matter what the reasons may be,  constitute one of the basic evils of our time and are a source of danger to peace. We there-  fore co-sponsored document 16 C/DR/PLEN/5 concerning "Unesco's contribution to peace",  which was introduced by the delegation of the United States of America. Accordingly, my  government also rejects categorically any form of colonialism.
6.6 The Federal Government considers that a world-wide educational effort is needed for  the solution of those problems, because primarily we have to get racialism andcolonialism  out of the minds of men. For this reason, in my country, priority is given to the training  of teachers, to youth exchanges and adult and civic education. In this context more and  more use is made of the mass media,
6.7 My government also wishes to underline the importance attaching to international cul-  tural co-operation and to recall in particular the results of the Venice Conference. We sup-  port anything that is likely to make people better aware of the cultural values of other na-  tions, because this strengthens and intensifies co-operation and friendship among peoples,  If we open up our frontiers to the cultures of other peoples, there will be no room for pre-  judice and clichés any more,
6.8 As already explained in paragraph 40 of document 16 C/12, National Commissions for  Unesco have an important part to play in these efforts. They are centres for public and  private initiative alike and can ensure a wide dissemination of the ideas referred to. The  Federal Government will thérefore fully support the German Commission for Unéscoinits  efforts for peace arid its struggle against racialism.
6.9 Permit me.to make one supplementary suggestion, The Federal Government believes  that the tendentious presentation of historic events and the distorted image of other peoples  {unfortunately so often found in textbooks) exert a dangerous influence on our children and  have serious consequences for the peaceful co-operation of nations. I should like to draw
29  your attention to the work that has been done by the International Textbook Institute in Ger-  many for almost twenty years now. My delegation believes that the ex-  perience gained in this field may be of use in Unesco's work for peace,

We categorically reject any form of colonialism, racial discrim-  ination, fascism and all other forms of totalitarian ideologies. Let there be no doubt in this  respect. :
6.12 We cannot deny, however; that in our opinion not all the draft resolutions before us do  serve this purpose in the manner desired. "The purpose of this Organization is to con-  tribute to peace and security by promoting collaboration among the nations through educa-  tion, science and culture..." 10.1 Mr. INGLES (Philippines):  Mr. President, ladies and gentlemen, during the general policy debate the Philippine  delegation made two observations on the Director-General's proposals for a long-term plan  of integrated action on Unesco's contribution to peace (document 16 C/12). Firstly, my de-  legation suggested that the Director-General and the Secretariat give the same attention to  the eradication of colonialism as they proposed to do with respect to the eradication of ra-  cialism in 1971. Secondly, in view of the basic unity of human rights, my delegation em-  phasized the need for the Organization's increasing commitment to the promotion of univer-  sal respect for the observance of human rights and fundamental freedom for all, not limit-  ing its horizon merely to the enhancement of the right to education and freedom of opinion,  information and communication.
10.2 During the general debate at the fifteenth session of the General Conference, Mr. Roses,  Chairman of the Philippine National Commission for Unesco, drew attention to the general  breakdown of law and order which constitutes a threat to the peace and development of Mem-  ber States. The Philippine delegation then recommended that Unesco pay greater attention  to the social science field and it specifically suggested that the whole battery of the social  sciences be levelled against the serious threat to all nations and to society at large posed  by the general breakdown of peace and order. In the view of my delegation this important  problem which confronts all nations great and small is but a reflection of the sad state of  affairs in the world at large. It cannot be gainsaid that education of the peoples of the world,  especially of the young, concerning the necessity of law and order and the outlawing of vi-  olence cannot but influence national attitudes on the international scene, Indeed, education  can be and should be geared to the orientation of education to peace in general in order to  imbue the citizenry with respect for law and justice, not only at home but also in interna-  tional affairs..
10.3 The Philippine delegation is pleased to note that the Director-General has stressed  the importance of the social sciences in his Long-term Outline Plan for 1971-1876 (docu-  ment 16 Q/4), and that he has adopted a scientific approach for studying human behaviour,  specifically by research into the phenomena of human aggressiveness and the identification  of the causes of tension and conflict among peoples, in his long-term plan of integrated ac-  tion on Unesco's contribution to peace (document 16 C/12). -
10.4 Needless to say, the Philippine delegation supports in general the Director-General's  plan for peace, subject to the observations which were made at the beginning of my inter-  vention. We should like to draw attention in this connexion to Recommendations 19 and 22  of the Fifth Regional Conference of Asian National Commissions for Unesco, which called  upon Asian National Commissions to do their utmost to reinforce their structural frame-  work and operational methods for the task of development and international co-operation,  and specifically to review their respective compositions, particularly their working com-  inittees, with a view to sécuriug the combination of the ablest specialists in each country  and representatives of the most varying shades of opinion, so that the National Commis-  sions might cope with their increased activity in undertaking the additional task, among  others, of promoting peace and international understanding.

This is due, no doubt, to the vary-  ing points of view no less than to the different if not conflicting motivations of Member de-  legations.

(16.2) The Executive Board hag dealt with the first two problems at a number of ita sessions and  in some detail, The fifteepth session of the General Conference also dealt with the problem of  human rights - all of which lie within the competence of Unesço, The Arab delegates were care-  ful not to plunge into the political problems of the Palestinian cause, which include the existence  of Israel, its occupation of Arab territory in one unjust aggression after another, and the ques-  tion of its withdrawal from the occupiedjerritories. They left these political aspects tobe raised  by their Arab colleagues at the United Nations which is the appropriate forum for them.  i
(16.3) But Israel's representative, instead of confining himself to these three probléms, andre-  plying to the evidence we submitted, chose to plunge into political questions, including the pauses’  of the conflict and the state of war between the Arabs andIsrael. He made lightof the refugee prob-  lem, which he described as unavoidable, He then turned to the internal affairs of the Arab and  other Socialist countries, propounding fallacies and distorting facts in the worst possible manner.  As for his reference to certain events in Iraq, it only reveals his interest in, and the connexion.  of his government with, those conspiracies against Irdq and other Arab countries. The ‘govern-  ment of Iraq, like the governments of other countries which have regard to their security, safety,  and territorial integrity, deals with such conspiracies in accordance with the accepted principles  of criminal law and with a view to punishing the conspirators as they deserve.
(16.4) This is not the time to expound the Palestinian cause: it will be ériough to rebut the Israel  delegate's claims by mentioning the principal facts, concerning which there can be no possible  dispute.
(16,5) In 1917 - that is to say, fifty-threé years ago - ninety-two per cent of the population of  Palestine were Arabs (Muslim or Christian) and eight per cent Jews. The Arabs had been liv-  ing on this land for thousands of years. An exemplary tolerance prévailed among the three re-  ligions. About 1,400 years ago, Omar Ibn el-Khattab, the second Caliph, visited Jerusalem to .  receive the keys of the city. He guaranteed the security of its Christian inhabitants, and refused  to pray in the church lest his comrades should use it as a mosque after him. He performed his  prayers outside the church, on the site upon which the Omar Moâque was subsequently erected.
(16.6) As regards the Jews, it is a well-attested faët-that, throughout their history and among  the many different peoples with whom they lived, nowhere did they enjoy such fraternity and well-  being as those they encountered in the various Arab countries. This situation continueduntil as  recently as the period following the First World War to such an extent that a relationship akin to  brotherhood often subsisted betweeh children of the three religions who happened tohave been born  on the same date. Sayed Haj Amin El-Hoseini, who fought against Zionism throughout his life,  had three Jewish brothers of this sort.
(16.7) The Israel delegate referred to the flourishing existence which the Jews enjoyed in Arab  countries: and indeed, we know of some of them who rose to the highest positions in politics,  cultural’ life end commerce. However, they left their original homes voluntarily and of their own  accord to become second-class citizens in Israel.
(16.8) Such was the situation, What ig it today? Israel has occupied the whole of Palestine by  armed force, and has also scized Sinai and the Golan Heights. .The Arabs in these areas have  remained under occupation, with all the tyranny and terror which that implies. How did this happen,  and what are the main causes that brought about such a situation?
(16.9) There are two reasons for what has happened, The first is that political forces foreign  to Palestine and the interests of the Palestinians have imposed, and are still imposing, their  views on the destiny of this country and its people. The second is that Zionism has not onlyex-  ploited these political forces, but has also made a totally merciless and unscrupulous use of the  methods of violence and terrorism.
(16.10) As regards the political forces involved, perhaps I should begin by referring to the Bal-  four Declaration of 1917, in which the British Government declared that it supported the idea of  creating a national home for the Jews in Palestine, without prejudice to the rights of those in-  habitants of the country who’belonged to other religions. The British Government made this de-  claration - which it had no right whatever to make - at the same time 4s it was promising the  Arabs independence for their countries, including Palestine, if they revolted agaînat the Turks.  It used the period of its mandate as a cover to facilitate Jewish frimigration into Palestine. No  sooner had the Arabs felt the dangers of this immigraticn than they résisted it in one revolution  after another, and towards the end of the thirties the British Government came to the conclusion  that there was no alternative to the establishment of aunitedState which wouldembrace both parties.  {16.11} However, Zionism was not content with his, but demanded more. The persecution of the  Jews by the Nazis, like the earlier persecutions they had undergone in Europe, provided a pretext  for exploiting the inferiority complex of alarge number of statesmen, who were thus led to sup-  port the United States partition plan for Palestine in the United Natiôns. Hence it came about that  the political fate of Palestine was settled by external forces unrelated to thé interests of its peo-  ple - the most oppressive and unjust course imaginable - and indeed was largely determined by  election requirements in Leeds, Manchester, Néw York and California and by the immediate in-  terests involved in those contexts. (16. 12) I now come to the second reason. I begin with’ the partition decision and the acts of vi-  olence subsequently carried out by Zionist gangs, culminating in the massacre at Deir Yasseen  in the spring of 1948. Hundreds of victims, including women, children and old people, were sac -  rificed in this slaughter, in which the crimes perpetrated by the Zionists were such as to place them  on a par with the Nazis. 300,000 Arabe fled infear and terror, as Belgians, Dutchmen and French-  men had fled before the Nazi advance ‘in 1940, These Arabs were the vanguard of the refugees.  This happened before 15 March 1948, and it is true to say that war was a consequence of the ter-  ror inflicted upon the refugees. The refugees did not run away after the war as alleged by the  Israel representative.
(16.13) Zionist propaganda has gone so far as to claim that the refugees fled in response to a call  from the various Arab leaders and governments. This claim was challengedby the Irish historian  Erskin Childers, who studied the whole of the British Museum Library's cdllection of tape-  recordings of Arab broadcasts made during the months of the crisis and did not come across a  single word calling upon the inhabitants of Palestine to leave their homes. (16,14) According to a report carried by the "Herald Tribune" inthe summer of 1969, the Foreign  Minister of Israel declared that during his visit to Washington in March 1967 he had met with peo-  ple who asked him, "Why do you hesitate?" He thereupon returnedto his country, and the decision  to commence hostilities was not long in following.
(16.15) The Israel representative, like other experts in misrepresentation, seeks to judge the  Arabs by their words, but will not allow Israel to be judged, as it deserves to be, by its deeds -  deeds of aggression against the rights of peoples. But the peoples will have their say and give  their judgement, and the kingdom of falsehood will pass away.
(16.16) Let us now revert to the violation of human rights that took place inthe occupiedterrito-  ries of Palestine after the war. I draw my evidence from papers by no means notorious for their  friendship towards the Arabs. Last year, the chief foreign editor of the London "Times" visited  the occupied area of Palestine and spent séveral weeks there, He had been acquainted with the  area for years, On his return he wrote an article for his paper in which he des¢ribed at great  length the terror to which the Arabs were subjected, including the destruction of entire villages  by way of collective punishment and various forms of torture during trial police interrogation.  All this he compared with Nazi practices, This brought forth a flood of letters to the editor, but  none of them could persuade the writer of the article to revise his opinions.

All of us here endeavour to express ourselves in language suitable to the dignity of this gather-  ing and in the spirit of educated men of the world: but the delegate of Israel insisted on employ-  ing the sort of language of which he and some of his colleagues have made a speciality in their  world-wide efforts to propagate falsehood in the guise of truth, The delegate of Israel insisted  on dragging the Middle East conflict into the deliberations of this distinguished assembly -acon-  flict which we for our part, since the beginning of the Conference, have taken great care not to  allude to in an inflammatory manner such as that adopted by the delegate of Israel.
