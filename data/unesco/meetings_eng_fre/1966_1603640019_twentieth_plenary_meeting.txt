15.1 Mr, CUMMINGS (Liberia):  Mr, President, Mr, Director-General, distinguished delegates, ladies and gentlemen,  may I first of all associate myself with those who have preceded me in paying tribute to  Professor Sissakian, President of the thirteenth gession of the General Conference who has  gone from labour to reward and has left as a memorial his outstanding work as a great  scientist of his age. And may I also express my delegation's congratulations to Professor  Tuncel of Turkey on his unanimous election to the presidency of this distinguished assembly.  I hereby pledge him my delegation's co-operation and support in his great task of leadership.
15,2 We have met here to review the draft programme and budget as a prelude to embark-  ing upon new efforts for the years ahead. The important thing about this international as-  sembly is that we meet here every two years not to seek personal gloryor satisfy individual  ambitions but, rather, to discuss ways and means designed to meet the needs of men and  women everywhere. 1 feel sure, ladies and gentlemen, that the accurate evaluation of what  has been accomplished through the generous contributions of this Organization to Member  States to help them identify and achieve their goals, will encourage and stimulate Unesco  in the further consideration and planning of future programmes.
15,3 The record of Unesco's previous activities in the promotion of education, science and  culture, in the attempt to abolish poverty, disease and ignorance from the face of the globe,  have created a lasting impression on the minds of the world's less developed nations. We  fervently hope and trust that the conclusions arrived at at this session of the General Con-  fererce will be useful and conducive to a just fulfilment of our obligations.
15.4 My delegation wishes to extend thanks to the Dircctor-General, the Executive Board  and the Secretariat for the magnificent task performed in drawing up the proposed pro-  gramme, It is gratifying to note that through the prompt action of some Member States the  Director-General and his officers were able to speedily complete the draft programme and  distribute it to Member States in record time for study. For this I want to thank them.  The document now before this assembly for discussion and our collective action reflects,  to my mind, clear thinking, and is evidence of hours of strenuous concentration on the part  of its authors. The proposals made show an understanding of the problems which exist in  the fields of education, science and culture in most, if not all, of the Member States. The  provisional budgetary appropriation of $61, 506, 140 appears no doubt inadequate for the  tasks which lie ahead, However, if this is all that Unesco can find from various sources  we must make the best use of it and support the proposals contained in the document before  us. For I believe that the development of any society, no matter what system it practices,  and the perpetuation of its cultural values, depend on a sound education without which all  systems are bound to crumble and disappear, Therefore, our enthusiasm and willingness  to share in the speedy approval of this draft programme, which has been so brilliantly pre-  sented by the Director-General, willbe proof of our sincerity inupholding this Organization,
15.5 With the whole world in a state of tension and uncertainty concerning its future, itis  encouraging to note that educators and men of goodwill have come together to consider  seriously the means that may one day bring peace to this unsettled world. Since the end of  World War II, nations have come together in many different circumstances inorder to devise  ways and means of making a better world. But as always, blinded by hatred, by prejudices  of one kind or another, driven by a desire for control and revenge, unmipdful of the inter-  dependence of nations in a highly-mechanized age, and unwilling to recognize his mistakes,  man has refused to live in concord with his fellow men. Our efforts thus appear not to  have yielded much fruit.
15.6 Civilization cannot afford to repeat the tragedy of the past. If our objectivesaretobe  accomplished, they must be accomplished through a system of universal education. We
544   Sesiones plenarias/llxenapune 3acedanus xx  must stand firm on the foundations already laid, so that through education we can do what  should be done in order to save our civilization, We can do this, if we can come to realize.  the needs of the hour and direct our resources to the task of increasing the understanding,  the loyalty and the competence of millions of people throughout the world with speed and  efficiency for the safety of the world, This type of education must be related to the exigen-  cies of the times. It will require a strategy and also our confidence in the sincerity and  integrity of those entrusted with the task of planning. Above all, it must be based on the  aims and procedures which will lead to united and effective action.
15,7 The winning of political, social and economic rights means little for pcople unless  these are accompanied by the development of their capacities to profit by and utilize their  liberties. The right of individual development must be prior to and more fundamental than  any other human right. Pestalozzi, one of the world's greatest educational philosophers,  once wrote, " my ultimate goal is the social regeneration of humanity", His sole aim was  to stop the misery in which he saw people around him, "I lived like a beggar," he wrote,  "in order to learn how to make beggars live like men", Ibelieve that the only way to change  society for the better is by changing the individuals that make up society. The genuine re-  form of society must begin with the elevation of the individual, but the individual must be  elevated only by putting into his grasp the power to help himself,
15.8 My delegation feels that the programmes proposed by the Director-General and the  Executive Board for consideration by Member States, are unique and appropriate, Their  importance to national development is vital. We therefore endorse them and I would beg  your indulgence as I outline my delegation's position,
15.9 The chapter on educational planning is worthy of particularly careful consideration,  The foundation of a good educational system is sound planning and if developing countries  are to enjoy the benefits of such an educational system, then we shall do well to support  this aspect of the programme in whatever shape or form it may be necessary for it to as-  sume in the future. My government takes the task of educational planning very seriously,  and on the basis of the National Plan of Economic Development, the Department of Educa-  tion is in the process of preparing a constructive plan for the next four years. The plan pla-  ces very high priority on secondary education, great emphasis on the improvement of the  quality of education rather than quantitative expansion; the provision of textbo ks and instruc-  tional materials, the strengthening of the existing teacher-training programmes and the  expansion of adult education facilities and, finally it takes into consideration the construc-  tion of new facilities and the improvement of existing ones,
15.10 With regard to the item on the World Literacy Campaign, this is anissue upon which  the progress of world society depends. Its importance concerns millions of the world's po-  pulation. I refer to the population of the underdeveloped countries of the world. If this  world in which we live is to be safe from fear and mistrust, then, the majority of its peo-  ple must be given the opportunity to think and act as their consciences direct. There will  never be peace in our time as long as one half of the world's population remains illiterate  and ignorant both of the nature of the problems that engulf them and the forces they might  command to overcome them.
15.11 The aim of education must be to analyse the interests of man in order to discover  those that are most beneficial for his life in society and, by means of instruction, to enable  him to develop and apply these interests in the various situations of life. The campaignfor  the eradication of illiteracy throughout the world needs careful study and planning. Although  Unesco deserves credit in this connexion, her efforts muat be doubled and considerable  time and money be allocated to this project since it 1s a long-term operation, Since 1948  the Government of Liberia has mobilized all its available resources for the fight against  illiteracy. All organizations, governmental and non-governmental, church missions, pri-  vate institutions have joined forces in this fight and we are determined to fight until the  battle 1s won. We have come to believe however, that the real problem will never be solved
545   XX Plenary meetings / Séances pléntères  if literacy is considered as an end in itself, It must be associated with a goal, Thus when-  aver literacy projects are established it is necessary to ensure that appropriate content is  provided for adult education and the training of adult education workers, This is where  Unesco's assistance, as in the past, is urgently needed and we trust that in the distribution  of funds and means for adult education we shall be considered in this regard,
15,12 A word on the access of women and girls to education. No society, whatever its sta-  tus, can rise above the level of its womanhood, One particular way in which Unesco could  co-operate with Member States in making education available to women and girls is through  a programme of home economics, which is a field that covers education in all areas of liv-  ing. Unesco's contribution to the development and the enhancement of such a programme  would open up immense avenueg for women and girls, not only in providing employment op-  portunities but also In training qualified housewives and mothers capable of bringing up their  children to meet the requirements of a sound and healthy society,
15,13 Science instruction, especially in secondary schools and colleges, is an area of  timely and strategic necessity. In the world of today with all its technological advances,  the knowledge of science is imperative. Increased industrialization and mechanized farm-  ing require a great deal of organized scientific research for better and greater output. It  is gratifying to see the extent of Unesco's support for the natural sciences and their appli-  cation to development as indicated by the budgetary appropriation of over $9 million. The  lack of certain types of scientific knowledge in the development of programmes which are  of crucial importance for national growth, has often stood inthe way of the economic growth  of many of the developing nations, It is for this reason, that my government has commenced  to import professors from India for our secondary schools and colleges, so as to train  Liberian young men and women in the basic sciences, Unesco's continuing assistance by  way of equipment, supplies, and experts in this field is recorded with gratitude.
15,14 I fully agreed with the Director-General when he said that science teaching is use-  less without some basic research. Indeed, it is becoming more and more important and  clear to my government that attention must be given to this phase of scientific development.  The problem, however, has been and continues to be the lack of adequate resources with  which to foster this very important activity.
15,15 The question of the improvement of the status and training of teachers, deserves a  great deal of consideration. The explosive situation of the world's population and the in-  crease in the child population demands a great increase in the facilities for the training of  teachers and improvement in their status. Our delegation strongly urges Unesco's support  to Member States in this direction. We fully support the idea that the teaching profession  should be given as much consideration as any other profession. All efforts are being made  by my government to make available training facilities to deserving and prospective tea-  chers throughout the country, for it is clear that the quality of our students depends large-  ly on the quality of the teachers that instruct them,
15.16 Education for international understanding includes opportunities which cannot be  ignored, for the preservation of world peace. Surely, education and the school can play a  unique réle in developing in people the right attitudes towards world co-operationand giving  them the understanding and the knowledge that seem to be the prerequisites for that world-  wide gocial education which is the basis for a just and lasting peace, Most wars have been  the result of misunderstanding and mistrust, The study of the vital interests and the cul-  tural forces of other nations in our schools must continue to serve as an effective means  for diminishing the danger to international peace. If we place before our students evidence  of the ethical, aesthetic, historical, economic, political and cultural heritage of another  nation, compiled by scholars and authors representative of that nation, we are building a  common bond of understanding and sympathy which would help preserve international peace.
15,17 May 1 say then that while the objectives set by this Organization for 1967-1968 are  numerous and will require considerable expenditure, they are also highly laudable, It there-  fore appears to my delegation that the increase in expenditure is justified by the goals that  are set. The accomplishments of them will have far-reaching effects on the lives of mil-  lions of people the world over, Therefore, ladies and gentlemen, in the interest of huma-  nity and the cause for which Unesco stands let us give these proposals our sincere consider-  ation and support.
16,18 It has always been our feeling that time should not he wasted in debate by giving a  history of our country's achievements and development in respect of the programmes of  Unesco, Rather, it is our opinion that such information should be submitted in a report to  Unesco. My delegation has prepared a synopsis of such actions that have been takenby the  Government of Liberia in priority fields of education, science and culture, This informa-  tion we intend to circulate among delegates during the present session.
15.19 My delegation wishes to add its welcome to the new Member and Associate Member  States of this Organization, We hope that their association with Unesco will bring them and _  their people peace and happiness, and draw them closer to the brotherhood of man which  is our ultimate goal.
15.20 Before concluding, I wish to add a cautionary word, and that is that the international  organizations upon which the world depends for the perpetuation of peace are still far from  the attainment of their goal. The war in Viet-Nam, the minority government of the illegal  Smith régime in Rhodesia, the economic imbalance in the world today, the unprecedented  decision of the International Court of Justice in the South-West Africa case against Liberia  and Ethiopia, just to name a few, are all problems which call for our most serious consi-  deration. We pray that a day will surely come, when man, having grown tired of walking  alone, will turn to his brother, On the day when we have learned to feel the sorrows and  the joys, the suffering and the hopes of others, as our very own, that world order of love  and justice for which the universe yearns and of which the planets in the stillest night are  the splendid but imperfect symbol, will have come nearer. On that day alone, the brother-  hood of man will have become a reality.
15,21 Finally, Mr. President, as we celebrate the Twentieth Anniversary of Unesco, let  us rededicate ourselves, our energies and our resources to the task of bringing peace, hap-  piness and understanding to the whole of mankind.
