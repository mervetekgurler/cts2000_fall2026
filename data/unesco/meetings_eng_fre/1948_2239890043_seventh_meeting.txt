Professor RIVET (France) warmly supported  the project for the publication of a Scientific and  Cultural History of Mankind. He considered that
264  the unfavourable reception given to the project in  the Sub-Commissions might have been caused by  misunderstanding of the project by the delegates.  Some of them were under the impression that  the project was to be carried out by one individual:  that was not the intention of those who had  initiated it, who realized that such a project could  only be the work of an international team of spe-  cialists, co-ordinating their efforts in a harmonious  and flexible whole.         Other delegates had thought that the project  was impracticable because of its magnitude; it  was not, however, intended that the work should  be of an encyclopaedic nature and the initiators  of the project had recognized that, at the outset,  the activity of the team of writers would have  to be limited to a definite field of study. The  French delegation had proposed that a history  of technology should be tackled first, leaving  aside for future study a history of philosophy,  of religion and of art. It was already possible  to find fragmentary information on those subjects,  while a history of the technical progress of man  throughout the ages had never been produced.  Still other delegates thought that the necessary  material for such a work was lacking; Professor  Rivet was convinced, however, that it would be  possible to find the necessary material in special-  ized monographs already published; it would be  for the writers of the work to co-ordinate and  synthesize such fragmentary data.  Lastly, some delegates thought that the project  was impracticable because it would take too long  to bring to fruition; Professor Rivet maintained  that a work of the kind envisaged could be pro-  duced in four years at the most and could then  form the basis of more enlightened textbooks to  replace the badly written books in common use.  Existing textbooks were bad in that they were  written from a nationalistic point of view. They  were Incomplete, concentrating on great events,  such as wars, instead of on the discoveries which  formed the basis of modern life throughout the  world such as the invention of the boomerang by  the aborigines of Australia, and of blow-pipes by  the Malays. The relative importance of eras was  wrongly stressed, as the Middle Ages, for instance,  fruitful in invention and craftsmanship, were  neglected in comparison with modern times. If  the project for a Scientific and Cultural History  of Mankind was adopted, it should prove an im-  portant factor in the development of international  understanding, which was one of the prime aims  of Unesco.
