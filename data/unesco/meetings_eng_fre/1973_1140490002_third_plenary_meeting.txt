6. 1 Mr. CHATTERJEE (India):  Mr. President and distinguished delegates, this extraordinary session of the  General Conference has assembled to consider the financial crisis facing Unesco which,  as we are well aware, is largely due to economic and monetary factors over which the  Organization has little control. TO some extent, the financial difficulties of the Organi-  zation are connected with the problems of its growth and show that it has had to pay the  price of endeavour and advance. We are a11 committed to the achievement of the three  main objectives of Unesco: firstly, the contribution of Unesco to economic and social  development; secondly, the contribution of Unesco to peace, and thirdly the contribution  of Unesco to ethical and moral ends. Looking to the future of Unesco and its expandimg  raie in the evolution of a world community, it is the duty of the Member States to rally  to the support of the Organization in this hour of trial, and to ensure that Unesco is  strengthened and reinvigerated to function effectively in the vast and significant fields of  its action.
6. 2 Mr. President, the budget for 1973-1974 approved by the 1972 General Conference  amounted to $119, 954, 000. The additional costs arising from inflation as on 1. 9. 1973  are estimated to be of the order of $7, 847, 900. The Director-General’s proposa1 that
3XCl3  these additional costs may be met by economies in expenditure and by utilizationof the  appropriation reserve has been approved by the Executive Board. This leaves the major  deficit resulting from the devaluation of the US dollar to be dealt with. This deficit is  estimated to be of the order of $15, 852, 100. After taking credit for further economies  to the extent of $1, 600, 000 and contributions by two new Member States estimated to be  about $1, 600, 000, we have still to find resources to the extent of $12, 652, 100. We want  to express our appreciation of the work done by the Director-General in restructuring  the budget SO as to secure substantial economies without cutting into the main elements  of the programme. The position now reached is that the Member States are beingaskeci  to agree to the adoption of supplementary estimates to caver this very substantial deficit  of $12, 652 million.
6. 3 Mr. President, 1 would like to reiterate two important considerations bearing on  the phenomenal increase in the budget deficit, which were emphasized at the last meeting  of the Executive Board by the Indian member, Mr. G. Parthasarathi. The first is that  the budget deficit has arisen because of domestic inflation in the developed countries and  the instability and periodic fluctuation of some international currencies, and particularly  the devaluation of the US dollar vis-à-vis the currencies of certain other developedcoun-  tries. Speaking as a representative of a developing country, 1 must say that it seems to  me inequitable that the burden of a part of the deficit caused by currency instabilityin the  developed countries should be passed on by them to the developing countries as a result  of increasing the latter’s proportionate contribution towards the general budget of Unesco.  The developing countries, by and large, are not responsible for the crisis and they are  least able to bear the extra burden of an increased contribution.
6.4 In order to prevent such an eventuality in the future, the Consultative Committee  on Administration Questions had made the following proposals:
(1) The use of Special Drawing Rights on a “unit of account” related to the par  value of the dollar to gold as the basis of assessment.
(2) Use of a currency other than the dollar for budgeting and the assessment of  contributions.
(3) Assessment of Member States, partly in dollars and partly in the currency  of the host country, the latter at a rate fïxed at the time the budget is voted.
6. 5 Mr. President, we feel that the General Conference should give serious consid-  eration to these proposals. The fluctuations in the rate of exchange of important cur-  rencies may continue for a considerable time, and Unesco may be hamstrung with un-  expected and heavy deficits at any time due to manipulations in the rates of exchange.  This pinpoints the need to work out fundamental and long-term solutions.
6. 6 1 would suggest that, in view of the very heavy deficit caused by the present cur-  rency crisis, this General Conference make an appeal to a11 Member States whose cur-  rencies have appreciated in terms of the dollar to pay their contributions for thecurrent  biennium in their own currencies at the rates pledged by them before devaluation. Such  action on their part Will be a valuable palliative and help the Director-General to balance  the budget for the current biennium.
6. 7 Mr. President, 1 now corne to the second important consideration which 1 have in  mind. We know that steps have been taken to effect substantial economies in the pro-  gramme to meet the joint challenge of inflation and devaluation. We are in full sym-  pathy with the view that such economies should not impinge on the operative efficiency  of the different programmes, particularly those designed to assist the developing Mem-  ber States. We would suggest that the search should be continued for further sizeable  savings in the administrative and other expenditure combined with the rationalization  and streamlining of operational programmes. There is the apprehension that the re-  ductions in the Budget may affect programmes, but if we take appropriate measures of  rationalization and streamlining, which are justified by the present emergency, the in-  tegrity of the tore of the programmes Will not be jeopardized. 1 would urge that Unesco  should continue to adopt stringent measures of economy in administrative expenditure  and overheads, however unpalatable some of them may be. This is what we do in our  own countries when faced with similar situations.
6. 8 Mr. President, speaking from the long-ter-m point of view, 1 think that by decen-  tralizing the work of the Secretariat and by entrusting more and more of the responsibili-  ties of the programmes to the National Commissions and the Regionsl Offices, which cari  certainly operate at lower cost, substantial savings cari be made.
6. 9 Consistent with what 1 have stated above, my delegation is prepared to vote in fa-  vour of the supplementary estimates of the order of $12, 652 million proposed by the Ex-  ecutive Board. If, however, the deficit further increases due to inflation and fluctuations  in exchange rates, we consider that the additional liability should be made good byfurther  adjustments in the budget and not by an increased appropriation. On the other hand, if  due to appreciation of the US dollar in terms of other currencies the deficit is reduced  below the estimated figure of $12, 652 million, we consider that the additional assess-  ment on Member States should be correspondingly reduced. Thank you, Mr. President.
