2.6 Now, as regards the second category, Member States' reports, as you know, Sir, not  all aspects of Member States' reports are submitted to the Reports Committee. The Re-  ports Committee considers only those aspects of Member States reports having specific  international implications, namely, conventions and recommendations, and such legal in-  struments as have been approved by Unesco. Member States were therefore requested at  previous General Conferences and in accordance with the Rules of Procedure, to do two  things: to submit such instruments to the competent bodies in their own countries, and then
902   Sesiones plenarias / Hsex pusie saceoanus XXXIII  report back to Unesco on what they have done. Such reports are communicated to Unesco  as special reports, as distinct from the general reports covering all aspects of Unesco's  specialization in education, science and culture.
2.7 We do not, as a Reports Committee, concern ourselves with what every Member State  has said about schools in their country or institutions of learning or such matters, but we  are concerned with international Instruments relating to our fields of specialization in  education, science and culture. Reports on such recommendations, instruments or conven-  tions are called special reports rather than general reports, and Member States are re-  quested to make the proper distinctions between these two categories of report.
2.8 Special reports are to be communicated to Unesco, usually a few months prior to the  opening of the first ordinary sessfon of the General Conference following that at which such  recommendation or convention was adopted. You will find in Annex III the general report on  the initial special reports relating to three different instruments referred to on page 9.
2.9 The first of these - a very important one - was a protocol instituting a Conciliation  and Good Offices Commission to be responsible for seeking the settlement of any disputes  which may arise between States Parties to the Convention against Discrimination in Educa-  tion. It is a very valuable protocol, a very valuable instrument, and we dearly hope that  it will receive more approval and wider acknowledgement than it has so far received. The  second is the recommendation concerning technical and vocational education, and the third  is the recommendation concerning the safeguarding of the beauty and character of land-  scapes and sites. Although this is a perfectly harmless subject we have had only 36 re-  ports about it. Out of a membership of 116, only 36 reports. Regarding the recommenda -  tion concerning technical and vocational education, we received 35 reports. Perhaps one  more has come in since this report was prepared, but still the number is very small.  True, it is a little better than it was at the last session of the General Conference, but it  is still very gmall. With regard to the most ir:portant of all, however, the protocol in-  stituting a Conciliation and Good Offices Commission to seek the settlement of any disputes  which may arise between States Parties to the Convention against Discrimination in Educa-  tion, we were only able to get 27 special reports - 27 only.
2.10 Annex III of the report before you makes very clear all aspects of Member States!  reports, the steps Member States should take, how they should implement such conventions  and recommendations and how they should report back to Unesco. These reports are more  or less automatic and are given at every session of the General Conference, pointing out  certain forms of action that should be taken with regard to international instruments of a  legal kind, having special importance for Unesco.
2.11 This year the document contains an innovation, however, which you will find in para-  graph 19, on page 11, which states:  "In order to make it easier for Member States to prepare special reports, in accord-  ance with the provisions of resolution 50, the General Conference was of the opinion  that it might be useful to bring together in one document for the benefit of the govern-  ments of Member States, the various provisions of the Constitution and the regulations  applicable, together with other suggestions that the General Conference itself has found  it necessary to formulate, at its earlier sessions, concerning the submission of con-  ventions and recommendations to the competent authorities, ..." .  Thus, when we communicate with Member States with regard to the special reports, we  shall now give, in one document, all the necessary information concerning the drawing up  of those special reports.
2.12 Now, this general report on the initial special reports of Member States is considered  to have been prepared by the General Conference itself. It was approved in a similar way  at the last session of the Conference, and requires your approval again during this Confer-  ence. The resolution approving this general report is found in Annex H, page 7.

2.14 This brings me to the last recommendation of the General Committee, which con-  cerns human rights. Now, in agreement with the United Nations, Unesco has undertaken  certain responsibilities in respect of three important aspects of human rights, the  sectors covered by Articles 19, 26 and 27, which are chiefly concerned with education.  Unesco therefore invites Member States to submit to the Director-General their special  reports on their progress in implementing the recommendations of the United Nations with  regard to human rights on these three items. Once the reports have been received from  Member States, by agreement with the United Nations, an analytical summary of them is  prepared and transmitted by the Director-General direclly to the Secretary-General of the  United Nations. The text of the resolution on this subject (in document 13 C/13 Rev.  Annex V) is as follows:  "The General Conference,  Having noted with appreciation the information presented by the Director-General re-  lating to the periodic reports received from Member States in connexion with  Articles 19, 26 and 27 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights;  Invites all Member States to submit to the Director-General, at his request, before
30 June 1966, a report describing developments and the progress achieved in the  sectors covered by Articles 19, 26 and 27 of the Universal Declaration of Human  Rights during the calendar years 1963, 1964 and 1965, for inclusion in the analytical  summary to be prepared by the Director-General in accordance with the following  paragraph;  Authorizes the Director-General to prepare and transmit directly to the Secretary-  General of the United Nations, at his request, an analytical summary of the periodical  reports submitted by Member States, in accordance with resolution 624.B (XXII) and
888 (XXXIV), and such other resolutions as may be adopted by the Economic and Social  Council, describing developments and the progress achieved in the sectors covered  by Articles 19, 26 and 27 of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights."
