From the  latest information available to him, relief from  Unesco was urgently necessary, in view of the  large number of refugees in the Middle East. It  was Unesco’s duty to assist them in its special  field, as the Food and Agriculture Organization  and the World Health Organization were doing  in theirs. He therefore asked that the Programme,  document 3C/5, should be suitably amended.  The only effect of referring the question to the  Official and External Relations Commission would  be to throw the responsibility of the Sub-Com-  mission on Reconstruction upon a Commission  which was not concerned with the matter and  which did not appear to be competent to suggest  alterations in the programme.  The CHAIRMAN was sorry that he could not  admit that argument; the resolution had been  communicated to the Official and External Rela-  tions Commission because it raised the question of  relations with the Specialized Agencies. However,  there was no reason why a joint meeting should  not be arranged to deal with the matter.  After a discussion between the delegates of the  UNITED STATES of AMERICA and AUSTRALIA  on the advisability of the amendment in question,  Dr. DRZEWIESKI (Head of the Department of  Reconstruction) stated that the Secretariat had  already considered the Sub-Commission's under-  standable anxiety about assistance for refugees in  the Middle East. He had ascertained what were  the financial resources for the current year and  had secured the assistance of a member of the  Indian delegation to spend a fortnight surveying  the needs of refugees in the Arab countries. He  had also cabled to the World University Alliance  in Geneva, asking for assistance to refugee stu-  dents in those countries. He concluded by  reading a telegram from the United Nations,  announcing the formation of an organization to  assist refugees in the Middle East.
