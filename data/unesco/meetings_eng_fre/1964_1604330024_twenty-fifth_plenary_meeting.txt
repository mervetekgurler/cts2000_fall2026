4,1 Mr, HICKS (Canada);  Mr, President, the Canadian delegation has serious reservations both about the con-  cepts which are set forth in the draft resolution which was originally 13 C/24, and the work  plan attached to that resolution, which would require Unesco to shift this work in accord-  ance with the central ideas in the draft resolution. We also feel that they have not been  altered substantially in the rewording of draft resolution 72, which is actually before us now.
4,2 As for the resolution itself, the Canadian delegation is disappointed to find that a meas-  ure which proposes to set out guidance for.the conduct of relations between nations should  give such prominence to the concept of "peaceful coexistence", which to some members of  this body has a static and partisan connotation, On a number of occasions in the past, in  both the United Nations General Assembly and other United Nations bodies, attempts have  been made to gain support for the proclaimed doctrine of " peaceful coexistence”, but with-  out exception these have been rejected. Indeed, there has been wide acceptance, including  agreement in the General Assembly of the United Nations itself, of the more dynamic and  practical concept of friendly relations and co-operation among States,
4.3 The Canadian delegation, like other delegations here, is anxious, indeed determined,  to see progress made in the improvement of relations between all States, but considers  that this must be done on a positive and forward-looking basis, and be carried out through  a wide range of practical and useful measures, rather than by the adoption of slogans which  tend to divert attention from our main tasks.
