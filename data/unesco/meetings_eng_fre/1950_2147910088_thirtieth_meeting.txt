[1] Mr. JAWAD (Iraq) recalled what he had  previously said in the Budget Committee, namely  that he had asked for an increased budget item  for educational assistance to Arab refugees in  Palestine, and failing that, the provision of at  least $150,000 for this purpose to be requested of  the Programme and Budget Commission. Iraqi  delegation considered that this matter was a  responsibility which Unesco could not evade, and  which was for the time being far more pressing  than the issue of documents or the erection of  public monuments.  [2] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) poin-  ted out that Unesco had worked on the principle  with regard to relief assistance that, since the  funds available were never adequate to the needs,  Member Governments should primarily be encour-  aged to subscribe to such purposes on their own.  Last year, for example, while Unesco had contri-  buted $60,000 to this end, the Governments of  France, the United States of America and the  United Kingdom had contributed far bigger sums.  The important thing was that the flow should not  be interrupted.  The resolution in document 5C/120 encouraged  the Director-General to do more, but still left it  to Member States and their national organizations  to do the main work.  [3] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Administrative  Management and Budget) pointed out that the  Committee was not concerned with document
5C/120, which had already been adopted by the  Programme and Budget Commission, and in any  case had no budgetary implications.  [4] If the delegation of Iraq maintained that  $150,000 were needed to provide for both adequate  educational facilities and scholarships, as sug-  gested in its draft resolution, the Committee had  two alternative courses: either to instruct the  Director-General to use $150,000 of the budget of  $188,000 provided under the Relief Assistance  Programme, or to decide that those $188,000  should remain available for war and other emer-  Miss CaroL Laise (United States of America)  gency relief, and that a sufficient sum be added  to the 1951 budget for Arab refugee problems.  [5] Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France) asked whe-  ther the sum of $150,000 could be found within  the economies already made on the $8,200,000  budget, or whether the Budget would have to be  raised or new economies made. If they adopted  the first proposal suggested by Mr. Weitz, the  amounts available for emergency relief would be  seriously depleted. He wondered whether this  could not be ranked among the special projects  concerning which the Director-General could  appeal for funds from public or private insti-  tutions outside the regular budget.  [6] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) thought  that, as had been indicated by Dr. Drzewieski at  a previous meeting, they might be able to find  approximately $60,000 for this purpose out of the  fund of $188,000, as had been done in past years.  [7] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Administrative  Management and Budget) said there was nothing  sacrosanct about the way in which this fund was  distributed by the Executive Board. It had in fact  been allocated on an increasingly broad basis  over the past four years.  [8] In answer to a question from the ChAIR-  MAN, Mr. WEITZ added that it would be in order  for the Committee to point out that the only alter-  native to dropping other items or increasing the  budget, would be to fall back on the $188,000  fund.  [9] Commenting on a suggestion from Mr. JA-  WAD (Iraq) that they should recommend drop-  ping certain items with less priority, Sir Ronald  ADAM (United Kingdom) pointed out that this  was impossible now that the programme and  budget had been approved.  [10] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) also thought it  would be impraticable, in the light of the fixed  policy of the Conference to limit the funds for  relief assistance during the past three years and  to prohibit transfers for its increase, to suggest  augmenting that fund now. The question was  whether the Committee was accepting the sum                                   of $150,000 without further examination. For  himself, Mr. WEEDEN feared that, the more  Unesco contributed directly to this purpose, the  more it would prejudice the chance of successful  appeals as contemplated in document 5C/120.  [11] In reply to a question from the CHAIRMAN,  Mr. JAWAD (Iraq) suggested that, in the first  resort, his delegation would put the figure needed  at $250,000 rather than $150,000.  [12] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) wanted to  know whether the main operative clause of para-  graph (a) was to instruct the Director-General to  provide education facilities, or to co-operate with  other authorities and agencies, since the figure  would not be the same in either case. For the  past five years, it had been the policy of the  General Conference to finance relief projects of  this type by only a token amount, and to rely on  the Organization's ability to raise outside funds  for the bulk of the needs. The Committee was  embarking on a question of substance.  [13] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) reinforced the  plea put forward by Mr. Weitz for more infor-  mation, and thought they should confine them-  selves to the provision of co-operation.  [14] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) wanted to know  the relationship of the proposed project to the  Klatt Plan of the United Nations. The United  Nations had already made available $40,000,000  for Middle East relief, and France, the United  States of America and the United Kingdom had  in addition contributed large sums during the  past year.  [15] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) did not know to  what extent the United Nations funds were to be  devoted to educational needs as opposed to general  relief,  [16] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) pro-  posed a statement on the following lines:  The Budget Committee having considered the main  document and the amendment in the light of previous  policy, consider that a sum of not less than $150,000  IS necessary to provide the relief services required, and  understand that of this amount about $60,000 would  in the normal way be obtained from the budget appro-  priation of $188,000.  [17] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) suggested that  the reference to a total figure be omitted, since  they Were unfortunately unable to obtain detailed  figures at this stage.  [18] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) agreed that the
065  question of cost was a very difficult one. He sug-  gested that they should rather say that $150,000  was the most reasonable cost beyond which they  should not go.  [19] Professor FANTAPPIE (Italy) was afraid  that the adoption of such a figure now might  upset the balance of the budget.  [20] Mr. JAWAD (Iraq) did not agree that there  was such a danger. Resolution 5C/51 included  an appeal for funds to competent authorities and  agencies, and there had not so far been any  question of exceeding the budget limits,  [21] The CHAIRMAN suggested that, since the  problem was to decide what instructions to give  the Director-General, they might accept the pro-  posal put forward by the United Kingdom dele-  gate, amended by the delegate of Australia, stating  that they were unable to discover the true bud-  getary implications but that, within the sums  available for emergency relief, up to $150,000  might be allocated for this purpose.  [22] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) sug-  gested that the $60,000 made available last year  might be mentioned, and that some of the scholar-  ships could be provided out of this.  [23] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) stated empha-  tically that he could not commit himself without  knowing more about the relationship of this pro-  ject to the Klatt Plan. The sum of $150,000  was a drop in the ocean compared with the  $40,000,000 voted by the United Nations for this  relief and by other countries individually. His  Government would want to know what additional  purpose could be served by this sum.  [24] Mr. JAWAD (Iraq) suggested that the  United Nations fund was for general relief and  not intended to cover educational needs. There  were 200,000 children of school age in Palestine  for whom no schools were available.  [25] The CHAIRMAN pointed out that a new  fund was now being started by the United  Nations, and that the Director-General had already  approached the Secretary-General of the United  Nations with a suggestion that some of it be used  for providing educational facilities. She sug-  gested that the Committee might recommend that  something be done by Unesco if it appeared that  the United Nations plan included no provision  for such facilities.  [26] Mr. JAWAD (Iraq) said he was willing to  accept this proposal and the figure of $150,000.  [27] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) moved that the  Chairman be asked to report to the Programme                 and Budget Commission in the light of the pro-  posal put forward by the United Kingdom dele-  gate and of the discussion thereon.  [28] It was agreed that the Chairman would  present to the Programme and Budget Commis-  sion a report on their discussion with a recom-
566  mendation that a maximum of $150,000 be  allocated for the purpose of educational assistance  to the Arab refugees, in the event of the United  Nations plan not including adequate provision  for this purpose.  The meeting rose at 10.30 a.m.
