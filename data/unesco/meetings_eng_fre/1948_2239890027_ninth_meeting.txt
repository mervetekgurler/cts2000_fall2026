The meeting rose at 11 a.m.  MEETING  Monday, 29 November 1948, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman: Hamid Bey FRANGIE (Lebanon)  Decision of the Credentials Committee on the  Credentials of the Czechoslovak delegation.  The SECRETARY observed that the Credentials  Committee had studied the question and that its  report was before the meeting; the matter would  also be dealt with in the afternoon by the Pro-  cedure Committee. It was essential that a deci-  sion should be taken on the reports of both Com-  mittees by the plenary meeting, before the  meeting of the Nominations Committee on the  afternoon of 30 November.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) asked  if the report was considered to accept in principle  the fact that the credentials of a delegation were  valid, even if it was stated, in transmitting them,  that the delegation had no intention of attending  the Conference.  The SECRETARY replied that the question was  determined by Article IV. A. of the Constitution.  He pointed out, however, that a proposal from an  absent delegation was not valid.  M. SEYDOUX (France) observed that that pro-  vision of the Constitution might lead to the curious  result that all delegations had been legally appoin-  ted by their governments, but none of them  attended the Conference. He therefore suggested  adding to the report of the Credentials Committee  the following words :  The Committee draws the attention of the General  Conference to the consequences which might result,  in practice, from the provision of Article IV. A.  Adopted.  The General Committee agreed that the report  of the Credentials Committee should be referred
229  to the Procedure Committee, and that both reports  should be submitted to the Conference for approval  the following morning.  Amendments to the Constitution of Unesco  (proposal of the delegation of India).  The SECRETARY drew attention to Article XIII  of the Constitution, stipulating that :  The draft texts of proposed amendments shall be  communicated by the Director-General to the Member  States at least six months in advance of their consider-  ation by the General Conference.  Professor KABIR (India) stated that the Indian  delegation was aware of that rule; that was why  the head of the delegation had requested the  Director-General to have the proposed amendments  circulated to Member States. It had, however,  been felt that a preliminary, informal discussion  of the matter by the Conference would be useful;  otherwise, each Member State would have to take  a decision without knowing the views of the  others.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) asked  the delegate of India what would happen 1f the
1948 Conference signed a six-year contract with  the new Director-General, and the Fourth Session  of the General Conference then adopted the second  amendment proposed by the Indian delegation  to Article VI, paragraph 2, of the Constitution,  substituting the words “not less than three, but  not more than four, years” for “six years”, and  the words “shall not be eligible” for “shall be  eligible”.  Professor KABIR (India) replied that his dele-        gation had never intended the amendment to be  retroactive.  Count Stefano JACINI (Italy) thought it would  be disagreeable for the new Director-General to  know that his contract was longer than the usual  term settled by the Conference.  M. SEYDOUX (France) supported the delegate  of Italy and asked the Indian delegation not to  press for a discussion of the second amendment  by the current session of the Conference.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) seconded the  request of the French delegation.  Professor KABIR (India) agreed not to press  his proposal.  The CHAIRMAN suggested that the Director-  General should be requested to circulate the whole  proposal to Member States, and that the first  amendment proposed (to paragraph 9 of Article IV  of the Constitution) :  The General Conference shall meet in ordinary ses-  sion every alternate year.  should be submitted to the Chairman of the Pro-  cedure Committee, with a request for it to be  discussed informally, if the Committee's agenda  was not too heavy.  The Chairman’s proposal was adopted by seven  votes to four.  Proposal by the New Zealand delegation that  the Ordinary Session of the General Confer-  ence should be held every second year in  Paris.  At the suggestion of the CHAIRMAN, it was  agreed to refer the proposal to the special Com-  mittee which was to be set up to consider the  preparations for the next session of the General  Conference.  Other Business.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) asked  the Director-General for information about the
230  possibility of closing the session of the Conference  a day or two earlier, in view of the progress  already made.  Replying on behalf of the Director-General,  M. MONTAGNIER (Head of the Bureau of Con-  ference Planning) stated that everything depended  on the work of the Budget Sub-Commission, but  that, in any case, it would not be possible to save  more than twenty-four hours.  Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) recalled that  the same question had been considered at Mexico  City. He hoped that, at the stage the Conference  had reached, no attempt would be made to shorten  it: the meetings of the Budget and Programme  Sub-Commissions were so complicated and so im-  portant that no attempt should be made to hasten  their work. It was not worth while, in any case,  trying to shorten the Conference by twenty-four  hours: and, if it was suggested that it might be  shortened by three or four days, Mr. Hardman  would oppose the suggestion on the ground that  it would be slightly discourteous to Unesco's Le-  banese hosts.  The DIRECTOR GENERAL pointed out that  there was no question whatever of saving more  than twenty-four hours and, even if that was done,  delegates would remain for the last day or so to  attend the reception given by the President of the  Republic of Lebanon.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) observed  that his delegation had merely asked for infor-  mation and took no stand in the matter.  M. SEYDOUX (France) asked what the kExe-  cutive Board was to consider at its afternoon  meeting.  The SECRETARY recalled that the General Com-  mittee had decided that a special committee should  be set up to consider preparations for the next  Session of the Conference; that Committee would  be constituted the following day and, in the mean-  time, the Executive Board, which was responsible  for the working plans of the General Conference,  would prepare proposals for consideration by the  special Committee.  The meeting rose at 10.35 a.m.    Il
