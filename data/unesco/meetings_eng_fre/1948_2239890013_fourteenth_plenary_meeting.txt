26. Consideration of resolution submitted  by the Joint Committee of Fifteen  regarding the Department of Exchange  of Information.  The PRESIDENT : I call upon the Rapporteur  of the Joint Committee of Fifteen to explain the  resolution before us.  Mr. A. J. A. NELSON (Australia) (Rapporteur  of the Joint Committee of Fifteen): Mr. President.  [ have the honour to submit to the Conference  that part of the Report of the Joint Committee of  Fifteen which deals with the resolution, set out  in document 3C/91, on the Department of Fx-  change of Information.     (°°) See Volume II, Section X, p. 45.     The Committee discussed at some length the  project labelled in the Budget Estimates “World  Centre for the Exchange of Information”, and  came to the conclusion that the title of the pro-  ject was misleading, since the activities budgeted  are primarily centralized services for the Secre-  tariat, such as the maintenance of the library.  archives, statistical unit, publications and sales  division. It was established that these facilities  are designed primarily for internal use and not  for use by the general public. The Committee  agreed therefore that the title “World Centre  should be modified.  It further agreed that reference, documentation,  publication, and statistical services, on a continu-  ing basis are essential to the work of Unesco, and  do not necessarily need programme authorization.
150            But the Committee was convinced also that the  danger of duplication, by the Department of  Exchange of Information, of the work of other  Programme Departments necessitates investiga-  tion, and it unanimously passed the resolution  now before the Conference.  This resolution was forwarded to the Admi-  nistrative Commission, and referred by it to the  Conference without comment. At the same time  the Committee of Fifteen was asked by the Chair-  man of the Administrative Commission to make  it quite clear, in reporting this resolution, that it  was not the purpose of the Committee to recom-  mend curtailment of essential services of Unesco,  but rather to indicate that the present organ-  ization of these services should be re-examined  with a view to eliminating the danger of dupli-  cation.  The PRESIDENT : Does anyone wish to speak?  Mr. A. A. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa):  When this resolution was discussed in the Admi-  nistrative Commission, there was a division of  opinion, and we did not think it advisable to go  into a matter which had been carefully considered  by another Commission. For that reason the  Commission passed this on without comment.  Since that meeting, most of us have had time to  reconsider it and it does seem to me that there is  really no difference of opinion as far as the sub-  stance of the resolution is concerned. It is only  a matter of wording.  I therefore propose that, in order to avoid any  misunderstanding of our intention, this reso-  [ution should stop after the date “1949”.  It is understood that the full minutes of the  Commission, which went into this matter so  fully, will be made available and that the Direc-  tor-General will take account of these findings.  But many others feel that it would be undesirable  to appear to prejudge the findings of the Director-  General. I submit that there is no danger what-  ever of his overlooking these facts, which have  so pertinently been brought to his notice. On  the other hand, once those facts are brought to  his notice, it is desirable that we merely ask him  to investigate, without saying, in effect, that we  expect him to bring about a reduction in the  scope of the Department's activities, especially  those concerned with the provision of information  services to the Programme Departments.  In order to make the position of my own dele-  gation quite clear, I want to say that this infor-  mation service is regarded by us in South Africa  as one of the most important functions of Unesco,  so that when we support this motion for an inves-  tigation, we do it with no desire to curtail legi-     - timate information services, but we do feel that
151  here, as in all the other services rendered by  Unesco, there is the greatest need for economy,  both of effort and of money. 1 move then,  Mr. President, as an amendment to this motion  or, if it has not been formally moved and  seconded, I would move as a substantive motion,  that the resolution read :  The General Conference invites the Director-General  to reconsider as soon as possible the organization and  title of the Department of Exchange of Information,  and the work-plan suggested for 1949.  The PRESIDENT : Will anyone second the  motion ?  The delegate of the United States of America  seconds the motion.  The delegate of New Zealand wishes to speak.  Mr. M. H. HOLCROFT (New Zealand)  Mr. President, the New Zealand delegation 1s  opposed to any curtailment of this resolution.  If the original resolution is curtailed, it will not  convey to the Director-General the sentiments of  the Joint Committee of Fifteen. Those senti-  ments were formed in a long and exhaustive dis-  cussion. The functions of the Department for the  Exchange of Information were explained in  detail. It was clear from the nature of the dis-  cussion which followed this explanation that  delegates were not satisfied. They were agreed  that there should be no curtailment of essential  information services for Unesco, but they believed  that a central department with a large staff must  be in danger of encroaching on functions which  can be carried out more efficiently by Programme  Departments. They believed that there should be  a clear distinction between central and programme  services. They were not convinced that the cen-  tral information section required a large number  of specialists. It was in this mood that the reso-  lution was adopted. If it is now amended, no  indication will be given as. to why the title and  organization of the Department should be recon-  sidered. The resolution will, in fact, become  innocuous. The Committee of Fifteen reached  this decision after a long discussion, which can-  not be repeated here. The New Zealand dele-  gation believes that the resolution should stand,  without alteration, as an expression of opinion  reached by a competent body with a full sense  of responsibility.
