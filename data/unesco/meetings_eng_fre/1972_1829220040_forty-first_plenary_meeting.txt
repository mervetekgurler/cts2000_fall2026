4.1 Mr, MOLDT (German Democratic Republic): :  Mr, President, distinguished delegates, the General Conference of Unesco at its  seventeenth session has just decided to admit the German Democratic Republic to member-  ship of the United Nations Educational, Scientific and Cultural Organization. This decision  is a timely one, It is a significant move towards universality in multilateral co-operation  and marks a turning point in the relations between the German Democratic Republic and the  organizations of the United Nations system,
4.2 On behalf of the Government of the German Democratic Republic, I should warmly  thank all delegations which in the spirit of Unesco's Constitution have supported the German  Democratic Republic's admission, I also extend this gratitude to the President of the  seventeenth session of the General Conference, Mr. Toru Haguiwara, the Chairman of the  Executive Board, Mr. Prem Kirpal, and the Director-General of the Organization, Mr, René  Maheu, for all the efforts they have made. We thank the thousands of cultural workers all  over the world who have supported the participation, on an equal footing of the German  Democratic Republic in Unesco's work,
4,3 Permit me to reaffirm the willingness of my country, declared in its application, to  do all it can to co-operate in the attainment of Unesco's purposes. The German Democratic  Republic has always considered that its membership of Unesco should in the first place be  understood as an obligation for constructive co-operation.
4.4 At all times, the rôle of representatives of the intellectual world has been measured by  their perso:al commitment to social progress and understanding among the peoples. The  more effectively Unesco works in this sense, the more its authority will increase, In ac-  cordance with the basic aims of Unesco, the German Democratic-Republic takes thé view  that fruitful international co-operation in the fields of education, science and culture is only  possible if cultural progress is not prevented or even annihilated by war, colonial oppres-  sion or discrimination against peoples, ; .
4.5 For this reason we give full support to the multiple efforts by Unesco to'make a speci-  fic contribution towards strengthening international security and towards eliminating all  forms of colonialism and racism. This seventeenth session of the General Conférence now  coming to its end has recorded its will to promote European and world-wide policies of  détente in the interests of Unesco's humanitarian objectives, = )
4.6 The German Democratic Republic will also play its-part in bringing life into the Organi-  zation's programmatic ‘decisions at the international level, aro
4,7 Mr, President, distinguished delegates, living up to the Constitution of Unesco, the  German Democratic Republic enables all its citizens to have access to the treasures of  culture and science. The right of our citizens to education and to participate in cultural  life, irrespective of social status, of race or sex, is not only fixed in our Constitution,  but has become a living reality of social life. Respect for the dignity of man, esteem for  the cultural achievements of other peoples, as well as the striving for peace and inter-  national understanding, are binding aims of the educational policies in our Republic.
4.8 Our country devotes particular attention to the education of youth, Therefore we are  happy to be hosts to the World Festival of Youth and Students in the snmmer of 1973, a  festival whose motto is "international understanding among young people and their joint  action for peace".
4.9 The policy followed by the Government of the German Democratic Rzpublic in the  fields of science and culture aims at increasing the material and intellectual wealth of the  people, At the same time our Republic is willing to make a contribution to developing  peaceful international relations by means of culture and scientific research, By participa-  ting in the international exchange of experiences in scientific fields within Unesco, my  country expects an enrichment of the knowledge of its scientists, and of its educatjonal  and cultural workers, For its part, the German Democratic Republic is ready to contri-  bute to the successful solution of common tasks and problems in the fields of education,  science and culture,
4,10 On this occasion permit me to remind you of the fact that the German Democratic  Republic - although until today it has not been a member of Unesco - has supported the  Organization's work by many activities in the past, activities in which the Commission for  the work of Unesco of the German Democratic Republic has played an important part since
1955, Let me quote, as an example only, "International Book Year' and the participation  of experts in Unesco's scientific projects. We were also happy to receive in our country  representatives of the International Commission for the Development of Education,
4,11 Our Republic is in many ways assisting the countries which have liberated themselves  from colonialism to develop modern educational systems. At present thousands of students,  post-graduates and skilled workers from almost all developing countries are being trained  in our country. As a member of Unesco the German Democratic Republic can co-operate  now in a more comprehensive way in implementing the tasks of Unesco,
4.12 The admission of the German Democratic Republic to Unesco falls effectively in line  with the efforts to place the relations among States on the solid foundation of the principles  of sovereign equality, non-interference and mutually beneficial co-operation, It emphasizes  the viability and effectiveness of these principles, which, in spite of various kinds of oppo-  sition, assert themselves in international practice, Unesco has set an example which will  be followed by other organizations.
4,13 The Socialist States, amongst them the German Democratic Republic, are striving  congistently for peace and international security. With satisfaction we state the fact that  efforts are being increased throughout the world to eliminate war as a means of solving  international conflicts. Above all, the hotbeds of conflict at present still existing in Indo-  china and the Middle East must be liquidated on the basis of the right to self-determination  of the peoples.
4.14 There can be no doubt that the admission of the German Democratic Republic to  Unesco will have also a favourable impact on the détente in Europe which led to a number  of important treaties, I only draw your attention to the Moscow Treaty between the Union  of Soviet Socialist Republic and the Federal Republic of Germany, the Warsaw Treaty be-  tween the Polish People's Republic and the Federal Republic of Germany in 1970, and to  the Treaty on the bases of relations between the German Democratic Republic and the Fed-  eral Republic of Germany, which was initialled recently.
4,15 In the interests of security and co-operation between peoples and States on an equal  footing, today's admission to Unesco of the German Democratic Republic will contribute to  bringing about relations between the German Democratic Republic and the Federal Republic  of Germany on the bases of peaceful coexistence and sovereign equality. The people of the  German Democratic Republic regard this decision taken by the seventeenth session of the  General Conference also as appreciation of their own efforts for peace, relaxation of ten-  sion and social progress. :
4,16 The German Democratic Republic is certain that its co-operation in Unesco on an  equal footing will also benefit its own people, For its part it is firmly determined to live  up to the requiraments of Unesco's Constitution.
4.17 In conclusion, permit me once again cordially to thank all those who have rendered  us their support. In saying this, ladies and gentlemen, I would express the desire for fruit-  ful co-operation with you for the benefit of Unesco and its Member States, Thank you very  much.

11.2 This session of the General Conference has been marked by the participation of China,  Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Bahrain and Bangladesh, and the admission today of the  German Democratic Republic, Such an enlargement of the Unesco family makes our Organi-  zation more universal, and opens wider perspectives of better understanding and fruitful  co-operation among the nations of our planet, We all look forward to significant contribu-  tions from China, which has just been elected to the Executive Board, and whose people and  civilization have always occupied an important place in world history,
11.3 As our work comes to an end, we gather our tools in the hope of putting them ta as  good if not better use in the future: not only the physical equipment and facilities that have  made our task easier to accomplish but, more important still, the tools which life itself  has given us - our vision, our instincts, our thoughts, our words and recollections so that,  out of our common ideals, labour and experience, we may fashion a better, kinder and hap-  pier world. It is this memory of what we have done and what we can do that will enable us  to come closer to realizing "the objectives of international peace and of the common welfare
41  of mankind. For as the poet has said, ''God gives us memory so that we may have roses  in December", no
11.4 It may be said that in this age of computers and missiles there is no place for the  poet. But is the poet not a prophet? And is it not his dream of the future that makes man  strive for something bigger than himself, for a world bigger than his own? Let ns, there-  fore, incorporate and preserve the dreams of man in Unesco, for it is our vision and the  things of the spirit that will keep us together and make nur work endure,
11,5 We have continued during the past weeke the tasks begun by our predecessors. But .  in a world of constant changes and new challenges, we have responded by reviewing our ob-  jectives, utilizing our resources to the utmost, and improving our methods of work. Thus,  we have approved a budget of $120, 000, 000 - huge compared to what it was four years ago,  but insignificant compared to the billions of dollars spent by the nations of the world in one  day, one single day, on armaments and weapons whose ultimate effect can only be the des-  truction of man. The sum of $120, 000, 000 - Unesco's budget for the next two years - is  just like a pebble cast into the ocean of needs of more than two billion human beings. Small  as this amount is, however, we would like to see it, like the pebble, have an ever-widening  effect on the peoples and countries where it may be spent. That is why it is so important  that the maximum use and the greatest benefit should be derived from it. And this, too, ls  the reason why we hope that the Executive Board, whose membership has just been ircreas-  ed, will not only be more representative of Member States, but also more responsive and  better able to attain the objectives of Unesco.
11,6 Finally, to ensure that the activities of the Organization are in line with the needs of  its members, we have approved a resolution setting forth guidelines on the form and con-  tent of its draft programme and budget and of the Medium-Term Outline Plan, as well as  effective working methods for the Unesco General Conference,

17.1 Mr. JONES (United States of America):  Mr. President, I should like to talk briefly this morning of my impressions of two  things. First, what Unesco has come to be and what is its future. In looking back over the  past five weeks, hesides remembering the glories of this beautiful city of Paris, and of  the many friends that I have made during the conference, I must soberly reflect on what  Unesco has come to be,
17.2 Since its creation, Unesco has come to be an organization little resembling that of -  its founders and it has unfortunately come to be an organization which does not seem to be  solely concerned with its great mission - namely to promote intellectual co-operation in  education, science, culture and communication throughout the world. But, then one may  say, the world has changed - it little resembles that world of 1946 which did not reflect in  its international relations the equal participation of so much of the world. We must admit  that Unesco in 1946 was largely reflective of only a small part of mankind, But, the point  is whether this broadening of the international relations of the world, this active participa-  tion by representatives of all continents, of all cultures, of all races makes the original  ideals of Unesco obsolète and useless. I, speaking for those whom .I represent, do not  believe so. The ideal of a non-political organization concerning itself with bringing man-  kind together, concerning itself with promoting education, science, culture and communi-  cation to all of mankind is not, I think, obsolete. Rather, that concept, it seems to me,  is still vital, necessary, and unfortunately still largely unattained. The ideal of an inter-  national organization devoted to bridging differences - whether they be of ideology, race,  continental locale, status of economic development, religion, national origin, ethnic belief .  or difference is more vital now than ever before, And why is it more vital? It is precise-  ly becaus the world'has broadened, because we do now have representatives of all contt-  nents, races and cultures that the task is infinitely more difficult, that the task is infinite-  ly more essential.
17.3 But let us look back over the past weeks.. We note a strong trend towards introducing  politics into our debates which deeply exposes us to losing sight of our efforts toward  harmony, our willingness to overlook differences, our efforts at establishing a dialogue,  and our desire to submerge ideology. There must, of course, there must always be differ-  ences; we can never have perfect harmony, But we must begin some place. Unesco was  created to be the place where we, as a world of one common human species could actively  and vigorously work to set the example, to lead the way to harmony and compromise. Yet  every major political issue before the world today found its way, one way or the other, into this  forum. And what do these political moves accomplish? Can Unesco ever really be a forum  where political differences can be solved - and if it is not for the purpose of seeking a solu-  tion, then what is the purpose of bringing these issues into this forum?
17.4 My Government and the Government of Canada have taken the position that we will not  bring into the Unesco forum any question which is of a political nature and which should be  dealt with in another organ of the United Nations system:, Everyone here knows very well  that, no matter how difficult our relations may be with a particular country, we will never  mention these problems in this forum. This policy, of course, puts us at a certain dis-  advantage since other countries do not seem to have the same misgivings. But no matter  how great the provocation, we will not discuss the substance of political problems because  they simply do not belong in Unesco. The delegations which have brought such’ political  problems for discussion by the General Conference of Unesco are doing our Organization  great harm and we often wonder if Unesco will be able to overcome these difficulties.
17.5 We have also seen Unesco turn more and more into a developmental ‘organization. It  is perfectly clear to us that this is what the lesser developed nations want. And we can see  that there is a legitimate need along these lines, But we see words and votes for higher  budgets, while we do not see a corresponding concern for’ efficiency, evaluation, or for a  realistic view that growth must conform to the economic realities of the world - it cannot  always be up. Development programmes that depend on the contributions of" the | Tore de-  veloped nations must be with the consent and goodwill of those countries. Goodwill and
14  understanding, which must be a co-operative effort, should not be sacrificed.on the altar of  the ability to receive a majority vote. This must be a co-operative effort. The peoples of  the developed nations, from whose taxes dollar funds must come, should not be alienated.  It simply is not realistic to expect these people to see their countries continually criticized  and still to be willing to‘offer part of their tax dollar to provide assistance, and I say this  in a spirit of co-operation and a willingness to be of assistance. Therefore, if Unesco is  to be successful, its efforts to assist in development must be in a spirit of goodwill, it  must be within the framework of its original purpose. .
17.6 And so in summary, Unesco unfortunately has become an essentially political organi-  zation, It does not seem to be moving forward in bringing mankind closer together; per-  haps it is moving in reverse. Unesco has come,to be an organization mainly concerned with  development and this may be perfectly legitimate. Yet there is little concern for a systema-  tic, well-planned, careful approach. Rather there is a belief that growth and increased  budgets are the sole panacea. There is little evidence that in its efforts to pursue develop-  ment the interests of the principal donors are being involved, Further, Unesco seems to  be moving toward imposing regulations, I feel that this was manifested most directly in'the  area of communication, the area which, in many ways, can do the most to further mutual  understanding among nations and peoples. - A
17.7 We are also somewhat disturbed by a statement made early in our deliberations which  seemed to imply that Unesco's Constitution, in so far as it speaks of the question of the free  flow of information and ideas, is outdated and therefore is to be questioned, This, in our  view, is not only a contradiction of the Constitution which has stood the test of time but is  also a contradiction of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and the United Nations  Charter itself,
17.8 We understand, equally, the desire of so many States to preserve and develop their  own cultural heritage, However, I think one must reflect upon the possible dangers of  building fences around national borders, Cultures flourish and are nurtured by mutual inter-  change, As stated in the very excellent Faure Report, each must be able to preserve his  authentic-identity and, finally, through each individual's feeling that he belongs to the entire  species of mankind. In this way, the twin poles of the singular, which is irreducible, and  the universal, comprising diversity within identify, will achieve expression, Both Canada  and the United States represent multi-racial and multi-cultural societies and you will note  that Mr. LeBlanc, head of the delegation of Canada, and I both reflect this, In many res-  pects, this multi-racial, multi-cultural aspect has been one of the foundations of our  strength, our growth, and our elasticity. For we have benefited greatly from this cultural  interchange within our own borders, and because of this, perhaps we have been more re-  ceptive than most in welcoming international interchanges. We hope that such international  interchanges and exchanges will be assisted rather than hindered,
17.9 But I would like to end on a positive note, for there have been many positive features  to come out of this meeting. Many actions were sound: I point to the decision to take ac-  tion against drug abuse as a concrete example, Despite the obvious growing cleavage  between the lesser developed and the developed countries which is unfortunate, there were  instances where we all did work together and there were many instances of unanimous or  near unanimous action,
17.10 The manner in which this meeting was conducted by our distinguished President  has been exemplary. The Secretariat is to be commended for the arrangements amd for  the hard work of all the staff during these gruelling weeks. We have seen persons from all  areas, races and cultures working together, and this is heartening. But, I think, we must  not lose sight of our ultimate goals for we still have time and we must not let this golden  opportunity slip through our fingers. We must not let hope and progress flounder on the  rocks of emotion or disagreement. With goodwill and purpose - with a willingness to com-  promise and to recognize the interests of others - Unesco can be a viable organization, I
14  can assure you that we of the North American area want a viable Unesco - a non-political,  active, constructive, Unesco - and we will ‘continue to work i» make this dream a reality. .
