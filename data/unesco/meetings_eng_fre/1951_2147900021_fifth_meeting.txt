GENERAL C-OM M IT TEESE FIFTH MEETING Wednesday, 27 June 1951, at 9.30 a.m. Chairman: [1] The CHAIRMAN submitted to the General Committee the following draft time-table for the week:
(1) The Programme Commission, which was already behindhand with its work, would meet both morning and afternoon, every day.
(2) The other Commissions would meet at the following times: Wednesday, 27 June, morning: Procedure Com- mittee; afternoon: Administrative Commission. Thursday, 28 June, morning: Official and External Relations Commission; afternoon: Official and External Relations Commission. Friday, 29 June, morning: Official and External Relations Commission; afternoon: Administrative Commission. Saturday, 30 June, morning: Official and External Relations Commission; afternoon: Administrative Commission. [2] The above time-table had been drawn up in consultation with the Chairman of the different Commissions. If approved, the Chairman proposed that, in view of the Commissions’ extremely heavy agenda, the General Committee should fix the times of meetings at 10 a.m. and 3.30 p.m. [3] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL proposed that, as the Programme Commission had such a full agenda, it should meet at 9.30 a.m. and 3.30 p.m. It would then be possible to avoid evening meetings. [4] TAHA HUSSEIN Pasha (Egypt) would prefer meetings to be held at 10 a.m. and 3.30 p.m. [5] Mr. PIAGET (Chairman of the Programme Commission) pointed out that the Programme Commission was already seven meetings behindhand. He therefore seconded the Director-General’s pro- posal. [6] It was finally agreed that the Programme Commission should meet at 9.30 a.m. and 3.30 p.m., while the other Commissions would meet at 10 a.m. and 3.30 p.m. [7] Mr. BENDER (Chairman of the Budget Com- mittee) recalled that the General Committee had decided that the Budget Committee should not meet at the same time as the Administrative Commission. He would prefer the Budget Committee not to be Mr. Howranp Sarceant (United States of America) convened on Friday and Saturday afternoons, since the items to be considered at those meetings were not very urgent. [8] The above suggestion, seconded by the CHAIR- MAN and by Mr. van der STRATEN-WAILLET (Chairman of the Administrative Commission), was adopted. [9] The CHAIRMAN thought there would be no need for the General Committee to meet before the following Monday. [10] Sir John MAUD (United Kingdom) thought that a meeting should be planned provisionally for Friday. It could be cancelled if there were nothing to discuss on that day. [11] The proposal was supported by the CHAIR- MAN, and it was agreed to allow for a meeting of the General Committee on Friday, 29 June, at 9.30 a.m., it being understood that, if the meeting were held, the Programme Commission could start work under the Chairmanship of one of its Vice- Chairmen. [12] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL thought that the Programme Commission should settle a date for the discussion of the Special Fundamental Education Project and the Technical Assistance Programme; several delegations had already questioned the Secretariat on that subject. As the Special Project might affect the Technical Assistance Programme, he suggested that the Programme Commission should consider first the Programme, then the Special Project and, lastly, the Technical Assistance Pro- gramme. He added that he had invited the Directors- General of WHO and FAO, Dr. Chisholm and Mr. Dodd, to take part in the debates on those questions, and that they would be in Paris on 4 July, or thereabouts. [13] Mr. PIAGET (Chairman of the Programme Commission) accepted the Director-General’s sug- gestions and said that they would be borne in mind when the Programme Commission fixed its time- table. [14] The procedure suggested by the Director- General was adopted.      | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | | |
253 GENERAL [15] The CHAIRMAN announced that the World Jewish Congress, in accordance with the correct procedure, had submitted a statement to the Director- General on programme resolutions relating to Human Rights. He proposed that the statement, bearing the reference number 6C/11, should be referred to the Programme Commission, which could, if it considered it necessary, allow a represen- tative of the Congress to present its views. [16] The proposal was adopted. [17] Sir John MAUD (United Kingdom) recalled that the Procedure Committee, meeting the previous day, had appointed a working party to study the effects of the adoption of the principle of biennial sessions. He thought that the Administrative Com- mission should be asked to consider the financial and budgetary aspects of the question, so that a single report could be submitted to the General Conference. [18] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL was in favour of carrying Sir John Maud’s suggestion further by re- questing the Budget Committee to study the prob- lem of the form and presentation of the budget and asking the Administrative Commission also to present its views on that subject. A single report could then be submitted to the General Conference, COMMITTEE thus avoiding any regrettable differences of opinion. [19] Mr. BENDER (Chairman of the Budget Com- mittee) seconded the Director-General’s suggestions. [20] Mr. van der STRATEN-WAILLET (Chair- man of the Administrative Commission) asked whether the procedure suggested by the Director- General would mean that the problems involved would be considered successively by the working party, the Procedure Committee, the Budget Com- mittee and the Administrative Commission. He hoped that the working party’s terms of reference would be clearly defined. [21] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL thought that the working party should submit certain definite questions to the Budget Committee and the Adminis- trative Commission; it would then submit its report to them, in order that a final report might be pre- pared for presentation to the General Conference. If still closer co-ordination seemed desirable, a joint Committee could be convened when the final, single report was prepared. [22] On the CHAIRMAN's proposal, the Director- General's suggestions were adopted.
