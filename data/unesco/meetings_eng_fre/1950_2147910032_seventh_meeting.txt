Statement of Methods [document 5C/5 (I),  Rev., Part V].  [31] Sir John MAUD (Chairman, Executive  Board) reminded the meeting of the suggestion  made at the 1948 General Conference that a draft  code of policies should be framed. A draft code  was prepared and Member States were then  invited to suggest improvements. The 1949 Gen-  eral Conference suggested that some statement of  standing directives might be necessary.  In this connexion the Executive Board had  come to the conclusion that it was as yet too early  to frame a statement of standing directives.  Accordingly, the Board had drawn up a State-  ment of Methods, because it considered the time  had come to make some statement which should  not be binding on the Director-General or the  Executive Board, but which could give some in-  formation on the methods by which Unesco car-  ried out its work in the light of the experience of  the previous four years. Such a statement would  be useful:
1. To the General Conference, which in con-  sidering any resolutions would, by reference to  the Statement of Methods, be able to ascertain  easily whether or not such resolutions would call  for pioneering methods. If so, the General Con-  ference could note that some amendment to the  Statement of Methods would ensue. In this way,  it would save the time of the General Conference,  by obviating the necessity for any discussion of  the methods by which a resolution should be  implemented, and reduce the length of the annual  Programme by making any reference to methods  superfluous.
2. To Member States; the Director-General  and the Secretariat, in explaining the work and  methods of Unesco to the public, had emphasized  that the Statement of Methods was not intended  to restrict the freedom of the Director-General or  the Secretariat in any way. It was less definitive  than the resolutions of the Basic Programme or  of the Annual Programme. The Board intended  it to be one of Unesco’s basic documents which  would provide continuing guidance for the Gen-  eral Conference and do for methods what the  Basic Programme did for the work of Unesco.
