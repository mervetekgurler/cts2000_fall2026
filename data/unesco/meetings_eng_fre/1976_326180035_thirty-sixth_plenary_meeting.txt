(41.1) Mr. AL BASSAM (Iraq)(Translation from the Arabic):  Allow me, Mr. President, to explain my vote in favour of Parts I and II of resolution 19 C/PLEN/18  and my support for them at this stage. This imposes two tasks on me.
(41.2) First, I must reaffirm the Iraqi delegation's view that Unesco has an essential part to play in seek-  ing to establish a world peace based on right and justice, -to consolidate human rights and to ensure that  the peoples enjoy those rights on a basis of equality, social solidarity and international co-operation; and  that the struggle against colonialism and racialism in all their forms should be a principal means of  achieving those objectives.
(41.3) Secondly, I feel that some remarks are called for regarding the functions of the Drafting and Nego-  tiation Group in whose work I participated, and the principles governing its procedures. This body was  first established during the current session of the General Conference and by way of an experiment, so it  is reasonable that the plenary should have an opportunity to consider how well the experiment has worked.  My observations will be no more than a modest - and I trust appropriately self-critical - attempt to arrive  at a reasonably balanced evaluation of what has been achieved. First, then, a word about the Drafting and  Negotiation Group itself - and here I should like right away to express my appreciation of the skill and  dedication which the Chairman and my fellow-members of the Group brought to their task. On the nature  and performance of that task I have this to say: First, the nature of the Group's composition was not clear  when it started its work. Did it consist of Member States represented by the heads or other members of  their delegations; or of individuals selected by their delegations to work on the Group? It is no secret  that this question arose in another form with respect to the Executive Board, and I supported the first  viewpoint. But the distinguished Chairman of the Group, who participated as a member of the Executive  Board, in the preparation of document 19 C/2 Add. concerning the composition and methods of work of  the Group, endorsed the second viewpoint. Consequently, a member of the Group was entitled to designate  one or two advisers who were not actually entitled to participate in the Group's work. This adversely  affected a number of developing countries with small delegations - which sometimes grew even smaller  before the end of the Conference. Members drawn from these countries tended to be late for meetings  of the Group because they were busy with committee meetings, while others left Nairobi without there  being anyone else on their delegation available to replace them, so that ultimately the Group lost four or  five of those taking part in its work. Furthermore, some members kept making reservations on the  various draft resolutions pending their contacts with their governments, which usually took several days.  Their attitude was a confirmation of the view that they were representing their governments rather than  acting in a purely personal capacity. It is also to be noted that the title of the Group includes the word  ‘'hegotiation'', which is normally a matter for governments represented by their delegations. These ob-  servations, in my view, support the contention that the Group should comprise government representatives,  who could be replaced when necessary by fellow-members of their delegations. Secondly, the Chairman  of the Group stressed in his comments that the Group dealt only with the essence of questions and was not  bound by the documents submitted to it, including draft resolutions submitted, severally or collectively,  by Member States. I believe that the examination of questions in their essence does not necessarily mean  disregarding such documents as draft resolutions, which, being submitted by delegations on behalf of the  States concerned themselves, presumably deal with the essence of questions and are not just meaningless  pieces of paper. The fact that they emanate from the accredited delegations of States gives them a special  significance as an expression of those States' opinions and attitudes and, as such, they have an a priori  claim to be aceepted and considered, and not to be wholly or partially discarded except for good reasons  which ought to be mentioned in the reports presented on the draft resolutions. Indeed, it seems to me that  the Group cannot do its work properly unless it welcomes the draft resolutions submitted by Member States,  accords them special attention and bases its deliberations on a detailed and careful study of them. At a time when the voices of war propaganda, racism  and apartheid are becoming increasingly shrill and at a time when agents of regional and inter-  national tensions are everywhere on the march, the Philippine delegation supports and endorses  the draft declaration adopted on fundamental principles governing the use of the mass media in  strengthening peace and international understanding,
80.2 In this connection, Mr. President, may we define an area where the competence of Unesco  could help initiate and implement a continuing programme for the strengthening of peace and the  promotion of international understanding. We are thinking of an international programme for  journalists of Member States whereby media men are given the opportunity to make on-the-spot  reports on the activities and affairs of other countries, starting off at the regional level. In this  way they would be given a chance to see for themselves what is actually happening in a troubled  area, and would not have to rely on news reports coming off the ticker-tape and teleprinting  machines, This specific programme, I submit, is all the more welcome to member nations of  the Third World, to which we have the honour to belong, since many of us cannot afford to send  correspondents to cover every nook and cranny of the globe. As itis now, Mr. President, the  flow of information is often one-sided and unrepresentative, in the sense that news developments  are almost always written from the point of view of the originating country, not to mention the  point of view of multinational agencies. As the saying goes, ''seeing is believing'. In the area  of cultural exchange for instance, we also believe that credibility and better understanding would  be best achieved if say, a team of Kenyan broadcast journalists were given a chance to produce a  documentary programme about my country, the Philippines, which would be presented here in  Kenya in a manner which would project the Kenyan journalists' point of view, and the reverse  would also be true. The details and mechanics of such a scheme have yet to be worked out, but  the need for such a programme cannot be over-emphasized now. Mr, President, we believe that  this is one way in which the wisdom and competence of Unesco could be harnessed in the interests  of strengthening peace and international understanding as well as in combating war propaganda,  racism and apartheid. Thank you, Mr. President.

86.1 Mme PINTASILGO (Portugal). -  Monsieur le Président, je donnerai comme mes collègues une explication de vote à propos d'un  vote qui n'a pas eu lieu, J'aimerais dire quelques mots de l'importance que ma délégation attache à  cette résolution et à la déclaration que nous adopterons lors de la vingtième session de la Conférence  générale.
86.2 Ma délégation a essayé, au cours du débat qui a eu lieu en Commission III, d'évoquer les ques-  tions de fond que pose le rôle des mass médias dans le renforcement de la paix, Nous avons souligné  que, pour nous, il ne s'agissait pas seulement d'un affrontement de positions politiques, mais qu'il  y avait là d'autres questions, notamment un problème immédiat qui est le déséquilibre entrele Nord  et le Sud, Des résolutions ont été adoptées pendant la présente session de la Conférence générale,  que nous considérons comme des étapes vers la grande déclaration finale. Mais il reste la question  de fond qui, elle, est d'ordre épistémologique, sociologique, culturel, ainsi que d'ordre politique  stricto sensu. Et sans vouloir me ranger aux côtés de très grandes puissances, j'aimerais deman-  der à mon tour que l'intervention de ma délégation en Commission III soit reproduite dans les actes  de la Conférence générale,
86.3 Cela dit, nous aimerions faire deux petites suggestions, La première concerne le paragraphe 1  du dispositif de la résolution que nous venons d'adopter, Nous estimons tout d'abord que l'autorité  dont a fait preuve le Directeur général et les efforts qu'il a déployés à tous les stades, qu'il s'agisse  de la présente session ou des réunions et des échanges de vues qui l'ont précédée, permettent à  toutes les délégations de lui accorder leur entière confiance et de le remercier profondément, Tout  à l'heure, le délégué des Pays-Bas a parlé de la nécessité de faire appel, dans le cadre des consul-  tations élargies prévues, à des experts non gouvernementaux, et notamment, m'a-t-il semblé, ades  experts professionnels ; j'aimerais suggérer au Directeur général que cette consultation s'étende  aussi aux sociologues et à d'autres types d'experts, qui puissent envisager la question dans son en-  semble, y compris le rôle que les mass médias jouent en fait dans l'opinion publique et dans la for-  mation de la pensée.
86.4 Ma deuxième remarque concerne le paragraphe 2 du dispositif, où nous aurions souhaité qu'un  appel fût lancé aux Etats membres. Il est dit en effet dans ce paragraphe que le Directeur général  présentera aux Etats membres, en temps opportun, le projet de déclaration. Or l'expérience nous  à très souvent enseigné que les réunions d'''experts'' ou les consultations des Etats membres se  placent plutôt au niveau proprement politique qu'au niveau des experts. Aussi, voudrais-je lancer  un appel à tous les Etats membres pour que leurs réponses au Directeur général se situent non seu-  lement au niveau politique bien entendu, puisqu'elles viendront d'Etats, mais surtout au niveau cul-  turel, où se situe le rôle essentiel de l'Unesco. Merci, Monsieur le Président.
