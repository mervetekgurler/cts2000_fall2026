(2.1) Mr. VAKHRUSHEV (Union of Soviet Socialist Republics):  We voted for the draft resolution drawn up by the Drafting Committee, and supported the con-  structive amendments introduced during its discussion at the morning meeting. We consider that  the resolution adopted by the General Conference, despite some shortcomings, is a further con-  tribution by Unesco to the cause of peace and co-operation in Europe and elsewhere, and to the  struggle against colonialism, neo-colonialism and racism, We express our satisfaction that all  our basic proposals concerning this very important issue have been covered in this draft resolution.
(2.2) We only regret that the delegations of the United Kingdom, the People's Republic of China  and some other countries have not supported a number of the provisions of this resolution con-  cerning Unesco's contribution to the cause of peace and its task of eliminating colonialism, We  also regret that the representative of the People's Republic of China, during his two statements,  directed some remarks at the so-called super-Powers. Without desiring to go into the substance  of these remarks, we nevertheless wish to stress that, in so far as they concern us, they cannot  be accepted, since the foreign policy of the Soviet Union is a policy for peace, disarmament, the  dovetailing of international co-operation and opposition to colonialism, neo-colonialism and  racism. This policy was confirmed afresh in the peace programme adopted at our 24th Party  Congress, It is supported by the peoples of all nations and is being successfully executed. This  is not due to chance, for it expresses the very nature of the Soviet socialist system. This is why  our State has pursued it consistently since the triumph of the Great October Socialist Revolution.

20.1 Mr. BORGEN (Norway):  Mr, President, on behalf of the delegations of Denmark, Finland, Iceland, Sweden and  Norway, ! wish to explain our vote on the resolution which has just been adopted, It is well  known that the Nordic countries are strongly opposed to racialism and colonialism in all  their forms and manifestations, and on numerous occasions we have stated our opposition  to the policies of apartheid and racial oppression. As a consequence of that position we  endeavour to contribute as actively as possible to the United Nations efforts in this respect.  When the Declaration on the Granting of Independence to Colonial Countries and Peoples  was passed in the United Nations General Assembly in 1960, all the Nordic countries voted  for it, and we have tried in the international organizations to further the objectives of the  Declaration by supporting any action which appeared to us to be constructive and to be con-  sonant with the Constitutions of the organizations concerned. We are also fully aware of  the economic aspects of independence and we are trying to contribute within our means to  assist developing countries in their struggle against underdevelopment.
20,2 The Nordic countries agree on the desirability of gaining insight on the impact of  multinational corporations although we do not quite see how the ECOSOC resolution could  best be pursued. As far as disarmament is concerned we hope that Unesco's contribution  will be a programme where the main emphasis is made on providing all of us with factual  and objective information on this complex problem. As regards the liberation movements  in Africa, we regret that because of amendments proposed to the texts submitted by the  Drafting Committee we had to abstain, We wish to stress however, that we would all have  voted for the paragraph as proposed in the annex to document 17 C/109, without the later  amendments, We have, in fact, difficulty as régards the exact meaning of the word "'asso-  ciate' and we therefore preferred to leave the definition to the Executive Board. 20.3 However, in dealing with the complex set of problems referred to in the resolution,  we feel that we should always keep in mind the division of responsibility established within  the United Nations system, and that, while we appreciate the difficulty of drawing clear  lines of demarcation, Unesco is, and should continue to be, the Specialized Agency for
