The meeting rose at 10.20 a.m.  TENTH MEETING  Tuesday, 13 June 1950, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman:  Work of the Programme and Budget Com-  mission (continuation).  [1] Mr. SEYDOUX (Chairman, Programme and  Budget Commission) stated that the Commission  had completed its consideration of the Mass Com-  munications chapter the previous evening and that,  with the discussion of the work of the Relief  Assistance Services, consideration of all pro-  gramme resolutions would be concluded that  morning. The Commission would then turn its  attention to an Egyptian motion and various  other resolutions. The afternoon meeting would  deal with important resolutions on peace, which  might give rise to a lengthy discussion, and with  resolutions concerning Human Rights, publica-  tions and the general work of the Conference.  It would also be necessary to consider how to  deal with certain resolutions or certain parts of  the programme once the Budget Committee had  provided estimates for the new proposals sub-  mitted to it; it was already to be anticipated that  the total budget figure would exceed the stipulated  ceiling of $8,200,000.  [2] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL also thought that  the budget ceiling would be exceeded by some
200,000 dollars, and feared that the Programme  and Budget Commission would be unable to reach  any decision at the few remaining meetings with  regard to the necessary reductions. He sug-  gested the setting up of a small committee, to be  assisted by the Chairman of the Executive Board  and himself, and by the Chairmen of the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission and of the Bud-  get Committee; it would be instructed to lay  down an order of priority for resolutions and to  suggest the deletion of certain projects for which  no budgetary provision could be made, in the
269  Count Sterano Jacint (Italy)  light of the Programme and Budget Commission's  recommendation that larger funds should be  allocated to the most valuable projects.  The Director-General thought that this work  could be completed in two meetings, for the dis-  cussion could be restricted to certain specific  points instead of covering the whole programme.  [3] Sir John MAUD (Chairman, Executive Board)  stated that he, too, had considered the need for  singling out the items that were really worth  incorporating in the programme and of deleting  the less important resolutions. Such action was  essential in order to keep within the $8,200,000  limit. Sir John feared that if all suggestions for  such changes were to originate from the Budget  Committee, they would give rise to discussions  that could not be completed in the very limited  time the Programme and Budget Commission still  had before it. He therefore considered the  Director-General’s suggestion to establish the  committee most helpful; in his opinion, the new  body might be regarded as a joint committee of  the Programme and Budget Commission and of the  Budget Committee, since the chairmen of both  bodies would be serving on it. Sir John also  suggested that the Chairman of the Administrative  Commission should be a member of the proposed  committee, which seemed to be the only body  competent to submit a programme of work that  could be covered by the fixed Budget and was  likely to be acceptable to the Programme and  Budget Commission.  [4] Dr. BENDER (Chairman, Budget Committee)  acknowledged that the final responsibility for the  necessary cuts rested with the Programme and  Budget Commission rather than with the Budget  Committee. He was not against the establish-             ment of a committee to undertake that task, but  he wondered when such a committee could meet,  as a heavy time-table of meetings had already been  arranged.  [5] Mr. SEYDOUX (Chairman, Programme and  Budget Commission) approved the Director-  General’s suggestion and agreed with Dr. Bender  that the difficulty was to find time for the com-  mittee to meet. He thought such a committee  should be regarded primarily as an offshoot of  the Programme and Budget Commission, especially  appointed to deal with a matter that could not  be considered in plenary meeting because the  Commission had too large a membership.  [6] The CHAIRMAN agreed with the Director-  General, but felt it necessary to avoid giving the  impression that the Committee would supersede  the Programme and Budget Commission; like  Mr. Seydoux, the Chairman thought the Com-  mittee should be responsible to the Programme  and Budget Commission, to which it should report.  [7] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL said that this  had been his intention in putting forward his  proposal. Sir John MAUD (Chairman, Executive  Board) also supported that view.  [8] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL suggested that the  Programme and Budget Commission might cancel  the evening meeting scheduled for that day, to  make room for a meeting of the new committee.  [9] Dr. BENDER (Chairman, Budget Committee)  thought it would be better to wait until the Budget  Committee had finished its work before holding  a meeting of the new committee. Since the  Budget Committee was to meet for the last time  the following afternoon, he suggested that the  committee proposed by the Director-General should  meet the following evening.  [10] Mr. SARGEANT (United States of America),  made a similar comment with regard to the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission, advising delay  until that Commission had completed its work,  so that no doubts should remain.  [11] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL admitted the  cogency of these arguments, and suggested that  the evening of the following day should be set  aside for a meeting of the new committee; if it  did not finish its work, one of the plenary meet-  ings should be cancelled so that the committee's  second meeting might be held. The discussion  at the plenary meeting could be considerably  shortened if the committee in question were able  to submit a clear and concise report.  [12] Dr. WALKER (Chairman, Nominations  Committee) thought that the committee would be  able to complete its work in one meeting, for it  could only make general proposals for a kind of  summary execution, without embarking on any  detailed discussion.  [13] Hamed ZAKI Bey (Egypt) was in favour of  establishing the committee in question, but  thought that it should confine itself to eliminat-  ing less important matters, without attempting  to fix priorities.  [14] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL suggested that  the committee should consist of the representatives  of the United States of America, the United King-  dom, France, India, Brazil and Iraq (the delega-  tion which had suggested that such a committee  should be established), together with a Scandinav-  ian and a Spanish-speaking delegate.  [15] At the suggestion of Professor NIELSEN  (Chairman, Procedure Committee) and Dr. PAR-  RA PEREZ (Venezuela), Sweden and Cuba were  chosen for these last two places.  [16] It was decided to submit the proposal to  the Programme and Budget Commission and to  suggest that the Committee should meet the fol-  lowing evening, arrangements being made for an  additional meeting of the Programme and Budget  Commission on Thursday afternoon (in place of  a plenary meeting) to consider the findings of the  new committee. [1] Dr. BENDER (Chairman of the Budget Com-  mittee) understood that an appropriation resolu-  tion, submitted by the United States delegation  to the Programme and Budget Commission, had  been adopted. He expressed a certain astonish-  ment that the resolution had not been submitted  in the first instance to the Budget Committee. Agreed.  Method of work during the last two days of  the Session.  [3] The CHAIRMAN observed that the main  questions considered by the three Commissions  were to come before the plenary meetings that  Count STEFANO Jacint (Italy)  day and the following day. He foresaw two  dangers; firstly, that detailed discussions might  take place which would be very lengthy and might  annul the work done by the three Commissions;  secondly, that, on the contrary, the Conference  might simply take note of the conclusions of the  Commissions, without any discussion of the sub-  stance of their reports. He therefore proposed  the following procedure:  (a) That the Rapporteurs should present their  reports without being limited to any length of  time.  (b) That delegates who agreed with the con-  clusions of the Rapporteurs should not request  to speak.  (c) That delegates who disagreed with any  points in the reports and had amendments to  present should state their position, their speeches  being limited to a given number of minutes.  (d) That the Rapporteurs should reply to the  points raised.  (e) That a vote should be taken without further  discussion.
