[1] Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America)  said he would confine himself to restating certain  matters of principle.  The first concerned formal agreements, à  question which had been very thoroughly con-  sidered by the Sub-Committee; unfortunately,  the latter had been unable to accept the United  States point of view. Formal agreements had  been developed in order to enable non-govern-  mental organizations to receive grants-in-aid or  subventions from Unesco: under the new direc-  tives, however, it was provided that they could
990                              receive grants or subventions without having a  formal agreement. Recognizing, therefore, that  formal agreements had outlived their purpose, the  Executive Board had recommended their discon-  tinuation, a decision which was strongly sup-  ported by the United States delegation.  [2] Another reason which prompted the United  States delegation to support the Board's decision  was that it considered it unwise to make a  distinction between the non-governmental organ-  izations admitted to consultative status.  Mr. Mulliken therefore formally moved the  deletion of the reference to formal agreements in  the Preamble and throughout the document.  The CHAIRMAN stated that Mr. Mulliken’s  proposal would be examined together with Sec-  tion I.  Section I (PLAN oF RELATIONSHIPS).  A. General Arrangements.  [3] Professor SOMMERFELT (Chairman of the  Sub-Committee) briefly stated the views of  the majority, which were as follows: certain  organizations, some of them older than Unesco,  occupied a special position, in that they co-  operated very closely with Unesco and actually  carried out certain parts of its programme: the  majority of the Sub-Committee had therefore felt  that those organizations should receive the special  recognition expressed in a formal agreement, al-  though the latter did not imply any special  privileges.  [4] Mr. MULLIKEN (United States of America),  seconded by the delegates of AUSTRALIA and  INDIA, again moved the deletion of the references  to formal agreements in the new directives.  [5] Speaking in opposition to the United States  motion, Professor von MURALT (Switzerland)  gave the following reasons in favour of the re-  tention of formal agreements:
(1) As Dr. Huxley had pointed out in the Sub-  Committee, formal agreements were valuable as  a form of mutual recognition between Unesco and  the organizations concerned.
(2) In the field of natural sciences, much  work had already been done by ICSU and, in  order to avoid any duplication of work, Unesco  had entered into a formal agreement with that  body, handing over much work to it and con-  sidering it as a business partner; such formal  agreements had proved satisfactory to both part-  ners, and there was therefore no reason to abol-  ish them.
(3) It had been suggested that formal agree-  ments should be discontinued because their word-  Ing was out-dated and clumsy, but the Sub-Com-
591  mittee had agreed that the wording could easily  be simplified and standardized and that there was  no reason to abolish them on that account.
(4) It was not a question of making a distinc-  tion between different organizations, as suggested  bv Mr. Mulliken, but of giving certain of them a  greater assurance of continuity than they could  otherwise expect from Unesco, whose programme  and budget were on an annual basis: even if it  were decided to hold the General Conference  biennially, it would still not be a sufficient  guarantee to organizations like ICSU, ITI, etc.  which had to work on a programme extending  over several years; formal agreements were also  a stimulus towards active co-operation in Unesco's  programme.
(5) The Director-General himself had recom-  mended to the Executive Board at its Nineteenth  Session that formal agreements should be re-  retained for organizations ‘““covering a large area  of Unesco’s interests”.
(6) The Conference of Observers  national Non-Governmental Organizations,  during the current Session,  of Inter-  held  had unanimously  voted in favour of maintaining formal agree-  ments.  [6] Sir Charles WEBSTER (United Kingdom)  seconded these remarks, and expressed the hope  that the Commission would support the Sub-Com-  mittee’s conclusions.  [7] Professor AGO (Italy) favoured the retention  of formal agreements. It seemed to him that  there were two main reasons to be considered.  For one thing, a privileged position should be re-  served for certain organizations of very wide inter-  national scope. Then again, the termination of  formal agreements concluded between Unesco and  certain international organizations might be taken  as a sign of disagreement between Unesco and  these organizations, and might even compromise  a form of collaboration that had so far proved  fruitful.  Professor Ago nevertheless emphasized that his  approval of the text submitted by the Sub-Com-  mittee, advocating the retention of forimal agree-  ments, was conditional upon a particular point.  He drew attention to Paragraph (2) on page 7 of  Annex I, which specified that the organizations  eligible to conclude formal agreements should be  “of a professional character’. Now this clause  limited unnecessarily the choice of organizations  with which Unesco might wish to conclude agree-  ments. It also prevented the conclusion of agree-  ments with two out of seven of the organizations  already enjoying the advantages of a formal agree-  ment. These organizations were the World  Federation of United Nations Associations and the        International Studies Conference, both of which  were older-established than Unesco, had great  authority and had accomplished most important  work in the field of education for international  understanding.
