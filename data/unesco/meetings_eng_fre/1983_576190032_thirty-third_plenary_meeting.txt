(18.1) Mr WAGNER DE REYNA (Peru), Chairman of Commission I (Translation from the Spanish):  the pleasure and honour to preside, allow me, Mr President, to make a few brief remarks.
(18.2) Traditionally, Commission I has dealt with education, the basic objective of  Unesco, as is indicated by the fact that it is placed first in both the name and acronym  of the Organization. Commission I decided to adopt a horizontal approach to three general  aspects throughout the four principal themes that we might term vertical: education,  science, culture and communication. The Medium-Term Plan, in force since 1982, to which  our Director-General is deeDly committed, has in this respect changed the approach which  has prevailed in the Organization for forty years. Now we see that it is essential to con-  sider these issues as part of a single whole and this was the task assigned to Commission I,  so that it might grasp the main structure of these issues before observing them and trans-  lating them into terms of practical action, in its various forms and ramifications.  Commission I had three closely-linked concerns: major world problems, the development of  individuals and peoples, which necessarily constitute the means of solving them, and the  improvement of the status of women, which is the culmination of this endeavour to integrate  and reconcile man with himself. Major world themes are proposed as subjects of reflection.
(18.3) Some have expressed doubts as to whether it is Unesco's responsibility to under-  take so general a task. While it is true that the United Nations family possesses special-  ized bodies to analyse and, in some cases, resolve world problems in their differing aspects,  the hearts and minds of men in which the defences of peace must be constructed, do not  accept divisions decided upon from outside. We are dealing with units of action which must  be treated as such, globally, of a piece, which is exactly what is proposed in Major Pro-  gramme 111.1. If action is to be effective, it requires preliminary clarification of  concepts, and a calm examination of its possibilities, scope and objectives. Hence the  need for reflection, which is precisely the tool used in Major Programme I. We are not in  a make-believe world; it is a question of knowing what we want, and how we are to achieve  it so that action is adapted to reality, is effective and morally worthwhile. The ends  determine the means and vice-versa, actions and deeds provide matter for thought. Harmony  and balance, inter-penetration, pluralism and the understanding of all the factors involved  thereby become the norms of Unesco's work.
(18.4) As I said, major world problems are linked to the development of humanity, of all  individuals and all peoples, development that involves man in all his dimensions. Within  this conception, however, there must be recognition of a hierarchy of values which is  crowned by the spirit or the cultural dimension. It is one of the merits of Unesco that it  has not only introduced the cultural element into development, but that it has also sup-  ported the guiding function of culture as being proper to man, as a quality of life,  freedom and dignity.
