The meeting rose at 11.30 a.m. SECOND MEETING Tuesday, 20 September 1949, at 9.30 a.m. Chairman: Mr. Grorce V. ALLEN (United States of America) Nominations for the offices of Chairmen, Vice-Chairmen and Rapporteurs of the Com- missions and Committees of the Conference (document 4C/NOM/2). The CHAIRMANcalled on Dr. Walker to report on the suggestions made by the General Committee as to the nomination of a Chairman for each of the three Commissions, namely the Programme and Budget Commission, the Administrative Com- mission, and the Official and External Relations Commission. Dr. WALKER (Australia), Chairman of the Gen- eral Committee, emphasized that, apart from the consideration of the personal value of the nom- inees, the members of the General Committee had borne in mind, as an important factor, the desirability of ensuring an equitable geographical distribution. They proposed the election of M. Paulo de Berredo Carneiro (Brazil) as Chair- man of the Programme and Budget Commission, stressing his wide knowledge of programme matters and his valuable work during the Beirut Conference. The General Committee suggested M. Léo Mundeleer (Belgium) for the chairmanship of the Administrative Commission. For the chairmanship of the Official and External Relations Commission, two nominations had been considered by the General Committee, namely Mr. Antonio Castro Leal (Mexico) and Mr. Przmy- slaw Ogrodzinski (Poland). The CHAIRMAN moved that Professor Carneiro be proposed as Chairman of the Programme and Budget Commission. Unanimously adopted.
448 The CHAIRMAN moved that M. Mundeleer be proposed as Chairman of the Administrative Commission. Unanimously adopted. The CHAIRMAN called for expressions of views concerning the suggestions of the General Com- mittee for the Chairmanship of the Official and External Relations Commission. Dr. WANG Te Yio (China) seconded the nom- ination of the Polish delegate, which he felt was more in accordance with the principle of geographical distribution, as a representative of South America had been nominated Chairman of the Programme and Budget Commission. Mr. SEYDOUX (France), although he admitted the value of the Chinese delegate’s argument, thought that geographical distribution should not be a predominant factor during the Fourth Session of the General Conference, as this was mainly a Working Conference. The choice of Mr. Carneiro as Chairman of the Programme and Budget Commission had been made, not because of his nationality, but because of the ability which he had shown in Beirut. He therefore thought Mr. Castro Leal’s nomination should stand. Professor HERCIK (Czechoslovakia) stressed the personal qualities of Mr. Ogrodzinski and reminded the members that one of the chief aims of Unesco, as stated the previous day by the Director- General, was the exchange of ideas. He said that the delegate of Poland represented a set of ideas which were those of a very large group of people and consequently thought that M. Ogrodzinski            should be nominated Chairman of the Official and External Relations Commission. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) seconded the nomination of Mr. Antonio Castro Leal. The nomination of Mr. Antonio Castro Leal as Chairman of the Official and External Relations Commission was approved. The CHAIRMAN drew the attention of the members to Rule 47 of the Rules of Procedure: Each commission or subsidiary body set up by the General Conference shall elect its own Chairman, Vice-Chairman and, if necessary, Rapporteur and, in doing so, may take into account any recommen- dation of the Nominations Committee in these re- spects, in accordance with Rule 30 (2). He invited the delegates to express their views on this matter. Mr. SEBASTIAN (Philippines) believed the Commissions should be given a free hand in the election of their officers. The CHAIRMAN read Rule 30, paragraph 2, of the Rules of Procedure and asked members if they wished any suggestions to be made by the General Committee. Mr. MENEMENCIOGLU (Turkey), while agree- ing that the Commissions should be given a free hand in the election of their officers, thought that it would be more practical for the Nominations Committee to suggest names to them. Mr. HOLLAND (United States of America) supported the views of the Philippines delegation and stressed the difficulty, at this early stage, of ascertaining who was able and available to assume the important duties of an officer. He thought it would be more profitable to leave it to the Commissions to suggest nominees and elect them. This proposition was adopted. The meeting rose at 10.30 a.m. THIRD MEETING Friday, 30 September 1949, at 3.30 p.m. Chairman: Mr. George V. ALLEN (United States of America) Nominations for the election of six members of the Executive Board to replace those whose terms of office expired in 1949. The CHAIRMAN drew the attention of the Com- mittee to two points in the Report which the Procedure Committee had submitted to that morning’s plenary meeting, and which had been approved:
1. The Procedure Committee had taken note of the reply given by the Arbitral Tribunal set up by the Executive Board on the question whether a member of the Board whose term of office expired, and who came up for re-election, should, in order to be eligible for re-election, be a member of his national delegation at the session of the General Conference at which the election took place. The decision of the Arbitral Tribunal was negative. The Chairman gave the statement for information only, as at the present Session the members of the Board whose terms of office expired were all members of their delegations, and so eligible for re-election.
2. The Procedure Committee had examined, on the basis of document 4C/PRO/5, the problem created by the decision to change the date of the General Conference from autumn to spring, and decided to recommend to the General Conference Alternative III (page 3 of that document). The Chairman explained that the change in the time of year at which the General Conference would be held, meant that some members of the Board would serve only for two and a half years. Nothing, however, could be done about that until the Constitution had been modified, presumably at the session. The only thing to be done at the present session was to go ahead and have a normal election for a three-year term of office.
