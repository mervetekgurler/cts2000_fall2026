14,11 There are thus 27 peoples whose right to self-determination has been eagerly pro-  moted by my country, almost as many as the monstrous list of speakers which you have on  this topic today, Mr, President, In these circumstances it seems to me that these strident  incantations should endure only, if at all, on ‘the gravestone of colonialism as an inscrip-  tion burying the past. They should not, I suggest, be found on the lips of delegates who in  their own lifetime have seen the virtual disappearance of colonialism as a form of imposed  relationship between peoples. I also think thai the narrowly-directed beam of anticolonial  accusation still exemplifies in a discriminatory manner what has been well named the "galt  water fallacy, Let me explain; to exercise political responsibility - even if this is transi-  tory in historical perspective - over peoples separated from the metropolitan power by the  oceans of the world has been held to be morally obnoxious, But to exercise political domi-  nation over contiguous peoples in the same land mass as the metropolitan power is repre-  sented as beneficial guidance, This frankly seems to be odd,
14,12 In his address to us yesterday the Director-General referred to paragraph 30 of the  section dealing with general hypotheses in document 16 C/4, I quote his words from that  paragraph: "As for the struggle against colonialism, since it involves an essentially poli-  tical concept, the working out of a practical programme is dependent on a more precise  definition of the nature of Unesco's contribution and its limits", I fully agreeandlsee that  in the same paragraph the Director-General seeks some guidance. I will help him, Per-  haps - since Unesco is graced by an elegant Department of Philosophy - one of its under-  takings might be to commission and publish a monograph on this remarkable conceptual  paradox to which I have referred, It might be entitled "the place of the salt water fallacy  in the metaphysics of colonialism'", I cannot imagine why this paradox has so far escaped  philosophic scrutiny and has enjoyed an unquestioned international currency, It is perhaps  time for the world community to start thinking of human relationships in universal and ah-  golute terms and not in this distorted language adapted to traditional antipathies between  nation-States or designed to perpetuate distrust between ethnic groups,
14, 13 I come now, Mr, President, to a few comments on some of the draft resolutions be-  fore us, We have a draft resolution 16 C/DR/PLEN/2 (Rev, ) which, admittedly, covers a  lot of ground, but which deals in part specifically with these two topics, to which I have been  referring, colonialism and racialism, We have made it clear in this forum before that the  coupling of colonialism and racialism in a political slogan does nothing to make our taskin  Unesco any clearer or easier, Colonialism and racialism are far from synonymous terms.  The differing problems relating to Portuguese colonial policy, to apartheid in South Africa,  and to the illegal régime in Southern Rhodesia, are not within the competence of Unesco to  resolve, My own government's policy on racialism is well known, and I hardly need to re-  hearse it here, We deplore apartheid and racial discrimination in any form, We have both  signed and ratified the Convention on Racial Discrimination. And in the United Kingdom,  in our 1968 Race Relations Act, we have instituted comprehensive legislation for dealing  with racial tension in our domsetic affairs and have made discrimination based on race un-  lawful, The representative of Trinidad and Tobago, who asked yesterday what was happen-  ing in the industrialized countries in this field, is, I am sure, aware of our legislation and  action,
14,14 Turning back to the draft resolutions before us, there are certain parts of the draft  resolution 16 C/DR/PLEN/2 with which my delegation will be unable to concur, I must  reserve our right to explain our vote on the paragraphs concerned here and indeed in any  other resolutions which may come before us for voting, But I notice, in passing, that in  draft resolution 16 C/DR/PLEN/2, we arenow being asked to include fascism among the  evils to be condemned, This leads me to wonder whether we ought not to provide for the  inclusion of other totalitarian ideologies and practices; or are there not supposed to be any  others? With that thought in mind, I would like to suggest a possible amendment to pre-  ambular paragraph 7 and oper ative par agraph 2 of document 16 C/DR/PLEN/2, namely,  to insert after "Fascism" the phrase "and other totalitarian ideologies and practices", An  addition of this kind would improve the balance of these paragraphs, Furthermore, I am  not entirely happy with operative paragraph 7, While I of course fully support the desire  to create a climate of peace, the question of a European Security Conference raises far  wider issues than those falling within Unesco's competence, I agree, of course, that peace  in Europe is an essential ingredient of peace in the world and we should all be concerned  about it, But I am bound to agree with the Director-General when he expresses doubts about  whether he could be expected to respond in any significant way to the request in the last two  lines of paragraph 7 (a), On paragraph 7 (c) I should have thought it sufficient to include : a  report on the steps taken as a consequence of the resolution - if it is, in fact, adopted -  the Director-General's annual report to the Organization, rather than to add a ope.  cific special report which would only increase the proliferation of documentation. 1 pro-  pose to substitute a new 7 (c) as follows: "to include in his annual report to the Organiza-  tion, a report on steps taken in consequence of the present resolution",
14,15 There is, however, another implication in document 16 C/DR/PLEN/2, which is  also a clear feature of document 16 C/DR/PLEN/7, on which a number of speakers have  dwelt and on which I feel bound to make known my concern, In the debate on the item cor-  responding to this one at the fifteenth session of the General Conference, and at more than  one Executive Board meeting during the last biennium, we have by majority vote placed  upon the Director-General the invidious task of interpreting resolutions calling upon him  to comply indirectly, that is, through the Organization of African Unity, with requests for  assistance from the so-called national liberation movements in Southern Africa, In my  government's view, Mr, President, the granting of assistance by States, either directly or  collectively, through an international organization to a national liberation movement in re-  bellion against the government of a Member State of the United Nations (or any part of its  territory) raises the most serious questions in relation to the principle of non- intervention  which is reflected both in Article 2, paragraph 7, of the Charter of the United Nations and  in Article I, paragraph 3, of our own Constitution,
14,16 I think it as well, Mr, President, that we should remind ourselves of these ‘provi-  sions. Article I, paragraph 3, of the Constitution of Unesco reads: "With a view to pre-
27  serving the independence, integrity and fruitful diversity of the cultures and educational  systems of the States Members of this Organization, the Organization is prohibited from  intervening in matters which are essentially within their domestic jurisidiction", This is  a direct reflection, Mr, President, of Article 2, paragraph 7 of the Charter of the United  Nations which states: ''Nothing contained in the present Charter shall authorize the United  Nations to intervene in matters which are essentially within the domestic jurisdiction of  any State", In draft resolution 16 C/DR/PLEN/2, operative paragraph 7 (a), reference is  indeed made to the principles of the United Nations Charter and this reference clearly in-  cludes Article 2, paragraph 7, which is to be found in Chapter I of the Charter, headed  "Purposes and Principles",

It is virtually impossible, Mr, President, to prove that sort of negative, If ‘the Director-  General after, say, June next year, which is his first real opportunity to consult the Execu-  tive Board, sends out this circular, no doubt the headquarters of these non- governmental  organizations will then have to address this question, or whatever question is finally put to  them, to their affiliates, branches and sections, These branches and sections will then,  no doubt, have to consider their position and reply. If the reply is: "Yes, we are afraid  that to some extent we are involved in a situation of racial segregation’, then the head-  quarters of the non- governmental body is placed in a very serious predicament, It is in ef-  fect being subjected to blackmail, being asked either to disassociate this African branch or  to break off all contacts with Unesco, We heard today from Professor Chagas how this sort  of dilemma, if it arose, could not be examined by the General Assembly of ICSU before  September 1972, Surely it would be a disaster for our co-operation in world scientific af-  fairs if, as from 31 December 1971, before we had actually had a chance to consider this  matter, all relations were broken between Unesco and ICSU and the subvention terminated,  Mr. President, I am really extremely worried at the implications of this resolution and I  hope that those who have promoted it will reflect deeply upon those implications before they  proceed,
14,18 I am also concerned that in his speech yesterday Senutor Wynter said that the Director-  General need be in no doubt regarding the action he is expected to take in relation to libera-  tion movements, namely, at the least to follow resolution 7,3 adopted by the Executive  Board at its 85th session. ! voted against that resolution, While this Organization may  well deplore South Africa's racial policies and disagree with Portugal's colonial policies,  as indeed I do myself, it is setting a very dangerous legal precedent indeed for Unesco to  support a group of people acting within a Member State of the United Nations, or within that  Member .States' territories, against their legal government, It is not only in Southern  Africa that bodies exist, or may come into existence, that have the character of national  liberation movements seeking to overthrow governments by violence, For this reason Iam  also unable to support draft resolution 16 C/DR/PLEN/7, which again asserts that the Or-  ganization should do just this,
14,19 As the British Prime Minister sald last week at the General Assembly, "If we are  to be true to our ideals we should do nothing to strengthen or encourage those who believe  in violence as a political weapon, We should support all those who, with patience and under-  standing, are working for peaceful change, Sometimes the resort to violence is a legacy
27  of past errors, the product of desperation andof frustration at the failure of society to make  room for legitimate aspirations, But that does not excuse violence, though it throws a  heavy responsibility on those who have the duty to rectify injustice",
14,20 I would like to assure you, Mr, President, that to iake this position on the limits of  competence of the Organization, as I have, is not mere legalistic obstruction, Iwas glad  to hear the Director-General himself declare yesterday that the rôle agsigned to Unesco in  these matters must be squarely and strictly within the limite of its competence, We must  support him in this; otherwise, as he said, we will find Unesco's proper action in the fields  of education, science and culture distorted in ways alien to its purpose, Of course Unesco  must fortify peace and must buttress freedom, But we are concerned with true freedom -  freedom under the law, It was the British philosopher, T. H, Green, who said: "that man  is truly free who is conscious of himself as the author of the law which he obeys. We join  together to frame the laws of international behaviour - notably the Charter of the United  Nations, our own Constitution and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, We should  share the same loyal satisfaction in conforming to these laws as we feel collective pride in  their establishment, If we cannot keep our actions in this Organization within the clearly-  defined precepts of international law, then we are actively encouraging the loosening of the  constraints of law, To abandon law is to promote anarchy, And anarchy is the obverse of  peace, Thank you, Mr, President,

(18,1) Mr. EVMENOV (Byelorussien Soviet Soclalist Republic) (Translation from the Russian):  Mr. President, ladies and gentlemen, the struggle of peoples for national independence, for  social and racial equality and for the peaceful solution of political problems is having a marked  effect on the whole international situation today, These popular movements are 50 broadly based  and command such respect, and their progressive nature is so evident, that even their opponents  cannot deny fit,
(18, 2) However, notwithstanding the struggle of progressive forces, grave international crises,  which imperil peace, are constantly arising. Indo- China remains the object of imperialist agres-  sion, and Israel continues to pursue her expansionist policy in relation to the Arab countries.  Despite the numerous motions of censure that have been passed, Portugal, South Africa and  Southern Rhodesia continue their criminal activities, As well as this, a number of countries in  the militarist, political NATO bloc are continuing to send arms to Portugal, and some of them  intend to sell arms to the racialist South African régime, Under these conditions, Unesco should  play an important rôle in the struggle for peace, security and the abolition of colonialism and  racialism, This is its sacred duty.
(18,3) How is our Organization fulfilling these important obligations and carrying out the basic  task for the execution of which it was founded?
(18.4) It is a good thing that Unesco has been continually asking this question from the very first  days of its existence, It is quite clearly dealt with in the Constitution, In the speeches which  have been made here and in the Organization's publications, the importance of the struggle for  peace and security and for the abolition of racialism and colonialism and the need to solve these  problems through the work of Unesco have been very frequently emphasized, Many admirable  resolutions on these matters were adopted at the eleventh thirteenth, fourteenth and fifteenth ses-  sions of the Unesco General Conference and at a number of sessions of the Executive Board and  also at the Conference on cultural policies held recently in Venice,  {18,5) These resolutions s-¢ forth accurately andin a practical, specific manner, the basic prob-  lems for which the Organization must seek a solution and the principal ways in which it can take  action, They could become a valuable programme on which the practical activity of the Unesco  Secretariat could be based, But the fact is that some Unesco departments do nottake much really  practical action to implement these quite practical resolutions. The measures they take are  frequently far removed from the problems raised in the above-mentioned resolutions, Thus, in  a pamphlet published this year, intended to show the measures Unesco is taking to promote peace,  we read of research into the psychological and biological causes of human aggressiveness, of a  symposium devoted to research on the brain and human behaviour and of a number of other prob-  lems and measures of a similar nature, It is quite obvious that these do nothing to solve prob-  lems connected with the struggle for peace and the abolition of colonialism, Thatkind of research  not only gets away from the heart of the problem, it also hampers the struggle for peace,
(18,6) This does not mean, of course, that Unesco is ignorant of the kind of action that should  be taken or of the proper way to organize it, This has been discussed by the Meeting of Experts  on Unesco's contribution to peace (May 1966), by the Round Table discussion on Unesco's contri-  bution to peace (October-November 1966) by the Advisory Meeting of Experts on Unesco's rôle  in developing regearch on peace problems (July 1969) and by the Symposium on the Impact of  Violence in the Mass Media (July 1970), Theso are practical and necessary steps, but there  have not been enough of them, One is therefore forced to conclude that in Unesco's activities  concert ing these matters there is a gap between its constitutional obligations andthe resolutions  of the General Conference on the one hand and the practical activity of the Secretariat on the other,
(18.7) Mr, President, the Executive Board at its 83rd session discussed the Director-General‘a  report and the long-term plan for Unesco's activity which has been submitted to the General Con-  ference at its sixteenth session lor consideration, It is an important step that the Director-  General has prepared a programme document on the Organization's work to promote peace, se-  curity and the abolition of colonialism and racialism, But if the practical proposals set out in  this document are to be implemented, it is first of all necessary that all the principal depart-  ments of Unesco and all members of the Organization should participate in practical work for  their implementation, Secondly, in our opinion, Unesco must define its views as to how these  measures are to be carried out, - At present, as we have heard, the Organization's activity con-
27  cerning these matters is based on the idea that the very fact that we are making progress in edu-  cation and culture or that we are undertaking various kinds of scientific research means that we  are struggling for peace and for the abolition of colonialism,
(18,8) Of course, there is an element of truth in this, But it is a very relative kind of truth, In  order to fulfil its basic rôle, in order to implement its resolutions and plans, Unesco must ensure  that the ideas of peace and the equality of peoples permeate the educational system; it must in-  culcate in pupils a hatred of colonialism, neo-colonialism and racialism; it must see that scien-  tific symposia and research are directed towards the discovery of the economic, political and  social causes of world crises and conflicts and of waysof settling them peacefully; it must foster  and disseminate through the various cultural institutions the ideas of friendship and brotherhood,  peace and the equality of nations, This is how Unesco, while remaining within the limits of its  competence, can effectively fulfil its constitutional obligations.  {18,9) Mr. President, a draft resolution has been submitted by the Union of Soviet Socialist Re-  publics, the Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic, the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic and  a number of other countries, in which the tasks of our Organization .n relation to the struggle  for peace, security in Europe and the abolition of colonialism are set out in a practical, detailed  and clear manner,
(18, 10) Unesco's principal tasks, according to this resolution, are: (a) to implement the resolu-  tions adopted by the General Conference; (b) to see that the ideals of peace and friendship among  nations are reflected in Unesco's programme of activities; (c) to intensify the struggle for peace  and the security of nations by means of culture; (d) to undertake practical action in the struggle  for peace and security in Europe and for the abolition of colonialism, neo- colonialism, racialism  and fascism, using the means available to the Organization; {e) to intensify its action, within the  limits of its competence, to help nations struggling for freedom from colonial enslavement,
(18.11) We consider that the tasks defined in this draft resolution are in accordance with the as-  pirations of all peace-loving peoples, and we hope that this draft resolution will be supported and  approved by the General Conference at its sixteenth session.
(18,12) Mr, President, from this platform the delegate of the United States of America has  called for the “depoliticization” of Unesco, It is impossible to agree with such an idea, because  it means, in effect, that Unesco must not concern itself with questions related to the struggle for  peace or the struggle against colonialism, This proposal aims at keeping mankind deaf to the  sufferings of hundreds of millions of people who are today in the bondage of colonialism and  * mercilessly downtrodden by foreign intervention, The proposal to “depoliticize" Unesco is made  with a view to justifying the aggressive acts taking place in Indo-China and the Middle East and  concealing them from the eyes of the world, It ie amazing that anyone can introduce a resolution  concerning peace while at the same time carrying on an aggressive war.
(18,13) The delegation of the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic declares yet again that we  are absolutely convinced that Unesco should give no help to those countries which oppress other  peoples and wage wars on foreign soil against people struggling for freedom and independence,
(18, 14) The Byelorussian delegation expresses the hope that Unesco will intensify its fight for  peace, security and the abolition of colonialism, thus carrying out its principal duty to mankind,  Thank you for your attention,

(20.1) Dr, de SOLA {Venezuela) (Translation from thé Spanish):  Mr. President, on behalf of the delegation of my country, I wish to set forth our positi 1 vith  regard to various draft resolutions that have been put forward at tk! meeting, for, while being  in general agreement with their basic principles, we must unhappily wailitraw our support from  some of them or abstain from voting on them, for they contain statements rat are at variance  with the principles Venezuela has consistently upheld in its relations with international organiza-  tions,
(20.2) Firstly, I! wish to state that our country has at all times shown its disapproval of situa-  tions that are incompatible with the self-determination and advancement of peoples, considering  as it does that colonialism constitutes a grave threat to peace and progress, Thus, it has sup-  ported the efforts of international organizations to eliminate colonialism, in the belief that  All peoples, whatever their race, convictions or religious beliefs, have the right to self-
27  determination, as also to adopt the social and political system that they consider to be the best,  In view of this, the delegation of Venezuela will support any anti-colonialist action designed to  put an end to the abnormal and anachronistic situation existing In territories administered by  colonial powers, provided that such action is within the competence of the General Conference  and does not conflict with Unesco,
(20,3) These broad lines of Venezuelan policy are not only consistent with the efforts of the  people of Venezuela to promote peace and social justice, they are also embodied in the constitu-  tion of our country. In the preamble to the constitution of the Republic of Venezuela, certain  principles are set forth which are subsequently elaborated in the constitution itself, These prin-  ciples, which constitute the cornerstone of our political system, our ideals as a people and our  fundamental juridical tenets, include the assertion that it is the basic responsibility of the Repub-  lic to protect labour and see that it is respected, to foster human dignity, to promote the general  welfare and security of society, to ensure that all have their fair share of the nation's wealth in  accordance with the principles of social justice, to stimulate the economy so as to promote the  welfare of the people, to maintain social and legal equality without discrimination as to race,  creed, sex or social status, to co-operate with other nations - and particularly with the republics  which are our neighbours in our continent - for the benefit of the community of nations, on the  basis of mutual respect for sovereignty, the right of peoples to self- determination, the universal  observance of individual and social rights and the repudiation of war, conquest and economic  domination as an instrument of international policy.
(20, 4) Clearly, these principles, which are the cornerstone of our Constitution, cannot conflict  with the spirit of most of the draft resolutions that have been submitted to the General Conference,  It is the view of the Venezuelan Government, however, that, just as these principles must be de-  fended, so must others on which the nation's legal foundation rests - respect for the sovereignty  of other nations and the right of peoples to self-determination, We consider that certain of these  resolutions respect thie principle, whereas the general tenor and import of others thathave been  submitted to the Conference for its consideration do not, An example of respect for this prin-  ciple, which is upheld by Venezuela as a cardinal point of its international policy, is document
16 C/DR, 101, submitted by the Byelorussian Soviet Socialist Republic, which establishes and  reaffirms the principle of the inadmissibility of intervention in the internal affairs of other States  and their right to protection of their independence and sovereignty,
(20.5) Furthermore, we consider that Unesco's action with respect to colonialisim and racialism  must to some extent be limited to its present framework of activities, Increasingemphasis being  laid on those aspects designed to instil into men's minds the idea of peace and fill them with re-  vulsion for discrimination and racialism. Accordingly, we concur with the conclusion to docu-  ment 16 C/12, submitted to this Conference by the Director-General, in which it is stated that  "Unesco, which is the tangible expression of [Member States'] will for peace and the organ of  their co-operation in education, sclencé and culture, can only play an instigatory rôle or act in  a stimulating, research or advisory capacity, with respect to affairs that so closely affect funda-  mental decisions relating to national sovereignty’. Accordingly, we consider thatthe long-term  plan and the observations of the Director-General contained in this document should guide any  action that the General Conference may authorize the Director-General and the other competent  bodies of Unesco to take in the near future,
(20,6) Furthermore, it is our view that the good intentions of some of the resolutions submitted  which request Unesco to withhold all assistance from countries in which colonialism’ or racial-  ism is practised, might in a sense produce the opposite effect tothatintended, Unesco's action  is intended to'aid peoples, not governments, If we withheld such aid, would not the effect be, to  some extent, to victimize those very peoples whom we desire to free from colonialism ‘and ra-  cial discrimination? 1 believe that Unesco's presence in those areas in which discrimination is  practised and in which the legacy of colonialism is still apparent will always be conducive to the  advancement and well-being of peoples, It is our view - and one which we may be permitted to  express, having at different times lived under political régimes that isolated our country from  all contact with the outside world - that to isolate such peoples, to deprive them of the presence  of those who support peace and oppose discrimination, might hamper Unesco's work for peace  rather than furthering it,
(20.7) Accordingly, we propose that the Director-General receive all suppos¢ with regard to  document 16 C/12, and we shall support all resolutions that are truly informed with the spirit of  the conclusion thereto, which I have read out, Unfortunately, however, and this despite our  agreement on principle, we feel bound to abstain from voting on others.
(20,8) This morning, I ventured to suggest to the General Committee of the Conference that a  special committee be appointed to follow the course of the discussions and observe the trends  that emerge, and that, in the spirit of these resolutions and within the Organization's field of  competence, it should try to formulate a resolution that would satisfy the wishes of all, while at  the same time being positive, leaving all purely political undertakings to the competent interna.  tional organizations, :
(20.9) I have one other remark tomake: one of the draft resolutions raises a question of a purely  political nature, namely, the specific problem of European security, We know that major discus-  sions have taken place on this matter between tho foremost representatives of the various Euro-  pean powers during the most recent political conferences held in this region. 1 do not think that  a resolution of this kind can be of direct relevance to any action Unesco might take in this field,
(20,10) Such, then, are the observations that 1 venture to offer on behalf of my country and our  delegation; I hope that a committee will be set up to formulate a joint resolution, designed to pro-  mote peace and understanding among peoples, Thank you, Mr, President.
