3.1 Mr, WIJEYERATNE (Ceylon):  Mr. President, ladies and gentlemen, the delegation of Ceylon has given serious con-  sideration to item’ 30 of the Agenda. It is our intention to abstain in the voting on this re-  solution.
3.2 You will notice that all resolutions presented'to this assembly have been submitted in  the name of a particular country; and so it is the considered opinion of my country that Exe -  cutive Board members have no individual status. Article V.A of the Constitution of Unesco,  referring to the composition of the Executive Board, states very clearly: "The Executive  Board shall be elected by the’ General Conference from among the delegates appointed by  the Member States and shall consist of. thirty - four members each of whom shall represent  the government of the State of which he is a national”, May I repeat, ',.. each of whom  shall represent the government of the State of which he is a national”, Itis quite clear that  each Executive Board member represents the policies of his particular. government. There -  fore any resolution he wishes ‘to sponsor must have the imprint and the sanction of the govern-  ment he represents, Then again inthe same Article V.A of the Constitution of Unesco, para-  graph 4 states: "'Inthe event of the death or resignation of amember of the Executive Board,  his replacement for the remainder of his term shall be appointed by tl:e Executive Board on  the nomination of the government of the State the former member represented''.. May Ire-  peat: ' his replacement for the remainder of his. term shall be appointed.by the Executive  Board, on the nojnination of the government of the State the former member represented",  It is quite clear therefore that both these items envisage the authority of a particular gov-  ernment entering into the selection and replacement of Executive Board members.
3,3 1 regret to say. that I do not find myself in agreement with the statement made by à very  learned and distinguished delegate who emphasized the first part of the relevant section, that  is: "In the event of death or resignation of a member of the Executive Board, ..". He ap-  peared to restrict the manner in which Executive Hoard members could be replaced to  these two categories alone, death or resignation. ‘It is precisely with this limited interpre-  tation that my country humbly but totally disagrees. The present situation on the Executive  Board is an anomalous one. A government nominates a member who is duly elected as a  representative of the country and then the country concerned is expected to have nofurther  control over its own representative except in the event of his death or resignation. The re-  presentative may conceivably speak and act in a manner totally. contrary to the interest of  the country and even inimical to the policies of the government, both of which he represents,  but the government concerned apparently can do nothing whatever about it. ‘My country is  therefore quite clear on the point that an Executive Board member shall hold office only so  long as he enjoys the confidence of the country which originally nominated him ‘and assisted  in hia election. No foreign organization must arrogate to itself the right to refuse a Mem-  ber State control over its own representative. This would surely infringe on the sovereignty  of a free people. We claim therefore that any government hus the right to change its repre-  sentative on the Executive Board of Unesco whenever it so desires, Unless these intérpre-  tations and modifications are taken into account my governme nt cannot vote for this. resolution,
3. ‘ I wish to announce now that my country. will propose in the near future a resolution em -  bodying these points for consideration-by Mernber States, the points being (a) a limitation  of the period for which a Member State can be reprèsented on the Executive Board, and (b)  the right of the Member State concerned to appoint individuals to and remove individuals  from the seats it occupies on the Executive Board. I thank you, Mr. Président.
