The CHAIRMAN explained that a meeting of  the Nominations Committee had been made  necessary by the receipt of a cable from Dr. Ben-  jamin Carrion of Ecuador, who had been elected  to the Executive Board by the Mexico City Confer-  ence. The cable was received on 6 December 1948
443  and reported to the General Conference two days  later. It ran as follows :  “Being unable to attend meetings of the Exe-  cutive Board, I hand in to Conference my  resignation from the seat to which 1 was elected  at Mexico.”  The Chairman read the Article of the Consti-  tution (Article V, paragraph 4) governing elec-  tions to the Board in case of the death or resigna-  tion of one of its members, and added that, since       Dr. Carrion’s resignation had reached the Confer-  ence in the middle of a session, the session  referred to as “next” in the Constitution was, in  fact, the current one. Accordingly it was not for  the Board to choose a substitute, but for the  Conference to elect a new member to serve until  the end of 1950. The Nominations Committee  had to propose a candidate to the Conference.  The Chairman reminded the meeting that the  Constitution provided that no two nationals of  the same country could be members of the Board  at the same time. He read the list of the coun-  tries whose nationals were members, and stressed  that the person nominated should be elected on  account of his qualifications and merits, taking  into consideration geographical and cultural dis-  tribution, but not as a representative of his  country.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) respec-  ted and admired the members of many delegations  and felt able to choose a dozen people whom he  could support with great enthusiasm; the task of  nominating one individual was, therefore, very  difficult. His delegation had decided to propose  Mr. A. A. Roberts, of the Union of South Africa,  who had failed to get an absolute majority at the  last meeting of the Committee by a very small  number of votes. He praised his candidate’s  energy and knowledge of Unesco’s work and aims,  and pointed out that his election would widen the  geographical distribution of the Board.  Dr. PARRA-PEREZ (Venezuela) said that, as  geographical distribution was taken into conside-  ration as well as individual merit, the delegates  of the southern half of the Western hemisphere—  a big continent which had made important con-  tributions to culture—wished to suggest Dr. Diego  Luis Molinari of Argentina, a new member of the  Organization.  Dr. del RIO y CANEDO (Mexico) outlined  briefly the circumstances of Dr. Carrion’s resigna-  tion; he considered them arguments in favour  of the election of a person from Central or South  America, so as not to disrupt, after Dr. Carrion’s  departure, the balance of geographical distribution  which the Mexico Conference had deemed desir-  able. He seconded the proposal of the delegate  of Venezuela.  Professor RIVET (France) stated that, in  Mexico, he had helped to elect Dr. Carrion as an  excellent representative of the Latin American  civilization, for which he had the deepest regard.  He regretted that Dr. Carrion had been unable to  attend the meetings of the Board and approved his  resignation. He thought his seat should go to a  Latin American but, in his opinion, the represen-  tatives of Latin America should agree on a name  after a thorough and objective discussion. Pro-  fessor Rivet appealed to them to meet again and  propose a candidate chosen by ballot. He would  then be quite ready to vote for him. If his sug-  gestion was not followed, he would nominate, on  behalf of the French delegation, the great scien-  tist, Professor Piaget of Switzerland, which had  that morning become a Member of Unesco. He  moved a thirty-minute adjournment of the  meeting, to give the Latin American represen-  tatives an opportunity to make a carefully consi-  dered nomination.  Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil) seconded the  French motion; he urged the Committee to give  him and his colleagues from Latin America thirty  minutes to agree on the name they would submit  to the Committee.  Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) spoke  against the motion, not because he wanted to  rush such an important decision, but because he  both agreed and disagreed heartily with the  French delegate. If the facts mentioned by Pro-  fessor Rivet were correct, Mr. Allen agreed that  the method of selection left much to be desired,  but he did not agree that Latin America, as such,  should have a candidate. There was no reason,  in that case, why Europe or America should not  ask for a representative. Such a course of action  would not be constitutional. The delegate of  Mexico had said that Argentina was the candidate  of the Latin American bloc; the forming of blocs  was a very improper procedure.  To obviate further discussion, M. HUYSMANS  (Belgium) wished to nominate M. Piaget.  The French motion to adjourn for thirty  minutes was carried by a substantial majority.
