3,14 Ladies and gentlemen, I am in duty bound now to bring to your attention two incidents  which occurred during recent months, both of which raised questions affecting not merely  the rights and interests of my own country, but also certain fundamental principles of great  importance to all Member States of Unesco as well as to Unesco as a whole,
3.15 One of the incidents concerns the Second International Oceanographic Congress which  was held in Moscow from 30 May to 9 June this year, in accordance with a resolution  adopted by the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission and endorsed by the thirteenth  session of the General Conference of Unesco, Under the terms of this contract concluded  between Unesco and the National Commission for Unesco of the Union of Soviet Socialist  Republics acting on behalf of the Soviet Government, the Soviet Union was bound "to addrebs  personal invitations to all participants whom Unesco and the other sponsoring organizations  will have selected from marine scientists from all countries of the world", and "to im-  pose no restriction upon the participant's entry into, sojourn in and departure from its  territory". However, a well-known Chinese marine scientist, Professor Chu Tsu-Yu, who  had been officially informed by the Director-General of Unesco that he was invited to par-  ticipate in this Congress, wat not issued a visa or permit for entry into the Soviet Union,  although he had applied in due time to the Soviet Embassies in Rome and Paris and although  Unesco itself had intervened with the Soviet authorities on his behalf, Because of the failure  of the Soviet Union to issue the necessary visa or permit in accordance with the clearly  stated requirements of the contract, this Chinese scientist and certain other similarly in-  vited participants from other Member States of Unesco were prevented from attending the  Congress, Such deliberate failures on the part of the host country of the Congress cannot  be regarded otherwise than as violations by the Soviet Union of the terms of the contract  formally concluded between Unesco and the Soviet Union, Under such circumstances, my  delegation considers it important that Unesco should not permit such a matter involving an  important principle to pass without remedial action, Apart from an unequivocal statement  from the Director-General regarding the merits of this question, my delegation considers  that the Soviet Union should henceforth be deprived of the privilege of serving as the host  country of any international conference convened under the auspices of Unesco,
