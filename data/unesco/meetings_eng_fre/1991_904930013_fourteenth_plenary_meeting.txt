1.1 The PRESIDENT:  Ladies and gentlemen, I declare open the fourteenth plenary meeting. May I begin by informing you  that the General Committee at its third and fourth meetings examined a proposal by Morocco to add a new item  to the agenda of the General Conference, to be entitled: "The Roads of Faith’. The General Committee  recommends that the General Conference approve the addition of this new item to the agenda, in accordance  with Rule 14 of the Rules of Procedure. If the Conference so decides, it will become item 15.4, and will be  assigned to Commission IV. Is it the wish of the General Conference that we approve this recommendation by  the General Committee?
2. The PRESIDENT:  Ladies and gentlemen, we shall now continue the general policy debate. Our first speaker is  H.E. Mr Dainis Ivans, First Vice-President of the Supreme Council of the Republic and head of the delegation  of Latvia.
3.1 Mr IVANS (Latvia):  Mr President, Mr Chairman of the Executive Board, Mr Director-General, Excellencies, ladies and  gentlemen, in the name of the Parliament of Latvia I wish to convey our thanks to UNESCO and to each and  every member of this venerable Organization for the warmth and the consideration which has met our  delegation since the first day in your midst.
3.2 Since 21 August 1991, the day when our independence was restored, the political priorities in Latvia  have changed. The primacy of recovering independence is replaced by a focus on development. First, there is  the urgent need to reform the system of our administration and deploy new and democratic structures. Second -  but not far behind - are the problems of education and cultural life.
33 The problems facing our education system are different in nature from those encountered in countries  of Asia and Africa. Our problems have grown out of the imposition for 50 years of a very reductive vision of  man, his history and his place in the world. Indeed, nobody who has not lived under communism can really  understand how bleak and colourless is the spiritual landscape left behind after the passage of this souless  machine.
34 Our teachers of today have been formed by this system. We realize that without retraining our  teachers, without changing the content of the curricula in our schools, our universities and beyond, and without  a new understanding of social relations and responsibilities, it will be impossible to ensure economic  development and social peace in our country. This is where we are looking for consultation and help from  UNESCO. It could take the form of advisory services by your experts to working teams of our parliament, of  student exchange, or of teacher-training workshops.
3.5 We think we should start with the teachers and professors. It is not by accident that the physical  destruction unleashed after 1940 was directed primarily against Latvian teachers, scientists and cultural  workers. At that time, the level of education in Latvia was among the highest in Europe. I am convinced that,  with your support, we shall rebuild it and make it even better.
3.6 Another problem which we could solve with your help, possibly by means of a UNESCO workshop, is  the problem of minorities. Wars, genocide and a deliberate policy of repopulation have brought the Latvians to  the point where they are becoming a minority in their own land. After 4,000 years of tilling their fields and  building their cities, Latvians are now under the threat of being linguistically and culturally submerged. At the  same time, 50 years of intense Russification have deprived other minorities - Poles, Jews, Lithuanians and  Ukrainians - of their cultural autonomy. Prior to the Second World War, all these groups had their freedom and  benefited from governmental budgets to run their own schools up to university level.
3.7 It is therefore an urgent question for us in Latvia and for you at UNESCO. What should be done? How  can we ensure the continued survival and flowering of the Latvian language and culture? How can we satisfy  the just needs of all the national minorities in Latvia? How can we use the new spiritual potential born with  independence to the best advantage in the field of education and culture?
3.8 We have recently passed legislation in our Parliament which guarantees the rights and liberties of  minorities. We have helped the formation of cultural associations for as many as 20 national minorities. Jewish  and Polish schools have been opened. We have been fortunate to experience no ethnic conflicts; on the  contrary, election results show a clear majority in favour of independence for Latvia in all ethnic groups. This  does not mean that there are no problems? It is normal to have problems and we are solving them as best we  can.
3.9 A more visible presence and a more active participation of UNESCO in the plans and programmes of  education and culture in Latvia would contribute towards political stability and peaceful social development in  our Republic. Our ambition is to become, with your help, a kind of model State for peaceful and prosperous  multicultural development in a small country, a model likely to be an inspiration for other countries or  continents in comparable situations.
3.10 For UNESCO, the Baltic countries - Latvia, Estonia and Lithuania - are a "Terra incognita’. It is not  true that they lack monuments of universal cultural interest. I therefore take this opportunity to invite UNESCO  to mount an expedition, a new Columbus expedition, towards the East. I am sure it would find a special melody  that would enrich the concert of nations. Latvia has unique sites of natural beauty to be discovered by the West  and recognized by UNESCO.
3.11 All this has suffered in the years of occupation and many a resource has been removed, but the core  remains, a core of Western and Nordic culture and civilization which cannot be forgotten. Now we are  attempting to save what can be saved. This is not limited to monuments and the environment; we give new life  to spiritual movements and centres of research - one example is the Nicolai Roerich Association.
3.12 We are deeply obliged to our Nordic neighbours for all they are doing in the field of education and  culture in Latvia. In recent weeks an information office of the Nordic countries has been opened in Riga to join  the Danish Cultural Institute which opened more than two years ago. Partly as a result of this active influence  translations of books from more than 60 different languages are going to be printed in Riga. It is an old  tradition in Latvia to stress the intellectual and to give priority to cultural values, and it was this - and not some  miscalculation by the KGB - which was the root cause of our success and gave in the end the advantage to the  Baltic States as they faced tremendous military strength and an iron-clad system.
3.13 Thus, the priority which Latvian intellectuals in their reborn State have accorded to education, science  and culture is clearly part of the struggle for its security and its permanence and as such exceeds the limits of  our particular geographical situation. We come to UNESCO with great expectations. Latvia 1s at the crossroads  of its development just as Latvia and the other Baltic States are at the crossroads of Europe. The direction we  take will be reflected in the civilization which the coming century will produce. Our expectations include a  sense of generosity. Joining UNESCO we do not wish to be only on the receiving end. Our geographical  position makes exchange a priority. It is for this that we are building our 'Great Library’, which will also  contain a data bank as complete as we can make it that will share its information east and west.
3.14 We shall advance, even if it takes time to set up a National Commission for UNESCO in our country.  We shall advance, although our struggle to obtain the return of our property is undecided. Clearly, a fully  deployed activity of the Latvian delegation to UNESCO will be possible only after our Embassy becomes  operative. The building of the Embassy of Latvia in France is still occupied by the services of the Soviet Union
14   and we hope that with the help of the French authorities and gentle pressure from UNESCO we will be able to  recover our property.
3.15 Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, Latvia is conscious of its responsibilities in the fields of education,  science and culture and protection of the environment, responsibilities which are the consequence of our joining  the Organization. Latvia remains open to UNESCO's programmes and activities. Our country and our people  would feel particularly honoured by a visit of the Director-General of UNESCO. Thank you for your kind  attention.

PANKINE, ministre des affaires étrangères de l'Union des républiques socialistes soviétiques  (traduit du russe) :  Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Directeur général, Mesdames et Messieurs, c'est pour moi un grand  honneur et un moment émouvant que cette occasion qui m'est donnée de m'adresser, dans cette grande salle de  l'UNESCO, aux délégués de 163 pays participant à la présente session de la Conférence générale.
(6.2) Avant tout, je me réjouis, en notre nom à tous, que de nouveaux membres aient été admis au sein de la  famille de l'UNESCO. Nous saluons cordialement les délégations de la Lettonie, de la Lituanie, de l'Estonie et de  Tuvalu, Etats qui ont rejoint les rangs de l'UNESCO à la présente session de la Conférence générale.
(6.3) Permettez-moi de vous transmettre les chaleureuses et cordiales salutations du Président de l'URSS,  Mikhaïl Gorbatchev, et de vous donner lecture de son message.
(6.4) "A la Conférence générale de l'UNESCO :
(6.5) Les fondateurs de l'Organisation des Nations Unies estimaient que la paix devait être fondée non  seulement sur l'accord des gouvernements, mais aussi sur la solidarité intellectuelle et morale des êtres humains.  C'est précisément dans ce sens que travaille l'UNESCO.
(6.6) Nul n'ignore l'œuvre accomplie par l'UNESCO en ce qui concerne la diffusion de l'éducation, la  préservation de l'identité culturelle des peuples, la sauvegarde des monuments historiques et la mise en place  d'une coopération internationale dans les domaines scientifique et culturel. Tous les pays qui adhèrent à  l'UNESCO ont de ce fait la possibilité de bénéficier de l'expérience acquise dans le monde entier et de mettre à  profit les réalisations des autres pour accélérer leur propre développement.
(6.7) Cependant, le monde attend de l'UNESCO davantage d'efficacité. C'est bien naturel, puisque nous  entrons dans une époque où les organisations de ce type seront appelées à jouer un rôle croissant. I importe  d'autant plus que ces organisations aient un caractère universel et, en particulier, qu'elles comptent parmi leurs  membres les Etats les plus influents.
(6.8) L'époque où l'activité de l'UNESCO était entravée par les batailles idéologiques et politiques de la  "guerre froide” est à jamais révolue. Humanisme et intégrité du monde contemporain, tels sont aujourd'hui les  deux principes qui, à mon sens, permettront à l'Organisation de prendre un nouvel essor et de bénéficier d'un  soutien universel.
(6.9) C'est dans cet esprit que je forme des voeux pour le succès des travaux de la vingt-sixième session de  votre Conférence générale.”
(6.10) Permettez-moi à présent, Monsieur le Président, de faire quelques observations en mon nom propre.
(6.11) Aujourd'hui plus que jamais, la survie de l'humanité et son progrès dépendent de la solidarité  intellectuelle et morale de la communauté internationale.
(6.12) Cette solidarité est indispensable au moment où s'opèrent dans le monde des changements radicaux, les  plus importants peut-être qui se soient produits depuis la création de l'UNESCO. Une ère nouvelle s'ouvre devant  nous - celle de l'effondrement du totalitarisme et de l'affirmation, douloureuse mais inéluctable, de la démocratie.  Ce processus en cours touche tous les aspects de la vie et ne connaît pas de frontières géographiques.
(6.13) Comme l'ont montré les événements qui se sont produits dans notre pays au mois d'août dernier, bien  des dangers et des obstacles se dressent sur cette voie. Il ne s'agit pas, en effet, d'une transition linéaire d'un état à  un autre, ni d'un progrès mécanique qui conduirait d'une forme inférieure à une forme supérieure de civilisation.
534   En réalité, le processus en cours relève de la recherche constante de la vérité qui concerne l'humanité tout entière  et qui révèle l'interdépendance et la globalité du monde dans lequel nous vivons.
(6.14) Nous assistons à une libération d'énergie de la part de tous ceux qui font l'Histoire, énergie qui ne peut  être réellement créatrice que si elle est ennoblie par la culture et fondée sur toute la richesse de l'expérience  humaine.
(6.15) Nous sommes les témoins d'un phénomène nouveau, en vertu duquel la dignité de l'individu -la  souveraineté réelle de l'être humain - accède au premier rang des valeurs universelles, créant du même coup les  conditions nécessaires pour que s'imposent, en politique, un principe moral unificateur et les idéaux humanistes  inhérents à toute culture véritable. Certes, ces idéaux n'avaient pas été totalement effacés de la mémoire humaine.  Mais en ce moment précis, l'humanité, l'humanité tout entière, se voit offrir une occasion unique de s'unir sur la  base d'un idéal démocratique qui consacre le pluralisme, l'identité culturelle et les droits et libertés  fondamentaux.
(6.16) L'humanité n'a pas d'autre issue. 27.4 Ladies and gentlemen, in the first year of independence the efforts, energies and initiatives of the  government have been directed towards the creation of strong foundations from which programmes could be  initiated and activities launched to achieve national objectives. While gratefully acknowledging assistance from  the international community during this initial phase, Namibia has now reached the critical point of project  design and implementation. We have the vision and commitment to embark upon the road towards becoming a  proud and self-supporting nation. However, due to the massive social deficit left behind by apartheid, we need  now and in the immediate future the financial and technical assistance to translate ideals into realities.
27.5 Mr President, turning to Namibia's relationship with UNESCO, it needs to be reaffirmed that we fully  share the Organization's principles and ideals and support its work in the interests of peace, international  understanding and human development. Namibia is determined to play its part and to contribute (on the  national, regional and international levels) towards the solution of major challenges, be it in the fields of peace,  development, culture, science or protection of the environment. Since independence Namibia has been actively  involved in regional UNESCO conferences (including MINEDAF VI in Dakar, Senegal in July 1991). An  excellent working relationship has been established with the UNESCO office in Windhoek and the Namibian  Government has the highest appreciation for the co-operation and genuine support this office has demonstrated  since its establishment. We are firmly committed to strengthening established ties and increasing our  involvement in the regional and international activities under the aegis of UNESCO.
276 In this spirit Namibia wishes to congratulate the Director-General, the Executive Board and the  Secretariat on the Draft Programme and Budget for 1992-1993. We congratulate you on the clarity of  presentation of aims, objectives and programme details and budgets provided in this exemplary working  document. In broad terms, two general aspects need comment from my delegation. Firstly, Namibia appreciates  and fully supports the identification of three categories of beneficiaries whose urgent needs and expectations  represent special target groups for priority action, namely: the least developed countries (LDCs) including  Namibia; the Member States of the Africa region, of which Namibia is part; and women, who account for more  than half Namibia's population. This represents a realistic way of targeting resources to areas and groups whose  social and economic structures are in danger of total collapse, unless outside assistance is made available to  bolster national development initiatives.
27.7 The proposed reduction in expenditure to be achieved by streamlining operations, internal savings  through the redeployment of staff and other measures, are fully supported. As savings are to be redirected to  high-priority activities and particularly to the urgent needs of the most disadvantaged countries, this will truly  benefit Namibia and other Member States facing crisis situations.
27.8 Focusing on the seven major programme areas, general support is expressed for the various  programmes and programme actions. The Namibian delegation's position on specific issues will be clarified in  the sessions of the various commissions. There is a need, however, to highlight a few areas of special  significance for Namibia and the southern Africa subregion.
27.9 Major Programme Area I (Education and the future) is of particular importance to us for its strong  emphasis on developing the full educational capacities of Member States. Programmes aimed at improving  access to schools, enhancing the quality of primary education, literacy and adult education programmes,  strengthening science and technology in developing countries through appropriate education, fostering the  growth and the quality of higher education - all of these programmes are in line with the aspirations and  objectives of my country. Namibia can greatly benefit from support in these areas, with particular needs  associated with the education of marginalized groups. In higher education we face the task of restructuring and  strengthening our university. In this Namibia can benefit from assistance in the training of university  administrators and various other programmes.
27.10 Major Programme Area II (Science for progress and the environment), focusing on science, technology  and environmental issues in fostering development is of particular significance for all LDCs like Namibia.  Although generally rich in natural resources, our capacity to harness natural assets for the improvement of the  quality of life of our population is still sadly lacking. Namibia is heavily dependent on optimal utilization and  the responsible management of its natural resources, while having to cope with a fragile semi-arid  environment. Support in the development of marine science and marine resource management will prove vital  to Namibia's economy, while programmes on hydrology and water resource management are literally a matter  of life or death for our rural population.
27.11 Major Programme Area IN (Culture: past, present and future) earns our full support as an integral part  of man's full existence and a meaningful life. Since gaining independence, Namibia has entered into bilateral  cultural agreements with a number of countries, thus promoting and participating in cultural exchanges and  mutual appreciation of cultures. Internally, a high premium is placed on the preservation and enhancement of  our cultural heritage. Support from UNESCO and its Member States is sought for the return or restitution of  cultural property removed from Namibia during its dark century of colonialism. Namibia could further benefit  from and participate in programmes for the promotion of performing arts in Africa and the strengthening of  national and regional capacities for textbook production.
27.12 Under Major Programme Area IV (Communication in the service of humanity) Namibia fully supports  the free flow of information and is indeed proud of the freedom of its press. There is, however, in this area a  pressing need for full involvement, particularly of rural populations, in the development process - an aim only  to be achieved by means of effective communication. The linkage between communication and development is  undisputable, and the communication needs of Namibia and fellow Member States in the southern African  region deserve international support as a prerequisite for development.
27.13 Under Major Programme Area V (The social and human sciences in a changing world), the planned  activities in this area are broadly supported by Namibia, with particular emphasis on women, youth and  population initiatives.
27.14 Major Programme Area VI (UNESCO's contribution to prospective studies and to strategies concerned  with development) may at this moment present the greatest challenge to Namibia and all other developing  countries. Programmes aimed at developing human resources as the basis for economic and social development  fully deserve the support of all Member States.
27.15 Major Programme Area VII (UNESCO's contribution to peace, human rights and the elimination of all  forms of discrimination) touches on an area of deep concern for the Namibian population, and enjoys its fullest  support. Emerging from a history of suffering under apartheid, and being a next-door neighbour to South  Africa with close economic and infrastructural ties, Namibia is uniquely placed among UNESCO Member  States to make a valuable contribution to programmes and activities related to policies, practices and needs of a  post-apartheid South Africa.
27.16 Namibia further expresses its support for transverse themes and programmes targeting women, youth,  general information, statistical programmes and services.
27.17 Mr President, Namibia reiterates its sincere appreciation for UNESCO's and its Member States’ role in  assisting and support to our struggle for political independence. With that target achieved, our goal-posts have  shifted on to the fields of economic and social development, thus posing new and demanding challenges. While  assuring UNESCO of our full co-operation, involvement and contributions towards achieving the ideals and  objectives of this Organization, Namibia pledges its full support for the initiatives taken by the Director-
560   General, especially within the context of Priority: Africa and the World Declaration on Education for All and  its Plan of Action. We look forward to mutual benefit within the ambit of co-operation with UNESCO. Thank  you.
