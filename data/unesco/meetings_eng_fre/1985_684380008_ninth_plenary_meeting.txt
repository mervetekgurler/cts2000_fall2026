3. Le PRESIDENT :  Je remercie le chef de la délégation du Koweït et j'invite S. Exc.  M. Jackson Makwetta, ministre de l'éducation et chef de la délégation de la  République-Unie de Zanzanie, à prendre la parole.
4.1 Mr MAKWETTA (Tanzania) :  Mr President, Mr Director-General, Mr Chairman of the Executive Board,  Honourable Ministers and Heads of Delegations, Excellencies, distinguished  delegates, ladies. and gentlemen, it gives me great pleasure to join other  delegations in congratulating you, Mr President, on your unanimous election to  the presidency of the twenty-third session of the General Conference. I am con-  vinced that, under your guidance and wise leadership, our proceedings will be  conducted to a satisfactory conclusion.
4.2 Mr President, my delegation wishes, through you, to express its thanks  and appreciation to the Government of the People's Republic of Bulgaria .for  making it possible, safe and comfortable for us to meet here in a very cordial  atmosphere and to participate in the deliberations of this Conference. I wish to  assure you, Mr President, that my delegation enjoys the Bulgarian hospitality and  the beauties of your city, Sofia. .
4.3 Mr President, we cannot afford to shy away from our responsibilities as  true and committed members of Unesco. To us all, this is an important means to  development and peace. This Conference should therefore provide us with a forum  for reaching agreement on a meaningful and realistic budget for the coming bien-  nium. Document 23 C/5 is our main focus comprising the priority areas. We should  all strive to co-operate and support the activities which we know are fulfilling  the goals of our Organization.
4.4 We all know that the Organization has gone through a period of turmoil  and uncertainty. What has gone out through the mass media has not been very help-  ful either. My delegation commends the Director-General and all staff members for  their perseverance and the way they held firm to the ideals and principles of  Unesco during this critical period. Had it been that the Unesco administration  and its Secretariat were uncommitted to the Organization, it would have been  difficult for this Conference to take place, and we would not have had such a  well-constructed draft programme and budget today. The document we have before us  has incorporated ideas from various Member States, and has in it the new phenom-  enon of prioritizing the actions planned.
4.5 Mr President, the vigour, dedication and will of the Director-General  and the Secretariat of Unesco have not only been manifested in the draft pro-  gramme and budget, but also in the efficient manner in which the work of the  Organization has been conducted and administered against all odds.
4.6 Mr President, my deleqation also wishes to commend the Director-General,  Mr Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow, for all the measures he has taken to effect reforms in  Unesco, which have been geared towards the more efficient running of the Organiz-  ation. The changes made, and the steps taken, have not only contributed towards  the successful implementation of the biennial activities just ending but also  constitute future strategies for better work in Unesco. The presentation of docu-  ment 23 C/5 is one piece of positive evidence of these efforts.
4.7 Despite the withdrawal, and threats of withdrawal, from membership of  Unesco by some countries, we hope the members remaining in the Organization will  stand firm to maintain the principles and objectives of the 0rga.nization; and we  look forward to greater unity among members in the future. We call for the same  spirit - a spirit characterized by a positive and constructive approach - in our  decisions about what we want our activities to be for 1986-1987. Unesco, like  other United Nations agencies, has and will continue to have problems; but they  are not insurmountable. It is therefore our duty as Member States to find ways of  solving them, rather than to run away from them. It should also be recognized  that there is no human organization which is one hundred per cent perfect. So  Mr President, instead of emphasizing issues that divide us, let us concentrate on  those which unite us.
4.8 Mr President, allow me now to make some general remarks concerning the  Draft Programe and Budget for 1986-1987, which takes us to the second phase of  the current Medium-Term Plan. There has been a serious attempt to follow the  Medium-Term Plan, which remains the Organization's mandate for the planned  activities. My delegation is aware that the present budget has been reduced to  fit our pocket; as the saying goes, 'cut your shirt or coat according, to your  cloth'.
4.9 We also note with satisfaction that most of the planned activities  reflect man's need to create, and through research to increase, his level of  knowledge in the fields of education, scientific, cultural and information. Such  plans will succeed with the co-operation and support anticipated from the inter-  national community and Member States of Unesco.
4.10 Mr President, the prioritization of activities has basic advantages. It  ensures the implementation of the most important programmes and helps to cut down  costs. The selected activities will, I am sure, stimulate intellectual thought  and promote development and co-operation in the areas within the competence of  Unesco.
4.11 My delegation supports all the major programmes which have been pre-  sented in the draft programme and budget. We also notice, Mr President, that  Major Programme I has been given the weight it deserves, as a basis for solutions  to existing and predictable problems. This programme is indeed the basis for  scientific knowledge and serves as a guide for all the other programmes. The  emphasis given to Major Programme I is also reflected more clearly in the planned  activities and in the amount of funds allocated to it. We hope that Member States  will co-operate and support the implementation of this programme.
4.12 Mr President, Major Programme II on education has been given due atten-  tion. We commend the efforts made which are intended to foster lifelong education  for all. These efforts have led to the regional project for the eradication of  illiteracy in Africa. As a consequence of this, we are witnessing a gradual  improvement in the social and economic conditions of our people. My delegation  therefore supports the suggested programmes designed to help Member States pro-  mote access to general education.
4.13 Mr President, the strengthening of communication capacities in the  developing world as planned in Major Programme II is a measure to ensure the  establishment of an equitable communication network. The implementation of  activities under this programme will go some way towards bringing about a balance  of information access systems in the international community. Third World  countries badly need a balanced access to, and mutual exchange of, information  with the rest of the world. If the less-developed nations are helped to improve  their infrastructure for information gathering and dissemination, the existing  gap in the information exchange network in the world will be reduced.
4.14 Mr President, the planned activities in the sciences under Major Pro-  qrammes VI, IX and X are vital to the promotion of development in our countries.  The application of science is the basis for all our development activities. We,  therefore, need more practically oriented science programmes. Activities which  are urgently needed include those which facilitate research work geared towards  the exploitation of natural resources.
4.15 Through Unesco's programmes relating to the acquisition and dissemin-  ation of scientific and technological knowledge and information, it is possible  for us to benefit from the co-operation of scientists on a regional or inter-  national basis.
4.16 In order to enable our countries to make some positive strides in the  areas of social and economic development, more advanced training in scientific  and technological research is needed. We appreciate the work done by the Regional  Network of Educational Innovation for Development in Africa (NEIDA). We also  support the proposed activities and call upon the Organization to continue fund-  ing the NEIDA unit in order to achieve objectives which are of interest to Member  States in the region.
4.17 Mr President, scientific and technological extension activities can only  be effected well if there are efficient information exchange systems. The scien-  tific journal Impact of Science on Society is one such attempt to bring scien-  tific knowledge closer to the people and vice versa. As a forum for the exchange  of information and education innovations, Unesco is expected to strengthen its  operational machinery in this field. Part of the success of Unesco in this  endeavour is due to the Courier, which I am happy to say is also available is  Kiswahili, the lingua franca of my country, through Unesco's assistance. This has  helped many people in my country to understand more about the activities of  Unesco and the world around us.
4.18 Mr President, the principles underlying Programmes XII and XIII, that  is, 'The elimination of prejudice, intolerance, racism and apartheid', and  'Peace, international understanding, human rights and the rights of peoples,  constitute the most fundamental basis on which the United Nations and Unesco were  founded. To ignore these two programmes is to ignore the very ideals and prin-  ciples on which this Organization was founded and, therefore, its Constitution.  The devastating catastrophe brought about by the two world wars and the lessons  learned after these wars made the creation of the United Nations and Unesco  necessary to avoid further calamities in the future. So after the Second World  War, craving for peace, nations met and on 16 November 1945 adopted the Consti-  tution of Unesco. And so Unesco was created. I must add, Mr President, that the  majority of Member States represented here today were not even represented by
287    their own people. But the irony of it is that those States that were not the  creators or original signatories of the Unesco Constitution have greater respect  for it than some of the founding Member States.
4.19 The events taking place in apartheid South Africa, the daily massacres  of the black people of South Africa by the racist regime, the destabilization of  the whole region of Southern Africa by the apartheid regime makes it an inevit-  able preoccupation of the international community. When a people are turned into  perpetual refugees, as the Palestinian people are, peace and development cannot  prevail. Mr President, how can a decent and civilized people ignore this? We all  have to work for peace, development and human dignity.
4.20 Mr President, my delegation fully supports Major Programmes XII and XII1  alike and urges this Conference to give them the attention they deserve.
4.21 Mr President, my country reiterates her support for these programmes in  response to the ideals of the Organization ever since its establishment. These  programmes have drawn up activities for the promotion of understanding and co-  operation among and between nations in such areas as research, the dissemination  of research findings, the provision of information, and possible links between  disarmament and education, scientific and cultural development. And yet, these  issues are said to be political. What is not political in development?
4.22 Mr President, issues on women have also received due weight in the draft  programme and budget. The planned activities here are evidence of the great con-  cern which Unesco has with the status of women. Major Programme XIV is cross-  sectional. It is important that the Organization continues with these efforts and  takes regular stock of the achievements made.
4.23 Mr President, allow me now to make a few remarks on the implementation  of the planned activities. The plans we have before us may not achieve the  targets set if the mechanism for implementing them is not well designed. In this  regard, my delegation would like to support decentralization as one of the ways  for better implementation of the Organization's activities. But, while this is  our opinion, we would like at the same time to see a carefully planned structure  of Unesco and a clear and effective supervisory mechanism for all the programmes  and activities. We feel that the Regional Offices should be strengthened and,  where feasible, some of them merged to reduce costs and increase efficiency.
4.24 We, the representatives of governments, also have a role to play in  strengthening our National Commissions. The restructuring and consolidation of  Unesco's administration is also a necessary step towards the same goal. We  commend the Director-General for steps already taken, and we believe that in the  course of our discussions the commissions will come up with suggestions for even  greater improvement of Unesco's programme supervision machinery.
4.25 Mr President, with regard to cost reduction, my delegation is of the  view that posts that can be delayed in being filled without harm to the Organiz-  ation should be frozen until the financial situation has improved. This process  should help the Organization to implement other urgent activities, such as  strengthening of Regional and Subregional Offices and making them effective pro-  gramme supervisory centres.
4.26 Mr President, my delegation supports some of the present staff recruit-  ment procedures of Unesco. The principle of equitable geographical distribution  is indeed the best way of achieving equity in employment within Unesco. It is our  belief that more efforts will be made to encourage countries which are under-  represented in Unesco to send competent candidatures for vacant posts. My  delegation hopes that the Director-General will continue to look for the best  personnel whenever there is a need for the recruitment of new staff.
4.27 Mr President, as I mentioned earlier, it is not possible for me to  comment on the programme in its totality. However, it is my sincere belief that  the well-presented programmes and activities will be carefully analysed in the  relevant commissions and that the Director-General will be given the means he  required to implement them. For this reason, the Tanzanian delegation wishes to  support the appropriation resolution proposed in the Draft Programme and Budget  for 1986-1987.
4.28 Finally, Mr President, let me assure you that my country will remain  committed to the principles and objectives of Unesco as laid down in the Consti-  tution and will offer the fullest co-operation possible in the implementation of  its ideals.
4.29 Mr President, ladies and gentlemen, let us all strive to make the Unesco  Conference in Sofia a success! I thank you, Mr President.

I am  confident that you will guide the work of this very important twenty-third  session of the General Conference of Unesco to a successful conclusion.
6.2 Please permit me, Mr President, to thank the Chairman of the Council of  State of the People's Republic of Bulgaria, and the friendly people of Bulgaria,  for the hospitality extended to my delegation and for the enormous efforts they  have made in preparation for the success of this Conference.
6.3 Let me also, at this point, Mr President, express, on behalf of the  Government of Kenya, appreciation of and full confidence in the Director-General  and the entire Secretariat in their great attempt to discharge the Organization's  mandate under the most trying circumstances, particularly now and in the recent  past.
6.4 Mr President, the twenty-third session of the General Conference is  taking place at a time when Unesco is faced with many problems. We believe that  we do not have to regard these problems only in a negative manner. We must con-  sider them in the historical perspective, and as you know, Mr President, with the  passage of time and the changing world political and economic order, the con-  ception and magnitude of the problems and the approaches to them have changed.
6.5 Mr President, from its humble beginnings with 20 Members in 1946, today  Unesco has reached a membership of 160 States, representing all shades of  opinion. This has consequently placed upon the Organization many demands, thereby  necessitating the diversification of its programmes and continuous growth of its  budget and staff. My delegation consequently believes that the current problems  plaguing Unesco are inherent in its growth, and that rather than being the cause  of disagreement they should form the basis for a more vigorous and concerted  effort in our collected attempt to solve them. If Unesco has outstretched its  programmes, it is because of these changed circumstances coupled with the dynamic  nature of the international community to which the Organization belongs. My  delegation, Mr President, believes that Unesco has done a good job, notwithstand-  ing the many problems it has had to face. We believe that these problems can only  be solved competently by recourse to dialogue, mutual understanding, the recipro-  cal exchange of views and a spirit of solidarity among nations through active and  effective participation, not by withdrawals or threats of withdrawals. Indeed, Mr  President, Unesco needs now, more than ever before, the support and co-operation  of all its members if it is to survive and continue to carry out its mandate.
6.6 Mr President, I shall now confine myself to specific comments on the  major programmes of the Organization. First, let me state that we support a  proper balance between reflection and action. Major Programme I, Reflection on  world problems and future-oriented studies, provides a conceptual framework for  the organization of future programmes. We hold the view the research and reflec-  tion provided for in this major programme would serve Unesco well.
6.7 Mr President, concerning the programmes on education, my delegation  notes with satisfaction the continued emphasis on the right to education, the  quality of its content and its relevance to the development needs of society. The  provision of education remains a priority commitment for every Member State, not  only because education is a fundamental right of every citizen in a democratic  society, but also because it is the most effective agent of social and economic  development. It is because of its central position in national development that  every country, despite the heavy pressure on meagre resources, is still prepared  to make sacrifices for the development of education. At the global level, Unesco  shares this commitment and continues to help in stimulating the development of  education and the eradication of illiteracy in Member States.
6.8 We welcome the emphasis given to training activities, more particularly  because developing countries like Kenya still face inadequacy of expertise in  many fields. Indeed, Mr President, a lot of our development problems stem from  this inadequacy. Ao matter how well-meaning our development strategies are and  how well-designed the development projects may be, problems have arisen at the  implementation stages due to lack or shortage of trained manpower. This, there-  fore, is an area that should receive the greatest priority in Unesco's pro-  grammes. Mr President, my delegation therefore supports  Unesco's objectives of co-ordinating, at the national, regional and international  levels, methods of inventorying, collecting and preserving the different forms of  non-physical heritage, including oral traditions and non-verbal traditions.
