(6.1) DR, FUAD. SARRUF (Lebanon) (Translation froin the Arabie): dut taf,  It may be useful fo mention here that the Convention hd Recorhmondation ‘against Discrimi-  nation in Education adopted by the General Confe rence in its eleventh session. in 1960 are a ‘fun-
1225   damental part of the activities of the United Nations and the Specialized Agencies engaged in the  .campaign against discrimination in education. However, when Unesco was preparing these two  instruments it had in mind two separate but complementary objectives, It sought on the one hand  . to eliminate discriminatiôri, and on the other to strengthen the right to education through equality  of opportunity and treatment in this field, Therefore the undertakings of member gountries dif-  fer in scope according to their views on discrimination and/or on equality of opportunity.
(6.2) Within the framework of eliminating and preventing all forms of discrimination, according  to the meaning given in the Convention, we find that States are invited to agree to'a number of  obligations which should bé undértaken immediately. On the other hand, the realization of equa-  lity of opportunity requires complicated action and has financial implications in many countries;  it can, therefore, only be achieved gradually. The principles applicable to the two questions  and included in the Convention and Recommendation seek thus to pave the way towards the elabo-  ration, development and application in the different countries of national educational policies  tending to extend and strengther équality of opportunity, without any discrimination whatever, in  education,
(6.3) Consequently we find that both the Convention and the Recommendation are highly important  and cover in this respect far more ground than any of the other instruments drawn up by Unesco,  And yet, twelve years after the adoption of these two instruments, only fifty-eight countries  have signed them. |  ‘
(6.4) Since one of the essential tasks of Unesco and other international organizations is to verify  the extent to which Member States apply their conventions and recommendations, consultations  with the Member States and their presentation of periodic reports on this matter are acknowledged  to be highly important. Unesco has therefore been initiating these consultations since 1964 as an  essential element in the practical application of these two instruments, the Convention and the  Recommendation.
(6.5) Yet we find that this method has not even yet been fully applied by Member States. Regret-  tably, only forty-one States have submittizd their second periodic reports on time, and fourteen  have submitted them late. We were therefore pleased to see the proposals drawn up and submit-  ted by the Committee on Conventions and Recommendations in the field of education in document
17 C/5, paragraphs 203-208. These proposals indicate new ways which may help to make future  consultations more satisfactory and fruitful; and we should like to lay particular stress on the  proposals in paragraph 205 referring to the services of foreign or national consultants which  Unesco may provide for countries desiring to receive suggestions which would help them to over-  come the difficulties they encounter in implementing the Convention and Recommendation, It  seems to us that the proposal that national institutions should support these consultants in their  work is useful and constructive; and I hope that it will be welcomed by all those countries which  have not submitted any reports so far, or have submitted them incomplete, or have included in  them irrelevant information, owing to diverse difficulties, including the lack of competent per-  sonnel,
(6.6) Another point raised with the object of improving consultation methods relates to the form  that leads to a closer relation between the Secretariat and the Member States, We therefore  find that the Committee proposes in paragraph 207 of its report that no third questionnaire should  be sent but that Member countries should merely be invited to submit reports drawn up in general  terms, This proposal, however, was not approved by the Executive Board, which recommended  in its comments on the Committee's Report (document 17 C/5,Add,) that the same questionnaire  be revised and simplied. For my part, I think that this proposal of the Executive Board is wise,  because it gives Member States the necessary guidance to formulate their replies, and at the  same time facilitates the Committee's drafting of its third analytical report on the subject.
(6.7) The Lebanese delegation would therefore like to express its support for the draft resolu-  tion (DR /PLEN. 15) presented by Argentina.

1, The Member countries which have not yet subscribed to the Convention ought to take the  necessary measures to do so;
2. The obligation to submit periodic reports should be fulfilled;
3. Member States so wishing should invite Unesco to appoint national or foreign experts to  assist them in drawing up their reports; . ; ce ‘ ‘
4. The questionnaire should be revised and simplified, and sent to the Member States in  time. :
