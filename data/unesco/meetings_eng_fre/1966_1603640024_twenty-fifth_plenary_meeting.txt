(13,1) Mrs, GLADKAYA (Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic) :  Mr. President, delegates, Unesco is an organization created by nations which have known  * the horrors of War, an organization intended by its Constitution and work to promote peace and  international understanding - inevitably so, since education, culture and science, contributing  as they do to the good of mankind, can flourish only in peace, Unesco, indeed, has no greater  or more important task, none dearer to all nations, than the consolidation of peace by all the  methods appropriate to the Organization: this is why we regard the matter being discussed  today at this plenary meeting of the fourteenth session of the General Conference as of extreme  importance,
(13.2) This delegationhas already spokenof results obtained by Unesco in this respect over the  last twenty years, and now draws attention to the great tasks facing it in the future; in assess-  ing the results, we hope that the future will prove Unesco even more efficient and effective,
(13,3) Resolutions for the defence of peace are already to its credit, but in our view not enough  attention is devoted in the draft programme and budget for 1967-1968 to practical measures to  give effect to them, particularly in the education, communication, social and human sciences  sectors,
(13,4) Thedefence of peace requires co-ordinated action, with all Unesco departments and scc-  tions working not only at exposing the dangers of aggression but at handling it firmly and unani-  mously, always with the underlying idea of peace, and States with different social and economic  systems living peacefully together,
(13.5) This is obvious to all nations, particularly those which have suffered aggression, Our  nation, our republic, suffered terribly in the Second World War, Millions perished, Over
700 towns and 28,000 villages were destroyed and burnt, 10 million were left homeless, and  gome 33,000 schools, technical colleges and establishments of higher education were des-  troyed, together with cultural property and works of art,
(13,6) We therefore wellknow the full significance of peace for the free, all-round development  of man, and we know the cost of the efforts needed to defend peace. So, when we gpeak of the  work of Unesco, we returnonce more to the need for action, without which the decisions  adopted cannot be implemented,
(13.7) Naturally, research (including peace hypotheses and their typology, mentioned on page 72  of the Director-General's 1965 Report) has acertainimportance; but peace cannot be preserved  and strengthened by research alone, but by positive action to eliminate war as a means of set-  tling disputes between States, and by mobilizing public opinion everywhere for a resolute con-  demnation of aggression in all shapes and forms.
