[20] The CHAIRMAN announced that the Pre-  sident of the Syrian Republic had sent a telegram,  dated 10 June, instructing Mr. Wadji Mallat to re-  present Syria for the purpose of electing the mem-  bers of the Executive Board. As that country had  stated that it would not be represented at the  current session, the Credentials Committee would  have to meet as soon as possible to consider  Mr. Wadji Mallat’s credentials and to submit a  report to the plenary meeting on Thursday morn-  ing, before the election of the members of the  Executive Board. [21] The CHAIRMAN read a registered letter,  dated 7 June, from the President of the Union of  Slovene Cultural Associations, making certain com-  plaints against the Italian Government on the  subject of the status and syllabuses of Slovene  schools in Italy, and asking Unesco to forward the  communications to the United Nations to secure  the observance of all the clauses of the peace treaty.  The Chairman recalled that the General Committee  had discussed a similar question at its fourth  meeting and had decided to acknowledge receipt  of the request without putting it on the agenda,  and to inform the Secretary-General of the United  Nations of the action taken.  It was decided to follow the same procedure.  The meeting rose at 10.40 a.m.  ELEVENTH MEETING  Friday, 16 June 1950, at 9.30 a.m.  Chairman:  Appropriation Resolution.  [1] Dr. BENDER (Chairman of the Budget Com-  mittee) understood that an appropriation resolu-  tion, submitted by the United States delegation  to the Programme and Budget Commission, had  been adopted. He expressed a certain astonish-  ment that the resolution had not been submitted  in the first instance to the Budget Committee.  [2] In the absence of a satisfactory explanation,  Sir John MAUD (Chairman, Executive Board)  pointed out that, whatever had happened, it was  quite clear that the Appropriation Table was for  the Budget Committee to draft and present to the  General Conference. Agreed.  Method of work during the last two days of  the Session.  [3] The CHAIRMAN observed that the main  questions considered by the three Commissions  were to come before the plenary meetings that  Count STEFANO Jacint (Italy)  day and the following day. He foresaw two  dangers; firstly, that detailed discussions might  take place which would be very lengthy and might  annul the work done by the three Commissions;  secondly, that, on the contrary, the Conference  might simply take note of the conclusions of the  Commissions, without any discussion of the sub-  stance of their reports. He therefore proposed  the following procedure:  (a) That the Rapporteurs should present their  reports without being limited to any length of  time.  (b) That delegates who agreed with the con-  clusions of the Rapporteurs should not request  to speak.  (c) That delegates who disagreed with any  points in the reports and had amendments to  present should state their position, their speeches  being limited to a given number of minutes.  (d) That the Rapporteurs should reply to the  points raised.  (e) That a vote should be taken without further  discussion.             [4] Sir Ben THOMAS (United Kingdom) pointed  out that certain matters would be referred to the  plenary session for decisions; for example, the  Administrative Commission had taken no decision  on the United States proposal regarding the prob-  lem of bridging the gap between the estimated  revenue of $7,600,000 for 1951 and the pro-  visional total budget figure of $8,200,000 approved  by the Programme and Budget Commission. The  matter was therefore being referred to the plenary  session for a decision.  [5] Sir John MAUD (Chairman, Executive Board)  thought that it would not be out of keeping with  the procedure proposed by the Chairman if there  were a free debate on matters upon which the three  Commissions had arrived at no conclusion. The  Budget Committee, in its final consolidated re-  port, would then have to take account of any such  decisions taken by the plenary session.  [6] Dr. BENDER (Chairman, Budget Committee)  pointed out that the Budget Committee did not  intend to submit a consolidated report. It had  already submitted an interim report, a final re-  port was being prepared, and it would also  submit an appropriation resolution and an ap-  propriation table.  | 7] Sir John MAUD (Chairman, Executive Board)  proposed that the plenary session should hear the  report. of the Administrative Commission before  that of the Programme and Budget Commission,  while Dr. CHAND (Chairman, Administrative  Commission) and Mr. SEYDOUX (Chairman,  Programme and Budget Commission) were in  favour of the inverse procedure.  [8] On the other hand, Mr. SARGEANT (United  States of America) agreed with Sir John Maud  that it would be more appropriate for the plenary  session to hear the Report of the Administrative  Commission on the income which could reason-  ably be expected in 1951 and its suggestions for  finding additionnal funds to arrive at the  provisional budget total of $8,200,000 before it  considered the programme as proposed in the  report of the Programme and Budget Commis-  sion.  Dr. PARRA PÉREZ (Venezuela) recalled that  the Administrative Commission had not contested  the provisional budget total of $8,200,000 adopted  by the Programme and Budget Commission, but  that the disagreement of several delegations to the  proposed methods of financing that budget had  crystallized, so that it had been felt necessary to  refer the matter to the plenary session.  [9] Dr. CHAND (Chairman, Administrative Com-  mission) observed that, if the plenary session first  adopted the budget total of $8,200,000, then the  first recommendation of the Administrative Com-  mission to base the total budget for 1951 on the  figure of $7,600,000 would be eliminated; but its  suggestions regarding certain measures which  could be taken to implement a budget of $8,200,000  would still be operative. He therefore did not see  any real difficulty, and suggested that the plenary  session should first consider the report of the  Progamme and Budget Commission, which would  mean that the provisional figure of $8,200,000  would be converted into a final figure.  [10] It was agreed that the report of the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission should be sub-  mitted to the plenary session before that of the  Administrative Commission.  [11] The General Committee then fixed the pro-  gramme of work for 16 and 17 June.  The meeting rose at 11. a.m.
