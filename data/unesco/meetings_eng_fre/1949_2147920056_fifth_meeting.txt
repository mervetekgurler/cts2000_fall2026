The Director- General recalled the great services rendered by Dr. Kuo as Special Adviser for the Far East, and observed that it would be most useful to have other representatives, not stationed in different quarters of the world but prepared to go anywhere to establish contacts. The Director-General remarked that his proposal fell far short of the ideal he had in mind, which was to have enough representatives to be able to take action simul- taneously in all the National Commissions—some twenty or thirty people, some of them being able to deal with several countries. That, however, would require a budget of over $800,000, even if those representatives were not of a very high grade. As the increase in the budget for 1950 had been reduced to some $300,000 owing to the readjustments consequent on the various currency devaluations, the Director-General could obviously not ask for such a large sum for one activity alone. 399 three, to be supplemented by national cor- respondents. In that connexion, he mentioned the excellent results obtained by that means by the ILO in the last twenty years or more. There were various advantages in introducing such cor- respondents: they were nationals of the Member State in which they were working and therefore there was no need for a period of adaptation; the cost would not be very high, as salaries would be paid in the currency of the country concerned and on the local scale (the correspondents might be paid salaries corresponding to those of the senior officials in government departments, for instance). The Director-General had calculated the cost of the scheme and did not think it prohibitive; full implementation could not, however, be con- templated immediately, and he therefore proposed to introduce the system gradually and to see what the results were. The correspondents would be chosen from people with a knowledge of Unesco's work and responsibilities, and would act, as it were, as ambassadors of the Organization. If the General Conference decided to institute full-time posts of Secretaries-General to the different Na- tional Commissions, it would certainly produce excellent immediate results, but the Director- General thought that, if the ground were prepared by national correspondents, the results would be still better; adaptation to the local conditions of each country was essential. The Director-General therefore asked the Commission to consider his proposal from the point of view of the advantages which Unesco might derive from it, as well as from that of the cost entailed. The representatives of these governments at the proposed conference would be free to examine this question of the establishment of a centre from all aspects, geo- graphical, cultural and linguistic. Professor CHEN Yuan (China) supported the proposal of the delegate of India, since his draft resolution stated that the meeting to be convened was “for the purpose of exploring the pos- sibilities...”. Mr. de BLONAY (Head, Bureau of External Relations) remarked that the Director-General would not be able to carry out the recommendation unless the Conference made some corresponding provision in the budget; he therefore proposed that the draft recommendation should be amended by the addition of the words: ... The Director-General is instructed to consider the effect of this recommendation on the budget, and to report to the Programme and Budget Commission which is alone competent to deal with the matter. Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) called attention to the fact that the cost should be considered and, while he shared the interest of his colleagues of the South and East Asian countries in regard to this project, he suggested that they themselves should explore the possibilities of providing such a meeting and such an Institute.
