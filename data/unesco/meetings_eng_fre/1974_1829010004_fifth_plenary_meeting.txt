4,2 Today, as we start upon the eighteenth seasion of the General Conference of Unesco, we note  that the world hae undergone great and fundamental changes since the end of the 19508, As a world  Organization, Unesco has succeeded remarkably wellinnccommodating itself tothese changes.  What fs more important, on several instances in the past, it successfully performed its function  of inftiating changes through the introduction of programmes of activities and through innovation,  Adaptation to change and anticipation of new conditions - In spite of conspicuous difficulties - have  in fact become adopted principles of policy in the development of the Organization.
4,3 1 have therefore come to this Conference with the conviction that Unesco fulfils a very basic  need of our human community, the need of nurturing moral and intellectual enlightenment through  education, science and culture, If we are determined in our common search for a lasting peace  and if we are decided In our efforts to lay the foundations for a brighter future and greater happi-  ness for future generations, we must reconfirm our belief in the goodness of Unesco.
4,4 The forthcoming biennium needs to be viewed in terme of an up-to-date assessment of changes  in the world situation, The Draft Programme and Budget for 1975-1976, carefully prepared by the  competent Secretariat of this Organization is, to my mind, an Impressive document, It attests to  the expanding rôle of Unesco in responding to the major educational, scientific and cultural prob-  lems of the world, And yet, as any large-scale organization requires, a critical assessment can  and should be made,
4.5 Since many newly independent States have joined Unesco, rapid development in general has  brought about significant changes in their capacity to identify major problems, to assess the nature  of these problems, and to find ways and means for their resolution, It is becoming increasingly  obvious, for example, that developing countries have hud, in the course of their independence,  experience which may benefit countries at similar stages of development or with similar social  and cultural characteristics, At present, the channels available in multilateral collaboration under  the auspices of international organizations in fact leave too little room for such countries to bene -  fit from each other's experiences.
4.6 The experience gained in many of the newly independent States quite naturally leads to a  demand for changing relationships between them and the older States, and between these new  States and international organizations, such as Unesco, Interaational organizations are in a  unique position to develop and to introduce new forms of international co-operation and assist-  ance in responding more effectively to the cause of development,
4,7 In the development of international assistance, especially in the initial stages of planning and  programming, all too frequently not eufficient attention ie given to making optimal use of the  experience, knowledge end Intellectual resources actually available in the States concerned. Too  often fact-finding and advisory missions, to which reference is made In the Introduction to docu-  ment 18 C/5, take the form of transient consultants, who ususally do not have sufficient time to  inform themselves adequately about the local situation, Consequently the work may be based on  hurried discussions and hasty conclusions. This type of work is surely not to be commended. I  submit, Madam President, that such a work method Is in no way fitting for our Organization. I  believe, and I trust you agree, that there would be much more effective transfer of experience and  knowledge if, firstly, the available manpower of the countries concerned ia properly identified  and, secondly, ways and raeans are found to utilize it more fully.
4.8 I am, of course, not referring to the top-level experts who come to our country to discuss  basic problems with our own top-level experts and top-level officials. It fe assumed, and we  trust that we are not v.rong, that these top-level experts have an abundance of relevant empirical  and theoretical knowledge, Besides, we are aware that this type of expert is not in a position to  spend a long period of time In the host country,
4,9 It is heartening that fn the case of collaboration between my own country and the Unesco  Secretariat in the field of educational and cultural development considerable progress has been  made to overcome the problem to which I have just referred, Consultations preceding and follow-  ing the planning and programming of activities have been conducted on the basis of the principle  of the full utilization of national intellectual resources and in full recognition of a two-way com-  munication of ideas,
4,10 I am pleased to note that the Draft Programme and Budget, if compared to those of previous  biennia, shows a marked improvement in its attempt to develop an interdisciplinary approach to  certain selected problems, It Is recognized, of course, thr the document does not encompass  all major world problems, It pertains rather to those problems which we have agreed should come  within the purview of Unesco and as such are relevant to its much-cherished goals.
4.11 Nevertheless, I do not think that the problems of poverty have been made sufficlently explicit  in the listing of problems and analyses, The problem of poverty - in many areas of our common  world, dire poverty - unfortunately still affects the majority of the nations of the worid, If we are  willing to recognize painful realities, it is almost inevitable that this stupendous, but at the same  time delicate, problem effects practically all spheres of Unesco's activities: education, science  and culture, It is not a problem to be relegated only to economice, to those concerned with  economic development, Unless the policy and strategy of development are specifically focused on
84   the gigantic task of abolishing poverty, problems of poverty cannot be automatiéally assumed to  fall under the géneral rubrié of development. ' The overall programme of Unesco and also its  medium-term plan should reflect the problem of poverty if we are concerned with the effort to  improve the quality of life of mankind, the culture of all humanity and not only of selected  categories of people,
4.12 Madam President, as Unesco expands its activities and organization, endeavours to decen-  tralize its programming, administration, and finance are becoming more meaningful, However,  in my view, the rate of decentralization is generally not commensurate with the urgency of relat-  ing programmes to the particular needs of the couniries concerned,
