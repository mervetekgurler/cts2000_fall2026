[1] In the interests of speeding up the Com-  mission's work, Professor NIELSEN (Denmark)  agreed to withdraw his earlier proposal, but  thought an explanation might be useful for those  who had been unable to follow the development of  the question. After the Session of the General  Conference which had instructed the Director-  General to pursue the plan, the Committee on  Unesco of the National Research Council in the  United States had set up a Sub-Committee which  prepared a most inspired report, to which the  Director-General drew the attention of Member  States, with a request for comments. This step  was endorsed at the Fourth Session of the General  Conference.  In its report, the Sub-Committee in question had  suggested that the proposed institute should noi  confine itself to routine work in computation, but  that its principal task should be to foster and  develop the mathematical theory behind modern  computation; to adapt the problems of pure and  applied mathematics to the new and powerful tools  of the day and to collect and publish the results.  In order to avoid any overlapping and because the  action recommended was not sufficiently covered  by the name “International Computation Centre”,  the Sub-Committee of United States experts pro-  posed that it might more properly be called the  “International Mathematical Statistical Consulting  Centre”.  Professor Nielsen considered that the important  point was not the name of the body, but that it  should develop along the lines which had char-  acterized it during recent years.
