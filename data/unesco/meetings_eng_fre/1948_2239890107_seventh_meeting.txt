The CHAIRMAN stated that the question of  adopting Arabic as a working language had been  referred, the day before, to the Budget Sub-Com-  mission. That body was not able to deal with the  matter until it had received from the Secretariat  an estimate of the cost entailed. As soon as it  wa available, the report of the Budget Sub-Com-  mission would be submitted to the General Com-  mittee, which would decide how the matter  should be handled.  With regard to the amendment to Rule 51  (Working Languages) a small drafting committee  had been at work, but agreement had not been  maintained on the wording it had at first adopted.  The Chairman suggested that, in accordance  with Rule 32, paragraph 2, the Committee should  appoint a Sub-Committee of nine members to  deal with the question. He proposed that the  members of the drafting committee should form  that Sub-Committee.  Dr. ARTAS CUENCA (Argentina) informed the  meeting that the drafting committee was assem-  bled at that moment, and at his request, sup-  ported by M. GIAMBRUNO (Uruguay) and  Dr. CHEDIAK (Cuba), the CHAIRMAN postponed  the question, in order to enable the Committee to  reach agreement.  Amendment to paragraph 9 of Article IV of  the Constitution, submitted by the delega-  tion of New Zealand (document 3C/PRO/3).  Mr. RIDLING (New Zealand) explained that the  proposed amendment was a very simple alteration  of the restriction placed on the General Confer-  ence by the Constitution. The New Zealand  Government was in favour of the broadest possible  exchange of views through frequent visits by  Unesco officials to various countries. Better  mutual understanding invariably resulted from  such contacts. However, the annual Conference  took up a great deal of the Secretariat’s time and  of the Organization’s money and slowed down the  implementation of the programme. Therefore  his Government was anxious to eliminate the last  seven words of paragraph 9 of Article IV and thus  to remove the obligation for the Conference to  meet in a different place each year.  The amendment was seconded by Professor  SOMMERFELT (Norway) and Dr. SASSEN  (Netherlands).  Dr. WALKER (Australia) wished to support  the New Zealand proposal because, under the  amendment, the Conference would still be free  to choose the place of its sessions but, as the  decision would rest with the Conference itself,  it would have greater freedom.  The CHAIRMAN stressed that the discussion  was a preliminary one only, since all amendments  to the Constitution required a two-thirds majority  in the Conference.  The New Zealand proposal was adopted unani-  mously. (14%)  Draft amendment to Article IV. E of the  Constitution, submitted by the Director-  General (document 3C/PRO/}).  The DIRECTOR-GENERAL explained that the  addition of a paragraph 14 to Article IV.E was a  consequence of a resolution passed by the Mexico  City Conference. The amendment had been sub-  mitted to Member States by a circular letter, dated
17 April 1948 (Annex I to document 3C/PRO/4).  The CHAIRMAN pointed out that the amend-  ment was only an adjustment concerning the  sending of observers to the Conference.  Dr. ALLCOCK (United Kingdom), who had  been responsible for the proposal made at Mexico  City, formally moved the resolution.  M. ARIAS CUENCA (Argentina) seconded it.  Dr. WALKER (Australia) supported the sub-  stance of the amendment but thought its wording  could be improved.

However,  the Belgian proposal was acceptable, provided that  a new draft was examined in writing before any  decision was taken.  The CHAIRMAN thought that the wording of  paragraphs (3) and (4) presented no great diffi-  culty and that, in view of the few meetings left,  the Committee should try to settle the matter  without delay. The CHAIRMAN read out the re-draft of Rule 51  which had been prepared by the drafting com-  mittee :  English and French are the working languages of  the General Conference. Wherever there are facilities  for simultaneous interpretation, Spanish shall also be  considered a working language.  Mrs. BRUNAUER (United States of America)  pointed out that a change in Rule 54 would also  be necessary.  EIGHTH  Dr. NANNETTI (Colombia) reported that the  small drafting committee had unanimously  adopted the English text just read out by the  Chairman.  The proposed wording of Rule 51 was unani-  mously adopted.  In Rule 52, the only change necessary was the  deletion of the word “two”.  Agreed.  Rule 53 remained unchanged.  In Rule 54, the words “in English and in  French” were substituted for “in the two working  languages of the Conference”.  Rule 55 remained unchanged.  Paragraph (1) of Rule 56 was deleted and the  word “languages” was substituted for “language”  in pararaph (2).  Thus amended, the whole of Chapter X was  adopted.
