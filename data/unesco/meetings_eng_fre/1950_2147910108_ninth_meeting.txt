[16] Mr. ROSEMAN (United States of America)  stressed that, if receipts of funds fell short of the  amounts anticipated, there would have to be a  corresponding curtailment in the programme.  Any economies effected would result in additional  funds for the programme. It was not sound  financial practice to keep on adding to the  Working Capital Fund to make up deficits. The  Government of the United States, if fully con-  vinced that the Unesco programme was worth  supporting, might be prepared to increase its  contribution; but they had certain reservations  about the programme as presented by the Ex-  ecutive Board. With these additional comments,  he supported the views expressed by the delegate  of Australia.  [17] Mr. POLLOCK (Canada) declared that his  Government would also feel more confident in  supporting Unesco if there were less confusion  and more concentration of purpose in the pro-  gramme. He believed there could be no alternative  to the proposal made by the Commission, that  the programme be divided into two parts. Mem-  ber States should not propose programmes to  which they were unwilling to contribute finan-  cially, and the Programme and Budget Commis-  sion must face this situation realistically.  [18] It was agreed that the committee would  draft a resolution for presentation to the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission, recommending  that the latter divide the programme into two  parts, as previously proposed by the Admini-  strative Commission in document 5C/ADM/18:  the first part would be financed out of approxi-  mately 93 per cent of the budget funds and the  second part would be undertaken only if funds  were actually available. The actual expenditure  on the programme should therefore be about 7 per  cent less than the budgetary ceiling. Further-  more, the resolution should contain a statement  to the effect that, in the course of the discussion,  it was made clear that some Member States would  find unacceptable any increase of the budget over  the 1950 figure. [1] Mr. KIRPAL (India) stated that his dele-  gation was not satisfied with the composition of  the Secretariat: out of a total of 345 posts, 185  were filled by Europeans, while Asia was re-  presented so that the criteria laid down in Arti-  cle IV of the Constitution were not fulfilled. In conclusion, he moved the fol-  lowing resolution:  The Administrative Commission receives the report  of the Director-General on the distribution of members  of the Secretariat by nationality, takes note of the fact  that the distribution of personnel has not so far been  made on a wide geographical basis and recommends  that the General Conference instruct the Director-  and to take all necessary steps to improve the distribu-  tion of posts by nationality as soon as possible.  [2] Dr. EL DIWANY (Egypt) seconded the pro-  posal of the Indian delegate. In his view no pro-  ogress had been made as regards better geogra-  phical distribution within the Secretariat, despite  numerous recommendations to that effect.  The Egyptian delegation would have something  to say regarding the recruitment of the staff of  the Secretariat. On the present occasion it merely  wished to point out that if certain delegations  raised the problem of geographical distribution,  the object was not to secure appointments for  their nationals nor even observance of the Consti-  tution; they were concerned to increase the effec-  tiveness of the Organization and to give confidence  to the peoples.
