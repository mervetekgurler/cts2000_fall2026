11] In examining the Provisional Agreement be-  tween the International Bureau of Education and  Unesco, Mr. ABRAHAM (United States of Amer-  ica), had noted certain anachronisms, such as the  provision made for a committee later to draw up  a definitive agreement and the proposal for the  fusion of the two organizations. He therefore  proposed the deletion of these items. Further-  more, the third solution for the future relationship  of the two organizations, as proposed in the third  paragraph of document 5C/OXR/7, Addendum,  should now be eliminated.  [2] He proposed further that, at some later  date, the Unesco Department of Education might  issue a brochure explaining the relationship be-  tween the clearing-house functions of Unesco and  the work of IBE, whose task it was to assist  Ministries of Education and educators through-  out the world.  [3] Mr. MONTEL (France) thought it 1m-  possible, during the current session of the Gen-  eral Conference, to make the changes requested  by the United States delegate. He therefore sug-  gested that the Joint Unesco-IBE Committee  should place on the agenda of its next meeting  the revision of the text of the provisional agree-  ment, in accordance with the views expressed by  the United States delegation.  Professor PIAGET (Director, International Bu-  reau of Education) saw no objection to this pro-  cedure.  [4] Mr. de BLONAY (Head, External Relations  Service) proposed that the following words be  added to the last paragraph of the resolution on  page 2 of document 5C/OXR/7: “after revision  of its wording in the light of experience, and tak-  ing the changed circumstances into account”.  At Professor PIAGET’s request, the words “the  changed” were altered to “present”. 087        [10] Mr. MURTINHO (Brazil) wished to raise a  point which he thought had not yet been con-  sidered. In some countries the National Commis-  sion, which was under the authority of the  Ministry of Education, collaborated closely with  the local organizations directlv responsible for  carrying out the programme. But in other coun-  tries, and particularly in Brazil, the National  Commission was quartered in the Ministry of  Foreign Affairs, and was therefore outside the  organizations dealing with activities of concern  to Unesco.  [11] In order to minimize the disadvantages  arising from this lack of liaison, he suggested the  addition of a fifth paragraph to Resolution A, re-  commending that Member States establish in their  Ministry of Education or other bodies entrusted  with carrying out Unesco’s Programme, a depart-  ment with direct responsibility, which would  ensure contact with the National Commission  attached to the Ministry for Foreign Affairs.  [12] The CHAIRMAN expressed interest in  Mr. Murtinho’s proposal, which would affect many  countries in the same position. However, this  must be stated as a recommendation not of the  National Commissions, but of the Official and  External Relations Commission.  [13] Mr. MONTEL (France) considered that this  problem did not arise in France. The French  National Commission was responsible both to the  Ministry for Foreign Affairs, where it was  quartered, and to the Ministry of Education, which  had set up a permanent department to maintain  contact between the French authorities and  Unesco. He did not think it advisable to insert  a new paragraph in Resolution A, and considered  that a slight change in the text of paragraph 2  would meet the Brazilian delegate’s point.  [14] Dr. CHEDIAK (Cuba) stated that the Cuban  National Commission would find itself in a dif-  ficult situation if tied to the Ministry of Foreign  Affairs. The National Commission received assist-  ance from Governmental Bodies and from the  Ministries of Foreign Affairs and of Education.  However, in order to avoid duplication of work,  he proposed that Unesco should transmit informa-  tion to the National Commissions through normal  international channels. National Commissions in  some countries were linked with the Ministry for  Foreign Affairs, and in others with the Ministry of  Education. The Cuban National Commission was  linked with both. It submitted to each Ministry  questions which fell within the competence of that  body. The structure of the National Commission  would be endangered if it were to be tied exclusive-  ly to the Ministry of Education.
588  The situation explained by Mr. Murtinho was  an international problem, and in each country the  position was different and required individual  solution. It had been agreed in the Procedure  Committee that invitations to the next General  Conference should be sent by the Ministry for  Foreign Affairs, as well as by the Ministry of  Education. It was inadvisable to have two  Ministries dealing with the same question.
115] Mr. MURTINHO (Brazil) maintained his  original stand-point. In his opinion the Ministry  of Foreign Affairs could only act as a “post office”.  It seemed to him that, in each Member State,  questions of education, science and culture should  come within the orbit of three organizations: the  appropriate Ministry, the National Commission  and an executive body.
116] Dr. HENAO HENAO (Colombia) seconded  the Bazilian point of view, and asked whether he  might make a definite proposal for the constitution  of such a body.
117] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) stressed that, as  the situation varied so greatly from country to  country, no specific recommendation could be  made. He proposed, therefore, that the best way  of meeting Mr. Murtinho’s wishes was to adopt  an amendment to resolution A, paragraph 2, as  proposed by Mr. Montel, and to call attention to  the records of the discussion on this matter.  [18] The CHAIRMAN agreed that it was un-  desirable to interfere in the local problems of  Member States, and found Mr. Weeden’s proposal  acceptable.  [19] Mr. MONTEL (Frence) proposed that re-  solution A, paragraph 2, be amended by the addi-  tion of the following words: “and to link the  National Commissions with the Ministries of  Foreign Affairs and of Education.”
120] Referring to Article VII of the Constitution,  which leaves Member States free to make such  arrangements as suit their own particular con-  ditions, Miss PEDERSEN (Denmark) could not  agree to the inclusion of any recommendation  such as that proposed by Mr. Montel.  [21] Mr. ABRAHAM (United States of America)  proposed that the Commission take no action  beyond advising Member States in an informal  manner of the proposal made by the delegate of  Brazil, and calling attention to the record of the  discussion on the subject.  [22] Dr. KADHIM (Iraq), while agreeing that  a modification of resolution A, paragraph 2 would  be acceptable, could not agree with Mr. Montel's  specific recommendation. [24] Sir Charles WEBSTER (United Kingdom)  agreed that it was inadvisable to recommend that  governments should link National Commissions to  Ministries, and thought governments might be re-  quested to provide National Commissions with  adequate opportunity for consultation with the  Ministries.
