[28] Mr. DARD called the attention of delegates to the conclusions of the Committee which had sat in Florence to examine reports of Member States
(5C/Resolutions, page 118). That Committee had decided that reports should serve two purposes: (a) “To inform the Organization itself what its Member States had done to carry out their obliga- tions”; and (b) “To provide the public with a readable account of educational, scientific and cul- tural activity carried on in conformity with the programme and objectives of Unesco”. The Com- mittee had realized, however, that there was a certain contradiction between those two aims, in that, whilst the information designed for the public was too general and too vague to be useful to the Organization itself, the technical details required by the Organization were barely intelligible to the public. It was for that reason that it had been decided to draw up reports in two parts. It was originally intended to publish the part designed for the public separately; but that suggestion raised certain technical difficulties, besides which it had proved to be impracticable because not all Member States had adhered to the instructions to divide the report into two parts. Mr. Dard was, nevertheless, of opinion that the experiment should not be aban- doned and that it might be expedient, in the future, to publish a volume of reports specially designed for the general public.
