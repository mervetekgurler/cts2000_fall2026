3.3 Unesco has fast become a household word all over the world as a non-political and  cultural wing of the United Nations complex, Engrossed with the day-to-day routine o! its  administration and the execution of its programmes, and tora at times between conflicting  political digressions, members of the Organization might be tempted occasionally to lose  sight of the ultimate goals for which it was founded twenty years ago, But it is essential  that, however much vision may occasionally be blurred by organizational routine or inevi-  table political digressions (which are not its proper function), the ultimate goals of Unesco  should never be lost sight of. These goals are, of course, to promote world peace and un-  derstanding through international collaboration in education, science and culture. It is a  matter of great satisfaction that Unesco conferences have always afforded the best possible  forums for a constant reminder to all Member States of all that the Organization stands for.  Its ideals of universal peace have re-cchoed time and time again through many a conference.  This is one reason why my country and, in fact, most other Member States have come to  regard active participation in Unesco's activities almost as an article of faith and as anin-  tegral part of their educational development policy.
3.4 Unesco may not be in a position to provide the panacea for all the world's ills; nor is  it the intontion that it should pretend to any such claim. But there is little doubt that the  emergence of Unesco has taken mankind one step ‘.rther in the direction of resolving the  conflicts created by the major forces and preju.ices which divide mankind - political, eco-  nomic and racial. The political struggle among men has assumed more and more ominous  dimensions since the birth of nation States and the advent of international power politics.  The widening economic and social gap between the haves and have-nots, between the rich  and the poor, between the developed and the developing nations, is a major source of fric-  tion which seems to elude ready solution. Racial conflict in certain parts of the world con-  stitutes the most highly explosive potential source of a major conflagration, In the lightof  all this, the positive achievement of Unesco does not lie in direct participation in political  programmes. Rather, by promoting programmes of education and cultural intercoursebe-  tween nations and people of divergent races and cultures, Unesco, in its quiet and unob-  structive way, is serving 10 preserve the bonds of international co-operation that inevitably  lead to the solution of these problems: I feel that our presence here together on this Twen-  tieth Anniversary has heightened for us the pervading consciousness of the word-wide fra-  ternity that Unesco offers,

13.9 My delegation is pleased to note the inclusion in the Draft Programme and Budget for
1967-1968 of the concept of mass communication as it was envisaged in Unesco's Conatitu-  tion, as a means for the achievement of the aims of the Organization. We heartily agree  that the basic human right to freedom of information can only become a reality if variedac-  tion is taken on many fronts throughout the world. But we cannot leave all this to the Se-  cretariat of Unesco; every Member State, and particularly the developing countries, must  take the initiative before coming to seek assistance. For information to flow from one  Member State to another requires improved means and modern techniques of communica-  tion, Through exchanges of TV or radio programmes not only information but the riches  of our cultural heritage can pass from one nation to another. The Government of Uganda  fully supports such exchanges, In a message to the Director-General on the Twentieth An-  niversary of Unesco, the President of Uganda said, "Man's ignorance of one another's way  of life has always given birth to prejudice, suspicion, misunderstanding and mistrust. All  these have formed through the centuries the factors that have from time to time led nations  to war". Assuredly, lack of information breeds suspicion, mistrust and finally leads to a  complete breakdown of relations, We must within Unesco, do everything possible to bring about  better understanding amongst Member States. My delegation is therefore very pleased to  see that due emphasis is given to the flow of information, a field in which Unesco can play  a very vital réle.
13.10 I should like now to review very briefly the progress made by my government in  this field. Our radio station was opened in 1954 while we were still under the colonial ré-  gime. We thank those who started it then because this is one of the most powerful media  we have to reach the people. Its purpose is not solely entertainment; we in a developing  country must use every available tool at our disposal to combat disease and ignorance. We  therefore need specialized training for our personnel, particularly those entrusted with  the duty of preparing and broadcasting school programmes, Unesco came to our assistance  and sent an expurt who still today is training our people in Uganda. For this we are most  grateful and we must add that the good services he has rendered cannot be described here;  they can only be appreciated when one sees the people he has trained in this work.
13.11 Mr. President, some couniries wonder why certain others go to great expense to  establish a television service. We had at one time felt the same way. But now, having es-  tablished a television network in Uganda, we would urge all developing countries to do like-  wise. We established our network in 1963 and have now come to realize how useful this

(15.1) Mr, BABICHOUK (Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic) (Translation from the Russian):  Mr. President and esteemed colleagues, we have now reached almost the end of the general  discussion, in which the representatives of most Member States have participated - a debate which  shows that, in its 20 years, Unesco has certainly contributed to the development of international  co-operation in education, science and culture. The projects in its programme for the immediate  future have met with approval, but many delegations have drawn attention to shortcomings and have  made constructive suggestions, particularly with regard to the more effective use of Unesco's re-  sources and potentialities to strengthen peace and international co-operation,
(15,2) We ourselves feel there has been a definite change in recent years and an improvement in  the programme. We entirely approve the emphasis on education, and on science and its applica-  tion; educational planning and the application of science to the vital needs of the developing coun-  tries are now receiving greater attention.
(15.3) Valuable work is being done in the further development of education in Africa, Asia and  Latin America, and through international co-operation in such subjects as oceanography, hydro-  logy and seismology - all down for further encouragement in the Draft Programme for 1967-1968,  This will help to concentrate Unesco's forces on the joint solution of important contemporary  problems.
(15.4) While I approve in general the draft programme submitted by the Director-General in doc-  ument 14 C/5, I should like to say something about certain matters which Unesco does not deal  with as effectively as it should, Discrepancies still occur between word and deed, and while  Unesco often-takes important and morally justified decisions with regard to pressing problems of  modern life, the Secretariat does not always take account of these decisions in its work, This,  of course, is why, both in the Organization's past activities and in the various provisions of the  draft programme now under consideration, we find no proposals that really meet the vital needs  of the peoples of the world and satisfy their passionate desire for the preservation of peace on  earth,
(15.5) Let us consider the facts. Unesco, as we know, was founded 20 years ago, as a resultof  the victory of the anti-Hitler coalition in the Second World War; it was one of the United Nations  agencies, and its activitiea were to be directed towards the maintenance and consolidation of  peace, The preamble to the Constitution states that Unesco is to advance the objectives for which  the United Nations Organization was established, Thia means, in principle, that all Unesco's ac-  tivities are subordinated to ita duty to strive to consolidate peace, international understanding and  co-operation,
(15,6) Unesco, therefore, cannot and should not refuse to play ita part in the consolidation of  peace, Recognizing this, the General Conference, at its thirteenth session, adopted resolution
6.2 concerning Unesco's tasks in contributing to peace, peaceful co-operation, and Hving peace-  fully together, among States with different economle and social systems,
(15,7) In our view, Unesco is not yet putting enough effort Into this most Important aspect of its  work, The draft programme makes no provision for any major activities deriving trom the reso-  lution just mentioned, In particular, no practical activities to increase Unesco's contribution to  peace in the next two financial periods have yet been planned for the programme or mass com-  munication departments or for the other bureaux and services of the Secretariat, We belleve that  in executing the programme the Director-General should do everything possible to implement he  resolutions adopted by the General Conference, and should take more effective measures to promote  activities such as spreading knowledge about the legal, socio-économic and cultural aspects of  living peacefully together and the problems of social development fn our day, Greateruse should  be made of Unesco's publications to expound the principles of living peacefully together and inter-  natfonal co-operation and to unmask the impertaltstic, aggressive activities of States that deny  these principles,
(15,8) Weare firmly convinced that discussion by the General Conference of Unesco's contribu-  tion to peace and the adoption of a resolution on this subject will stimulate the Organization's,  activities in the vitally important sphere of the nation's efforts to consolidate peace and livingpeace-  fully together, Ourdelegation whole-heartedly supports the USSR draft resolution, and is convinced  that it will have the support it deserves from Conference participants,
(15,8) I wish to stress once more, from this exalted platform, that the Ukrainian people, asin-  deed all the peoples of the Soviet Union, fully support the external policy of the Government of the  Union of Soviet Socialist Republics as laid down by the twenty-third congress of the Communist  Party of the Soviet Union - a policy that expresses the essential nature of a socialist society, It  is, of course, a policy whose object is to maintain and consolidate peace and living peacefully to-  gether among States with different economic and social systems, a policy of respect for the sov-  ereignty, independence and territorial inviolahility of all countries, large or small,  {15,10} The international situation in which Unesco works is extremely disturbing, The greatest  and most dangerous centre of tension in the world today is South Fast Asia, where the United  States of America has unleashed armed aggression against the people of Viet-Nam, and is con-  tinuing to extend its attacks, The aggressive acts of the United States of America constifute a  very real threat to the peace of the world and to International security. Almost all honest people  today are profoundly disturbed by the everits in Viet-Nam, and denounce the American aggressors,  Many right-thinking Americans, too, are manifesting anxiety.
(15.11) The escalation of the war in Viet-Nam, where the United States is flagrantly violating the  Constitutions of the United Nations and Unesco, 18 of course arousing anxiety among the members  of the Organization, too, as many of the delegations' speeches have shown, Whatever the Ameri-  can representatives present may tell us, whatever answers (if answers are possible!) they may  give, they should not be allowed to distract our attention from the events that are agitating the  whole world, or to mask the crirninal, inhumane actions of the American forces in Viet-Nam,  We respect the American people, but we passionately denounce the American aggresgors, We  denounce them not because we wish to quarrel with people living under a different social system  from ours, as the delegate of the United States has tried to maintain, but because we whole-  heartedly support the just cause of the Viet-Namese people and indeed of all other peoples fight~  ing heroically for their freedom and independence and for the right to live as they wish, And we  should like to tell the American representatives that the only satisfactory answer they can give  is the immediate cessation of United States aggression in Viet-Nam and the observance of the  Geneva Agreements,
(15,12) Mr. President, Unesco is faced with the extremely important task of co-operating active-  ly, with all the resources it can muster, in eradicating colonialism and racialism and their evil  consequences, Unesco's work cannot be effective while colonialiam persists - colonialism, which  for centuries has impeded the development of education and culture, as it does today, and which  keeps the masses of the people in darkness and deprives them of their rights,
(15,13) We belfeve that Unesco is not yet doing all it could to eradicate the last traces and the  consequences of colonialism, racialism and racial discrimination, This {s evidenced by the  Secretarlat’s fallure to implement effectively the resolution on this subject adopted by the Gen-  eral Assembly of the United Nations at {ts twentieth session and resolution 8,2 adopted by the  General Conference at its eleventh and twelfth sessions, Unesco's programmes should include  provision for more realistic, practical measures to promote the eradication of colonialism and

(15,14) Fellow delegates, we all know that highly-qualitied specialists are of the most utmost  importance to any country, At the Conference, however, our attention has been drawn to the fact  that trained personnel are being persuaded to leave the developing countries, where they should  be playing a leading rôle. Unesco should take an interest in this Important matter, and help to  ensure that specialists from the developing countries who have been trained elsewhere should be  allotted posts at home,

(15.16) Mr. President, the General Conference adopted a special resolution at its eighth session  on the need for making Unesco a universal international organization, Unesco's membership is  rising every year, and {t is becoming increasingly representative, Yet it is by no means univer-  sal, As a result of opposition and manoeuvring behind the scenes, especially on the part of the  United States of America, China's place in Unesco has been unlawfully usurped by the ChiangKai-  shek clique, which represents no one, The German Democratic Republic, the Democratic People's  Republic of Korea and the Democratic Republic of Viet-Nam are still unrepresented in Unesco;  their absence is detrimental to Unesco, and undermines its authority, and its own activities and  international co-operation in education, science and culture suffer as a resuit,
(15,17) The geographical distribution of posts in the Secretariat also needs improving; on this  subject, the Ukrainian delegation, like many others, has on several occasions expressed grave  dissatisfaction, Various resolutions have been adopted, including resolution 26,21 at the thir-  teenth session, Yet 23 Member States are still unrepresented on the staff, and 38 are under-  represented, Moreover, staff are still frequently chosen from over-represented States, parti-  cularly Western countries, We consider that the Unesco authorities should strictly observe the  principle of the equitable geographical distribution of Secretariat posts,
(15.18) Our delegrtion is also dissatisfied at the continuing increase in staff numbers which, we  believe, militates against efficiency and considerably raises administrative costs.
(15,19) Mr. President, ladies and gentlemen, next year the Soviet Union will be celebrating its  fiftieth anniversary, The Ukrainian Republic, with its 45 million citizens, rejoices to have lived  for 49 years in fraternity with the many nations that make up the great Soviet Union, Inthat short  period, the Ubraine, which was previously a backward agricultural region of Tsarist Russia, has  become a progressive industrial and agricultural State, The remarkable achievements of the  Ukrainian SSR are due to the fact that both in the years of peaceful communist construction and  during the terrible trials of the war years, the Ukrainian people always received disinterested,  fraternal assistance from the great Russian people and from all the peoples of the USSR, and at  the same time made a valuable contributfon to the building of communism in the Soviet Union,  The inviolable friendship and brotherhood of the peoples of the USSR furnish striking evidence of  the success of the national policy followed by the Soviet Government, inspired by Lenin,
(15,20) Science is developing rapidly in the Ukraine, 1 need only point out that whereas no more  than 24,000 Ukrainians had received a higher education before the October Revolution, there are  now over two million specialists in all departments of the national economy in the Ukraine work-  ing in higher educational establishments, The 132 such establishments have 690,000 students,  and the 697 secondary specialized schools have 646,000 pupils - all State-financed, An extensive  network of scientific organizations employs some 100,000 research workers,
(15,21) A culture that is national in form and socialist in content has attained an unprecedented  level of development: 8,670,000 pupils attending general schools; with 476,000 teachers, over
29,000 public libraries, over 26,000 palaces of culture and 95 professional theatres and music  associations. Amateur art associations have a membership of over two million including work-  ers, peasants and intelligentsia.
