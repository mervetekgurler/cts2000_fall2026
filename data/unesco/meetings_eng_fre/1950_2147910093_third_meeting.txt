Report on the Conference où Observers of Interna-  tional Non-Governmental Organizations.  [2] Mr. ENNALS (Chairman, Conference of  Observers of International Non-Governmental  Organizations) expressed his appreciation of the  opportunity given to him for reporting on the  Conference held on 23 May 1950 at the invitation  of the Director-General of Unesco, under the  Honorary Chairmanship of Ambassador Cora.  It was the first time that all non-governmental  organizations having consultative relations with  Unesco had been invited to discuss their relation-  ship. Despite the variety of interests represented  and the fulness of the discussions, all the recom-  mendations adopted by the Conference were  unanimously approved. These proposals therefore  represented the wishes of all the forty-two non-  governmental organizations represented.  [3] Mr. Ennals read the text of the letter  addressed on 24 May 1950 to the Director-General  of Unesco by himself as Chairman, and by Miss  Charrot, as Rapporteur, of the Conference:  sé  e) 80  You were kind enough to invite the international non-  governmental organizations enjoying consultative  status with Unesco to send representatives to a special  conference in Florence, at the same time as the  Fifth Session of the General Conference.  That invitation has made it possible for the repre-  sentatives of forty-two international non-governmental  organizations to consider the relations between Unesco  and non-governmental organizations and to make  a detailed study of the Executive Board’s proposals,  submitted to the Fifth Session of Unesco’s General  Conference, regarding future relations between Unesco  and those organizations.  You have done us the honour of telling us, In a  brief speech, how much importance you attach to our  collaboration with Unesco.  In the spirit of that collaboration, after a member  of your Secretariat had explained the Executive  Board’s proposals, and following a discussion between  representatives of organizations with very varied  interests, having relations with different Departments  of Unesco, the Conference unanimously adopted a  series of resolutions, which we have the honour to  transmit to you herewith.  At the end of the Conference, a Committee of twelve  was set up, with instructions to draw the attention  of Unesco’s General Conference to these resolutions.  On behalf of that Committee, we would reques!  vou to be so kind as to transmit the resolutions to  the competent body of the General Conference, in  order that the wishes expressed by the international  non-governmental organizations may be taken into  account by Unesco when it prepares directives for  relations with international non-governmental organ-  izations.  [4] Mr. Ennals pointed out that the twelve  organizations represented on the continuing com-  mittee had been chosen as representing the various  aspects of the co-operation of non-governmental  organizations with Unesco.                                            [5} Mr. Ennals then read out the five resolutions  adopted by the Conference [document 5C/OXR/10  - Addendum b}.  Referring to Resolution lI, recommending in-  creasing use to be made of the non-governmental  organizations, Mr. Ennals said that its authors had  noted that their co-operation with the External  Relations Service had greatly improved during  the past year. They had further taken note of the  Director-General’s remark that certain organiza-  tions were not co-operating as effectively as others,  and they were calling on all to do so more fully  and thus to justify the confidence placed in them  by Unesco.  Turning to Resolution IV, being proposed  amendments to the Directives presented by the  Executive Board, Mr. Ennals expressed the hope  that he would be given an opportunity to be pre-  sent when this matter came up before the sub-  committee.  As to amendments (v), (vi) and (vir) on page 3,  he stated that, while the present proposal of the  Executive Board was a great improvement on the  existing situation, the non-governmental organ-  izations nevertheless felt that their views should  be presented to the different organs of Unesco,  either orally or in writing, according to a reason-  ably liberal procedure, which would incidentally  correspond to that adopted in other international  bodies.  The point dealt with in Resolution V, recom-  mending closer contact between National Com-  missions and non-governmental organizations, had  in fact been discussed the previous week by the  Conference of representatives of National Com-  missions, and Mr. Ennals believed that the Na-  tional Commissions were greatly interested in it. [7] ANAS Khan (Afghanistan) wondered whether  religious organizations could be considered as  contributing to the spread of Unesco’s ideas, and  whether it was on that account that they had been  granted consultative status. Referring to certain  non-governmental organizations whose usefulness  to Unesco was indisputable, he expressed the  opinion that longer-term agreements should be  substituted for annual agreements with them, in  order that they might prepare long-term pro-  grammes for certain activities which took more  than a year to carry out. He proposed that those  organizations, like Member States, should be in-  vited to attend the General Conference with full  voting powers, on condition that they presented an  annual report on their activities. Finally, Anas  Khan agreed with Mr. Ennals that there should  be closer collaboration with National Commis-  SIONS.  [8] Mr. ENNALS (Chairman, Conference of  Observers of International Non-Governmental  Organizations) stated that some religious organ-  izations had been included, because they had made  a useful contribution to the work of Unesco dur-  ing the past years, and also because many of them  grouped large sections of public opinion through-  out the world. If there were any non-  governmental organizations with religious assoct-  ations which were not yet included among them,  Mr. Ennals was sure that they would be warmly  welcomed.  Turning to the question of the adjustment of  programmes to those of Unesco, he pointed out  that the answer was to be found in document 5C/  OXR/9. Besides, many non-governmental organ-  izations whose activities had previously been  rather limited, had, since their association with  Unesco, increasingly drawn attention to both  Unesco and the United Nations.
