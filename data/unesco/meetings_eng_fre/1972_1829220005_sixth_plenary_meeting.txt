4,1 Dr. OSAMU INABA (Japan) (Speech delivered in Japanese; English translation  provided by the Japanese delegation):  Mr. President, distinguished delegates, Mr, Director-General, ladies and gentlemen,  it is a great privilege and pleasure for me t speak at this General Conference on behalf of  the Government of Japan,
4. 2 I should like, first of all, to express to all the distinguished delegates present here,  the gratitude of the Japanese delegation for the honour you have given us by unanimously  electing Mr, Haguiwara as President of this General Conference,
4, 3 1 should also like to express my sincere respect for the Director-General, Mr, René  Maheu, who has guided the Secretariat of Unesco over the years and organized this impor-  tant session of the General Conference, A
4, 4 1 take pleasure in extending, on this occasion, my greetings to the delegations of  Bahrain, Oman, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates, which became Member States of  Unesco after the last General Conference, and to the delegation of the People's Republic  of Bangladesh which has just been admitted to this Organization, And in particular, 1  wish to extend my heartfelt welcome to the first delegation from the People's Republic of  China, as Japan has had close historical and cultural relationships with this great neigh-  hour country for the long span of about two thousand years,
4,5 Mr, President, may I be permitted to summarize, by way of introductory remarks to  the general policy debate, the problems that the Japanese people have keenly experienced  during the past century. This is the centenary of the establishment of a modern school ed-  ucation system in Japan, Looking back on the path Japan has trodden during the past hundred  years, we can point, in the first place, to the thorough dissemination of compulsory ele-  mentary education implemented under the first educational reform as the step of the greatest  merit toward the modernization of Japanese society and her economic advancement, This  process was repeated after World War II, when Japan made a fresh start toward new na-  tional ideals, The second educational reform then undertaken laid emphasis on the realiza-  tion of the equality of educational opportunity and became the motive power for the subsequent  social and economic development of Japan.
4, 8 On the other hand, for the Japanese people who had preserved their own culture in an  island country off the coast of Asia and who had to absorb heterogeneous west European cul-  ture and carry out an abrupt social renovation, those hundred years were a period oftrial  and an age of groping for a way to live as a nation in the world, Today, Japan's education  faces the need for a third educational reform, whereby to establish a new system of life-  long education comprehending home, school and society, in order to nurture creative and  moral men and women who can properly make use of the fruit of economic growth, At the  same time, Japan's future education is charged with the important task of cultivating those  people who will have a clear sense of responsibility and service for the common good of fu.  ture generations as well as the present age, and will be capable of taking part in, and con-  tributing to, the work of solving common problems of mankind, with mutual understanding  and respect as members of the international community,
4,7 These experiences of Japan and the problems before her are closely related to the  goals of the programme of Unesco. This is the reason why the Japanese Government is  seriously interested in its programme and heartily supports its consolidation and improvement,
4,8 From this point of view, Mr, President, I may now present straight away the obser-  vations of the Japanese Government on the programme and budget presented to this Gene-  ral Conference, limiting myself to three points,
4,9 First, the rôle of Unesco in implementing the Second United Nations Development  Decade, ! am convinced that man is the cause of development and development is for man,  Therefore, I believe that the programme of Unesco, which helps the intellectual activities  of man, through education, science and culture, in order to enable him toutilize effectively  the limited natural resources, should naturally be expanded proportionately as development  programmes of the United Nations are enlarged, Whereas the programme of Unesco, in-  tended for man, should be the foremost object of long-range pre-investment, it is apt to  be regarded as a matter of secondary importance, since investment in it does not produce  immediately tangible effects, as does economic investment, In this connexion, I have  seriously studied the problems raised by the Director-General, and I now advocate thatthe  functions of a Specialized Agency like Unesco should be reinforced in proportion to the ex-  pansion of development programmes,
4,10 Mr, President, as for international co-operation in the fields of education, science  and culture, I am convinced that its objective can be most properly attained by integrating  various bilateral co-operation programmes into the channel of multilateral co-operation,  which Unesco should essentially aim at. Furthermore, I believe that in Unesco's assistance  to its Member States, greater importance should be attached to regional co-operative pro-  grammes, By so doing, specialized assistance can be given to Member States most effec-  tively, through the best use of their initiatives and the incentive created by their mutual  co-operation, while giving careful consideration to their respective cultural backgrounds,  In this sense, 1 welcome the new idea, introduced in the proposed programme, to create  an Asian Centre of Educational Innovation for Development, and to establish, subject to  its co-ordination, a network of co-operation of national centres of the countries of the re-  gion, Japan intends to make a contribution to the realization of this scheme in order that  it may prove so successful as to become applicable to other regions of the world,
4,11 May I now proceed, Mr, President, to the second subject of the observations of the  Japanese Government, We should consider here the rôle to be performed by Unesco in con-  nexion with the serious problems which cannot be solved without efforts of worldwide pro-  portions, Environment, natural resources, population, contradictions inherent in contem-  porary civilizations - all these problems must find a means of solution through basic re-  search, to which we must now attach importance, It will become evident what an important  rôle Unesco should play in this regard, if we consider that such basic research involves co-  ordinated studies extending over the broad spheres of humanities, social sciences and na-  tural sciences, and must be carried out with the co-operation of all the Member States and  academic organizations throughout the world,
4,12 I support therefore the Director-General's proposal that emphasis should be laid, in  future programmes of Unesco, on international programmes centring on natural science  and interdisciplinary joint projects,
4,13 In this connexion, I should like to bring to the attention of the General Conference  the question of the establishment of a "United Nations International University". All of the  exploratory studies about it conducted by the United Nations and Unesco up to the present  positively recognize the need for creating a place where the academic wisdom of the world  can be gathered together for the study of the aforementioned global problems, The Execu-  tive Board of Unesco and the Economic and Social Council of the United Nations have rec-  ommended that the General Assembly of the United Nations should take steps toward its  establishment. I earnestly urge that the current General Conference adopt a resolution  supporting the basic concept of the proposed United Nations International University and  calling upon the 27th session of the General Assembly of the United Nations to take practical  steps for its realization, I wish to state here again that the Japanese Government is pre-  pared to extend every possible co-operation to its establishment, when it is approved bythe  United Nations,
4,14 1 shall now proceed, Mr, President, to the third subject of the Japanese Governments  observations, I should like to refer particularly to the promotion of peace with respect to  the inter-sectoral programmes in the Draft Medium-Term Outline Plan for 1973-1978, It  is a crucial problem for Unesco that there now exists criticism that, among Unesco's pro-  grammes, the work of laying foundations for peace through mutual understanding among na-  tions has lost vitality, The promotion of peace is not a matter capable of solution as a  natural consequence of the promotion of development or the solution of global problems,  History has shown that efforts for development are often likely to involve mankind in war,
4, 15 I highly appreciate the significance of the academic studies on peace which have been  conducted by Unesco, but I am afraid that such intellectual and moral activities alone are  not adequate to liold back the waves of distrust, prejudice andhatrednow prevailing between  nations and peoples, As a matter of fact, scepticism is growing among the youth of today  that the ideals put forth in the Constitution of Unesco may be a mere dream, an unrealistic  castle in the air and this seems to be obstructing in large measure its participation in  Unesco's activities, The Director-General has pointed out in the Draft Medium-Term Out-  line Plan that the General Conference has so far failed to formulate any definite policy on  this problem, ever present since the founding of Unesco, We all share his apprehensions,  I should like to request that the current General Conference study the establishment of a  special international advisory committee for the purpose of finding a new key to the solu-  tion of the present difficult situation,
4,16 In concluding my address, Mr, President, I should like to explain the overall posi-  tion of the Japanese Government toward the draft programme and budget for the next two  years. I have stated that the rôle of Unesco in respect of development should be further  strengthened in future, and have emphasized the importance of the basic research to be  conducted by Unesco for the solution of global problems, I have alsorequestedthat Unesco  exert fresh efforts for the furtherance of international solidarity, All of these are matters  requiring an increase on Unesco's budget, In order that Unesco may live up to the expecta-
120   tions of the peoples of the world in these matters, tha delegation of Japan agrees to adopt  the Draft Medium- Term Outline Plan as the general guideline for the programmes for the  next two biennia, and expresses hereby its support for the size of the budget for 1973-1974  as proposed by the Director-General and recommended by the Executive Board, However,  it is necessary that efforts be made for greater efficiency in all the programmes of Unesco,  and for clarification: of their priority, if adequate funds are to be allotted to the necessary  areas, To that end, I wish to request that the Executive Board undertake a special study by  establishing an ad hoc committee, If, as a result of this study, it is possible to specify  parts or areas of the programmes to be reduced on the one hand, and, on the other, to pro-  pose areas to be expanded or newly undertaken, I believe that greater understanding and sup-  port of Member States can be obtaired for growth in the size of the programme and budget of  our Organization,
4,17 Mr, President, I firmly believe that hereafter it becomes essential for our human  society to deeper mutual understanding and to promote peace by further intensifying mutual  exchanges among nations in various fields, Undoubtedly, Unesco is the international organi-  zation which has a responsibility for fulfilling such important duties, The Government of  Japan will be more than willing to extend its whole-hearted co-operation to Unesco in its  efforts to that end, Thank you, Mr, President.

(6.1) Mr, CANAS (Costa Rica) (Trauslation from the Spanish):  Mr, President, distinguished delegates, Mr, Director-General: The delegation of Costa  Rica desires to express its satisfaction at the welcome election of Mr. Toru Haguiwara as Pre-  sident of this General Conference, We are sure that with you as President, Sir, this seventeenth  session of the General Conference will succeed in full measure,  {6, 2) Unesco's immediate responsibility, as an international organization essentially concerned  with man's cultural problems, is immense. Since, knowledge, education, culture, which together  sum up man's spiritual expression, are today connected - or rather, what is more serious, con-  founded - with technological progress. Technology advances with such rapid strides that the  human spirit has difficulty in adapting itself day by day to the new processes, Mareover, tech-  nology has become in the eyes of its exponents an end in itself: it casts aside all transcendent  concerns and cares for nothing but its own immediate aims, which appear, judging from what  we observe around us, to be confined to making human lives increasingly comfortable,
(6,3) But what has material comfort to do with quality of life? If the progressive mechanization  of our home life is going to give us greater opportunities and longer hours of leisure, how are  we going to spend that leisure? Are we going to make it creative? If and when technology suc-  ceeds in perfecting the reproduction of sound and image, how are we going to use the new per-  fected apparatus? To reproduce what new types of sound and image? Do we not in many parts  of the world, including my own country, use that marvellous ability to communicate which tele-  vision has given us merely to increase the consumption of commercial products? The perfection  of the image, the quality of the sound, the speed of the vehicle - these are what we strive for.  We do not give a thought to what we are going to see and hear, to where we propose to go at that  speed, Confronted by these advances, we can hardly even stammer "Why?" or "What for? ".  And it sometimes seems to us that, if Unesco's name (education, science, culture) itself implies  the answer, then Unesco - and in the long run we and our various governments - are in duty bound  to give that answer in the practical form of solutions, results and aims,
(6,4) In paragraph 23 of the introduction to the Draft Programme and Budget for 1973-1974, the”  Director-General shows a clear view of the problem and makes a declaration that is itself a  whole programme deserving our support: ''To foster and to serve the organization and advance-  ment of the intellectual and social phenomenon of science in its inost fundamental and most  generel aspects’, Exactly: in its most fundamental and general aspects. There, no doubt, lies  the secret, Fellow delegates, it is in our hands.
(6.5) The crisis is profoundand affects us all. We must rethink all our problems, all our solu-  tions; we must be convinced that with every single day something which we have held incontro-  vertible is found wanting. Not because we think that any change is for the better; not because  we reject the sublime concepts that our distinguished past President Mr. Atilio dell'Oro Maini  expressed the day before yesterday : that change is not of today but of all time, that history has  been a process of continual change,and that man and his present circumstances are merely the  product of the experience of countless generations. However, in our day this movement has ac-  celerated; it might even be said, by stretching to a certain extent the ideas of Teilhard de Chardin,  that the pace of Darwinian evolution is so fast nowadays thal you can almost see it moving. That  is why the thinking man, the man concerned about the future, is bound to revise his ideas, That  is why the Faure Report, so much discussed today, is important by the mere fact of its existence,  because it questions what we know and are doing about education. If all these questionings, if  the discussion raised by the Report, produce a new outlook that will overcome all obstacles,  avoid all pitfalls and bring us nearer to the aims outlined in the Report iiself, much will be owed  to Unesco by future generations.
(6.6) In accordance with the spirit of the resolution adopted by this General Conference, and  taking into account national needs and realities, the Government of Costa Rica decided to with-
123   draw from the Ministry of Education its non-teaching activities and regroup them, together with  other agencies, departments and new programmes, in a new Ministry of Culture, Youth and Sports,
(6.7) This Ministry, besides administering library administration, official publications, m.-  seums and national theatres, has created a theatrical company and a new symphony orchestra  which do not perform in the theatres of the capital, but instead travel from village to village and  town to town, A Publications Department has been established which publishes books and re-  views for popular consumption free of charge or at a purely nominal price and is obliged to ensure  that they reach the most distant parts of the country, An office has been set up to prepare and  produce radio programmes within the privately-owned commercial radio broadcasting system  operating in Costa Rica, which gives cultural broadcasts - e, g. on the country's history, com-  posers and poets - mainly from regional rural transmitting stations, An inventory of the  country's historic and artistic heritage has been started and the necessary legislation has been  prepared for its preservation. The same is being done for research on folklore and for the making  and dissemination of phonographic recordings of musical works by Costa-Rican composers,
(6. 8) The Department of Youth co-operates unreservedly with youth agencies and associations  and has organized at various levels a voluntary service which is beginning to produce results,  Activities to encourage sport are principally directed towards building and equipping sports pre-  mises, such as gymnasia and swimming-pools. We have noted, however, with regret the lack  of any international financial aid or assistance for recreational capital investment of this type,
(6,9) In accordance with the recommendation of the Executive Board on the organization of the  work of this session of the General Conference, I request the President's permission to append  to the proceedings of this meeting details and statistics of the above-mentioned programmes,  {6,10) We are convinced that culture and knowledge must be brought to all men, even if they  have abandoned school, college or university; and that it is not enough to teach a man to read if  he is not given a continual opportunity to exercise what I will venture to call a vital acquired  dexterity. The State's interest in cultural progress must not be restricted to those sections of  the population that attend class-rooms and lecture halls, It must also promote rationally all the  best elements in our nation's life, and maintain close contact with the achievements of foreign  culture. My Government believes, with all due modesty, that it has begun to fulfil an eminently  important task in undertaking this new line of action, I should like to place on record our thanks  for the assistance we have received from Unesco in this task, for to express this gratitude in  words is one of the reasons for my presence among you.

12,1 Mr, WOOD (United Kingdom):  Mr, Fresident, I am proud to congratulate you, and grateful that we shall be guided  by someone whose association with Unesco is so long and distinguished,
12,2 When I attended my first Unesco Conference two years ago, I was a very new Minister  for Overseas Development, Since then, I have travelled many miles and visited many de-  veloping countries, and understand a little more of the problems and needs of many people.  Nor can one travel like this without becoming more aware of the value to developing coun-  tries of the advice and resources of the United Nations system, I have met specialists and  experts, working on projects under a number of different organizations, and I have been  encouraged by the increasing co-operation (this was mentioned by the distinguished repre-  sentative of Malaysia) between the various agencies of the United Nations in country devel-  opment programmes. Here, in Paris, at this time, we are concerned with Unesco, Weare  not here simply to telk and exchange ideas, but to make what improvements we caninUnesco's  programme and to co-ordinate it more closely with the other work of the United Nations.
12,3 Two years ago, when 1 first spoke here, I was rash enough, perhaps impertinent  enough, to offer some criticisms of this Organization. Today, particularly in the light of  what I have seen all over the world, and apart from commenting on some things about which  many of us may still feel doubts, I want to try to be wholly constructive.
12, 4 As the Director-General said in his speech yesterday, what we most value in Unesco  is its universality. In spite of setbacks from time to time, international co-operation is  certainly gathering strength, Nations seem to me not vastly different from individuals,  Even when individuals disagree, they generally see something of validity in the point of  view of the others, Similarly nations seldom find everything about other nations wholly  reprehensible, whatever thay may publicly say. But what is very often lacking, and sadly  lacking, is a point of contact between individuals and between nations on the things that really  matter, And this is where Unesco can play one of its most positive réles: in the better un-  derstanding between individuals, and, therefore, between nations. The great value of  Unesco is in bringing people and ideas into contact, without the interference of rigidly de-  fined ideological attitudes.
12,5 A body like this can be only as strong as those who serve it. We are fortunate inthe  high quality of Unesco's staff; and anyone who knows this Organization at all must be im-  pressed by the calibre of its senior people. The work of an international civil servant is  not easy. Dedication and total integrity are essential; but still more is needed, The men  and women who serve Unesco have to be genuinely responsible and creative; and Unesco  deserves praise for finding people of this kind, I fancy that no one finds life in an inter-  national organization more exacting than its own Chief Executive. All ofushere appreciate
6  how historic the contribution of the Director-General has been, It was widely appreciated  by my own countrymen during his recent visit to London, Under his leadership, the last  ten years have seen a transformation in Unesco's life,
12. 6 My Government is heartened by the success with which Unesco has so far adapted it-  self to the needs of the changing world community, In education, it has achieved changes  of outstanding value, The report of the International Commission on the Development of  Education which was mentioned particularly this morning by the distinguished representa-  tive of Tanzania is going to need close study. But I believe that the historians of the future  will describe the early years of this decade as the Years of the Environment, This is a  matter, as the distinguished representative of Finland and the distinguished representative  of Tanzania bothmade clear, which exercises, at the present time, many powerful minds  in many countries, Progress in areas of international scientific co-operation has a vital  bearing on the whole environment, but if we are concerned with man's natural heritage, I  think we must make at least cornparable efforts to safeguard our cultural heritage which the  forefathers of each one of us here in this building have handed down, During the travels  that I have been lucky enough to make in the developing world, I have begun to realize more  clearly the importance of this heritage to the whole quality of life, which we mean, or should  mean, when we talk about development, As the Chairman of the Executive Board recalled  yesterday, Unesco has been concerned in the preservation of the great temple of Borobodur  in the middle of Java, When I visited it myself last year, I saw for myself something of its  significance to the lives of those who work in its shadow,
12, 7 In this way, and in many others, I have become increasingly conscious of the close  relationship between human attitudes and the developmental process, Perhapsinour early  enthusiasm for development, we attached too little weight to the importance of the social  sciences. The most elaborate development plans will fail if they take too little account of  the whole needs of those whom development purports to improve, Those who are affected  by new development must feel themselves involved, and they must understand and be in  sympathy with the process. I hope that Unesco, which is rich itself in sympathy andunder-  standing, will never let us lose sight of this paramount objective of all development,
12.8 The two main working documents before us, although their meaning is not always  crystal clear, reflect the concern of this Organization for human problems, They see man  as a whole being, and they emphasize this, rather than any particular aspect or activity.  This is why so many projects demand an approach which involves many different disciplines.  This is no insult to the specialist, nor to the contemporary trend towards specialization -  it is merely a plea that those who specialize should always remember that their work is  valuable as an integral part of a still greater design.
12.9 The difficulty is that human beings, and the things they do are never simple and not  always easy to understand, And the difficulties are multiplied because societies are made  up of human beings and, therefore, societies change so fast that attitudes which even re-  cently have been safe guides for international action, can now actually block progress, It  is not long since many developing countries, naturally anxious to spread education more  widely, tended to follow the educational patterns of industrialized countries, I think that  the Director-General had this much in mind when he spoke to us last night, There is an  obvious danger in adopting patterns suited to a different social environment, Problems are  created rather than solved. This is why I particularly welcome Unesco's attention tq the  problem of rural education, I think few things are more important.
12.10 There is another danger, As part of a large international organization, we tend to  create an image of the world outside, which is comfortable for our own purpose, We tend  to try to fit the world to our solutions, rather than mould our solutions to the world's pro-  blems, For the actual world is not a comfortable place, It is neither tidy nor uncompli-  cated, This is why I welcome the way the Secretariat has been examining its own syllabus  of work, But there are pitfalls, Unesco has been well served by its administrators; but  administration cannot be an end in itself, It is inspiration and innovation that matter, and
6  if they begin to fade, administration - even the best administration - cannot replace them,  This is why Unesco must remain always responsive to the world outside, remembering that  established assumptions are always questioned, and that whatever is inflexible and institu-  tional will be suspect.
12,11 Some people today, unwisely I think, dismiss anything institutional as irrelevant,  Institutions are almost everywhere under attack, or at least under searching criticism.  Those of us who know that institutions, like this one, are essential, can justify their exis-  tence only if their purposes and effectiveness can be clearly shown, Complex procedures  are no doubt essential in a large organization like this, if only to ensure scrupulous fairness  between its Members. After twenty-five years Unesco has an administrative structure  which works remarkably well, although it has become extremely elaborate, In order to  justify the existence of this Organization, we need to demonstrate that it exists, not for its  own sake, but for a purpose which will bring benefit to all nations,
12, 12 I said earlier that Unesco had been willing to adapt itself to new situations, and to  meet new needs, But adaptation is a constant process. If organizations miss the tide of  change, they will die, Many societies were established in my own country during the vig-  orous years of the last century. Some still survive today, because they adapted to changing  social patterns, Others are dead, because they failed to change in a changing world, I  mention these anxieties now, becau e Unesco is an Organization with too much important  work still to do to run the risk of the slightest atrophy.
12, 13 I ask these questions. Are the objectives of Unesco's many activities sufficiently  precise and well defined? By what criteria are proposals judged? Keen as we are on pro-  posals for new activities, I am sure we must ask, with equal enthusiasm, whether there  are activities now undertaken by Unesco which should be discontinued. My own Govern-  ment cannot accept the idea of programme commitments running on indefinitely, without  any time limits at all.
12, 14 This Organization - I think this is the right figure after the decision we took this  morning - now has a membership of 130 countries, It is under constant pressure to extend  and spread its activities, But its resources are not limitless, this is why my Government,  and other governments, have pressed for this Conference to make the difficult choice be-  tween alternative activities, Unesco's present programme contains a collection of widely  differing activities, attempting to serve many different purposes, Have we an overall strategy?  We have general aims and purposes, but I think we should be looking for clear and precise  objectives. Can we together work out a set of principles and decide which activities Unesco  should pursue and which it should leave alone? If we had such a set of principles, it would  be far easier to decide whether or not Unesco should start a given activity, and at what  stage any particular project had outlived its usefulness, ;
12,15 There is one further danger. I should hate to see this great Organization become a  political forum, with all decisions subject to 'vell-drilled votes on predetermined lines,  This is an educational, scientific and cultural organization, If the matters which need our  attention are to be determined by bloc politics, then our activities will become increasing-  ly mechanical, and our future planning sterile. I am sure that this would be tragic for’ the  quality of all our deliberations, Those who founded Unesco gave it a special character, by  providing for individuals rather than governments to sit as members of the Executive Board.  The founders realized wisely that individuals have an irreplaceable contribution to make,  If they cannot make it, Unesco will decay.
12, 16 Finally, a few words about Unesco's growth and resources. We regard Unesco as  the servant of its member governments, not their master, I listened carefully, as no doubt  we all did yesterday evening, to the Director-General on this subject, and I'would like to  say sincerely to him that a government that cannot subscribe to proposals, which would in-  volve a substantial increase in its contribution, is not necessarily waivering in its support  for Unesco or for the causes which the Organization has at heart, If there are competing

12,17 We have a clear view on the question of budgetary growth within the United Nations  system. 12, 18 It seems to me that Unesco must now be near the limits of its development, in its  present form, as a single workable agency. It must certainly be near the limits - perhaps  it is already beyond them - at which member governments can hope to understand all its  operations, and contribute their own ideas and comments, The programme has become so  large that it has acquired a momentum of its own, and I sometimes wonder if even the Secre-  tariat is really in a position effectively to control it. 12.19 I believe that my Government's view of Unesco and its development is logical and  consistent. We have a high regard, as I have already made clear, for the people who run  Unesco, We admire the way the Organization has adapted itself to serve nearly five times  as many Member States as it had when it began. He meant that a man must  search his understanding both of himself and his existence, ifhe is to do justice to his nature.  By searching, he meant a rigorous process of self-questioning, and a willingness to follow  wherever the argument leads, however unexpected or unwelcome its conclusions, Unesco  can never be too Socratic, and I am sure that this constant process of re-examination will  produce a better and a stronger Unesco, It would not, I think, be a larger or more costly  Unesco, but an Organization more able to serve its noble purpose, Thank you very mach.

(16. 1) Mr, HALIM ABU-IZZEDIN (Republic of Lebanon) (Translation from the Arabic):  Mr. President, it gives me much pleasure, on behalf of the Lebanese delegation, to greet  you and congratulate you on your election to the presidency of the seven‘:enth session of our  General Conference, and also to express, first of all, our confidence that this Conference will,  under your wise leadership, adopt resolutions enabling the Organization to achieve its lofty aims  and proceed resolutely towards them, both in the Member States and in the international com-  munity. Next, we wish to express our earnest hope that Japan, in its second great renaissance,  has resolved - as we have seen, and as we heard today from its Minister of Education - to base  its relations with the world on close co-operation, particularly in support of the aspirations of  the developing world and in endeavouring to give its new life an impetus strengthening and devel-  oping its independence and basing this on the real, effective forces behind the awakening of  peoples. These are the forces of integral education, which makes of man, in the sublimity of  his intellectual and spiritual endowments, its aim and its means towards a world governed by  co-operation for the general good and the establishment of peace and understanding among peo-  ples. We have no doubt that Japan, with its ancient history, its two modern renaissances and  the events which have taken place between them, has ample means at its disposal giving it in-  calculable opportunities to affirm and consolidate this co-operation. I also grasp this opportunity  of expressing our feelings of gratitude and esteem for your predecessor President Atilio Dell'Oro  Maini, who so ably fulfilled the duties of the sixteenth session,
(16. 2} We also rejoice today that our Organization has at this session taken a most important  step towards universality by opening its doors to the delegation of the People's Republic of China  and enabling it to add its efforts, which stem from an ancient culture and a modern renaissance,  to those of the other Member States in an earnest concerted endeavour to create a better world  for all mankind,
(16,3) Our joy at this move towards consolidating the universality of our Organization is further  increased by the accession to membership of four sister Arab States: Bahrain, Qatar, Oman  and the United Arab Emirates,
(16.4) In the Introductions to documents 17 C/5 and 17 C/4 and to the programme sectors we find  a broad cohesive picture worthy of attention and admiration, drawn by the Director-General, of  the details and costs of the Orgenization's numerous projects for the coming two years (17 C/5),  and of the outline of the main trends and options in what it seeks to achieve by 1978 (17 C/4), We  also have document 17 C/6, in which the Executive Board has set out its observations and recom-  mendations on all these questions, after thorough study and examination at its 89th session. Thus  the three documents provide an ample summary of the Organization's activities during the next  two years and projections for the four subsequent years. Although every one of the programmes,  principles and trends in these three documents deserves our special attention and careful study,  which we hope - in fact we are confident - that they will receive in the various commissions of  the Conference, it will only be possible for us in this brief address to select some of these topics  on which to express our opinion within the general policy of the Organization,
(16.5) Among the essential terms and conditions which must exist and prevail to permit the pro-  per evolution of human socieities, whatever the level of their development, nothing can be more  basic and essential than the condition of man himself, What is the distant goal to which he as-  pires? What are his innate abilities which must be developed? What are the intellectual and  social values and virtues he must possess if he is to participate freely and steadfastly  in the establishment of an ideal society enabling him to enjoy a life both fruitful and dignified?  These are the basic challenges we face at this crucial stage of man's history on this earth,
(16.6) Society is able, under various material and practical conditions, to frame policies com-  prising projects for industrial and agricultural development, to build roads and bridges, to  harness water and energy resources and to organize health and other services, assisted by out-  side experience and finance requested and obiained either from friendly nations or from inter-  national agencies, However, society must prepare its members, both men and women, with  education, science and culture to persevere with these projects, administer them properly, and  continually improve them to keep up with the rapid progress of events and to meet the nation's  ever-increasing demands, Society must also equip and arm them with the knowledge and strength  of character that will enable them to raise their standard of living and enjoy the numerous diverse  pleasures of culture. Otherwise there is a great danger that all these plans will end in failure,  that these establishments will decay, and that these efforts will be of no avail, Although the im-  portation of knowledge and experience is undeniably useful and at times essential, it must be  accompanied for a time by proper préparation of the national manpower to take an increasing  share of responsibility. True development, continuous and creative, stimulating talent and pre-  serving dignity, is that inward self-development that grows out of the natural social environment,  Man, as our General Conference has often stated, is development's origin, means and end,
(16. 7) This cohesive philosophical view of development is most truly an origin, a method and a  conclusion of the causes for the preservation and promotion of which this Organization was founded;  because it is mainly concerned with and related to the essence man's intellect and personality,  his spiritual, individual and social development,
(16. 8) A fact which came most prominently to light at the celebration of the twenty-fifth anni-  versary of our Organization last year was thal the evolution of its aims and programmes and the  increasing support it receives have culminated in this correct approach to the nature of human  civilization, thus giving it both an aim and a direction. This occurred af an appropriate time,  for it coincided almost perfectly with the United Nations' declaration of the beginning of the Sec-  ond Development Decade. This, in our opinion, is the quintessence of the two introductions by  the Director-General, in which he concentrated the depth of his perception, the substance of his  experience and the broad horizons of his vision of the pressing demands made on us today by un-  born generations. Amid the almost incalculable profusion of the needs of the Member States,  and the solutions which differ according to their various economic, social and cultural conditions  and the limited resources available to them, the primary concern of the international organiza-  tions which assist their Member States, as our Organization does within its areas of competence,  should be to study the effect of this aid on the efforts exerted by the Member Statés themselves  to solve their problems and to improve their condition, and how to obtain from it a positive and  multiplying effect, For it is an established fact that international aid, both multilateral and bi-  lateral, is minute in comparison with the total resources allocated by.the Member States them-  selves for the full development of their respective societies, This, in our opinion, is a sound  principle, It should be one of the keystones of the Organization's general policy and of the plans  and projects to implement that policy and to reinforce the basic structure established for its im-  plementation in the Organization and in the Member States, The essential progress of develop-  ment is indeed a reflection of the anticipated success of international co-operation, measured by  the sound and viable national development taking place in the Member States themselves.
(16, 9) From this stems the correct view that the Member States are not mere recipients of aid  but true partners in a global effort to achieve a global objective; for global development, like  world peace, is indivisible, Here also we believe that the Director-General's suggestion in his  introduction to document 17 C/4: that the Organization, by virtue of its experience, should pro-  vide specialized advice to the Member States who seek it in formulating their national policies  concerning education, science, culture and information, is a wise suggestion and likely to be  most beneficial, That is why the Executive Board recommended the Conference to endorse it,
(16, 10) In the two previous paragraphs we have referred to international co-operation aimed at  assisting Member States to execute their integral development projects; and we therefore wel-  come the increased funds made available to them from extra-budgetary resources such as those  of UNDP, IBRD, WFP, UNICEF and others. We also welcome what we know to be the fruitful  co-operation between these bodies and our Organization,
(16, 11) Assistance made available to Unesco's Member States from these resources exceeds by  far the resources of its regular budget; and we believe that the availability and continued increase  of that assistance prove two very meaningful facts: first, that the relatively modest resources  approved by our General Conference have a truly multiplying effect, which gains the respect of  the other agencies of the United Ndtions system; and secondly, that the international financing  organizations are becoming increasingly convinced that true overall development can be stabilized  and can continue and progress only if based on the development of man through education, science  and culture,
(16.12) However, an increase in the funds allocated by these organizations to the Member States  and administered or supervised by Unesco cannot fail to increase Unesco's operational activity,  In order to guarantee the efficiency of Unesco's mission and to preserve its intellectual and moral  character, we must be careful to confirm the principle of intellectual and practical integration  in all its dealings. This principle was adopted by our General Conference at its twelfth session,  when it stated that each section of the programme should incorporate all activities sharing a  common objective, As some Member States feared that this increase of resources would lead  the Organization astray from its main function and reduce it to the level of a mere executive  agency, it was necessary to assert that the Organization would reject any such assignment un-  less it was fully compatible with the programme approved by the General Conference and persous  with suitable, indeed exceptional, experience and qualifications were available to execute pro-  jects thus financed or financially supported, so as to guarantee the fulfilment of their aims, This  naturally requires an actual and reasonable rate of growth of the regular budget and of the Secre-  tariat, close attention to administration, effective control; and sound methods of assessment and  evaluation,
(16, 13) Although the general policy of the Organization deserves careful scrutiny and exchange  of views, I hesitate to exceed the time allotted to this address, I shall therefore be brief and  mention only a few other topics, since our delegation, like-the others, will have opportunities for  wider discussion and more detailed analysis in the Conference's commissions,
(16,14) First, if education is to be given its due place in the development of individuals and com-  munities, we must admit that it is one of man's basic rights and that renewal of its content and  methods, in and out of school, to make it available throughout life to boys and girls, men and  women, are concepts which must be moved from the realm of theory to that of practice, in order  that society may be, as it should, the source of education and its ultimate aim, Education Year
1970 has given an impetus to this trend, and it is hoped that the report of the International Com-  mission for the Development of Education will give another,
(16.15) Secondly, for the last decade or so Unesco has extended intellectual and technical assis-  tance to its Member States, at their request, to form their scientific policy in such a way as to  make science one of the main bases of the integral education of the individual and the integral  development of society. This assistance has been most successful in various countries and re-  gions of the world, and we hope, in the light of the numerous recommendations of intergovern- « -  mental regional conferences on the application of science and technology to development, that  its success will continue. We are fully aware of the benefits of international scientific pro-  grammes, and fully agree to their continuation and reinforcement, first because they comprise  subjects of vital importance, such as water, the oceans, the biosphere and the exchange of  scientific information; and secondly because they promote international scientific co-operation  through effective participation and the training of qualified scientists in the developing countries;  also because they are continuous, as indeed they must be, In this context, however, we must  devote great attention to a new project concerned with a profound study and observation, during
144   this decade, of the human implications of scientific and’ technological advance, This is a field  in which scientists and educators, social scientists, pliilèsophérs and writers all take part, and  whose results bid fair to enlighten the human mind concerning the moral and social issues aris-  ing from rapid scientific and technological progress and from the increasingly tremendous and  fearful powers which it places in the hands of mankind, .
(16. 16) Thirdly, education enables man to contribute to growth and to enjoy its, fruits,, Science,  and technology give him effective means with which to innovate growth and promote’ te’ develop-  ment. The advanced methods. of the social sciences afe used to: ‘study the effeéts of sôclal devel-  opment on peoples, both as individuals and as groups, on their attitudes’ to inherited values, and  on their hopes and aspirations for their future, The studies undertaken by the social scientists  are therefore of great value for predicting the results of development, ‘correcting its' mistakes  and evaluating its progress; but their human, cultural and literary studies are even more valu-  able because they prepare the ground for the emergence of cultured man, who derives spiritual  satisfaction from the enjoyment of literature, philosophy and the arts, in his search for himself,  and because they increase understanding among the nations through comparative literary studies  and translation of the masterpieces of literature,
(16. 17) Here I must ask your permission, Mr. President, to mention that this thinking has led  us in Lebanon to establish in the ancient hills that overlook Mount Byblos, the fount of language,  an institute for the humanities and development, This institute will provide a forum for study  and discussion, a centre for research and documentation, and facilities for translition’ : and-pub- '  lication We decided, in agreement with Unesco, that the institute's board of trustees, its  scientific committee and its teaching and research staff should be intérnational, We ‘did this so  as to link our present to our past, and as a contribution to the wise and noble aims ‘sought by  Unesco.
(16, 18) Also with regard to culture, I must underline two significant aspects of the projects of  our Organization. The first concerns contemporary literary studies as an effective means of  promoting understanding among peoples, I refer in particular to our studies of contemporary  Arabic literature, and to the implementation of the scheduled stages for the full use of the Arabic  language, so rich in the arts and sciences, on an equal footing with the other four eminent ‘lan-  guages. The second concerns the efforts of our Organization, which have met with success  thanks to international solidarity, for the preservation and presentation of the cultural’ heritage  of mankind,
(16, 19) Here I come to the question of Jerusalem, that cherished city, sacred to all'believers  in God, whither the eyes of the faithful turn and their hearts and souls are drawn from far reaches.  of the universe, Jerusalem, City of Cities, the ‘side of revelation, which the great Arab poet  Shawqi invoked thus, addressing its conqueror in 1971:  "Conqueror of Jerusalem, lay aside your sword, for the  Cross was made of wood and not of steel",  (Then, alluding to Christ)  "If you saw how far His reach went, and hos His teachings  went further than the poles, you would know that there is  strength in weakness and that right, not might, finally prevails".  Although Jerusalem belongs to history, it is today subjected under the Israeli military occupa-  tion, not only to the sword but also to excavation and demolition, and to works aimed at chang-  ing its face. It has been surrounded by a choking belt of crowded buildings, alien to its spiritual  and cultural heritage, None of the nume rous resolutions adopted by the General Assembly of  the United Nations and by the Security Council, nor of those adopted ‘by Unesco.at its fifteenth  session and at successive sessions of its Executive Board, has in the least caused Israel todesist.  The Exeuctive Board, by a virtually unanimous decision taken on 12 October 1972 at its 90th  session, has seen fit to bring the matter before you as the gathering which embodies the sovereign-  ty of Unesco and the conscience of the world, ,We and the world await your action,
(16. 20) I have nearly finished. The Director-General, in his introduction to document 17 C/4,  gives us guidance worthy of all our attention and support. This is a profound survey, on a  global scale, of the major problems of contemporary civilization. Looking around us we see  that, as world interest in international organizations tends towards the organization concerned  with man's intellect and soul and devoted to establishing development and promoting peace and .
145   understanding, the responsibilities of our Organization grow accordingly, Unesco could find  in this survey inspiration which would guide it towards its future,
(18, 21) The day is not far away when the whole world will look upon Unesco as a mirror of the  conscience of man, his hopes and his expectations, reflecting the noble aspirations which guide  him and urge him onwards towards infinite horizons,

(18,1) Mr, KAMAL NAGI (Qatar) (Translation from the Arabic):  I should like first of all to congratulate you, Mr. President, on your election to the chair of  our Conference, We were closely associated with you when you were President of the International  Conference on Adult Education held in Tokyo last summer. We were able to appreciate your  superb ability and competence, and we are certain that you will, under God, lead this session to  a completely successful conclusion,
(18. 2) I should also like to express our joy and satisfaction as an Asian country at the admission  to our Organization of China, the birthplace of civilizations. Approximately one thousand four  hundred years ago our noble Prophet Muhammad - the Blessing and the Peace be upon Him! -  urged his Muslim followers to turn towards China to acquire knowledge, saying "Seek ye know-  ledge from the cradle to the grave; seek ye knowledge even in China". Iam confident that China's  admission to Unesco will give the Organization a powerful stimulus and will contribute to the  promotion of international efforts for the development of education,
(18. 3) I also wish to express my happiness at the admission of our sister States Bahrain, Oman  and the United Arab Emirates to Unesco, and hope that they will benefit from its experience in  the great efforts they are making in the various fields of education,
(18.4) My country's delegation listened most attentively to the excellent speech delivered yes-  terday by that most able man Mr. René Maheu, the Director-General of Unesco, In this present  brief address we can only express the thanks and gratitude which are rightly his due for the de-  voted efforts which he has exerted and which have made this Organization, under his direction,  one of the most active and successful of the United Nations agencies, We shall express our  views and comments more fully in the various commissions of this Conference,
(18.5) Ten years have passed since the admission of Qatar to this Organization as an Associate  Member, and I have had the honour to represent my country at this Conference since its twelfth  session. Today, however, I am addressing you for the first time since its accession to indepen-  dence and its admission to Unesco as a full Member, Qatar has been admitted- to the United
149   Nations and its various agencies, and will henceforth participate in them to establish peace in  this strife-torn world which looks upon the United Nations au its only hope for the preservation  of human rights, peace for weak peoples, well-being for underdeveloped communities, and the  deterrence of oppression and injustice,
(18.6) Barely a year has passed since Qatar became independent; but it has been a year of in-  tense activity and achievement. Since our country has been ruled by His Highness the Emir of  Qatar, Sheikh Khalifa bin Hamad the Second, and since he introduced his welcome reform move-  ment, the Government's budget spending has doubled, During the past six months new projects  and installations have been completed which would previously have taken years. Anyone visiting  us today would be astonished at the number of rapid changes he saw in every part of our life,  which have taken place in accordance with carefully-studied plans supervised by specialists and  generously financed and assisted by the State,
(18.7) I was impressed when past President Atilio Dell'Oro Maini said in his opening address  yesterday that man has at all times to decide on the next step he is to take, We in Qatar decided  as soon as we were blessed by the discovery of petroleum to change the conditions of our life and  always to seek a better future, I can say in all modesty that in Qatar we have benefited from. our  contact with the international community and from the experience of others in establishing an  advanced educational system which has embraced all children of school age without exception and  is free of charge at all levels,
(18,8) The greatest educational increase has been in secondary education, where the number of  students (male and female) has grown by over 50% in the last two years, To meet this we have  increased the facilities by 60% over their 1970 level, and made a comparable increase in the  number of teachers seconded from sister Arat States,
(18,9) In my address to you at the sixteenth session of the General Conference, I stated that we  had succeeded in becoming self-sufficient in primary school teachers and hoped to train Qatari  teachers for preparatory, secondary and higher education. We are negotiating with UNDP and  Unesco for the establishment of two training colleges for male and female teachers, and this  has in fact been approved; but I am sorry to say that consultations have been proceeding for two  years and have not yet led to action, although many experts have visited the country and numerous  favourable reports have been made. I need only say that recruitment of a project manager has  already taken six months and that we do not know how many more months will pass before he  arrives to start implementing the project. Originally the two colleges were to start work in  October 1972; but we were then advised to postpone execution to October 1973. The United Nations  was to send us an expert on school buildings and another on higher education who would, in con-  junction with the project manager, draft the budget required for the first year. This, however,  has not yet been done. I mention this example not to criticize any shortcomings but to bring to  your attention the long delays in proceedings of these bodies. I am certain that many developing  Member States in need of technical assistance and advice make the same complaint, and we  should therefore collaborate to find a decisive cure for this trouble.
(18, 10) For in-service training we obtained the services of an expert who, in co-operation with  our Department of Technical Research, laid out a programme for training and refresher courses  for a ten-year period, We have also established a language-teaching centre, equipped with a  modern language laboratory. Training sessions are held there for Qatari officials and employ-  ees whose work requires a knowledge of foreign languages, In-service training is also given  there to language teachers,
(18, 11) In its cultural co-operation with Unesco, Qatar responded favourably to Unesco's call  for this year to be an International Book Year. We have decided to hold a regional exhibition of  Arabic books next December in Doha, our capital. Qatar will be the host country, for the first  time, to the regional seminar organized by the League of Arab States on the free flow of Arabic  books, also scheduled for December. In addition, we are allocating funds for the publicat'én of  an old Arabic scientific manuscript, written about one thousand years ago; we have taken part  in the book exhibitions held in the Arab world and have issued commemorative postage stamps  to mark the occasion,
(18.12) Unesco's most prominent activities in the Arab world are the Arab States Functional  Literacy Centre (ASFEC) in Sirs el Layyan, and the Arab States Certre for Educational Planning  and Administration (ASCATEP) in Lebanon, Both these centres have made continuous construc-  tive efforts in many different fields, However, during the past few years four new Gulf States  have emerged in the Arab world, The circumstances of these States have prevented them from  benefiting from these centres, and Qatar requests that ASCATEP may continue to receive
150   Unesco's support until 1978, and to function as a regional centre in accordance with the General  Conference's resolutions (paragraphs 1595 and 1602-1609 of the Approved Programme and Budget  for 1971-1972).
(18. 13) We also hope that Unesco will request UNDP to continue to support ASFEC at Sirs el  Layyan, so that it may pursue its task and the emergent States in the area may benefit from its  activities, We agree with the proposals made in paragraphs 1353-1357 of the Draft Programme  and Budget concerning support for the centre and provision of international staff and consultants  and of the teachers and financial aid required to organize seminars and workshops. We also  support the recommendation made in this connexion by the International Conference on Aduit  Education held in Tokyo last summer,
(18,14) Qatar is doing its utmost to play its part in cultural co- -operation by sending students to  a large number of Arab and foreign States for higher education, This year we are sending our  first student mission to receive advanced training in various fields of study in France, that great:  country which has traditionally been a seat of civilization and which was the first to wphold the-  principles of justice, liberty and equality.
(18. 15) At this point we should like to express our warmest gratitude to the friendly Govern-  ment of France for the higher educational facilities it hus extended to us, This will have a most  evident effect on the life of the people of Qatar, who are always eager for further progress and  rapid development,
(18,16) Qatar offers material aid and scholarships to students from abroad to study in Qatari  institutions, The number of African and Asian students in Qatar this year was 212, from 14"  States. Qatar gives them free of charge all the educational services and living facilities they  require, It also collaborates with other Arab States to extend education to those areas of the  Arab world which have lagged behind in this domain, In some of them it builds schools and  teachers’ institutes and supplies them with teachers and textbooks.
(18.17) I should dearly have liked to spare you the recital of saddening topics; but the’ painful  reality is that hundreds of thousands of pupils from usurped Arab Palestine have been deprived.  of equal opportunities by the Israeli occupation forces, with their shameful practice of discrimi-  nation, It is this painful reality which causes me regretfully to remind you of the extent of the in-  justice and oppression which have been the lot of the Arab child in occupied Palestine, in his  land, the land of his fathers and forefathers, at the hands of the forces of evil and aggression  which have occupied this land by armed force, with the support of the oppressive imperialist  Powers,
(18,18) We must ask these imposters, who are seated here amongst us, by what right they have  deprived the Arab child of his uncontested right to an education befitting his beliefs and heritage; -  by what right they have falsified history and distorted textbooks, by what right they have .obli-  terated the Arab and Islamic relics which have for centuries been part of this holy land; ‘and by.  what right they continue to excavate in Old Jerusalem and under the Aqsa Mosque.
(18.19) But I am already certain that these questions are a futile waste of breath and will not  elicit any response from the Israelis, who have already totally ignored every equitable resolu-  tion adopted by the United Nations, by the Security Council and even by your Organization, ‘cal- -  ling on them to cease their discrimination, falsification and destruction. Iremind you, honoured  colleagues, of your responsibilities, I remind you of the resolutions adopted by your Conference '  at its previous sessions, and particularly 16 C/Resolution 8 on Unesco's contribution to peace  and its tasks with respect to the elimination of colonialism and racialism, reaffirming our faith  in the dignity of man and in the principles of the Universal Declaration of Human-Rights. As our  Director-General stated yesterday in his excellent address, the use of violence can in no way  contribute to peace or to the assertion of human rights.
(18, 20) Our age has brought about inevitable changes, and nobody can deny the great innovations  wrought in every aspect of our life, However, we continue to feel that we must strive, and in-  deed hasten, with all the means of modern education at our disposal, to protect man's great  spiritual heritage, which is undoubtedly the true reason behind his continued existence, his  security, his righteousness and his wellbeing, I feel that, as we have designated an Inter-  national Book Year, so should we designate an International Year for spiritual and religious  education, -
(18,21) The concepts of contemporary culture have changed, as have its interpretations; and  clarity, precision and cohesion have been lost in the vastness of these interpretations, For
151   example, there are still uncertainty and doubt about the concept of literacy; many obstacles -  racial discrimination and conflicting political ideologies - bar the way to the concept of a uni-  versal human culture, We here are responsible, as a generation which must overcome all these  difficulties in order to achieve peace, security and clarity of vision and hand them down to the  generations to come, Iam very confident that with God's help we shall be able, through addi-  tional efforts and sacrifice, to overcome all these obstacles. May His Peace be upon you!

(22,1) Mr, JASIM KHALID AL-MARZOUK (Kuwait) (Translation from the Arabic):  It gives me great pleasure to represent my country, Kuwait, at the seventeenth session uf  the General Conference, in which we are endeavouring, by close co-operation between Member  States, to achieve the noble aims for the fulfilment of which Unesco was founded: to serve human  knowledge and civilization.
(22, 2) I should like, on behalf of the delegation of Kuwait, to express my sincere congratula-  tions to the President on his election to the chair of the present session of the General Confer-  ence. I should also like to thank the outgoing President for his valuable efforts on behalf of this  Organization at the last session,
(22, 3) I must also welcome our sister States Bahrain, Qatar and the United Arab Emirates,  which have now joined Unesco after achieving full independence.
(22, 4) We have great faith in man's creative ability to confront challenges and to strive earnest-  ly and faithfully to proceed with the march of human civilization, Foremost among these chal-  lenges is thé liberation of the human race from ignorance ahd backwardness, which have been’ ~  experienced to the full by the Arab people of Kuwait, But we have worked earnestly and côn-  scientiously, backed by our God-given material resources, to achieve miracles of education.  We have provided a place in our schools for every child from kindergarten up to the end of the  secondary level; we have opened our university to all those capable of pursning higher education;
155   we have also made available various free educational and health services for students, and have  developed our educational curricula to keep up with the great advances of science,
(22, 5) In the next stage we shall attempt to review our aims, methods and means in order to  evaluate the balance we have achieved between bringing the Kuwaiti citizen to a full awareness  of his past heritage so as to awaken his sense of identity and develop his understanding of the  rôle he plays in human civilization, and giving him a future in which his intelligent and creative  mind and his manual and technical ability shall be integrated. Knowledge would thus take its  rightful place as an instrument serving and not dominating man, We therefore look to Unesco  in the hope that it will further assist us to develop and modernize our educational system,
(22.6) Our meeting here at this international gathering urges us to redouble our efforts and to  increase our capacities, to allow education to play a positive part in guiding the destiny of peo-  ples. We should also further promote international understanding and the unity of mankind, so  that love, fraternity and justice may be man's guiding principles in the twenty-first century, It  is therefore not compatible with our achievements that society should be ruled by jungle law where  might 11; right, nor that the international community should witness the eviction of an entire  people from tneir homeland, and that power should justify aggression and occupation,
(22.7) On this occasion the delegation of Kuwait would like to submit a number of questions for  the consideration of this distinguished Conference, in the hope that they will receive your sup-  port. The question deserving the closest attention is the need to increase the number of seats  allocated to the Arab States on the Executive Board from three to four, owing to the increased  Arab membership in the Organization.
(22, 8) We also hope that this distinguished Conference will resolve to increase the budgets of  the Unesco regional centres in the Arab States in recognition of their effective rôle in the devel-  opment of education in this sensitive part of the world. We further hope that Unesco will in-  crease the number of fellowships allocated to higher education in our region, especially those  intended for university professors, in order to develop educational leadership.
(22, 9) We also hope that this gathering will further confirm the right to education of the children  of the world, foremost among whom we place the children of the Arab people in Palestine, so  as not to add to that crime which has made them a vagrant people, the crime of keeping them in  ignorance and casting them out,
(22, 10) Our delegation therefore urges that the national, artistic, scientific and historical  heritage of the Palestinian Arab people should be protected from the danger of abuse and falsi-  fication by the occupation authorities, We also strongly urge that action should be taken to safe-  guard and to protect from loss and destruction Islam's holy places in the City of Jerusalem, this  city which has given to mankind and civilization their sublimest and noblest values,
(22. 11) Unesco cannot undertake all these efforts unless it receives the material support it  needs to face its growing duties, especially towards the developing countries. We therefore  appeal to all the countries of the world not to reduce their contributions to Unesco's budget, but  rather to work for their increase,
(22.12) In conclusion, I should like to wish the Conference all success in its endeavours to ful-  fil the hopes of the peoples of the world for a better life. I should also like to express my thanks  and appreciation to Mr, René Maheu for his worthy efforts in the service of education and human  civilization.
