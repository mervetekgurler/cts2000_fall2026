10.2 Comme le Président vient de l'indiquer, le premier rapport du Comité juridique porte  sur les points 16, 17, 18 et 19 de l'ordre du jour. Le libellé exact de ces quatre points est  donné au paragraphe 1 du rapport.
10.3 Conformément à la décision prise par la Conférence générale le 18 octobre 1972, le  comité a examiné les aspects juridiques de ces points, y compris la procédure à suivre en  séance plénière pour la mise aux voix des propositions soumises, Le comité a constaté  en premier lieu que toutes les propositions dont il était saisi étaient recevables, et il a  estimé que la Conférence générale peut, si elle le désire, procéder à l'adoption de ces  propositions,
10,4 Le Comité a examiné ensuite le projet d'amendement à l'Acte constitutif et au Règle-  ment intérieur de la Conférence générale soumis par la Suède (document 17 C/83), Il a  entendu à ce sujet un membre de la délégation de la Suède, dont la déclaration est résumée  au paragraphe 6 du rapport et il a procédé à un large échange de vues dont la substance  est présentée dans les par «2-aphes 7 à 10 du rapport, J'attire particulièrement l'attention  des délégations sur le paragraphe 9, dans lequel le comité a fait mention de certains effets  et conséquences juridiques qu'entrafnerait, à son avis, l'adoption du projet d'amendement  présenté par la Suède, J'aurai l'occasion de revenir sur l'alinéa (f) de ce paragraphe  lorsque j'exposerai la procédure que le comité recommande à la Conférence générale de  suivre pour la mise aux voix des différentes propositions dont elle est saisie,
10.5 Le Comité juridique a ensuite procédé à l'examen des diverses clauses du projet  d'amendement présenté par la Suède, du point de vue de la forme et de la rédaction, et il  y à apporté quelques modifications qui sont indiquées au paragraphe 10 du rapport,
10,6 Le comité a ensuite procédé à l'examen des propositions figurant dans le document
17 C/14, qui contient les recommandations du Conseil exécutif sur le mode d'élection et  la durée du mandat des membres du Conseil, Le Comité juridique a également examiné les  documents 17 C/80 et 17 C/80 Addendum, qui ont trait à l'augmentation du nombre des  membres du Conseil exécutif, et le document 17 C/82, relatif à la durée du mandat des  membres du Conseil exécutif, Il a constaté que les diverses propositions figurant dans ces  documents ne soulèvent pas d'obstacle d'ordre juridique et que leur rédaction n'appelle pas  d'observations de sa part,
10,7 J'en viens maintenant à la procédure que le Comité juridique propose de suivre pour  la mise aux voix, à l'issue du débat qui va maintenant s'instaurer, des diverses proposi-  tions soumises à la Conférence générale, Je précise que ces propositions, qui figurent
12  dans les annexes J à X du rapport, ont été classées par le comité dans l'ordre qu'il vous  recommande de suivre pour leur mise aux voix, afin de faciliter vos travaux.

10.9 Le projet de résolution qui figure à l'annexe I est celui que propose le Conseil exé-  cutif, Comme il implique la suspension de certaines dispositions du Règlement intérieur,  la majorité des deux tiers des membres présents et votants est requise pour son adoption.
10.10 Le Comité juridique recommande ensuite que la Conférence se prononce sur le pro-  jet de résolution figurant à l'annexe II, Ce projet de résolution tend à mettre à jour, avant  les prochaines élections au Conseil exécutif, la liste des Etats membres dont les noms  sont répartis entre les divers groupes électoraux, ainsi qu'il est indiqué dans le premier  considérant. Depuis la quinzième session de la Conférence générale, cing nouveaux Etats  sont devenus membres de l'Unesco et il convient de les inscrire dans les groupes électo-  raux appropriés, Le Comité ne s'est pas estimé compétent pour faire des propositions à  cet égard, mais des délégations voudront sans doute en formuler à l'intention de la Confé-  rence générale en ce qui concerne chacun de ces Etats, Le projet de résolution constate  par ailleurs que si, au 31 décembre 1972, le Portugal cesse d'être membre de l'Organi-  sation, il cessera automatiquement de faire partie du groupe auquel il appartient mainte-  nant (Groupe I). Je rappelle que, pour compléter et modifier une liste figurant dans une  résolution antérieure de la Conférence générale, la majorité simple des membres présents  et votanis est suffisante,
10,11 Le Comité juridique s'est ensuite penché sur la question de l'ordre à suivre pour la  mise aux voix des diverses propositions entraînant des amendements à l'Acte constitutif.  J'attire votre attention à cet égard sur le paragraphe 18 du rapport, où le Comité juridique  recommande que la Conférence générale se prononce d'abord sur les propositions visant à  augmenter le nombre des membres du Conseil et à répartir les sièges additionnels entre  les divers groupes électoraux, Le comité ayant constaté que les auteurs des diverses pro-  positions sur ce point avaient lié l'augmentation du nombre des membres du Conseil à une  certaine répartition des sièges additionnels, il conviendrait de se prononcer sur chaque  série de propositicns en une seule fois, Les amendements à l'Acte constitutif exigeant la  majorité des deux tiers, cette majorité serait requise pour l'adoption de chacun des six  projets de résolution faisant l'objet des annexes III, IV, V, VI, VII et VIII du rapport, Con-  formément aux dispositions de l'article 86 du Règlement intérieur, il conviendrait de voter  d'abord sur la proposition qui s'éloigne le pius quant au fond de la proposition primitive ;  les projets de résolution sont reproduits dans les annexes du rapport dans l'ordre selon  lequel il conviendrait de les mettre, le cas échéant, aux voix,
10,12 J'ajoute que le comité a été informé en séance que les Etats réunis dans le groupe  dit des 77 avaient demandé que la question de l'augmentation du nombre des membres du  Conseil soit examinée et mise aux voix avant les deux autres questions. 12  jorité des deux tiers des membres présents et votants, Si toutes les propositions sont reje-  tées, le nombre actuel des membres du Conseil demeurera inchangé.
10,13 Le Comité juridique recommande que la Conférence générale se prononce ensuite  sur la partie du projet de résolution figurant à l'annexe IX qui a trait à la durée du man-  dat des membres du Conseil exécutif. Comme il est indiqué au paragraphe 19 du rapport,  cette proposition tend à remplacer, à l'article V À du paragraphe 3 de l'Acte constitutif,  les mots "troisième session'' par "deuxième session'', c'est-à-dire à réduire la durée  actuelle du mandat, fixée à six ans environ, à quatre ans environ, C'est sur ce point pré-  cis que le comité invite la Conférence générale à se prononcer, à ce stade de ses travaux,  La clause visée est la pren:ière phrase du paragraphe 1 du projet de résolution figurant a  l'annexe IX, Pour cette décision, la majorité des deux tiers des membres présents et vo-  tants est requise.

10,16 La seconde question (l'amendement présenté par la Suède entrafne-t-il des obliga-  tions nouvelles pour les Etats membres ?) ayant été mise aux voix, neuf membres se  sont prononcés par l'affirmative, neuf par la négative et un membre s'est abstenu. 10,18 Si la Conférence générale répond par la négative aux deux questions posées et s'il  v a donc deux réponses négatives, il conviendra de mettre aux voix le projet de résolution  figurant à l'annexe X, avec les paragraphes 3, 4 et 5 qui figurent dans la cclonne de gauche,  Si, en revanche, la Conférence générale répond par l'affirmative à l'une ou l'autre des  deux ques'ions, une seule réponse affirmative étant suffisante, il conviendra de mettre aux  voix le projet de résolution reproduit à l'annexe X, avec les paragraphes 3, 4 et 5 qui fi-  gurent dans la colonne de droite. Quel que soit le texte retenu, il conviendra de mettreaux  voix le projet de résolution qui figure à l'annexe X, comportant les modifications décidées  en ce qui concerne le nombre des membres et la durée du mandat et l'une ou l'autre ver-  sion des paragraphes 3, 4 et 5, Il ne s'agit pas de voter successivement sur l'une et l'autre  version, mais sur une de ces versions seulement, seion la réponse qui sera donnée par la  Conférence générale aux deux questions dont j'ai parlé il y a quelques instants.

14,1 Mr. TORSTEN ORN (Sweden):  Mr, President, distinguished delegates, the draft amendment to the Constitution of  Unesco appearing in document 17 C/83 has been submitted to you on behalf of the Govern-  ment of Sweden with the full support of the Swedish National Commission for Unesco, The  Swedish Government has also been able to benefit from consultation with other Nordic Govern-  ments before submitting this proposal, which was circulated to Member States by the Director-  General on 14 April,
14,2 Mr. President, we have just heard the Report of the Legal Committee and the intro-  ductions of the proposals regarding continuation of the electoral groups, increased number  of seats on the Board and the reduction of the duration of the term of office of members of  the Board from six to four years, May I therefore, with your permission, at the outset of  my intervention indicate the position of my delegation on these proposals, We are going to  vote in favour of the continuation of the system of electoral groups, in favour of an increase  in the number of seats on the Board from 34 to 40 and in favour of a reduction of the dura-  tion of the term of office. We sincerely hope that all these proposals will be adopted by the  General Conference,
14,3 May I now explain briefly how the Swedish proposal relates to these other proposals,  Mr, President, our amendment is based, on one hand, on the assumption that the present  system of electoral groups will be maintained, and on the other hand, it constitutes a logi-  cal follow-up of the proposals for an increased number of seats and afaster rotation of these  seats, My Government has interpreted these new proposals first and foremost as being ex-  pressions of a desire on the part of Member States to participate more actively in the work  of the Board and as an expression of tne fact that Member States regard the Board as their  representative during the two-year period between the sessions of the General Conference.  The aim of our proposal, which is put forward in the same spirit and with the same purpose  as these other proposals, is to ensure that the composition of the Executive Board reflects  as clearly as possible this desire and to ensure that the executive organ of Unesco is made  truly representative of Member States as well as of the General Conference,
14. 4 We have, therefore, proposed an adjustment of the present constitutional provisions  governing the nature of the composition of the Board, These provisions, as you all know,  are at present ambiguous: on one hand, members of the Board represent Member States  and the General Conference, on the other hand, they cannot be replaced by their own Govern-  ments, If the Swedish proposal is adopted, this ambiguity would be eliminated, . What we  are suggesting in short is that the Board in the future should consist of Member States re-  presented by individuals instead of, as today, individuals representing Member States,  This revision of the Constitution would not only make it clear that the Board represents the  Member States, but it would also put an end to the present situation in which it is possible  for a governmental representative on the Board to remain in office although he has lost the  confidence of his Government, The present situation means that the Board to some extent  is neither the representative of individual Member States, nor consequently of the General  Conference as a whole,
14.5 The proposal that the General Conference should be allowed to elect the Board from  among its Member States provides a simple solution, which also has the advantage of adapt-  ing the constitutional rôles to the de facto situation, As you all know, the system of elec-  toral groups, which was adopted at the fifteenth session of the General Conference, in reality  means that the different regions come to an agreement as to which Member States should  be represented on the Board, Thus, to propose - as we do - that “ember States be elected  to the Board does not go further than to propose recognition of th» reality of the system  currently in force. Further, Mr, President, I think that it is appropriate to underline, in  this connexion, that we retain such basic principles as that each Government's representa-  tive on the Board should be selecied on the basis of his eminence in the fields of competence  of Unesco and that the Board should represent the General Conference as a whole,
14, 6 Briefly, Mr. President, what would be achieved by our proposal is an adjustment  which will clarify the fact that the Executive Board shall represent Member States between  the sessions of the General Conference, If the Constitution is amended as we propose, it  will furthermore be clear that when a representative speaks on the Board, his intervention  not only carries the weight of being his views in his capacity as an eminent expert in Unesco's  fields of competence, but also the full weight and prestige of being the views of his Govern-  ment, If we have interpreted the desire of Member States correctly, it is under such con-  ditions that they wish to participate in the work of the Board,
14,7 Mr, President, may I now try to dispel some misunderstandings and misgivings re-  garding our proposal, which still seem to exist in certain circles, First of all, it has been  said that our proposal is in contradiction with the other proposals relating to the Executive  Board submitted to this General Conference. I have already stated - and it is also evident  from the report of the Legal Committee - that this is simply not so, On the contrary, we  see all the proposals as parts of one parcel aimed at making the Board at the same time  more representative and more efficient. Then, Mr. President, we have heard the fear ex-  pressed that the quality of the representatives would suffer, if our amendment was adopted,  I fail to see why. Surely the first quality of a representative must be that he is represen-  tative, and that is precisely what our proposal sets out to achieve, Why, Mr, President,  why in fact shoald a government elect anybody but the most qualified man or woman avail-  able? I should also like to stress the fact that we propose that Member States should se-  lect their representatives on the Board in the same manner as they nominate their delega-  tions to the General Conference, that is in consultation with their National Commission for  Unesco, if established, or with educational, scientific and cultural bodies, As the preset  members of the Board are elected from among the delegations to the General Conference,  there should not be any change in the competence of the individual representative,
14, 8 As to the procedures to be followed for the election, we propose that the Executive  Board submit its recommendations thereon to the eighteenth session of thre General Confer-  ence, In the view of my delegation, a Member State seeking election to the Board might  well wish to enhance his chances by announcing the name and the qualifications of its re-  presentative, at the same time as it announces its candidature,
14,9 Anxiety has also been voiced that continuity would be endangered to the detriment of  the work of the Board, if our amendment was adopted, Again, Mr. President, this seems  to reflect an overly-suspicious attitude of certain delegates towards Member Governments,  Why should a Member Government wish to exchange its representative on the Board, as it  were, merely for the sake of exercising its power? ‘One would have to assume that a govern-  ment which takes the trouble of standing for election to the Board and selecting a qualified  representative will keep this representative as long as he enjoys its confidence. As we see  it, a replacement would thus constitute the exception tc the general rule rather than the nor-  mal practice. We furthermore feel confident that the Board, when considering the proce-  dures to be followed in the future for the election to the Board, wil! find a formula that will  oblige governments not to replace their representative on the Board unnecessarily,
14,10 An important question obviously is whether my Government's amendment entails any  change in the present financial dispositions, in short, whether the Organization or the Mem-  ber State should pay for the representative on the Board. I want to stress most emphatical-  ly that my delegation does not propose any change in the present system in that respect,  Had we wished a change here, we would have said so in our amendment, But it is more  important, I think, to underline that if we had thought that such a change would be unavoid-  able as a consequence of our proposal, we would not have presented our amendment, We  share the view that it would facilitate participation of the smaller member countries of  Unesco in the Board if the Organization, as such, continues to cover the Board's costs,  This is all the more logical since under our proposal, the Board would also represent the  General Conference as a whole,
14,11 Mr, President, some delegates seem to be worried by the re-election clause in the  Swedish amendment, Let me first of all point out that we, for our part, do not propose the  creation of permanent seats for any or several Member States, Secondly, under the pre-  sent system, the possibility already. exists of keeping a country represented continuously  on the Board, albeit by electing a different individual from that particular country every  new period, Thirdly - and this is most important in the view of my delegation - since the  introduction of regional electoral groups in 1968, for all practical purposes it is they who  make the selection, If a particular electoral group wants one of its members to serve more  than one term, this is up to that very group. If, on the contrary, the group wants to speed  up rotation +7 not re-electing any of its members, it is again for the group so to decide,  What the Swedish proposal aims at, in this respect, is to leave the freedom of choice to the  regional electoral groups.
14,12 Mr, President, to sum up, in proposing its amendment, my delegation is moved by  its concern for our Organization. Unesco is in many respects a changing Organization in  a changing world. Activities which were almost unthought of in the late 19408, now occupy  a large segment of the Organization's intellectual, personnel and financial resources, to-  gether with traditionally educational, scientific, and cultural pursuits, I am thinking of  space communications, assistance to national liberation movements and - not least - Unesco's  contribution to development assistance. It is only natural, that the structure of the Organi-  zation should be adapted in such a way that it best reflects realities and is best prepared to  cope with new tasks. This, ultimately, is the aim of our modest amendment, As we see  it, Mr. President, the Executive Board is already the key organ in the relationship between  the General Conference, the Director-General and the Secretariat, and the Member States,  which collectively constitute our Organization, And this key rôle seems most likely to be  even more accentuated in years to come, The Executive Board has gone a long way to ren-  der its work more efficient. We have been impressed by the results and hope that these  efforts will continue and will bear fruit, Our amendment, Sir, tries to encourage this trend,  Thank you Mr. President.

(16.1) Dr. MUSTAFA KAMAL HELMY (Arab Republic of Egypt) (Translation from the Arabic):  The Constitution of the Organization stipulates a balanced representation on the Executive  Board of the various cultures and geographical areas, There are at present thirty-four seats on  the Executive Board, distributed among the five electoral groups in a manner which we do not  consider to secure a balanced or equitable representation of the various cultures and geographi-  cal areas. Group V, which comprises the African and Arab States, has a special status which  may be summed up as follows, First, it is numerically the largest group, comprising forty-five  States now increased to forty-nine by the admission of four new Arab States. Second, geographi-  cally this group comprises more than one continent, for it includes all Airica in addition to the  Arabian peninsula and other parts of Asia, Third, it comprises a large group of cultures, Fourth,  all its members are developing States, with a greater need to benefit from Unesco's programmes.  Despite all this we note that Group V is proportionately the least represented on the Executive  Board,
(16, 2) Comparison of the number and calculation of the proportion of the seats allocated toeach  electoral group give the following results: Group I at present comprises twenty-seven States, to  which nine seats have been allocated, or one seat for every three States. Group II at present  comprises ter. States, to which three seats have been allocated, or one seat for every 3, 3 States.  Group III comprises twenty-four States, to which six seats have been allocated, or one seat for  every four States. Group IV comprises nineteen States, to which five seats have been allocated,  or one seat for every 3. 8 States. Group V comprising the African and Arab States, as mention-  ed earlier, consisted of forty-five States, Eleven seats were allocated to this group, or one  seat for every 4, 1 States,
(16. 3) It is apparent from this enumeration that the least represented group on the Executive  Board is Group V. Furthermore, the membership of this group has been substantially increased,  as we mentioned above, with the admission of four new Arab States - Bahrain, the United Arab  Emirates, Oman and Qatar - to the Organization. This group now has a membership of forty-  nine States, eighteen of which are Arab States,

12  understood that the basis shall be nine seats for the Africans and four for the Arabs, We believe  that this just request of ours is the first of a series of efforts to achieve a more equitable and  better-balanced representation on the Executive Board of the different cultures and geographical  areas,

However, with this proposed increase in the number of seats allocated to  the various groups, Group V will remain the electoral group with the smallest representation on  the Executive Board, It will have one seat for every 3. 8 States, while Group IV will have one  for every 3, 2 States, Group III one for every 3, 4 States, Group Il one for every 2. 5 States, and  Group I one for every 2, 7 States, Nevertheless, I repeat, we are ot opposed to increasing the  number of seats allocated to the other groups; and we shall vote for the proposal in Annex III  which would increase to forty the members of the Executive Board. We therefore do not agree to a proposal that  would entitle a State to replace its representative on the Board as it saw fit.

(18. 2) Under items 16, 17, 18 and 19 of the Agenda of the present session, the General Confer-  ence is to take important decisions on increasing the number of members of the Executive Board,  on a fairer representation on the Board of various groups of States, on the term of office of mem-  bers of the Board, and on their status,

(22, 5) Thirdly, I should like to clarify paragraph 2 of the operative part of our draît resolution,  replacing the present Article V. A, , paragraph 13 of the Constitution by the words: "Notwith-  standing the provisions of paragraph 3 of this Article, (a) Members of the Executive Board elec-  ted prior to the seventeenth session of the General Conference shall serve until the end of the  term for which they were elected.” The explanation is very simple, The principle of respect  for vested rights was accepted by the General Conference at its fifteenth and sixteenth sessions,  so that what we have done is to reproduce a text that exists in the Constitution itself, Sub-  paragraph 2 (b) states: "Members of the Executive Board appointed, prior to the seventeenth  session of the General Conference, by the Board ...'. It does not state "elected by the General  Conference", When a member retires from the Board, the State to which he belongs may submit  a candidature. The Constitution itself, when reformed at the fifteenth session, established this  principle, which today applies only to two persons, who have not submitted and presumably will  not submit their candidature. 26.1 Mr, CAHILL (United Kingdom):  Mr, President, ladies and gentlemen, I have the honour to present to you briefly this  morning the views. of the United Kingdom Government on the three main propositions under  discussion, It seems appropriate that these views should be conveyed to you by a member  of the United Kingdom delegation who is not himself a member of the Executive Board.
26. 2 The first proposition concerns the enlargement of the Board from 34 to 40 members,  My Government's position on this is easily stated. We were among the many co-signatories  of the letter proposing this enlargement, which we fully support, At the same time, I must  make it clear that my Government considers it imperative, assuming this enlargement is  agreed, that the Executive Board should take a very serious look at its own methods of work  and the composition of its subsidiary bodies. The viability of a Board of 34 members, given  its present procedures and arrangements, is already a matter of some doubt, With a Board  of 40, it would surely be essential that fundamental changes should be made in the present  system of committees and commissions. Not only in the interest of reducing to the minimum  the financial implications of the enlargement of the Board but, and perhaps this is even  more important, in the general interests of making the Board the most effective possible  instrument for the discharge of its functions. This is a point, Mr. President, on which my  Government cannot insist too strongly. We very much hope that if this enlargement is  adopted, the General Conference at its present session will call on the Executive Board to  concuct a fundamental examination of its own working methods on the lines of which I have  just spoken,
26. 3 The second proposition concerns the restriction from six to four years of the term of  eligibility of Board members, We are unhappy about this proposal, for reasons which 1  shall explain in a moment, The third proposition, put forward by the delegation of Sweden,  is that the members of the Board should be government representatives rather than indivi-  duals representing their governments, I understand that this idea is far from new, and that  at least one Member State was attracted by it a number of years ago, It is, of course, a  very obvious idea that Unesco should be brought into line, so to speak, with other bodies in  the United Nations system. My Government, however, is convinced that this is an instance  in which it would be a grave mistake to seek to be tidy-minded. Uniformity for its own sake  has no particular virtue, There is quite enough drab and dreary uniformity in the world  already.
26. 4 The proposal advanced by the Swedish authorities is, in my Government's view, a  point of key importance for Unesco, It is also one on which my Government holds firm and  carefully-considered views, Some of you may remember that the British Minister for Over-  seas Development, speaking at this rostrum last Thursday, used the following firm and  carefully-considered words: ''Those who founded Unesco'', he said, "gave it a special charac-  ter, by providing for individuals rather than governments to sit as members of the Executive  Board, The founders realized that individuals have an irreplaceable contribution to make,  If they cannot make it”, he said, "Unesco will decay". I submit, Mr, President, that the  effects of the change proposed will be much more far-reaching, and much more detrimental  to Unesco, than its sponsors imagine,
26.5 There are already ample opportunities for governments to put their views before the  Executive Board. Permit me first to recall that Article V, A, 1. of the Constitution speci-  fically provides that each Board member should "represent the government of the State of  which he is a national", Permit me, also to recall that under the Rules of Procedure of  the Executive Board, observers of Member States and other States and organizations may  be invited by the Board to address the Board on matters within their competence, My Govern-  ment finds it difficult to imagine circumstances in which such an invitation would be with-  held if a request were made, We think it would be appropriate for this provision to be much  more widely used, particularly to enable Member States which do not have one of their own  nationals on the Board to bring their views directly and authoritatively to the notice of the  Board,
26.6 On the United Kingdom side we have no vested interest in this question whatsoever.  Normally we have been represented on the Board by individuals in the service of the British  Government, and I cannot see that this practice is likely to change, But we attach the great-  est importance to the maintenance of Article V, A, 2 of the Constitution, This Article pro-  vides that: "In electing the members of the Executive Board the General Conference shall  endeavour to include persons competent in the arts, the humanities, the sciences, educa-  tion and the diffusion of ideas and qualified hy their experience and capacity to fulfil the  administrative and executive duties of the Board, It shall also have regard to the diversity  of cultures and a balanced geographical distribution'. This is surely a provision of the  highest importance for our Organization,
26.7 Our reasons for taking the view I have outlined are as follows, There are four of them.  First, if I may say so, individuals are more likely to be positive and creative than govern-  ments in their approach to questions within Unesco's field of competence; second, it is vital,  in our view, that there should be a strong element of continuity within the Board, if it is to  discharge its functions efficiently, Only persons who have followed the work of the Board  over a considerable period are competent, it seems to us, to act as Chairmen either of the  Board or its subsidiary organs, Government servants have their postings changed all too  often, Equally, the consideration of continuity leads us to express a distinct preference for  the present six-year term of service for Board members rather than the proposed four-year  term, Third, it is equally important that members of the Board should be of the highest  intellectual calibre, Again, I foresee a temptation for governments, should the present  proposal be accepted, to put their views to the Board through the medium of perhaps some-  what junior representatives, normally resident in Paris, Iamnot thinking of course of their  distinguished Ambassadors, but of members of their missions who may be less senior in  status and who may not always have had the opportunity to follow Unesco questions with the  attention these questions deserve. This would surely reduce the possibility of the best advice  and the wisest talents being made available to the Board, Unesco, and more particularly  its Executive Board, which works on behalf of us all, deserves the best people it can find,
26. 8 Lastly, could those countries which are furthest from Europe really afford to send  their best people if Unesco ceased to pay for the cost of their travel and their maintenance  in Paris? If the present proposal were accepted, I think it would be very difficult to justify  the continuance by Unesco of the present financial practice, It is certainly not the practice  in other organs of the United Nations system, such as the Council of FAO, which consist of  governments, Presumably if uniformity is sought, it will have to apply in this respect as  well, May I say that if this amendment is rejected, we in the United Kingdom are entirely  content that the present arrangement should remain unchanged, and if this means that the  more developed countries should indirectly help to pay for the attendance of Board members  from the less developed countries, then this is a state of affairs which we are very glad  should continue,
26.9 I repeat, Mr, President, that this particular proposal goes to the roots of Unesco's  Constitution, It would be most imprudent to tamper with this particular provision, or with  any other far-reaching provision of this kind, unless we are absolutely clear what we are  doing, why we are doing it, whether indeed it is necessary to do it at all and what the con-  sequences would be, I subniit, Mr, President, that in this instance our Conference cannot  give firm answers to these questions, and that to adopt the present proposal would be not a  step forward but a most undesirable step backwards - a step, Mr, President, which we  should all be bound to regret whatever our present opinion may be, Thank you, Sir,
