TWENTY-SECOND MEETING  Friday, 9 June 1950, at 9.30 p.m.  Chairman: Dr. F. Bennper (Netherlands)  later: Mr. Sipney Porrock (Canada)  Presentation of the Annual Programme and  Budget of the Organization [document 5C/12].  [1] The CHAIRMAN opened the discussion on  the United Kingdom resolution dealing with the  form of the Programme and Budget in future  years, which had been referred to both the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission and the Budget  Committee.  [2] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom)  explained that only paragraph (b) concerned the  Budget Committee. This was a matter he had  raised many times in the Committee—that is to  say:  Fach programme resolution in the Annual Programme  shall be separately and individually accompanied by a  Budget entry with full details relating to the estimated  cost of carrying out the work involved in that resolu-  tion; excepting only established personnel costs and  other standing administrative costs of the Organiza-  tion, which may be included in a consolidated estimate  placed at the beginning of each chapter of the Annual  Programme.  [3] Mr. ROSEMAN (United States of America)  stated that his delegation could not support this  resolution. They felt that it was a step in the  wrong direction which would break up the Pro-  gramme of Unesco into fragments. Neither the  United States government nor any other, to his  knowledge, submitted to pinpoint legislation of  this type. His delegation recommended a budget  on broad programme lines giving the Director-  General greater flexibility and freedom to carry  out his Programme. Far from adopting the  United Kingdom resolution, which would only  lead to greater rigidity, Unesco should abandon  even its present system in favour of one giving  more flexibility.  [4] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) pointed  out that delegations required precise information  regarding the cost of each item in the Budget  and that, if this were not given initially in the  Budget Estimates, much time would be lost dur-  Ing a General Conference while Directors of  departments supplied the information required.     [5] Mr. POLLOCK (Canada) said that, while  understanding and sympathizing with the object  of the United Kingdom resolution, he could not  support it, since it endangered that flexibility of the  Programme so essential to the Director-General in  carrying out his responsibilities. In addition, the  United Kingdom proposal would lead Unesco away  from the United Nations’ endeavour to standard-  ize the Budget presentation of all the Specialized  Agencies, and further it would prevent clear  annual reviews of the progress of the Organiza-  tion, since this kind of budgeting on a detailed  project basis would render any comparison of  operations from year to year most difficult. On  the other hand it was quite definitely essential for  delegations, by one means or another, to have a  very clear picture of the cost of each project.  Mr. Pollock understood that the Economic and  Social Council had just requested Unesco to  provide a budget on an individual project basis  for the detailed information of the United Nations.  If Unesco had to prepare a budget of this nature  annually for Ecosoc, it might be presented as an  information annex for delegations, the main  budget estimates being presented under the broad,  flexible form proposed by Mr. Roseman. In any  case he would be glad to hear the comments of the  Director-General on these proposals.  [6] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) said that although  he, too, sympathized with the desire of the United  Kingdom delegation, the method of presentation  of the budget was entirely dependent upon the  structure of the Programme. Since 1946 efforts  had been made to cost the budget entirely  by programme resolution. So far none of these  attempts had been successful to the extent re-  quired by the United Kingdom resolution, and a  certain flexibility had remained; for instance, the  Director-General had been able to increase the  number of permanent posts from 774, as approved  by the Fourth Session, to 824 at the present time.  His delegation felt that a certain amount of free-  dom within the fixed budget—perhaps more than  hitherto—was necessary, but that it would be  dangerous for Member States to approve a budget         on lines which were too broad and vague. On  the other hand, if the Programme and Budget  were presented in too much detail, it would  become increasingly impossible for the General  Conference to complete its tasks successfully in  the time available. This would raise the whole  question of the structure of the General Confer-  ence. He thought that the United Kingdom reso-  lution required amendment to render it practic-  able, but that the entire question was altogether  too large for the Budget Committee and would  have to be debated by the General Conference.  However, it might be useful to hear the comments  of the Director-General and the Executive Board.  [7] Dr. EVANS (Executive Board) emphasized  that the Board had not yet studied the proposal  contained in the United Kingdom resolution;  nevertheless he believed the Board would not con-  sider it possible to maintain a complete parallel  between the Programme and the Budget. It might  be possible to achieve a closer degree of co-  ordination than hitherto, but the United King-  dom resolution went too far in this direction.  [8] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) said that al-  though he had not yet been able to give this mat-  ter full consideration, he was inclined to agree  with the point of view expressed by Mr. Rose-  man.  [9] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom)  thanked delegates for their most interesting and  helpful comments, and announced that he would  withdraw his resolution for the time being in  order to bring these comments to the notice of  his delegation.  [10] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) said that the  Director-General recognized that this was an ex-  tremely involved problem, due mainly to the com-  plex way in which the Programme had been  developed; for instance, in 1951 approximately
279 separate actions were required. However, the  Director-General was not entirely in favour of the  United Kingdom resolution, since it was extreme-  ly difficult for the Secretariat to estimate the cost  of each separate action with any degree of  accuracy eighteen months or two years in  advance, in accordance with present procedure.  The detailed co-ordination of the Programme and  Budget required by the United Kingdom would  leave the Director-General entirely without free-  dom to make changes which were sometimes  necessary in the best interests of the Organization  during the year. Mr. Weitz pointed out that at  the present time there was danger of contradiction  between the terms of the Appropriation Resolu-  tion (stating that all expenditure must correspond
042  to a programme resolution and to the budget voted  by the General Conference) and the provision  whereby the Director-General was permitted, with  the approval of the Executive Board, to make cer-  tain transfers within the budget. He felt that  matter at least required clarification.  Detailed examination of the Budget Estimates  (continued) [document 5C/5 (II)].  Part IV. Common Service Costs.  A. COMMUNICATIONS.  [11] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) referred to  Resolution CSC-1, increasing postal costs by  $19,000 over the given estimate, owing to the  new rates in France. Approved.  [12] In reply to Mr. ROSEMAN (United States  of America), Mr. WEITZ explained that the Secre-  tariat had found it difficult to establish firm rules  governing the use of air freight instead of surface  mail. All possible steps were being taken to  ensure that documents were prepared sufficiently  early to permit despatch by surface mail. Circum-  stances often made this impossible, but delegates  could be assured that the Secretariat was making  every effort to deal with this problem, and to  restrict the use of airmail, air freight and cable  services to a minimum.  B. BuiLning RENTS, UTILITIES AND MAINTENANCE OF  BUILDINGS.  [13] In reply to Mr. BRUNSWICK (France),  Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Administrative  Management and Budget) explained that the in-  crease provided for electricity charges in 1951 was  made to allow for several extra machines and for  normal improvements in the lighting system of  Unesco House.  C. SUPPLIES AND MATERIALS.  [14] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) noted an in-  crease in this estimate over the figure for 1950,  and suggested a slight reduction to $125,000.  [156] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) explained that  the estimate for this item had been very carefully  calculated to cover no more than the Organiza-  tion’s actual consumption of supplies and mate-  rials for one year. However the small reduction  proposed by Mr. Brunswick wat not unreasonable.  [16] In reply to Mr. ROSEMAN (United States                            of America), Mr. WEITZ added that it was the  policy of the Organization to maintain a six-  monthly stock of expendable supplies. A large  order was placed twice a year and small local  purchases were made as necessary.  Mr. Brunswick's proposal, seconded by Mr. POL-  LOCK (Canada), was adopted.  D. RENTAL OPERATION AND MAINTENANCE OF EqQuip-  MENT.  [17] No comment.  E. STAFF WELFARE ACTIVITIES.  [18] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) raised two  queries in this section: (1) The salary of $3,500  paid for the part-time services of a French  physician seemed extremely high in relation to the  normal remuneration of a doctor in Paris. (2)  The sum of $6,000 provided for the staff language  training scheme also seemed unreasonably high.  In view of Unesco’s limited budget, it might even  be considered as unnecessary and deleted alto-  gether.  [19] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) explained that  he could not say exactly on what terms the con-  tract with the Paris hospital responsible for pro-  viding the Unesco medical service had been fixed.  This contract, however, had resulted in a definite  improvement of the medical service which was  used extensively by staff members. As to the  language training programme, Mr. Weitz said  that all the classes were extremely well attended  by the staff, who also contributed to the cost. He  believed that this scheme was followed by other  Specialized Agencies.  [20] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) said that he  would not insist on any reduction of the estimates  presented, but would propose (1) that the Secre-  tariat study the expenses of the medical services  before the next General Conference with a view  to proposing economies, and (2) that the staff  language training scheme be borne in mind as an  area where possible savings might be effected.  Agreed.  F. PERMANENT EQUIPMENT.  [21] Miss LAISE (United States of America) felt  that the estimate for the replacement of furniture  was extremely high.  [22] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Admini-  strative Management and Budget) explained that  Unesco had insufficient experience in this matter,  since the Organization had no furniture at all  when it was first established. This estimate for  replacements had been made with great care, but  it might be possible to reduce the figure in future  years.  [23] Mr. BRUNSWICK (France) suggested that,  in order to indicate the feeling of the Committee  on this matter and since there was considerable  flexibility in the budget of this section, the aver-  age of $200 per unit replacement be reduced to  $175. Since 77 unit replacements were involved  this would mean a total reduction of $1,925.  Agreed.  G. MISCELLANEOUS COMMON Costs.  [24] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) suggested the  provisional adoption of these estimates as this was  a section in which reductions might be made at  a later stage if necessary. Agreed.
