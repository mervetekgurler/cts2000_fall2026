The DIRECTOR-GENERAL : I might clarify the position a little perhaps if I remind you that, first of all, this is a project originally brought up in the early days of the Preparatory Commission, when it was called «An Anthology of Suffering and Resis- tance». I think that with some justification the title was rejected and, when the question was re- examined, it was decided to put the Anthology forward under the title it now has of «An Anthology of Crea- tive Writing under Axis Occupation », the idea being that it should be; as the delegate of Poland has in part indicated, an historical document of a socio- logical as well as literary interest, showing the ways in which men of letters reacted to the facts of enemy occupation and enemy oppression. As such, as the delegate of Poland has said, I personally feel that it would be of considerable value as a permanent document. As regards the specific queries raised by the dele- gate of Australia, I would like to say, that, first of all, the procedure adumbrated in document 2C/16, circulated to Member Governments on 23 September, is that prior selection and collection of possible material should be made in the various countries and regions which should contribute to this Antho- logy, and that the final selection should be made by the Unesco Secretariat; the book which would result would not be of more than, possibly, 400 pages. I think this would include a bibliography. Whatis in the minds of the Secretariat, and I under- stand was presented to the Working Party, was, as I said, an Anthology in the sense ofits selection ; as a bibliography it would be relatively complete. There would also be a preface by someone, a creative writer and critic, who had not actually suffered Axis occupation, but who had suffered the rigours of war. Someone from a country which has been in the war, but not under Axis occupation, would give a critical picture, an historical study of the reactions. The question whether we should publish the matter ourselves, I think, has never been seriously consi- dered. It was always considered that this is a work which should be offered to commercial publishing houses, and that there should be no difficulty what- ever in securing their acceptance of it. I feel myself, having seen the work of the Letters Sub-Section on the matter during this year, that we have proceeded to a pitch at which it would be rela- tively easy to collect the rest of the information this year, and to secure publication during the coming year.
