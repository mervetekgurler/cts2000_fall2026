Mr, President, distinguished delegates, ladies and gentlemen, I am delighted to con-  vey to you, Mr, President, mydelegation's cordial felicitations on your unanimous election. 3,2 My delegation wishes to associate itself with those who paid tribute to the memory of  Academician Sissakian, the President of the thirteenth session of the General Conference,  whose death is a loss to science and to international understanding in intellectual fields,
3,3 We have been celebrating the Twentieth Anniversary of Unesco at this session of the  General Conference, and I take this happy opportunity to reaffirm our faith in this Organi~  zation and rededicate our will to support its mission, The noble ideals which it was estab-  lished to uphold and promote are dear to the hearts of good men of all lands at all times,  Its fields of operation - education, science and culture - are the pathways on which human-  ity has marched down the ages to prosperity and progress, Inasmuch as these fields are  particularly centred on the oreations of the mind and the atrivings of the spirit, Unesco is  concerned with man's most characteristic and highest functions,
3,4 It is of great significance that the growth of Unesco has proceeded hand in hand with two  major historical developments that have been taking place since the end of the Second World  War: the tremendous expansfon of scientific knowledge, and the widespread emancipation  of peoples resulting in the emergence of new States resolved upon courses of independent  national progress, That the two developments are concomitant and closely linked 1s ample  justification for the existence of Unesco and underlines the fundamental importance of its  role in the modern world, It is gratifying to find Unesco not only intensifying its efforts in  intellectual international co-operation, which has been its traditional task, but also becom-  ing increasingly responsive to the demands of development in the new States, It should al-  ways endeavour to enlist the intellectual wealth of the developed countries and stimulate the  unfolding of potentialities in developing nations, We believe that there is good will and good  faith amorg peoples everywhere, sufficient to sustain Unesco not only in its intellectual and  operationai endeavours, but also in its third and more fundamental task, the moral task of  promoting truth, freedom, justice and peace, Indeed, Unesco's message should be both"  ethical and universal; it should entail continuous and great efforts to reach the masses all  over the world, so that they may all lead dignified, prosperous and peaceful lives, without  distinction of race, sex, language or religion,
3.6 We are gratified that the whole theme of Unesco's contribution to peace hasbeen stressed  in this Conference, and that the round table of eminent intellectuals devoted to the cause of  Unesco has carefully studied the question and recommended a solemn declaration on it.
3,6 My remarks concerning our future programme and budget, as presented in document
14 C/5, ere bound to be general, 1 should say at the outset that this document 18 an admir-  able reflectionof the principles which have been thrashed out in mature debates and finally  endorsed during the last session of the General Conference, The axioms on which they are  based, and the general policies which they define, are rightly to be considered as marking  a real breakthrough in the development of Unesco, We welcome the continuous efforts of  regularization that have been going on to impregnate them with logical consistency and real-  ism, We reaffirm our support for the priorities which have been evolved and recognized  their long-range nature,
3.7 The criteria applied in selecting the projects seem to be valid, though they should be  interpreted to imply and to encourage growth and expansion, The needs of Member States  are of course fundamental, and resources, administrative, technical and financial, should  be continuously developed and adapted to meet these needs, The new procedures introduced  in preparing the budget constitute a great improvement and {t is most satisfying to note that  the provisional budget ceiling was adopted unanimously, We wish to express our congratu-  lations and gratitude to all those who have contributed to the formulation of these programmes  and the budget - the Executive Board, the Secretariat, and particularly the Director-General,  Mr, René Maheu, He has, in exercising his constitutional functions, initiated proposals,  sought advice and consultation, and ultimately finalized the programmes. He has brought  to his task profound reflection, broad vision and sincere devotion,

As far as the priorities accorded  to education and science are concerned, the Special Fund, and the International Bank and

3.9 The United Nations has dedicated its Twenty-first Anniversary to the cause of refugees,  innocent peoples uprooted from their homelands and scattered all over the world, To them  all, everywhere, should go our goodwill and sympathy, and Unesco should do its utmost for  their welfare, The General Assembly has in particular debated the problem of the refugees  from Palestine, With its major dimensions and tragic consequences to hundreds of thousands  of people, the injustice meted out to these people has never been precedented in modern  times,
3.10 As Unesco has been co-operating with the United Nations Relief and Works Agency for  Palestine Refugees since 1961, and has been shouldering responsibility for the education of  their children as a major part of its programme in the Arab region, I feel it my duty to  refer to the serious difficulties that that task has been facing lately, With due appreciation  of the efforts of UNRWA and of Unesco, the welfare programmes have been felt to be sub-  standard, and improvement in both scope and quality has been called for, In recent years,  even that level has been threatened with subsfantial reductions owing to the constant deficit  in financial resources. The Commissioner-General of UNRWA has repeatedly sounded the  alarm and his efforts to overcome the difficulties are appreciated. The Conference of Arab  Ministers in Tripoli last April expressed its concern, and the Director-General of Unesco  has been taking active measures to prevent any serious reduction in the educational pro-  grammes, The matter received the careful consideration of the Executive Board, and it  has seen fit in document 14 C/6 to call for active attention to provide adequate financial  resources for these programmes, We appreciate the response of certain authorities, and  we are particularly grateful to the Swedish Government for their generous help, My govern-  ment provides for the education of refugees in Iraq and in the other countries and is earnestly  engaged in extending educational facilities for their higher education, In spite of its limited  resources, it has contributed towards meeting the financial deficit, Great hopes are pinned  on the response of this assembly to the recommendation of the Executive Board, on the re-  cognition by Member States of the seriousness of the problem, and on their contributions to  its solution, :

3.19 First, ‘priority should be given in the future to educational research; and the pro-  gramme should advance from providing information and documentation to the training of  senior research workers, establishing national and regional institutes, and organizing of  co-operative schemes of educational research at the regional and international level, The  whole field meets with increasing interest in the developed and developing countries; it  could be keyed to and closely linked with educational planning; and it could have direct  bearing on improvements in the quality of education, and on social and economic develop-  ment, Above all, unless national educational systems have their roots dug deeply into  their own soil, instead of merely being transplanted, their course of development must
