The SECRETARYsaid that, owing to the fact that the Fifth Session of the General Conference (to be held in May 1950) would be almost six months earlier than usual, there would be a certain altera- tion in the rhythm of the work of the General Conference and particularly in the terms of office of the Director-General and of the members of the Executive Board. But it had the disadvantage of preventing concurrence between the term of office of members of the Executive Board and sessions of the General Conference. Members of the Board were responsible to the General Conference for carrying out the programme adopted by the latter. If that alternative were adopted, however, they might, therefore, have to be carried out by other relatively irresponsible members. That disad- vantage was so serious that two other alternatives had been proposed to make the term of office of members of the Board coincide with the sessions of the Conference; either by lengthening it so that members of the Board would serve for three years and six months (Alternative II), or by shortening it to two years and six months (Al- ternative III). Alternative IT was fairly simple from the legal point of view. It would require an amendment to the Constitution providing that “elected mem- bers of the Executive Board shall serve for three working years from the close of the session of the General Conference at which they are elected until the close of the third ordinary session of the General Conference following that election...”. The text of such an amendment could be sub- mitted to Member States so that the Conference in Florence could vote an amendment to the Con- stitution. In the meantime it would suffice to amend Rule 97 of the Rules of Procedure (“The term of office of each member shall begin imme- diately on his election and shall end on the day of the election of a member for the subsequent term”) and to propose a resolution to the General            Conference (which would be simpler than a pro- tocol) providing for the lengthening of the term of office of members of the Board until the Con- ference in Florence and the cancellation of the elections that would normally be held at the Fourth Session. It was for that reason that Alternative III had been proposed: to shorten terms of office so that those which would normally not expire before November 1950 would in fact expire in May 1950, at the close of the Fifth Session of the Conference. 23 November 1949, unless the outgoing members who were not re-elected voluntarily, resigned. 1. In final form: Resolution 40.2.   Draft Amendment to Rule 33 of the Rules of Procedure of the General Conference (doc- uments 4C/PRO/3 and 4C/PRO/6). The CHAIRMAN explained that document 4C/ PRO/3 contained an amendment to Rule 33 pro- posed by the Secretariat, and document 4C/ PRO/6 an amendment proposed by the United States delegation. The SECRETARYsaid that the question was of particular interest to the Procedure Committee since Rule 33 of the Rules of Procedure defined a part of its functions. It was in the application of the Rule that its disadvantages had become clear. It was, in fact, a measure for giving effect to paragraph 2 of Article XIV of the Constitution, under which any question or dispute concerning the interpretation of this Constitution shall be referred for determina- tion to the International Court of Justice or to an arbitral tribunal, as the General Conference may determine under its rules of procedure. The General Conference could therefore refer the matter either to the International Court of Justice or to an arbitral tribunal. However, reference to international conventions and to the practice of the United Nations showed that the choice was not entirely free; for, according to Article 92 of the United Nations Charter, the International Court of Justice was a judicial body, and, ac- cording to Article 65 of the Constitution of the Court, it was authorized to give advisory opinions on disputed matters. It therefore seemed that international practice, jurisprudence and the con- stitutional texts justified the following interpreta- tion: all questions on which advice was sought came within the almost exclusive competence of the International Court of Justice, whereas differ- ences of opinion (that is to say whatever might constitute a dispute) were rather within the com- petence of an arbitral tribunal. The Secretariat therefore proposed an amendment to Rule 33 of the Rules of Procedure with a view to making it reflect more clearly the exact meaning of Ar- ticle XIV, paragraph 2, of the Constitution. It was not an amendment of substance, for it merely implied making a distinction between the respec- tive competences of bodies governed by texts which existed before the establishment of Unesco. The CHAIRMAN took it that no delegate wished to discuss the general position, which was quite clear. The first two clauses of the United States and the Secretariat’s amendments were similar, and he asked if there were any comments on them.
