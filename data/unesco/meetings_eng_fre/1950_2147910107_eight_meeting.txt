[2] The DIRECTOR-GENERAL said that at its              last meeting the General Committee had decided  to submit three questions to the Administrative  Commission to enable the Programme and Budget  Commission to reach a decision on the ceiling for  the 1951 budget:
(1) If the Programme and Budget Commission  were to adopt a ceiling of $8,205,000, would this  figure represent a real or a “paper” budget, having  regard to the fact that certain Member States were  in arrears in the payment of their subscriptions?  Assuming that it was a “paper” budget, what  should be the total of the real budget available to  the Organization?  “ (2) If the sum of $8,205,000 represented a  real budget, what should be the ceiling figure to  be adopted by the Programme and Budget Com-  mission ?
(3) If the Programme and Budget Commission  voted for a ceiling of $8,205,000 on the grounds  that this ought to be the amount of the real  budget, what would be the measures recom-  mended by the Administrative Commission for  making up the deficit caused by the default of  certain Member States?  The General Committee would be glad to have  the views of the Administrative Commission on  these three points sufficiently early for the Pro-  gramme and Budget Commission to be able to  reach a decision in the course of the afternoon.  [3] In the General Committee it had been  pointed out that economies could be effected if  the Conference decided to apply a new salary scale  in the case of local staff. The Director-General  was anxious to make his own attitude clear on  this point. When he took charge of the Organ-  ization, the staff, although it did not realize it,  did in fact consist of two categories: personnel  recruited internationally as international officials,  and personnel responsible for less intellectual  work and recruited locally. Though this prin-  ciple obtained in all international organizations,  it had remained a dead letter as far as a local  salary scheme was concerned, as regards Unesco’s  local staff, the latter being paid according to the  local scale of the United Nations based on New  York salaries (considerably higher than those in  Paris, and thus in practice receiving sums con-  siderably larger than those paid for similar ser-  vices to employees in Paris. Application of a local  salary scale to this category of Unesco staff would  affect 60 per cent of the staff as a whole. Having  regard to the importance of the material and  ethical considerations at issue, he (the Director-  General) had discussed the question with the Staff  Association and had urged the equity of the prin-  ciple accepted by the United Nations and all the  Specialized Agencies. This view had been rejected  by the Staff Association, which took its stand on     the fact that the Constitution made no distinction  in this respect between members of the staff and  was of opinion that the application of a different  scale to local staff would weaken the moral  authority of the Constitution within the Secre-  tariat itself. He had been struck by the force of  this argument and had raised the question in the  Executive Board, which had authorized him to  submit it to the General Conference. Since the  publication of document 5C/ADM/9 the situation  had changed in two respects: (1) the Food and  Agriculture Organization had taken certain deci-  sions in the matter; (2) the Secretary-General of  the United Nations had decided to apply the  measures in question to local staff in United Na-  tions European offices, though leaving unchanged  the position of staff recruited prior to that decision.  [4] The Director-General wished to stress that,  if the General Conference disregarded the recom-  mendations in document 5C/ADM/9 and decided  to go further in this connexion than the United  Nations had gone, it would be departing from the  principle of close co-ordination between Unesco  and the United Nations, a principle that had  recently been confirmed on the political plane by  the Credentials Committee, . whose report had been  adopted in plenary session.  Having regard to the seriousness of the salary  problem’s repercussions, the General Committee  had decided to ask the Administrative Commission  not to take this point into consideration when  studying the three questions submitted to it.  [5] The CHAIRMAN pointed out that the dif-  ference between the actual and anticipated receipts  on which the budget was based would have to  be covered either by increases in the contributions  of Member States or by increasing the Working  Capital Fund.  [6] Sir Henry FRENCH (United Kingdom)  agreed that it was unnecessary to discuss in detail  local salaries, but pointed out that all economies  realizable would have a definite effect on the  programme; economies effected in Part 1 of the  programme (covering priority items) would make  funds available for items in Part II (covering  items of secondary importance).  [7] Referring to the Director-General’s mention  of arrears, he pointed out that the report of the  Administrative Commission dealt not with arrears,  but with the possibility that Member States might  be unable to pay part or even the whole of their  contributions. In 1950, actual receipts in con-  tributions were expected to be $500,000 less than  the total assessments; it was probable that the  same proportion, would apply in 1951. If the  Organization was short of half a million dollars                        on a budget of $8,000,000 during 1950, then it  was probable that on a budget of $8,205,000 the  actual figure received would be approximately  $7,600,000. If the programme was based on an  amount of 8,205,000 as spending money, then,  on the basis of previous experience of failure to  receive full contributions, it would be necessary  to have a ceiling figure of $8,800,000.  [8] Sir Henry stressed, however, that the Gov-  ernment of the United Kingdom for its part could  not agree to such a large increase in the Unesco  budget from $8,000,000 in 1950 to $8,800,000 in
1951. Furthermore he pointed out that the rate  of expenditure during the first half of 1950 had  been in the region of $7,500,000 per annum. He  proposed that the Administrative Commission  advise the General Conference to pass a budget of  $8,200,000, but that expenditure should be kept  at $7.600.000. This would not be such a reduc-  tion as would cause a drastic curtailment in the  work of the Organization.
