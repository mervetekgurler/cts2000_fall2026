Cultural agreements and conventions now  in force and proposals likely to facilitate  the conclusion of bilateral or multilateral  cultural conventions [document 5C/OXR/17].  [35] Mr. GARCIN (External Relations Service)  reminded the Commission why the study set out  in document 5C/OXR/17 had been undertaken:  the Secretariat had felt that it was necessary to  collect the texts of approximately {wo hundred  cultural agreements between various Member or  non-member States, in order to make universally  available information of great diversity and also  of great importance for the extension and im-  provement of international cultural relations.  Unesco had been requested to prepare models of  contracts and clauses, in the hope of standardiz-  ing cultural relationships and initiating a real  international policy in that field. Mr. Garcin  pointed out that the Secretariat had managed to  make an almost complete collection of existing  agreements, and had studied their contents,  their application and, to some extent, the results  obtained. The study had shown that certain  tendencies existed, and had made it possible to          define a certain technique for international  cultural exchanges. Such exchanges were often  implemented generally by joint committees, com-  posed of representatives of the signatory States,  who were often called upon to do more than  merely apply the agreements, having also to  smooth out unforeseen difficulties. However,  these joint committees were composed of techni-  cians of civil servants who were not sufficiently  in contact with the nation’s intellectual circles,  and Unesco consequently thought that it would  be desirable to establish a very close relationship  between those bodies and Unesco’s National Com-  missions.  Mr. Garcin stressed the fact that document 5C/  OXR/17 was submitted to the Commission in  order that the General Conference might decide  whether it wished the study to be continued on  the lines indicated on page 4 of the document.  In conclusion, Mr. Garcin expressed the con-  viction that, in studying concrete evidence of the  broad trends of intellectual exchanges, Unesco  would perceive the strength and weaknesses in  that field and would be able to help in establish-  ing a world cultural policy.
