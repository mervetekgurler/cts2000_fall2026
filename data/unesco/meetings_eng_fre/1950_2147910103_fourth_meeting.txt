5C/ADM/18].  Mr. SMITH, Rapporteur, introduced the report of  the Committee on Member States’ contributions  and on possible amendments of the Financial  Regulations.  Although the Committee had not gone into the  question of disposal of surpluses and amendment  of financial regulations, paragraph 1 indicated  that the Committee did not foresee the possibility  of any budgetary surplus being surrendered to  reduce the contributions of Member States to the  budget of 1951.  In paragraph 2, the Committee expected that  the Administrative Commission would wish to  point out clearly that the serious financial situa-  tion of Unesco was due to the failure of certain  countries to pay their contributions.  In paragraphs 3, 4 and 5 the Committee had  stated that in its opinion expenditures for each  of the years 1950 and 1951 should be kept within  the income reasonably expected to be received in  respect of that year.  Paragraphs 3 and 4 referred more particularly  to the level of expenditure for 1950, which should  be kept within the estimated maximum income  of the Organization. The Committee felt it was  the duty of the General Conference to advise the  Director-General of the amount of income reason-  Dr. Tara Cuanp (India)  ably to be anticipated; but the Director-General  should be left free to modify the figure in the  light of the information at his disposal after the  session of the Conference. As the Director-  General, after discussion with the Executive  Board, had already made modifications to the pro-  gramme for 1950 to effect certain economies, the  Committee felt it desirable that any further neces-  sary economies be left to his discretion.  Paragraph 5, however, underlined that the  General Conference should assume responsibility  for deciding where economies were to be made,  and therefore recommended to the Programme  and Budget Commission that it divide the Pro-  gramme activites for 1951 into two sections:  firstly, priority activities the cost of which should  not exceed available funds; secondly, activities  of lesser urgency, to be undertaken if extra funds  became available.  The Committee felt that the scale of contribu-  tions of Member States would be an influencing  factor in the decisions to be taken by the General  Conference concerning the activities of the Organ-  ization and so had included paragraph 6 in its  report. In view of later discussions of the full  Commission, this paragraph was no longer ap-  plicable.
