La Présidente :  Excellences, Mesdames et Messieurs, nous aurons le plaisir de conclure les débats du Forum des dirigeants  par une conversation avec un lauréat du prix Nobel de la paix sur le thème « Paix et justice : le chemin d’accès mondial  de l’humanité vers un développement durable ». Notre invité d’honneur aujourd’hui est M. Lech Walesa, ancien  Président de la République de Pologne et prix Nobel de la paix. Nous nous réjouissons tout particulièrement de cette  occasion qui nous est donnée de l’entendre. Militant et ancien Président de la République de Pologne, S. E. M. Lech  Walesa a cofondé et présidé Solidarité, premier syndicat indépendant de la Pologne. Il a remporté un prix Nobel de la  paix en 1983 pour ses efforts visant à résoudre les problèmes de la nation par le biais de négociations et de la  coopération sans violence. S. E. M. Lech Walesa a commencé son combat pour la justice et les droits humains en 1968,  alors qu’il travaillait comme électricien au chantier naval de Gdansk. Il a organisé des grandes manifestations et des  grèves au cours des années 1970 et 1980, pour lesquelles il a été persécuté, placé sous surveillance, et arrêté à  plusieurs reprises. Il a joué un rôle fondamental dans les négociations politiques qui ont abouti à l'accord entre les  travailleurs grévistes et le gouvernement et, en 1989, il a conduit l’opposition au cours de la table ronde de négociations  avec le gouvernement, ce qui a pu permettre par la suite, et comme vous le savez, une transition politique pacifique en  Pologne. S. E. M. Walesa sera élu en 1990 et il restera Président de la République de Pologne jusqu’en 1995. Son  Excellence est aussi le fondateur de l’Institut Lech Walesa, une institution qui travaille pour le respect des droits humains  dans le monde. Et maintenant j’ai le grand plaisir de donner la parole à la Directrice générale qui tient à adresser  quelques mots de bienvenue à notre illustre invité. 9.2 Mr Walesa, your lifelong outstanding commitment to peace, non-violence and the resolution of conflict through  dialogue inspires respect and admiration. We live in turbulent times, when conflicts are mutating, with civilians on the  frontline, causing humanitarian tragedies and population movements. Societies are more connected and diverse than  ever – they are also more vulnerable, and intolerance is on the rise, along with violent extremism. Young people are  leaders of change in many societies but they carry also the heaviest burdens of poverty, unemployment and  discrimination, especially women. The world is globalizing quickly, but globalization is uneven and the planet faces  increasing pressure. Change is happening everywhere, and I believe every generation must face the challenging  question of how to build peace, how to strengthen the unity of humanity against the threats of violence, of fragmentation.  I believe we share the same conviction that peace does not flow only from treaties, however essential these are – peace  must be lived every day, by women and men, and taken forward by communities. This idea guides all UNESCO’s action  – in education, in the sciences, in culture, in communication. Sustaining peace means providing new skills for dialogue  and mutual understanding – it means defending humanity’s rich cultural heritage and diversity from attacks – it means  giving young people new opportunities to engage and shape the future according to their dreams. I believe, today more  than ever, we need leaders to take this vision forward, to make the promise of the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable  Development to leave no one behind real. This is our message today, and I am deeply grateful that you have accepted to  share your experience and thoughts with us. Thank you.

11. Ms Torres:   Thank you very much. Good morning, Nobel Peace Prize laureate, President Lech Walesa, it is a pleasure to be  here with you. Everyone here knows, and you and I am aware, that we have major challenges in the world: poverty,  social unrest, war, conflicts and humanitarian crises, and at the same time, we have the 2030 Agenda, which is bold and  ambitious. I think it would be very interesting to hear your thoughts around the areas in which UNESCO is working. How  can the “soft power” of UNESCO in the areas of culture, education and the right to free expression contribute to actually  bringing us forward to 2030? Please, let us hear your thoughts.
12.1 Mr Walesa (President of Poland) (interpretation from Polish):    Ladies and gentlemen, if I really were able to say what is important I would need to be able to talk about the  future, but in order to talk about the future I would need as much time as Fidel Castro used to take when he delivered  speeches – at least four hours in other words. You are not giving me that much time, however, so I will try to abbreviate  what I want to say, although that may result in some undesirable ambiguity – it may not be all crystal clear. Ladies and  gentlemen, our generation today, especially in Europe, has managed to eliminate a lot of divisions and schisms that  existed in the continent throughout most of the twentieth century. There was the division of the continent into two large  political blocs, which were also economic blocs, boundaries between nations were impermeable, and our generation, my  generation, managed to do away with a lot of these undesirable divisions. We brought an end to the era of territorial  conflict and we brought our civilization to a level where we no longer fit within the borders of nations. New technologies  require changes in societal structures, changes in programmes, and the new generation now must deal with this.
12.2 Your Organization, UNESCO, is absolutely crucial for the world as a whole, in order that the world may wake up  to and deal with the challenges standing before our generation. The issues are absolutely fundamental; things that were  positive in the former system were good for that level of civilization, but now we need to correct injustices and this can  only be done through the actions of organizations such as yours. The first question is a question to which I as a  revolutionary do not really have an answer – and the first question is: what are the new foundations that we need today  after the fall of the former philosophical foundation of our civilization? When I put that kind of fundamental question to a  room like this it immediately divides into two parts: some would like to build our future on the principles of liberty and free  markets with the law in the background just in case, but others say that it is not possible to build anything without  learning the lessons of the past, and the future must be built on the basis of values – and of course that is true, but the  values have to be agreed values because different groups in different nations have different values.
12.3 Therefore, I think one of the first tasks is to come to an agreement, a common agreement on what the founding  values of our future must be. If we manage to do this we can come up with ten secular commandments that we all would  recognize and all adhere to. If we manage to do this, then there would be a second question about what kind of  economic system we want to build on this new foundation – certainly not communism, but also certainly not capitalism as  it exists today. That worked very well in the past, but it needs to be something else. The third question is about what kind  of effective democracy we need to have today – we no longer manage to control demagoguery, populism and the  corruption of politicians, I mean we have people lying to the nation and winning elections. Yet another question would be:  what should the left today look like? What are the left and the right really today? These were concepts that had meaning
63  in the past but they are no longer appropriate today. Let me just say that we have an opportunity today that no former  generation has had, and we must introduce these changes, these adaptations that I have mentioned.
13. Ms Torres:   You talk about the economic system. We know that inequality is increasing in many countries and has become  one of the main reasons for conflict, social unrest, strikes and wars. You talk about democracy. We know that that  democracy is being undermined in many countries. You have led a civil society trade union and dominated protest for  peace and justice. If you go back to your first point of creating new rules for the world order and you take that inequality  and the undermining of democracy into account, what would be your ethics, your rules for the new world order?
14.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   Ladies and gentlemen, our problem consists in the fact that that former period, that former era has resulted in  those differences, those inequalities in all domains. At that time, we engaged in confrontation among nations and in  political terms were often dishonest. Today, despite the fact that we have these tremendous opportunities before us, the  past weighs upon us and so we need to come to an agreement on a number of points so that we can manage gradually  to eliminate the divisions, the inequalities and the injustices that remain with us as the heritage of the past. Once again,  that is why an organization like yours is so absolutely necessary today and has to work on these problems. However,  when we try to determine what is really needed we must take advantage of meetings like this in order to make the right  diagnosis and set out an orientation for the future. If we do not manage to do this, the lack of solutions and proposals and  programmes will result in these demons from the past waking up, with the street clamouring for solutions that the elites  cannot provide. And we cannot provide these panaceas easily because we all come from different backgrounds, different  profiles. At meetings like this one we need to discuss how we can resolve these points that I have referred to. We need  to come to a position of common management, and then we can build something.
14.2 The current situation is normal at the present time, under the present circumstances; we see this in Europe, we  open up the borders, we want to build peace, we want to ensure development and growth for all and sundry and that is  all well and good, but not on the basis of the old principles, the old structures. We need a major change, a major  transformation. Through to the end of the twentieth century we were building things gradually and it worked quite well for  a while, but then at the end of the century we were confronted with a revolutionary transformation, with the Internet,  computers, people connecting with one another very quickly. We need understanding but we also need different  solutions, and in order gradually to reach this I come back once again to your Organization and meetings such as this  one – these issues must be discussed in order that we come to some common understanding as to the challenges, as to  the opportunities, and as to the difficulties and injustices that may arise along the way and how to eliminate them. There  is no other road.
15. Ms Torres:   May I follow up on a couple of these points? I tend to think that for freedom of expression, for it to have the value  that we want to have for democracy and education, within the framework of democracy, peace and justice, we need an  organized civil society. May we hear your views, when you look at the world around you? I think many of us see what we  think is a weakening of organized civil society – it may be easy to mobilize thousands and thousands in organized  protests, but we have a shortage of organizations. With your background from the 1970s, building up an organization to  fight injustice, how do you see the world today and what needs to be done in order to support the freedom of expression  and democracy that we want?
16.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):  Ladies and gentlemen, almost all subjects of such kind need to be well positioned in time and space. I am a  practitioner; I was a worker for 25 years and a trade unionist for ten years, and then President for five years. I always  wanted to be the best at whatever I did in a very honest and open and frank way. What have I observed through that  experience? Everyone talks about freedom of expression, about liberty, but we forget about democracy because these  concepts are made up of certain elements and I never managed to measure or to quantify democracy. At certain  meetings people tell me “we have democracy in this country”, but when you observe it, it is not really there. So, I came  up with a simple equation for democracy – I took the whole problem and divided it up into three parts. In my democratic  model, one third is the Constitution and the laws prevailing in a given nation; another one third is the behaviour of the  society within this legal framework – the elections, the civil society action, and so on, and one third is a function of the  thickness of the social cheque-book if you will, the overall social richness.
16.2 Now, if we want to correct a democracy, or have doubts about a democracy, we have to ask ourselves where we  are not happy, in which domain: is it because the limits are too great, is it because people do not really participate, do not  vote, do not join civil society organizations? Or is it because they are not going to fight for freedom because they  consider themselves left aside and that the wealth has not been distributed? That would be a first comment. The second  comment to add is that for people who live on reservations – and there are still such people – freedom and democracy  means jumping over some fence, and to appreciate freedom, but that is not the type of freedom that many of us really  want. Freedom for us at the time in Poland was for us to achieve a level of freedom that was available in France at the  time. I once asked a French citizen: when do you really feel free in a democratic State? Have you thought about this in a  while? And he said: when I was born, it should have been written into my birth certificate that I would be President of the  nation for seven minutes; if you divide the number of people by a lifetime it comes out at seven minutes per citizen, so I  could be President for seven minutes.
16.3 So, let us be careful about these concepts of democracy and freedom, which can mean different things under  different circumstances and need to be managed in a different way. The divisions have set us apart to such a great  extent that we must come back together and your Organization needs to go in that direction of equalization, of bringing
64  people to a common denominator and listening to and understanding people coming from different backgrounds. People  have so many differences in terms of civilization, culture and religion, that it is difficult for us to understand one another,  but as I said earlier, I think that in this relay race of generations we have the greatest opportunity; we have received the  baton from the previous generation, so let us sit down, draw the right conclusions and then start to build and propose  solutions that are appropriate for this era. But we are still stuck in the past, we do not keep up with the developments of  civilization and technology and that is why we have difficulties.
17. Ms Torres:   Very interesting. I think there are a lot of people who will say that we need international institutions, the United  Nations institutions, more than ever before. We live, as you say, in a divided world; fragmentation is creating major  conflicts. Now, how are we going to go forward? Can you give us some ideas about action points to actually be able to  create that unity which you talk about that is really important, while we also try to build greater support for international  institution building? 18.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   Ladies and gentlemen, when some people look at the lack of solutions, at poor work being done by certain  institutions, they immediately want to dissolve them, do away with them, exit from them, and they think that that will  improve things. But I would look at it differently. I would say that institutions like this one and our overall multilateral  system should not be destroyed or done away with until we have a different solution. It is a different thing to renew, to  reform and to revolutionize. Which way is best is different. Even in my revolution I never destroyed something before I  could imagine what could replace it. I did not manage to take it through to the end because my combat was for freedom  and democracy. I was programmed to bring Poland to freedom and democracy and then to hand over to the victors, but I  did not observe at the time that my society was not prepared for freedom and democracy because they had had no  opportunity to prepare for this and therefore we have problems today in my country.
18.2 Poland, because of its geographical situation, has had many conflictual situations, being positioned between the  frying pan and the fire, with Germany on one side and Russia on the other. In the past, those nations have been quite  militaristic and they have visited one another from time to time, and when they did so they crossed our territory and then  undermined our system, our order. I felt that democracy would resolve things better than the former order, but I was not  quite right – we did not take it right through to the end. So, after this tremendous victory of Solidarity, of the independent  trade union movement, we had the reunification of Germany, the elimination of borders, the introduction of a common  currency and all of that was well and good, it was wonderful, but we forgot where our foundations were. At one point,  Europe was Christian from one end to the other and that ideology was thrown out, and then there was the Communist  ideology in the East, and we did away with that foundation, and today we do not have a foundation, and you cannot build  a house without a foundation. We need to come to a common understanding on this foundation; it can be a mini- constitution of existing values, but we have trouble doing this because everyone has a different point of view. So once  again, we should take advantage of this Organization and meetings like this one to bring us to a common agreement on  what should be rejected, what should be kept, and what should be added. The era of division has come to an end and  we are now in an era of computerization, the Internet and globalization, and we are sort of in a transition period; I refer to  this as the era of the word. I mean like in the Bible story – “in the beginning was the word”; but new words need to be  hammered out, need to be built up as a foundation for this era that requires from us completely new programmes. That is  the task for all of us if we hope to be able to live in peace and prosperity around the world.

20.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   Well, we have already said that all of this needs to be appropriately positioned, I mean “solidarity” and all of  these wonderful words – they mean different things depending on the level of development in a given society, where the  expectations of people are different. As I already mentioned, for people living in reservations, people who are not free,  who are fighting for freedom, and for people who have that freedom, the concepts are not exactly the same. These are  divisions, differences that remain as heavy baggage from the past organization of the world. All of this has resulted in a  lot of positive outcomes, but today the work and the programmes – all of this needs to be corrected and transformed, and  once again, it can only be done through conversations like this one, on the basis of complete mutual respect and an  identification of what is positive and what is negative. We are living in a period where one concept of the world has really  come to an end. I do not have answers to all the questions; if I did, I would have another Nobel Prize, which I have not  received yet and I do not think I will. This world  is so beautiful, and can be even more beautiful, but we have to observe the differences remaining from the previous  system of organization of the world and let us gradually eliminate those differences.
20.2 My revolution made it possible to start to introduce some order into the European continent – not globalization  but continentalization if you will. If we manage to improve that, then we can participate in globalization as well, and those  concepts are neither positive nor negative per se – it all depends on what kind of democracy, what kind of economic  system we write in to this new way of perceiving the world. Yes, of course, we can destroy things, but if we destroy an  organization like this one, what will be next? That would simply result in chaos and anarchy, so do not allow anyone to  destroy you. But you have to be aware of the fact that you may have good ideas and programmes, but there may  sometimes be poor implementation, because the people out there do not always understand what is being done and they  have to be shown that what is being done is positive. You have to learn to speak to people with different profiles, different
65  backgrounds and different levels of development. I cannot say something more concrete because I am not at that level. I  am at the level of appealing to you to try to build an even more beautiful world.
21. Ms Torres:   Very good. Now the goals are there, in plural, as part of the 2030 Agenda. The goals concern justice, reducing  inequality, education and peace, among others. 22. Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   Ladies and gentlemen, I have mentioned some of those points, first of all the foundations and then a new  perception of what the left and the right mean and then guaranteeing the safeguarding of democracy – those are the  main points.
23. Ms Torres:   How do we get everyone included? Including everyone is going to be critical here.
24.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   We have brought the world to a situation of so much difference, so much injustice that it will not be easy to get  beyond that. So, let us draw the conclusions by listening to the people. I am often invited to places where there are mass  demonstrations and when you listen to what people are clamouring for, you can believe in the possibility of designing  good solutions and programmes. About 10% of humanity owns 90% of the world’s wealth. The old system has brought  us to this. I do not want to take anything away from anyone – let them have it – but it has to work for us. For them to  hang it on their wall and enjoy it is not good enough. It has to work for everyone; if the wealth is concealed, that does not  really work for the people, so the people will start to burn the palaces and that is understandable.
24.2 If you want to own and possess even more, you must take what you have today and lend it to the people to  create economic growth and development, reduce unemployment and give work back to the owners of the production –  they are the ones who know best how to work in such a way that the strikers will no longer protest. If you use all of the  wealth that you have to generate work then you will in fact get richer, so you will earn from that and so will the  unemployed. It is not true that there is no work to be done. There is not enough work for people in the old system, that  system of rivalry, the rat race among nations. Yes, we have lost places of work for a lot of people, people who are less  qualified, so we have these high levels of unemployment. If we want to do business today, however, it has to be done by  taking those who have been left by the wayside and create work for them. Then they will be happy, they will earn a  decent living and you, the elites, will be happy as well, because it will be a more peaceful and secure world. That is  possible, but you have seriously to look at the challenges of the day and organize and use resources in such a way that  they really meet the challenges.
25. Ms Torres:   When you were with your comrades at Solidarity, you were a shining light to trade unions and civil society  organizations all around the world. You talked a little bit about what that led to – your struggles in Poland, in Europe, with  the East-West divide – but it also was a shining light to many trade unions and many struggles in the South. Looking at  what you achieved, how you built up the trade union movement, and seeing this as a genuine expression and  organization fighting for democracy, it would be interesting for us to hear some more about how you built that up and  what you consider were the critical parts of the organization that enabled you to succeed. Was it the organization? Was it  the leadership? Please enlighten us.
26.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):    Well, some older people remember and they pass on the information to the younger generation so that we try.  As a practitioner, I noticed that the system in which we were building, the division of Europe into two blocs, was an  obstacle to development, and that needed to be changed. Except that in my country there were 200,000 Soviet soldiers  who were controlling us and around the borders of Poland there were one million Soviet soldiers stationed to control us,  and there were silos with nuclear weapons. So, the Communist Party would say: “you have no chance, you in the  opposition, don't you see how much strength we have?” So we got in touch with people in the West and we asked  whether there was any chance, any opportunity to bring down that system which was such an obstacle to development  and such a source of conflict. I can tell you that none of the great people of the time, the presidents, the prime ministers,  even the kings gave us no chance whatsoever to bring Soviet Communism to its knees, and it seemed that it would last  for many generations to come as they would say that only nuclear war could change the prevailing order in the world.  Those were the interests and there was no way to overcome that. Together with some colleagues, however, we thought  that there must be some way to do it. The Communists had a very simple philosophy: they did not allow us to organize  ourselves.  Whenever we created a group, they would break it up, so for a long time we did not get to the point where we  could organize.
26.2 Then we had the good fortune that the second millennium of Christianity was coming to an end. Poland is a  country of faith and we all prayed together, and thanks to our prayers at that end of the second millennium of Christianity,  we were granted a Polish Pope. A year after he was elected, he came to Poland, and all eyes were turned on Poland.  What was going on in this Communist State with a Polish Pope? The entire nation was meeting with his Holiness in  different places and under different circumstances, and the Pope said “change the face of the Earth and do not be afraid”.  Then we believed. We counted how many we were, and we thought: “it can be done”. For 20 years I had tried to  organize groups in the struggle – sometimes there were just ten of us, because the others did not believe that there was  any chance, they were afraid – but after the election of his Holiness, the Polish Pope, we managed to attract ten million  people to our trade union. I had no extra money, I was not wiser than in the past, I did not do anything different, but the
66  people had woken up. I am not exaggerating – I am not saying that this Holiness fomented the revolution, but he did  make it possible for us to believe in ourselves, to understand that with a strong spirit, all coming together, we could win  against the Communist regime. And we did. So, what is the lesson for today? For our materialistic intellectual approach  to life, we also need to combine this with a spirit in the systems that we are building – it is not enough to have the  building and the material, we need a common spirit and if we can combine that with our potential then there is no  difficulty that we cannot overcome, no barrier that cannot be removed. At the time I was told: “listen, you have a family,  children, you have no chance, don't do this, you're just bringing problems on your head”; but I was convinced that  something could be done and there was this spirit of surrender, it is too much, we cannot overcome things, but I can tell  you that then we were really in danger, we were confronted with these thousands of soldiers that I mentioned, and yet it  was possible to win through combining our efforts and combining our spirit.
26.3 Today we have open borders, we can talk to one another, and we are not subject to threats. Should we really  not be in a position to deal with the problems confronting us? No, those are minor problems compared to what we were  confronted with at the time. But we have to come to an agreement on what needs to be built and how to build it. All we  need is mutual respect and mutual understanding. Are we really to fail in that? I have told you the world is beautiful. I  travel a lot these days. I can tell you that the world is beautiful, except for here and there where it is badly managed and  poorly governed. With the right solutions, that can be overcome. You mentioned the issue of trade unionism. World trade  unions were, are and will be necessary. At that time, I could not fight communism otherwise than on the basis of places  of work, of factories, because that is where there were large groups of people. My fight was really through “bread to  freedom”, when it really should be the other way around, through “freedom to bread”, but I could not act that way  because the Communists would have destroyed me very quickly. So, it was through the massive protests and the  massive membership of the trade unions, and the belief and the faith and the spirit, that we reached that point. Today, it  is less dangerous, it is safer. Once again, if you really want to solve the problems, you will solve the problems.
27. Ms Torres:   You talked about the importance of having spirit, the spirit of leading a democratic struggle. Here we have this  most amazing United Nations institution focusing on freedom of speech, education and science, and we have, as you  said,  relatively open borders. We have a totally different starting point in many ways to work with than what you had in  the 1970s. How do you think, in terms of action points, that this can be taken forward. You have talked a lot about  creating unity foundations. Can you elaborate a little more on that?
28.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   Ladies and gentlemen, once again we must really get a sense of why things are the way they are. At the end of  the twentieth century, on a massive scale, all of us believed there was a god – different gods from different faiths – but I  think that sort of kept us together. However, that is something that we rejected, and then we started to be afraid of our  neighbours and that they would attack us and there would be military action or war. That is something of the past as well.  Today, we have no good recipes, nothing that would be common to all of us, that would be a common reflection – we do  not have that foundation I have referred to in the past. We must work that out as quickly as possible, come up with the  many constitutions that I referred to and the ten secular commandments that would be our foundation.
28.2 Let us choose: should it be freedom-based development with everyone around the world free to do whatever  they want, with the law not regulating everything, with a completely free market? Well, after that kind of concept each of  us would have to be supervised or controlled by police officers to make sure that we stay honest. Rather let us base  things on values, on decency, on respect for law and order. Letters educate people with a sense of conscience, with a  sense of values – that would be less expensive, it would be more effective but there have to be rules and laws that would  lead us to really believe in those principles. That, I think, is the choice – some people want it this way; some people want  it that way, but if we do not come together on this, it will result in disaster and chaos. Your Organization will fall apart  completely. This will be done by irresponsible power-hungry people. It is really 50-50 – some people want to go this way;  some people want to go that way and this could just end up in mutual murder. Instead, bring people together in meetings  like this, with ideas being expressed, put on the table, agreed to, and that will result in a programme implemented by  people who enjoy common thinking. After all of the injustice that we have experienced it is difficult to be in a different  situation. You mentioned the term leadership. Well even today if some holy person were to come to the tribune you  would not listen to them because we are coming out of an era of corruption, of trickery, of populism, and this kind of  organization, this kind of meeting that we can correct the action, be more effective in our convincing of others and that  would start a genuine era of globalization. Today globalization is content free, it is structure free, it is idea free. We are  also intelligent, we all can communicate, we have computers. However, organizations like this have to propose solutions  quickly that can work for everyone. I believe that this is possible and I am happy to participate in building such solutions.
29. Ms Torres:   We are moving towards the conclusion of our meeting. You have talked a lot about the need for us to unite, the  need to build a foundation, the need to build a new set of ethical rules and values and the important role that UNESCO  will have in shaping that and, I presume, also as a basis for education, media, freedom of speech and so forth. Do you  have any last points that you would like to communicate to the audience and to the Director-General before we conclude?
30.1 Mr Walesa (interpretation from Polish):   Let me repeat once again that this Organization and its programme, as far as I know, has all of the ingredients  that the world needs. You are not that good, however, at convincing people. You have to think in terms of differences of  mentality, differences in levels of development, and take into consideration people who think differently, and you need to  try to convince them that these programmes are the right ones for the world. It is not just UNESCO; almost all multilateral  organizations have lost the ability to really believe in what they are pronouncing in order to convince others. Look what is  happening in politics, even in France. I know the French President, a wonderful person, but he managed to get away
67  from the party organizations. Today you cannot govern properly without the right managers and implementers. It is  wonderful that democracy like that can work and someone can come up like a jack-in-the-box.
30.2 But we have to go back. Yes, everybody says that parties are terrible and that they have terrible programmes,  and that they are all corrupt and unjust, but nevertheless some sort of organization is required. Everything is falling apart  everywhere. We are coming out of this old era and old programmes, and there are new challenges, new opportunities  and new technologies that give us these new opportunities. However, the way that we deal with them is not suited to the  times. Once again, I can tell you that this is a generation that has an extraordinary opportunity to build a third millennium,  the masses, the people in the street are clamouring for some system that would do away with the existing imperfections  and divisions. So, come together and make the diagnosis; very often the programmes are right, but must be more  effective. Sometimes the programmes are right but you are not convincing people that this is the right thing to be done,  and when that is corrected it will be said about this generation: not only did they want something beautiful, not only did  they manage to do away with the errors of the past, but they managed to bring about the wise building of the future. We  have that opportunity – if you really want it, the world will be beautiful.
