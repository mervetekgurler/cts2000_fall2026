At the request of Mr. CASTRO LEAL (Mexico), the SECRETARYexplained that, while the Official and External Relations Commission was considering the list of non-governmental organizations ad- mitted that year to consultative status, the French delegation had called for reconstruction of the application of the International Federation of Children’s Communities, which had not been granted. He stated there- fore that it would be placed on the Agenda for Monday, 26 September. The Chairman then announced that the Finance Committee would report to the Executive Board, at 2.39 that afternoon, on the possible effects of the revaluation of national currencies on the Bud- get, and that the Director-General would also give thought to that matter and would report to the joint meeting of the Official and External Rela- tions Commission and of the Programme and Budget Commission. Work of the Programme and Budget Commission and of its Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee. Mr. CAIN (France) said that the Conference was now at the beginning of its second week, and that the work of the Programme and Budget Commis- sion was not progressing. He wondered whether the General Committee could not organize the work of that Commission in advance. It would seem that last week, and due to the procedure laid down for the Commission, an unavoidable amount of time was lost and, despite the exceptional authority of the Chairman, other questions raised. If a calendar of work were laid down, and the Chairman given powers that no one could contest to organize the discussion, Mr. Cain thought that progress would be quicker, and he made this a formal proposition to the Committee. The CHAIRMAN said that the question was a difficult one. On the one hand, it was felt they were not progressing sufficiently rapidly, and on Dr. E. RonaLD WALKER (Australia) the other there was a feeling in some delegations that insufficient time was allowed for going into the substance of questions on the Agenda, and that there had not been a real discussion of some of the chapters of the Budget. Mr. ALLEN (United States of America), com- menting on the French delegation’s proposal, thought it somewhat dangerous, as a precedent, to place the power of controlling the debate in the hands of the Chairman. They were extremely fortunate in the present Chairman of the Pro- gramme and Budget Commission, but a precedent should not be created. He rather thought that the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee would be a better group to be given the responsibility for making arrangements to expedite the calendar of work. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) thought the question raised by the French delegation was obviously one that required consideration, but the really urgent matter was, as he had urged at a previous meeting of the General Committee, to tighten up the present procedure in discussion of            the items on the Agenda of the Programme and Budget Commission. He had also suggested that it would be very helpful if, during the week- end just passed, the Director-General would have time to consult other delegations and perhaps make suggestions of a practical nature. The DIRECTOR-GENERAL said that he was unable to make any suggestions, as he considered the question was one which should be settled by the Programme and Budget Commission, and the Chairman of that Commission might be asked his views. There was no information in the General Committee concerning the rhythm of work of the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee, and he thought it would be premature for him to make any suggestions with regard to procedural ques- tions. Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil), Chairman of the Programme and Budget Commission, proposed expanding the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub- Committee to, say, twelve members, to have a full record of its discussion and a room large enough to permit of delegations attending the debate. He thought also that the character of the discussions in the Programme and Budget Commission might be changed, considering that, in any parliament with a similar Commission, the items would not be discussed in such detail. A certain margin of error should be allowed for, and a thousand dol- lars here or there which could not be accounted for could not be regarded too seriously. The Sub- Committee would have powers to discuss minutiae. It was also felt that Unesco’s work should take place in the open. In Beirut and Mexico City, it had been carried out under conditions of secrecy, and sometmes without room for delegations to get in at all. Also, if one tried to find traces of the decisions taken at those meetings, it was impossible to reconstitute them. At those meetings, the impression had been that a very small number of the Budget Sub-Commission were taking the vital decisions, and that some of the other people present were there as spectators. It was undesirable that this situation should recur. Summing up, Professor Carneiro thought that the main Commission should go through the pro- gramme attentively, and let the Drafting and Co- ordination Sub-Committee deal with budgetary details. It should be laid down definitely that there be more members of the Sub-Committee, with full records of its discussion, which should be of an open and frank nature. The CHAIRMAN thought that the room atpre- sent used by the Executive Board for its meetings might be suitable for the proposed enlarged Sub- Committee. He understood that Professor Car- neiro’s proposal was that the General Committee recommend to the Programme and Budget Com- mission that its Drafting and Co-ordination Sub- Committee be enlarged to some ten members, and that the powers and functions of that Sub- Committee be increased so that it could consider any budgetary points it thought necessary, but that it should work mainly in the light of the decisions and points raised in the main Commission. He further suggested that any delegation that felt strongly on a particular point might be allowed to present its views to the Drafting and Co-ordination Sub-Committee without sitting as a member, and without generalizing the debate. Shafik GHORBAL Bey (Egypt) approved Pro- fessor Carneiro’s proposal, and also his suggestion that the Sub-Committee be increased to twelve members. Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) did not think the question of whether the Sub-Committee should be increased to ten or twelve was signifi- cant. The important thing was that there should be an exchange of ideas and that the members could “talk across the table”. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) approved of Professor Carneiro’s proposal and would prefer twelve members for the enlarged Sub-Committee. Professor CARNEIRO (Brazil), Chairman of the Programme and Budget Commission, said he would inform the Commission that, whatever the views of its Sub-Committee, the final decision rested with the Commission itself. The Chairman thought it would be useful if the Sub-Committee could report progres- sively, and a report on the whole subject might be desirable. Professor Carneiro’s proposal was adopted. Organization of the General Discussion (con- tinuation). The CHAIRMAN said that agreement had been reached on the groups of speakers. The main lines of each evening's debate would be discussion by three speakers, each of whom would speak for not more than twenty minutes. A discussion would then follow in which the last of the speakers would talk for fifteen minutes, or less. The time- limits were to be strictly observed, and the meeting limited to two and a half hours. It would be left to the discretion of the speakers whether they would make their remarks from improvised notes on what had already been said, or whether they would read a prepared statement or make a pre- pared speech. It would be noted that the opening speakers represented different points of view from different         parts of the world, and there would be also at least one speaker whose mother-tongue was French. The printed texts of all speeches would be available later. With regard to the chairmanship of the dis- cussions, it should be remembered that these were plenary sessions of the Conference, and from that point of view the discussions should be presided over by the President and Vice-Presidents of the Conference. The Chairman proposed to take the chair at the first meeting and to invite two of the Vice-Presidents to preside over the others. Three of the Vice-Presidents were speaking in the general discussion and, as Shafik Ghorbal Bey had already taken the chair in other plenary meetings, that left Mr. Gonzalo Zaldumbide, Count Jacini and Mr. Hardman. The DIRECTOR-GENERAL thanked the Chair- man for the work he had carried out. The CHAIRMAN mentioned that the Sub-Com- mittee of Seven was anxious for the Director- General to give an address; but the latter did not wish to speak, though, if need be, he would be prepared to take part in the discussion. The Chairman was sorry for that decision, for an address by the Director-General would have been bound to impress the general public. Mr. HARDMAN (United Kingdom) appreciated the Chairman’s suggestion that he should take the chair at one of the discussions, but he declined with thanks. International Federation of Children’s Com- munities (continued). The CHAIRMAN understood that the Executive Board had not approved a previous request from the International Federation of Children’s Com- munities to be granted consultative status. The Official and External Relations Commission desired the Conference to request the Executive Board to consider the matter. The nominal procedure would be for the Conference to make that request after receiving the Report of the Official and External Relations Commission; but, with a view to saving time, the question now arose whether the General Committee, in anticipation of the adoption of the Official and External Relations Commission’s Report by a plenary meeting of the Conference. might already transmit the request to the Board which would then possibly have an opportunity of examining it in the course of the session. Sir Sarvepalli RADHAKRISHNAN (India), Chair- man of the Executive Board, asked if there were any new facts which had not been brought to the attention of the Executive Board. Mr. ALLEN (United States of America) observed that, not being a member of the Executive Board, he had not been, in other cases, averse to making recommendations contrary to those of the Board on occasion; he wished at that moment to say that he thought the General Committee and the General Conference should be very careful not to overrule the findings of the Executive Board in a matter which required such detailed information. He himself would be reluctant to take a decision contrary to the Executive Board's in the matter. The CHAIRMAN assumed that Mr. Allen did not object to the General Committee's inviting the Executive Board to re-examine the question, but in no wayto exert pressure on the Board. Agreed.
