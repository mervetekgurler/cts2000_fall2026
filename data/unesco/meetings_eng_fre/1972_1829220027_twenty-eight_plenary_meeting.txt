(4.1) Mr. GARRIGUES (Spain) (Translated from the Spanish):  I will not repeat the arguments already developed by other delegations in favour of this draft  resolution. I wish to point out that in any case document 17 C/DR/PLEN/12 ; must be amended  where it refers to the Draft Medium-term Outline Plan, and this term, which is no longer valid,  replaced by "Draft Medium-term Objectives’. The Secretariat may take note of this slight but  important amendment, since it corresponds to what has already been agreed.
(4,2) Concerning the substance of this draft resolution, there is a French saying that advises us  not to live beyond our means, This is a very sound guiding principle which, by preventing waste,  both in private and public expenditure, has accounted for the spectacular progress of the western  economy in the last three centuries. This has been a development that has had no development  plan but has in fact been governed by a criterion just as rational as a plan, Nowadays econo-  mists tell us that it is just as bad to live below one's means as beyond them, Excessive saving  is not only morally harmful but also economically ruinous; and no one would wish this for-Unesco.
(4. 3) Nevertheless, it is fair to recognize that the resources available to an intergovernmental  organization are much more difficult to determine than those available to any other. economic  enterprise, public or private, In effect Unesco's economy is like that of a son of a well-to-do  family who has no resources other than those provided by his parents - its Member States, Their  economic possibilities, however, are so ‘heterogeneous that the available resources are very  difficult to predict. To begin with, in my view there are neither rich nor poor’ countries, but  some that produce more and others less; and the richer économies, which are the most delicate-  ly balanced and the most denendent on the world market economy, are precisely the most vul-  nerable, 1 fully understand, therefore, that the major contributors to the Organization have dif-  ficulty in contracting commitments for periods longer than the biennium of our Programme and  -921   Budget. I believe, however, that these justifiable apprehensions are fully covered by document
17 C/DR/PLEN/12, since the figures mentioned are always given as an indication and a working  hypothesis, In short, this means that the General Conference always retains the power; when  adopting its programme every two years, to decide what will be the real growth rate; since, as  we all know, the States are bound by the programme and not by any kind of plan,
(4. 4) Consequently I believe our draft resolution is perfectly acceptable and should be accepted  for what it is, namely, an attempt to provide a reasonable criterion by which to establish the  balance that should always exist in any adrninistration between means and ends, That is why my  delegation has the honour to sponsor this draft resolution.

(10.1) Mr, DELL'ORO MAINI (Argentina) (Translation from the Spanish):  Argentina supports the draft resolution contained in document 17 C/DR/PLEN/12 and does  not need to add anything to the arguments in its favour already put forward by the other co-sponsors.  My present concern is to review some of the opinions expresaed in the discussion that prompted  me to consider the arguments advanced against it, |
(10, 2) It has been said that the draft contravenes certain express Constitutional provisions, so  that, if we are to approve it, we must first amend the Constitution. I have examined this idea  with close attention and found it necessary to ascertain the précise scope of our powers. Thus  I have ascertained that the Conference is bound to decide the future policy of the Organization,  The fact that the Conference adopts a budget for the following biennium and imposes on States a  definite legal obligation to pay contributions naturally indicates a fiscal provision having its own  limit and motive, This draft imposes no legal obligation on States; on the contrary, as has been  repeatedly pointed out, it is only intended as a guide and corresponds to the obligation that de-  volves on the Conference to determine the future policy of the Organization, In reality, the bud-  getary components are an essential part of any forecast and it would be impossible to prepare a  medium-term plan without at the same time making an estimate of tlie resources required, The  very practice of setting a maximum budgetary ceiling thdicates that, in formulating the programme,  the Conference bears in mind a level of expenditure not to be exceeded; and if this practice is to  be applied to each biennial programme and budget, it is also necessary to eatablish indicative  figures for the budgetary elemierits of a medium-term plan, A growth rate is both an indication  and a ceiling, In this regard it has also been said that the resolution on the general discussion  put to the vote yesterday contains an estimate of necessary resources and real growth rates, In  my view, this is a statement of principle that should be embodied in a concrete resolution for one  or two specific biennia, It is not sufficient for the Secretary or the Director-General to estab-  lish a real growth rate as an indication, What is a real growth rate? Is it an increase in re-  sources required by financial, monetary ‘or economic, changes, or, in addition to these factors  that raise the budget every biennium, does it embrace programme growth, the increase in acti-  vities included in the Programme? The question is to establish a precise budgetary guide ‘for  the natural and dynamic growth of the Programme,
(10.3) It has also been said that the Organization has now attained its full growth, that it is not  possible to anticipate'continuous growth, because growth must end some time, ‘ Growth comes  to an end for a house, a tree, a man, I cannot conceive of Unesco as frozen, with a completed  programme and conferences meeting solely to vote expenditure for conservation and maintenance  and to fill in the gaps left by the termination, reduction or suppression of activities. in the Pro-  gramme. Our activity is dynamic by nature. Anything associated with cultural, intellectual or  spiritual matters is a force constantly seeking perfection, Consequently it is inconceivable that  no provision should be made for growth within minimum budgetary limits. For this reason the  Board has requested the Conference to set a growth rate. I believe this is absolutely necessary  for effective concrete programme planning; and I would stress that it is not an encouragement  to spend but rather a channel and at the same time a restraint, The need to chañnel‘the dyna-  mism of Unesco imposes the obligation to limit it; and the establishment of growth rates has the  advantage of giving a guide for the estimates to be submitted to later ‘sessions of the Conference.
(10.4) In conclusion, I wish to refer to certain concrete proposals, The representative of the  Netherlands proposed to leave out the word "useful" in the fourth paragraph and to insert words  in the fifth paragraph to take account of the discussions held by various bodies of the Conference.  I see no great disadvantage in incorporating these amendments, The delegate of Spain referred  to the change in the title of the Draft Medium-term Outline Plan for 1975-1980, The resolution  voted yesterday amended this title for the following financial period, since for the 1976 Confer-  ence that resolution requests the Executive Board to provide the Director-General with the neces-  sary guidelines to prepare a Medium-term Outline Plan, namely a C/4, Consequently the amend-  ment to the resolution contained in 17 C/DR/PLEN/12 should not apply to both periods, since  ‘925

31,2 Mr, President, in document 17 C/12 and Addendum there will be found a factual ac-  count of the steps taken to implement 18 C/Resolution 8, together with the decisions taken  by the Executive Board at its 87th, 89th and 90th sessions regarding Unesco's relations with  the 287 international non-governmental organizations which had official relations with Unesco  in 1971, The distribution of these was as follows: 31 in category A, 150 in category B and
106 in category C,
31,3 According to the request of the General Conference at its sixteenth session, the  Director-General sent a circular letter on 14 January 1971 to the aforementioned 287 non-  governmental organizations, At the 87th session of the Executive Board the Director-  General presented an interim report which contained the replies from 91 non-governmental  organizations: 18 in category A, 61 in category B and 12 in category C. After studying the  report of the Director-General, the Executive Board decided to invite him to write again to  the 196 non-governmental organizations which had not yet replied, seeking their co-operation  in making replies available by 15 July 1971, Furthermore, the Executive Board decided to  entrust its Committee on International Non-Governmental Organizations, under the distin-  guished charmanship of H, E, Mr, Etemadi, with the task of making a critical analysis of  the information received and compiled by the Director-General in his final report, with the  object of making recommendations to the Executive Board in respect of the action to be taken  by the Board in accordance with paragraph 10 of 16 C/Resolution 8, as well as of any further  action in respect of paragraph 9. Between 1971 and 1972 the Committee of the Board held
29 meetings, 19 in September-October 1971, 6 in June-Jyly 1972 and 4 in October 1972,
31,4 According to the decision of the Executive Board at its 87th session, on 11 June 1971,  the Director-General sent his second circular letter to the 196 non-governmental organiza-  tions which had not replied to his first communication, At the 88th session of the Board the  Director-General presented a second report containing replies from 82 non-governmental  organizations, During the 88th session of the Board the Committee drew the Board's atten-
28  tion to the following facts; firstly, that while it was relatively easy to interpret the first re-  quirement of 16 C/Resolution 8, paragraph 9 and 10, and ascertain that non-governmental  organizations in the Republic of South Africa, Southern Rhodesia or the Portuguese-dominated  African territories were not practising racial discrimination, it was most difficult to deter-  mine whether or not it had been established that branches in South Africa were not co-  operating with the policy of apartheid; secondly, the work of the Committee was rendered  difficult due to the fact that the main source, if not the only source, of information was the  replies given by the international non-governmental organizations themselves and that this  information was often, in spite of goodwill, very meagre; thirdly, additional difficulties  were caused by the fact that the non-governmental organizations were called on to provide  satisfactory proof of non-practice of racial discrimination and non-co-operation with apartheid  by their branches; fourthly, it was regretted that no guidelines had been suggested for the  interpretation of co-operation in a situation where apartheid was the law of the country and  where this law was being vigorously applied,
31,5 It was, Mr, President, at the 88th session that the Board decided to adopt the criteria  enumerated in paragraph 4, Annex IV, page 2 of document 17 C/12 for determining the non-  co-operation of non-governmental organizations in racial discrimination or segregation,  The Board also agreed. that individual members should not be regarded as constituent parts,  branches, sections or affiliates, In application of the criteria referred to by me just now  and further to other decisions taken by the Board at its 88th session, it was decided to sus-  pend relations with effect from 31 December 1971 with 42 non-governmental organizations,
21 of which had not replied to the Director-General's communications, 6 being in category  B and 15 in category C, and 21 in application of the criteria, 7 being in category A, 6 in  category B and 8 in category C, These numbers indicate the results of our scrutiny.
31,6 The non-governmental organizations affected by this measure were informed in writ-  ing by the Director-General in December 1971 and a third reportwas submitted to the Board  at its 89th session in June/July 1972, This report contained the replies of 30 non-govern-  mental organizations, After studying this report the Board decided to lift the suspension  with regard to 21 organizations and to invite the Director-General to keep the Executive  Board informed of any new factors which, in his view, called for a further report on the  non-governmental organizations suspended by the Board before submitting his report to the  General Conference at its seventeenth session, In consequence, the Director-General pre-  sented a fourth report to the Executive Board at its 90th session in September /October 1972  containing the replies from 9 non-governmental organizations and the Board then decided  to lift the suspension with regard to 5 non-governmental organizations,
31,7 Delegates should also note that the criteria adopted by the Board at its 88th session  were applied by the Board throughout 1972 when admitting non-governmental organizations  to categories A and B, and by the Director-General when admitting new organizations to  category C, There are actually 305 non-governmental organizations which have official re-  lations with Unesco, 34 in category A, 158 in category B and 113 in category C, Relations  have been suspended with 10 non-governmental organizations, 2 in category B and 8 in category  C. The resolution which the Executive Board recommends for adoption by the General  Conference will be found in paragraph 4 of document 17 C/12 Addendum.

31,9 I would now like to make some remarks arising from the experience gathered in carry-  ing out 16 C/Resolution 8 adopted by the General Conference at its sixteenth session, Firstly,  the inquiry showed that the information available in the Secretariat concerning non-govern-  mental organizations was often incomplete, While the Secretariat had, in general precise  and recent data concerning non-governmental organizations in category A, this was not al-  ways the case for category B and particularly with respect to the national branches of inter-  national non-governmental organizations, As to category C, the information was often non-  existent, even the addresses were sometimes obsolete, Secondly, ‘the inquiry also revealed
28  that the international headquarters of non-governmental organizations in the three categories  did not themselves have the data requested under 16 C/Resolution 8, consequently they had  to undertake detailed inquiries in order to obtain exact answers, Thirdly, it was also clear  that the Board encountered difficulties in ascertaining whether the national branches of  certain non-governmental organizations did not co-operate in any way with the apartheid  policy of the Government of the Republic of South Africa. Fourthly, the Secretariat will no  doubt have to envisage a revision of existing machinery for communication between non-  governmental organizations and Unesco and to invite international non-governmental organi-  zations to review and improve their own machinery for communication with their own national  members,

(57.1) Mr. BETANCUR MEJIA ( Colombia) (Translation from the Spanish):  Draft resolution 17 C/DR/PLEN/17 is an historic proposal for the group of countries sub-  mitting it. This resolution is based on the oft-quoted phrase in the preamble to our Constitution:  "Since wars begin in the minds of men, it is in the minds of men that the defences of peace must  be constructed’ - from which arises the great responsibility of Unesco for all matters relating  to peace,
(57.2) We are at present living in two historic decades proclaimed by the United Nations General  Assembly: the Second Development Decade and the Disarmament Decade. It was not by chance  that the two were proclaimed almost simultaneously: one supports the other; and I now wish to  read some of the paragraphs of the historic declaration of the United Nations at its twenty-fourth  session, I will read the English text, since it is the only one I have:

Unesco has been  called'the conscience of mankind", and is today, and rightly, the chief rostrum where the world's  intellectuals can discuss the most important problems of concern to man. It should therefore  use all its means of studying its contribution to the Disarmament Decade, as it is already doing  for the Second Development Decade,

In addition,  arms research multiplies in another way: eachnew weapon leads to the invention of an antagonist,

I will take this opportunity of congratulating the Organization  on the excellent articles on this subject published in the Unesco Courier, and to congratulate  governments which, like the Government of Sweden, have set up bodies so important to mankind  as the International Peace Research Association, whose positive efforts mean so much to those  of us who believe in the possibility of achieving a peace firmly based on international social ‘jus -  tice, It has been said that the disarmament enterprise is difficult, lengthy, next to impossible,  Those of us who love peace know that it may be difficult but is not impossible, and that, if it is  long, we had better begin quickly to make it strong, so that mankind will begin to benefit from  its fruits as soon as possible, And now, precisely since there are signs of peace, we wish this  resolution to bear witness to our belief in the ability of man to attain peace throughdisarmament  and international justice, Consequently we hope, we are sure, that if all the peoples of the world  were here in solemn session this draft resolution would be carried by acclamation. :
