(8) Le PRESIDENT (traduit .de l'espagnol) :  Nous allons commencer le débat de politique générale et j'invite  S. Exc. M. Goma, ministre de l'enseignement supérieur et chef de la délégation de la  Zambie, à prendre la parole.
9.1 Mr GOMA (Zambia) :  Mr President, on behalf of the delegation of the Republic of Zambia, it  gives me great pleasure to join those who have spoken before me in congratulating  you on your election as President of this Conference. May your leadership ensure  that the outcome of this Conference will not only strengthen our Organization,  Unesco, but also reinforce the collective efforts of mankind to bring about a  better world than the present one.
9.2 Mr President, I also wish to congratulate our indefatigable Director-  General, Mr Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow, whose continued able and committed leadership is  reflected in the significant follow-up activities our Organization has been able  to undertake, under extremely serious resource constraints, since our last  General Conference session and in the satisfactory preparations for the present  session. The Director-General and his staff have produced documents for this  Conference which not only show administrative and professional excellence but  also a singularly pragmatic responsiveness to the adverse economic circumstances  besetting the Organization, while at the same time providing important guidelines  and substance for our deliberations on the various major issues which fall within  the competence of Unesco.
9.3 My delegation also wishes to pay tribute to the Executive Board for the  competent manner in which it has addressed various crucial issues pertaining to  the operations of the Organization during the current biennium and for the  important recommendations it has made for our consideration at this Conference.
9.4 Mr President, my country sees the mandate of Unesco as one that takes  cognizance of a very fundamental view of as a spiritual entity that is truly  equal to all others. This - not defined according to his colour, race or  ethnic circumstances, nationality or the socio-political system of his country -  universally wants peace, stability and progress; he 'seeks and deserves freedom,  justice and respect for the dignity of his person; he should not be oppressed or  exploited by his fellow men. It is, therefore, quite proper that Unesco should,  without being apologetic to any country or group of countries, concern itself  with the fundamental issues of peace and the survival of mankind; international  understanding and co-operation; obstazles to and opportunities for the
86    development and progress of peoples and nations; profound inequalities that  continue to divide individuals and societies; discrimination on grounds of  colour, race and sex; and human rights and the freedom of peoples. My delegation  is, indeed, happy that the Draft Programme and Budget for 1988-1989 (document
24 C/5) embraces these issues under specific major programmes.
9.5 Mr President, the last 50 years have witnessed great changes in the  world. Empires have fallen, colonialism has been vanquished almost completely,  and the international community has expanded enormously with the emergence of new  nations. There have been spectacular advances of science and technology which  have contributed greatly to the betterment of the human condition generally.  However, in spite of all this, and at the same time, we are saddled with an  irrationally over-armed world, as the instruments of destruction, both nuclear  and conventional, have been further developed and stockpiled. Furthermore, in  many parts of the world, there exist serious tensions and armed conflicts,  resulting in untold loss of human life, destruction of property and homes, and a  tragic impediment to äevelopment. The circumstances of these regional conflicts  cover a wide range: continuing illegal occupation of other peoples' lands,  foreign intervention, minority or alien rule and denial of basic human and  peoples' rights, and the unabated evils of racism. In many of the affected areas,  development and progress, let alone the involvement of Unesco, are greatly  frustrated, if not impossible altogether.
9.6 The arms race and the persistence of various regional armed conflicts  make it clear that, in spite of all its efforts, mankind has failed, together  with its United Nations, to build world peace and security on the concrete and  enduring foundation of love, truth, social justice and fair play. In the various  dangerous confrontations to be seen, there is much prejudice, much ignorance  about one another.
9.7 Mr President, it is against this background that my delegation supports  strongly those activities related to the question of peace proposed under Major  Programme XIII. It is for the same reason that we welcome receiit developments  between the two super powers, the Soviet Union and the United States of America,  and the array of concrete arms-reduction, security-building, and confidence-  building measures that now seem to be within reach.
9.8 Mr President, in a world where the interdependence of nations and the  interrelatedness of issues have become facts of life, we need more than ever  before to discover those points of convergence where the interests of a wider  humanity become those of its separate parts. A growing awareness of the total  interdependence of all countries, rich and poor, should help to force upon  peoples greater rationality in international relations. Such awareness could be  greatly facilitated through reflection on and a deeper understanding of world  problems that confront our common humanity. My delegation is satisfied that the  activities proposed under Major Programme I would enable Unesco to make  worthwhile contributions towards this goal.
9.9 Mr President, it is accepted that culture is an indispensable aspect of  authentic national development. For what is development without the people's  music, dance, art, languages, etc.; or when these are despised, degraded,  distorted, or prostituted? It can also be said that cultural relations are among  the chief means of shaping the future of men and nations, of changing their  directions through creative mutual borrowing, and of strengthening an awareness  of shared values. In this regard, cultural relations can be viewed as a tool  contributing towards international community building, understanding, empathy and  coexistence. Therefore, as our countries desire material progress, they should  not be apologetic about borrowing tools or accepting institutions and ideas which  will facilitate that progress. But this must go hand in hand with the search for  self-confidence and self-reliance based on a past rediscovered and reappraised.
9.10 It should, however, be pointed out that the growing instability of the  international order affects cultural relations, among other things, through  differential access to international communications and information systems. The  problem is, therefore, not simply a matter of a gap in wealth, nor a gap in  knowledge; it is also a gap in access to opportunity.
9.11 My delegation, thus, considers the activities proposed under Major  Programmes III, VI1 and XI as valid and highly relevant in the light of the  interests and concerns expressed in the preceding twò paragraphs and the Plan of  Action for the World Decade for Cultural Development.
9.12 Mr President, it has been said that education is one of the few great  hopes of our time. It is certainly a potent instrument of individual and national  development and progress. This perhaps explains the fact that, everywhere, both  in the industrialized and in the developing countries, education has been  ~ ~ . .  ~~ ~~~ ~ ~~ . ~ ~~ ~ .~ ~~
07    accorded high priority standing, even where and when. the resources made available  to it may not be commensurate with this position. Again, everywhere the debate  continues relentlessly on many important aspects of education.
9.13 What all this boils down to is that it is important to try and ensure  that the education provided should be consonant with the prevailing realities in  our countries; but that it should be good education nevertheless. Accordingly, it  is important to realize that, while there are many problems which are common to  practically all countries, there are obviously certain major problems which apply  only to a specific category of country. Thus, the developing countries face  enormous difficulties that are unknown in the industrialized countries. It is,  therefore, not enough to see and deal with the common problems of education: it  is important also to pay adequate attention to special preoccupations. My  delegation is satisfied that Major Programmes II, IV and V will assist both  Unesco and our countries properly to address the problems and challenges of  education we confront both globally and specifically at regional and national  levels.
9.14 Mr President, although now a truism, it has to be said that nowhere in  the modern world have significant national development and progress been secured  without the intervention of science and technology. The so-called developed  countries have certainly attained their present superior industrial status and  living standards largely because of science and technological know-how. It is  clear, therefore, that overcoming the gross defici,encies and weaknesses which  characterize the state of science and technology in many developing countries is  a necessary endeavour for the effective onslaught on the conditions of poverty,  deprivation and diminished human potentialities.
9.15 The realities and challenges of the African continent in this regard  were again discussed, under the auspices of Unesco, at the recent Second  Conference of Ministers Responsible for the Application of Science and Technology  in Africa (CASTAFRICA II). Since the Director-General referred to this  Conference, I will not say more about it. But it is against this background that  my delegation fully supports the activities proposed under Major Programmes VI,  IX and X.
9.16 Mr President, Major Programmes XII, XIII and XIV draw our attention to  some of the*most horrendous cruelties perpetrated 'by men against fellow men,  flagrant outrages against humanity, the tyranny of ,'imprisoned' minds, and the  shameless support for, or propping up of, or collasoration with internationally  outcast racist regimes for reasons of ideology and economic gain, which continue  to occur in this day and age.
9.17 Take South Africa, for instance. I can only state what we all know: and  this is that there is in that country an open, arrogant, continued, gross  violation of the basic human and democratic principles of justice, freedom,  equality, respect for the dignity of the human person, and majority rule. This is  not a matter of failure in the implementation of accepted human and democratic  principles. Those in power in South Africa are not struggling for the attainment  of these principles: they are deliberately and actively organizing South African  society so as to destroy the hold of these principles in the minds of men. The  strenuous efforts to uphold minority white rule ir1 South Africa, the brutalities  committed against the oppressed people there, the so-called constitutional  reforms, the muzzling of the press and of freedom of expression, the  dismemberment and Bantustanization of South Africa, South Africa's continued  illegal occupation of and stranglehold on Namibia, the wanton destabilization of  neighbouring independent States in southern Africa, and South Africa's support  and master-minding of the activities of dissident elements and armed bandits  there - all these things are pursued and done by the Pretoria regime in defence  of the abhorrent policy and practice of apartheid.
9.18 Mr President, justice, freedom, equality, human dignity, majority rule  and national independence are values of life to be cherished; they are not mere  political or philosophical concepts. When oppressed men or colonized people  struggle for justice, for their dignity, for their liberty and for their  independence, they do so to uphold these values and to preserve them for mankind  as a whole. This is precisely what the oppressed and colonized peoples of South  Africa and Nâmibia respectively are struggling for. That is why we support them,  and why we all should support them, without equivocation.
9.19 It is not the reform or transformation of apartheid that is demanded,  but its total destruction. This, and the elimination of prejudice, intolerance  and racism generally, together with the upholding of human rights and the rights  of peoples, will permit peoples to realize their full potential for progress and  the good of our common humanity. That is why my delegation strongly supports the  activities proposed under Major Programmes XII, XIII and XIV.
9.20 Finally, Mr President, ï want to say a few words about the future of our  Organization. Antecedent to this Conference, we have witnessed some ugly and  dangerous precedents: namely, orchestrated postures against personalities, and  succumbing to political clout and the 'tyranny' o€ the absent. I: am referring to  the battle for the next Director-Generalship of our Organization. These things  raise serious doubts about the acceptability to the entire membership of the  Organization of any person presented under, these circumstances €or appointment as  Director-General. And yet it is essential that there be a consensus on this  matter. My government hopes that the Organization will not be subjected to these  events in future. Unesco is extremely vital to the development and progress of  our common humanity. Let us not destroy such an Organization. I thank you,  Mr President.

Car il y va de l'effi-  cacité du système éducatif qui n'est pas une fin en soi, mais un dispositif  stratégique au service de l'homme et de sa société, au service des missions  qu'ils se sont assignées dans un monde OU les maîtres mots sont : le développe-  ment des peuples et la maîtrise de ce développement par la science, la technique  et la culture. Cette maîtrise de la science, de la technique et de la culture,  condition de l'épanouissement physique et intellectuel des hommes de notre temps,  impose aujourd'hui aux pays sous-développés comme le nôtre, une plus grande ima-  gination créatrice et un plus grand investissement dans l'enseignement des tech-  niques industrielles, commerciales et de gestion administrative. Le retard que  connaissent nos pays sous-développés en général et le Cameroun tout particuliè-  rement en matière d'enseignement technique, assombrit très gravement nos pers-  pectives de développernent et de maîtrise de ce développement. C'est pourquoi le  Cameroun attache une importance toute particulière aux dispositions du grand  programme V qui voudrait faire de l'éducation et de la formation dans les  filières scientifique et technique le levier fondamental de notre action pour  les deux prochaines années.

11.12 La politique de maîtrise de la science et de la technique voudrait pon-  derer et adapter aux exigences de notre monde contemporain la dimension trop  exclusivement humaniste héritée de la période d'avant l'indépendance. 21.2 We have before us Unesco's programme of work for the next two years. It  is the last C/5 of the current Medium-Term Plan, and the third Medium-Term Plan -  which has yet to be drawn up - will already influence our thoughts on the  programme for the coming biennium.

21.4 Mr President, in the Specialized Agencies of the United Nations, it is  virtually inevitable that political debates should arise concerning the way in  which an agency's mandate is carried out. This is particularly bound to be the  case with Unesco, in which countries with different cultural and political  backgrounds seek to reach agreement on such sensitive matters as culture,  communications, education and science. However, political debates should be  constructive and should be concerned with matters which fall within the agency's  mandate.
21.5 Unesco has a very broad mandate. But this does not give the Organization  carte blanche to expand its programme ever further beyond its field bf  competence: on the contrary, the mandate should provide a basis for a programme  which reconciles political and cultural differences, thus contributing towards  the worldwide development of culture, communications, education and science.
21.6 Unesco's Secretariat should perform a supervisory and conciliatory role  here - 'supervisory' in the sense that it should ensure that political debates  remain within the confines of the mandate and 'conciliatory' in the sense that it  should seek to overcome extreme disagreement between members and enable them to  engage in genuine worldwide co-operation in the fields specified in the mandate.  The much debated 'Unesco crisis' therefore not only revolves around such matters  as members withdrawing from the Organization, politicization of programmes and  financial problems: its origins also lie in a failure by the Secretariat to  fulfil its supervisory and conciliatory role properly and a failure by members to  recognize that consensus is the pre-condition for international co-operation in  such important and correspondingly sensitive fields as those of Unesco's  competence.
21.7 One thing which is perhaps symptomatic of these failures is Unesco's  inability to ad just sufficiently to the process of cultural development and to  perform a clear, consistent and dynamic role in it. Four years ago, in this same  place and on a similar occasion, I referred to Unesco's inability to recognize  cultural tensions between individual identity$ and interdependence, between  uniformity and pluralism; and between convergence and divergence. These tensions  are particularly powerful at world level, and Unesco has perhaps developed too  few instruments for coping with them. Unesco should endeavour to find ways and  means of enhancing its role in these fields.
21.8 Something must be done about the weaknesses to which I have referred in  order to restore the prestige and dynamic role which should characterize Unesco  on the world scene. Unesco should be a worldwide policy-making instrument for the  development of culture and communication and of education and science.

The Netherlands Government believes that, if  improvements can be made in all these areas, Unesco's programme output could be  increased and broadened and Unesco's responsibilities could be defined more  clearly within the context of multilateral co-operation as a whole.

Par ailleurs, nous réalisons un programme  permanent de diffusion du patrimoine culturel, artistique et scientifique, nous  favorisons le processus d'intégration de l'Amérique centrale et nous contribuons à  l'instauration d'un nouvel ordre économique et social international respectueux des  valeurs, principes et biens culturels et sociaux des pays en développement, et  capable d'éliminer toute forme de colonialisme culturel, scientifique et  technologique.
