1.2 Le Programme de développement durable à l’horizon 2030 a été adopté il y a deux ans. L’UNESCO s’attèle  depuis à ce que les États membres assurent de la manière la plus inclusive qui soit les transitions de leurs sociétés, en  s’assurant que « personne ne soit laissé de côté ». Parmi les 17 Objectifs de développement durable adoptés par les  Nations Unies, l’UNESCO contribue plus particulièrement à la mise en œuvre de neuf d’entre eux. Le plus évident est  l’agenda Éducation 2030 (ODD 4), dont l’UNESCO coordonne la mise en œuvre avec ses partenaires. En 2015, plus de
264 millions d’enfants et de jeunes n’étaient toujours pas scolarisés ; 617 millions d’enfants et d’adolescents dans le  monde n’atteignent pas le seuil minimal de compétence en lecture et en mathématiques. Nous partageons tous cette  responsabilité de réduire ces disparités et d’améliorer la qualité de l’éducation à travers l’ODD 4.
1.3 Au-delà de l’éducation, l’UNESCO répond au Programme 2030 en intensifiant ses efforts en faveur de l’égalité  des genres et l’autonomisation des femmes et des filles (ODD 5) et en améliorant l’accès sécurisé à l’eau potable par  ses programmes de recherche sur la gestion des ressources en eau (ODD 6). L’UNESCO participe à la reconnaissance  des sciences, de la technologie et de l’innovation pour le développement durable (ODD 9) et renforce les capacités  permettant de gérer durablement les océans (ODD 14). L’Organisation soutient la protection et l’exploitation durable de  la biodiversité et de la gestion des ressources naturelles, notamment grâce aux sites du patrimoine mondial et réserves  de biosphère, en appui de l’ODD 15, et appuie les efforts d’adaptation aux changements climatiques (ODD 13).  L’UNESCO participe à la création de villes inclusives, sûres et résilientes (ODD 11) et milite en faveur de l’accès public à  l’information et de la sécurité des journalistes (ODD 16). La culture est un facteur-clé transversal de la mise en œuvre de  l’ensemble du Programme 2030.   (La Présidente poursuit en anglais)

4.2 Mesdames et Messieurs, l’UNESCO occupe une place de choix parmi nos partenaires : elle a toujours été au  côté du Gouvernement de Madagascar dans la mise en œuvre de son programme national de développement à travers  de multiples initiatives de coopération et des programmes tels que l’éducation de qualité pour tous, l’éducation des filles  et des femmes, l’enseignement et la formation techniques et professionnels (l’EFTP). L’UNESCO a également apporté  son soutien à des activités relatives à la restauration du patrimoine culturel, à la créativité et à la diversité des  expressions culturelles ou encore à la conservation de la biodiversité de Madagascar. Et aujourd’hui, Madagascar  entend pleinement jouer son rôle au sein de l’UNESCO et de la communauté internationale en participant activement  aux différents travaux de ses différentes institutions et en y apportant ses contributions. C’est dans ce sens que  Madagascar présente sa candidature au Conseil exécutif de l’UNESCO, ainsi qu’au Conseil intergouvernemental du  Programme Gestion des transformations sociales (MOST) et demande ici le soutien de tous ses partenaires et de ses  pays amis.
4.3 Mesdames et Messieurs, je salue le choix du thème de ce forum : « Les ODD et le rôle de l’UNESCO dans le  système multilatéral ». La bonne définition du cadre d’action de cette Organisation clé du système des Nations Unies  dans la mise en œuvre du Programme 2030 contribuera sans nul doute à la promotion de la culture de la paix et au  renforcement d’une société durable et prospère. L’UNESCO a cette chance d’être à l’avant-garde de la connaissance et  de se focaliser sur les solutions à long terme et les principes de base du monde que nous voulons. De par ses champs  d’action et les valeurs qu’elle véhicule, l’UNESCO est en soi un élément crucial dans la mise en œuvre des ODD. En
42  continuant dans sa voie actuelle, en se rapprochant davantage des gouvernants à travers les plates-formes sciences- politiques et des peuples, l’UNESCO sera mieux appréciée. Je ne citerai à titre d’exemple que les contributions actives  de l’UNESCO à travers la Commission océanographique internationale dans la réalisation de l’ODD 14 ou son appui à  l’intégration de l’ODD 4 dans le plan sectoriel de l’éducation de Madagascar. En donnant la priorité aux pays d’Afrique,  aux plus vulnérables et aux pays les moins avancés, l’UNESCO contribue à combler le fossé de la pauvreté et à réduire  les fractures caractéristiques de notre monde. Cependant, pour progresser dans la mise en œuvre des ODD et mieux  aider ces pays, elle pourrait également renforcer davantage les actions auprès des pays développés, pour que ceux-ci  assument leur responsabilité historique, et accélèrent les transferts de connaissance et de technologies utiles pour la  réalisation des ODD. Dans notre quête en faveur de la paix, de la sécurité et des droits de l’homme, condition sine qua  non des progrès dans la réalisation des ODD, le dialogue de la culture est sans aucun doute l’une de nos meilleures  réponses. Thématique transversale des ODD, la culture est à la fois un catalyseur et un vecteur des dimensions  économique, sociale et environnementale des ODD. Elle est également facteur de démocratie et de développement  durable car elle relie les sociétés à leur histoire et à leur territoire, elle les libère et les éclaire sur les choix qu’elles ont à  faire. L’UNESCO, aux côtés de ses États membres et de ses partenaires, doit remplir pleinement sa mission consistant à  promouvoir la culture et tout particulièrement la culture de paix dans le monde, notamment en luttant contre le fléau de  l’extrémisme violent qui touche surtout les jeunes, nos jeunes, notre avenir. C'est dans cette optique que Madagascar  entend continuer ses efforts pour la mise en œuvre de la stratégie antiterroriste mondiale et du Plan d'action des Nations  Unies pour la prévention de l'extrémisme violent. En partenariat étroit avec l’Organisation internationale de la  francophonie et l'Organisation des Nations Unies, Madagascar concentrera sa stratégie nationale antiterroriste dans les  actions de prévention tendant à éradiquer les causes profondes du terrorisme et de la radicalisation. Car notre démarche  devra être inclusive, impliquer les diverses parties prenantes et veiller à la cohérence de nos politiques, en particulier  pour le développement, en assurant un équilibre entre les piliers économique, environnemental et socio-culturel. Le rôle  de l’UNESCO devrait être de ce fait primordial dans cette lutte que nous menons contre le terrorisme, l’extrémisme et la  radicalisation.
4.4 Mesdames et Messieurs, conscient et convaincu que la réalisation des ODD ne pourrait se concrétiser sans la  contribution et l’engagement de nos jeunes, Madagascar s’attache aujourd’hui, dans le cadre de son plan de  développement national, à soutenir la jeunesse afin qu’elle puisse miser pleinement et durablement sur un avenir  radieux. Mettre la jeunesse au cœur du développement nous est aujourd’hui primordial car elle représente à la fois des  opportunités et des défis importants pour le développement durable du pays. À ce titre, le Gouvernement de  Madagascar s’est engagé à mettre en place les bases indispensables pour adapter nos orientations politiques avec la  structure de notre population. La promulgation de la loi portant sur la politique nationale de la jeunesse fait partie  intégrante de ces acquis. Dans le même esprit, des concertations entre diverses parties prenantes ont été menées,  orientées sur la population et le développement et axées particulièrement sur les investissements en faveur de la  jeunesse. Ces événements se concentrent plus particulièrement sur la population jeune en milieu rural, zone dans  laquelle les plus grands des défis restent à relever, notamment en ce qui concerne les filles. J’exhorte ainsi toutes les  parties prenantes – tant l’UNESCO que ses pays membres et ses partenaires – à prendre les décisions appropriées  pour améliorer et faciliter l’accès de chaque individu, notamment les filles et les femmes, à une éducation de qualité et à  un emploi décent. En cela, nous devons, et j’en reste convaincu, garantir les voies de réalisation des Objectifs de  développement durable en investissant en faveur des jeunes et en leur offrant le meilleur environnement possible pour  leur plein épanouissement. Pour ce faire, l’UNESCO, à travers ses différents programmes, demeure la mieux placée.  Pour illustrer mes propos, permettez-moi de citer le Programme de développement des capacités pour l’éducation  (CapED). Ce programme a permis aux jeunes non scolarisés des zones rurales à Madagascar de recevoir des outils  pour leur avenir. En effet, en ciblant prioritairement les zones rurales les plus pauvres qui présentent de faibles activités  économiques et un taux de chômage élevé chez les jeunes, CapED vise à fournir à 5 000 jeunes femmes et jeunes  hommes malgaches non scolarisés des compétences de base ainsi que des compétences professionnelles et  entrepreneuriales, et à identifier des opportunités d’emplois régionales et locales. Cette initiative consistant à promouvoir  l’entrepreneuriat chez les jeunes et les femmes, Madagascar en a fait une de ses priorités avec ses partenaires, afin que  le marché du travail puisse absorber les nouveaux arrivants et ainsi accroître la productivité globale. Ce programme  accompagne les jeunes dans leurs propres initiatives et leur permet d’exploiter leurs potentialités par eux-mêmes et de  contribuer ainsi au développement économique de leur pays.
4.5 Par ailleurs, afin d’améliorer l’insertion professionnelle des jeunes, le Gouvernement de Madagascar s’est  engagé à améliorer la pertinence et la qualité des formations selon les besoins requis par les secteurs prioritaires et a  renforcé le domaine de l’enseignement et de la formation techniques et professionnels (EFTP) au profit de tous les  jeunes, quel que soit leur niveau de qualification professionnelle ou leur niveau d’éducation. Ainsi, Madagascar, en ayant  comme ambition de faire de chaque citoyen un acteur du changement grâce à une éducation de qualité, s’est engagée  et s’engage à permettre à chaque enfant d’avoir accès de manière équitable à une éducation de qualité pouvant lui  assurer un emploi décent. Nous savons que c’est avant tout par l’éducation que nous pouvons effectivement parvenir au  développement durable tant souhaité et élever l’être humain à la dimension de toutes ses capacités. À cet égard,  Madagascar entend, dans son plan sectoriel de l’éducation, accroître de manière significative le taux de scolarisation  des jeunes à travers l’amélioration de la planification du système éducatif et de l’accès des jeunes dans les zones  rurales et enclavées. Une réforme au niveau de la langue d’enseignement et des méthodes d’enseignement répondant  aux besoins socio-culturels, économiques et environnementaux des jeunes a été également adoptée. C’est dans ce  sens que le pays a conçu un plan « Éducation pour tous » spécifique au contexte national et qui vise l’amélioration de la  qualité de la formation en réponse aux besoins de la croissance économique et sociale. Parmi les mesures prises par la  République de Madagascar aux fins de redynamiser son système éducatif figure également l’application effective,  aujourd’hui, du système Licence, Master, Doctorat (LMD) dans les différentes filières de l’enseignement supérieur et  universitaire de Madagascar.
4.6 Mesdames et Messieurs, Madagascar entend poursuivre et renforcer son partenariat avec l’UNESCO pour une  promotion harmonieuse des activités culturelles et artistiques menées au niveau national, sous-régional et international.  La Convention de l’UNESCO du 20 octobre 2005 sur la promotion et la protection de la diversité des expressions  culturelles doit également nous inciter à faire plus en ce qui concerne le volet « culture » du développement. Mon pays  plaide aujourd’hui pour le renforcement des moyens car les principes que porte la Convention de 2005 sont essentiels  pour enrichir la relation entre culture et développement et pour soutenir la création d’industries culturelles dans les pays  en développement. Le programme de l’UNESCO relatif à la promotion de la créativité et des industries créatives est en  ce sens salutaire et Madagascar réaffirme ici son attachement au fonds correspondant qui soutient de nombreux projets  en faveur de l’émergence d’industries créatives dans les pays en développement. Il permet aux jeunes de tirer profit de  leurs potentiels et les incite à innover et créer par eux-mêmes leurs propres entreprises. C’est dans ce sens que  Madagascar a bénéficié en 2016 de l’appui du Fonds international pour la diversité culturelle de l’UNESCO avec le projet  « Inciter les jeunes à utiliser le cinéma comme moyen d’expression pour la mise en place d’une industrie du cinéma », et  du financement en 2010 du développement de la production et de la commercialisation des lambahoany (le lambahoany  est une sorte de pagne traditionnel avec des motifs et dessins, qui s’accompagnent parfois d’inscriptions spécifiques  reprenant généralement des adages locaux). En 2008également, un soutien a été apporté au secteur de l’édition. Forte  de son engagement à soutenir la jeunesse dans le développement socio-économique, Madagascar a participé très  modestement au financement volontaire du Fond et invite les autres pays à faire de même.
4.7 Par ailleurs, dans le domaine de la promotion et de la préservation du patrimoine mondial, un accent particulier  est mis sur la nécessité de renforcer le rôle des biens du patrimoine mondial comme garants du développement durable.  Pour ce faire, chaque pays devrait valoriser et conserver les lieux à la valeur naturelle remarquable et renfermant une  biodiversité, une géo-diversité ou d'autres caractéristiques naturelles exceptionnelles, qui sont essentiels au bien-être et  à l’identité des êtres humains. En effet, cette préservation du patrimoine mondial, reconnaissons-le, générera une plus- value au pays à travers le tourisme durable grâce au prestige qu’entraîne l’inscription du site sur la Liste du patrimoine  mondial de l’UNESCO. Mon Gouvernement travaille actuellement à l’inscription de la Haute Ville d’Antananarivo sur la  Liste du patrimoine mondial, en collaboration avec la Région Île-de-France. Ce projet permettrait de revaloriser le  patrimoine architectural et historique de la ville mais aussi du pays en créant ainsi de nombreux emplois pour la  jeunesse de Madagascar. Je tiens donc à faire appel à l’UNESCO pour appuyer Madagascar dans cette démarche.  Madagascar compte sur le soutien indéfectible de l’UNESCO pour développer et pérenniser des initiatives en faveur de  la sauvegarde du patrimoine culturel et naturel.
4.8 Mesdames et Messieurs, une des priorités de l’UNESCO qui me tient particulièrement à cœur est la biodiversité,  et plus particulièrement celle de sa préservation, qui contribue au développement quand elle est au service des  populations locales, notamment en créant des emplois. La préservation de la biodiversité constitue en effet un élément  important en faveur de la lutte contre les changements climatiques qui affectent plus de 1 300 sites de l’UNESCO à  travers le monde. À l’image du Dialogue de Huangshan sur les sites UNESCO et le développement durable, j’estime que  l’UNESCO, à travers ses programmes, devrait intégrer et consolider les actions menées face au changement climatique,  notamment en faveur des pays vulnérables et des petits États insulaires en développement (PEID). Le Colloque  international sur les effets du changement climatique sur les océans du monde est une illustration parfaite de ces actions,  dont la portée ne se limite point au domaine de la science mais embrasse également celui de la culture, de l’éducation,  et de la sécurité alimentaire. Madagascar, en créant un département ministériel chargé de la mer, entend faire de la  protection de ces ressources halieutiques une priorité nationale. La promotion de l’éthique devrait également être prise  en considération dans la lutte contre ce changement climatique. À cet effet, je plaide pour le renforcement de la structure  du Programme sur l’Homme et la biosphère (MAB) qui constitue aussi un appui important à la conservation de la  biodiversité, indispensable dans la lutte contre les effets des changements climatiques.
4.9 Mesdames et Messieurs, pour terminer mon intervention, Madagascar estime que le temps est venu de mettre  en commun les ressources disponibles et de recentrer les programmes de l’UNESCO autour des défis qui interpellent  nos États, citons entre autres l’éducation, la valorisation de ressources humaines et la protection de l’environnement.  Face à ces défis, le multilatéralisme est notre meilleur atout. Nous nous devons d’être solidaires, nous devons mutualiser  nos actions et nos expertises pour réaliser les ODD. J’invite également l’UNESCO à promouvoir des formes innovantes  de financement, ô combien nécessaires pour soutenir l’action engagée. Je vous remercie de votre aimable attention.

6.1 Mr Lajčák (President of the 72nd session of the United Nations General Assembly):   Madam President of the General Conference, Director-General, distinguished Heads of State and Government,  Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen. I am honoured to be here at this Leaders’ Forum of the 39th session of the  UNESCO General Conference. I want to thank the Director-General for inviting me to address you. On behalf of the  international community, I wish to thank her for her dedication and the passion she has applied to her work as the  Director-General of UNESCO.
6.2 I will start by telling you something you already know: multilateralism is facing challenges. This is nothing new.  The stakes of disengagement from our multilateral system are, however, new. That is because they have never been  higher. If we do not take action, these stakes will not work in our favour. This morning, I will share with you four main  messages about the future of multilateralism, and UNESCO’s role in it.
6.3 The first message is that we need multilateralism more than ever before. The United Nations has been alive now  for 72 years. This is a full lifetime of multilateralism. It is against this backdrop that we approach today’s tests and  challenges. Cooperation between nations has brought us tremendous benefit. It has shown us that working together  yields more and better results than when we act alone. In 2015, our international community achieved two very important  milestones in the form of the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) and the Paris Agreement. If implemented, these  agreements will protect the planet, and make it a better place to live for everyone. Multilateralism has also had a major  role in advancing peace. It has allowed us to rally in support of peace agreements, and to mobilize against threats to  international peace and security. Recently, the United Nations and its Member States have worked to respond to the  changing nature of these threats. We have committed to taking quicker, earlier and more inclusive action to prevent  conflicts from breaking out. Led by the 2016 sustaining peace resolutions of the United Nations, we have tasked  ourselves with pooling the vast capacity and expertise within the United Nations system against old and new threats to  peace – from political violence to international terrorism. Additionally, very soon, we will negotiate the first international  framework to ensure safe, orderly and regular migration.
6.4 I want to point something out: these agreements are not like regular contracts. They do not tell us what will  happen if a party is in breach, or does not fulfil its duties. This is because the challenges which brought them about are  too urgent and interconnected for this. Humanity cannot afford for any of us to opt out or to choose to act on one part of  our agreements and not on the others. In an increasingly globalized world, no one body or country can face problems  alone. In the same way, if one body or country fails to respond, it could affect us all. This is why all of us need to fulfil all  of our promises. If we want the next generation to be born into a better world, we only have one option, and that is strong  multilateralism with the United Nations system at its core.
6.5 My second message, ladies and gentlemen, is that UNESCO plays a critical role in our multilateral system. It  has rallied actors from all over the world to protect our shared cultural heritage. It has promoted cooperation for  advances in education, science and technology, which benefit us all. As a critical specialized agency, it has offered vital  support to many United Nations initiatives. The role played by UNESCO in our multilateral system is really defined by  people – by the impact that its work has had on men, women, boys and girls. This impact has been felt by a Syrian  student, who can pursue her dreams through a UNESCO scholarship. It has been felt, too, by a Zimbabwean child, who  has helped his school transform into a “green oasis” with UNESCO’s support. And it has been felt by the young people  who gathered here last week for the 10th annual UNESCO Youth Forum. Their ideas will be presented to this session of  the General Conference. This is a welcome step to ensuring that young people drive their own solutions to the  challenges they face. Amidst the success of UNESCO’s work, it has suffered setbacks. I regret the decision of Member  States to withdraw from membership of UNESCO. Director-General Bokova aptly described this as a “loss for  multilateralism”. Make no mistake: any loss for multilateralism is a loss for people and our humanity. That being said, we  must continue to find ways to work together so that we can turn setbacks into advances. The key to this is maintaining  dialogue.
6.6 This brings me to my third message, which is that multilateralism must go beyond ceremony. It cannot be  something that takes place only on formal documents, or at high-level events. If we are to ensure this, we need to  prioritize real interaction, and real dialogue. This must happen at all levels. But it is particularly important for you, the  leaders. Political differences between leaders have traditionally meant the difference between life and death, peace and  war. Now they could also mean the difference between a sustainable planet and one that can no longer support the life  we are currently living. So dialogue is not an option – it is our duty. And it is a duty not for some, but for all.
6.7 We do come from diverse backgrounds. We do hold different positions on the world’s most pressing issues. Our  interests, at times, do diverge. But we have seen that convergence can be found – particularly when it comes to our  human dignity, peace, the protection of our environment, and our common humanity. Multilateralism cannot be a  competition where the most powerful prevail. Multilateralism cannot be something we opt into only when it suits our  interests. Multilateralism cannot see dialogue be replaced by a sequence of monologues. If we go in this direction, we  risk undermining all of the progress we have made since World War II. To quote a slightly modernized version of lines  from UNESCO’s Constitution, I would say: “Since wars begin in the minds of men and women, it is in the minds of men  and women that the defences of peace must be constructed”. We cannot construct defences of peace on the battlefield.  Similarly, defences of our planet will fall if the foundations are weakened by deforestation or rising sea levels. These  defences must instead be built in our minds, from dialogue and exchanges we have in rooms like this, all over the world.  They must be built before it is too late. And the United Nations must lead the charge. It must act as an honest broker,  leveraging its neutrality and experience for meaningful dialogue. I want to thank UNESCO for providing us with a platform  for this very important task.
6.8 Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen, my final message is that cooperation between UNESCO and the United  Nations General Assembly must continue and grow. I look forward to our collaboration on many issues including the  following: (1) Peacebuilding and sustaining peace. In April, I will convene a high-level event in New York to raise the  visibility of sustaining peace. It will focus on the United Nation’s reinvigorated commitment to conflict prevention, as well  as the need for stronger support for peacebuilding. My team has developed a roadmap for the event. It aims to ensure  that we draw from the rich expertise, experiences and ideas of stakeholders across the board in preparing for this event.  Today’s meeting is a key part of this roadmap. UNESCO’s work in fostering a culture of peace and “building peace in the  minds of men and women” recognizes that peace cannot be sustained on paper only. Peace must be felt in the hearts
45  and minds of people. I also hope that I will be inviting many of you to New York in April; (2) Water and sanitation. These  are two essential elements for a decent life. Unfortunately, they are not yet accessible to everyone. In March, I will launch  the “International Decade for Action, ‘Water for Sustainable Development’, 2018-2028”. UNESCO’s leading role in this  domain in undeniable, and I count on your support; (3) Youth, education and the prevention of violent extremism.  UNESCO has acted as a pioneer in these three areas. In particular, I commend its focus on education as a tool to  prevent violent extremism. Next year, I will be organizing a related event in New York. Its aim is to give louder voices to  young people on issues that affect them, including education and skills training, employment opportunities and youth-led  implementation of the Sustainable Development Goals. I look forward to engagement with UNESCO and all of you here  today; (4) Climate action. We need urgent action on climate change in order to secure a sustainable planet for future  generations. UNESCO’s work in exploring science for a sustainable future, as well as initiatives like “One Planet, One  Ocean”, will be critical as we implement the Paris Agreement; (5) Financing for development. The 2030 Agenda for  Sustainable Development has very significant resource implications across the developed and developing world.  According to the United Nations Conference on Trade and Development (UNCTAD), the total investment needs for the  Sustainable Development Goals are an estimated $5 to $7 trillion per year. In order to mobilize this much-needed finance,  we must work to integrate private investment and attract new financial resources, including from non-traditional actors.  We also need to address the bottlenecks hampering financial flows towards SDG implementation and the countries in  dire need of financial resources. UNESCO and Ms Bokova have time and again made the case for investment in  education. I am in full agreement with the thinking that putting resources towards sustainable development, including  education, will save us money elsewhere. History has discredited the wait-and-see approach of reacting to crises out of  fear instead of preventing them in the first place. Financing our global goals will be a priority for my Presidency. I will  count on all of you for your input and support; (6) Migration. The adoption of the Global Compact for Safe, Orderly and  Regular Migration will be a defining moment for the United Nations. The emphasis that UNESCO places on the “human  face of migration” is directly in line with my focus on people. Through its work, UNESCO has shown that promoting  respect for cultural diversity, and encouraging social inclusion of migrants, can enrich communities. It can also prevent  extremism on all sides. As we get deeper into the process of negotiating this Compact, I encourage continued  engagement from UNESCO. Suffice it to say, therefore, that there are many links between the priorities of the General  Assembly and the experiences and expertise of UNESCO. I hope that my participation here today acts as one of many  steps towards even deeper engagement throughout the 72nd session of the General Assembly.
6.9 Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen, today we meet in the format of the Leaders’ Forum. So, I address you all as  leaders, as those who hold the keys to a better future for us all. The tasks that lie ahead of us are vast. The challenges  facing this planet and the people who live on it have never been higher since the United Nations was created. I make a  call on you today: let us recommit to multilateralism. Let us fulfil the promises we have made to allow people to live a  decent life, in peace, on a sustainable planet. Let us mobilize to strengthen UNESCO, and the wider United Nations  system. And let us all start with a very simple step: by talking and listening to each other. I thank you very much.
7. The President:   Thank you, Your Excellency, for your presence here with us. It is surely a very strong signal that we are sending  to you. Our next speaker is His Excellency, Mr Bakir Izetbegović, who is a member of the tripartite Presidency of Bosnia  and Herzegovina – a country rich in history, where cultures mix and East meets West. The fruitful cooperation between  UNESCO and Bosnia and Herzegovina has grown stronger over the years, particularly with the successful reconstruction  of one of the most emblematic sites of the country: the Old Bridge of Mostar. A world heritage site today, the Old Bridge  proudly spans the two riverbanks as a symbol of the power of culture to connect people and build bridges between  communities. Bosnia and Herzegovina is devoted to safeguarding its cultural heritage and creating a space for peace,  dialogue and reconciliation. One such initiative was recently implemented together with UNESCO and other United  Nations partners, a joint programme entitled “Dialogue for the Future”, enabling a country-wide peace-building process  that promotes coexistence, trust-building and appreciation of diversity. Your Excellency, it is an honour to welcome you  to UNESCO. You have the floor.
8.1 Mr Izetbegović (Member of the Presidency of Bosnia and Herzegovina):   Madam President, Madam Director-General, Excellencies, distinguished delegates, it is a great honour and a  pleasure to be here with you today. At the outset I want to commend Director-General Bokova for her inspiring leadership  over the past eight years. Her commitment to UNESCO’s ideals and values, with her tireless efforts to carry out its  mission, deserve the highest praise. Despite facing many challenges, this Organization has managed under her  leadership to reaffirm its role and importance in the multilateral system of the United Nations. I would like to congratulate  Ms Audrey Azoulay of France for her nomination by the Executive Board for election as the new Director-General of  UNESCO. We look forward to being able to work with her to further advance our cooperation with UNESCO and to  safeguard the impartiality, credibility and unique mandate of this Organization.
8.2 Bosnia and Herzegovina is deeply committed to UNESCO’s mission and will remain fully engaged in its  programmes and activities. Our partnership has grown stronger over the past 25 years since we joined the Organization.  UNESCO has been a true friend and partner to the Government and citizens of my country in preserving and promoting  our cultural, ethnic and religious diversity and helping us to restore our cultural heritage that was so seriously affected by  the war. One of the annexes to the Dayton Peace Agreement which ended the war was specifically dedicated to the  establishment of an independent commission to preserve national monuments of Bosnia and Herzegovina. It was the  first time in modern history that preservation of cultural heritage was integrated into an international peace agreement.  UNESCO was tasked with implementing this important mission, which laid the foundations of our partnership. Over the  past 25 years UNESCO has actively supported Bosnia and Herzegovina in every part of its mandate, helping my country  overcome a traumatic war experience, whose impact is still present in many segments of our society.
8.3 UNESCO’s work in Bosnia and Herzegovina is no less valuable today, and its continuing support remains of  great significance to us. As its mandate is firmly enshrined in the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, Bosnia and  Herzegovina will continue to work closely with UNESCO to promote education, culture, science and communication as  drivers of sustainable peace and development. Sustaining peace and development was, in fact, our main objective when  in 2014 the Presidency of Bosnia and Herzegovina launched, together with the then Secretary-General of the United  Nations, Ban Ki-moon, an initiative called “Dialogue for the Future”, aimed at promoting coexistence and diversity in my  country. This first-of-its-kind project within the United Nations system was designed to create spaces for dialogue, to  promote trust-building and appreciation of diversity, to increase participation, awareness and influence of views on policy  debates on issues impacting our country’s development, to promote the role of education as a driver of greater social  cohesion and to emphasize the role of culture for citizens and communities to advance common peace-building and  development goals. The Presidency and the United Nations team in Bosnia and Herzegovina, including the United  Nations Development Programme (UNDP), the United Nations Children’s Fund (UNICEF) and UNESCO, successfully  implemented the first phase of this project from 2014 to 2016, and we are now working to implement its second phase,  scaling it up in our region to involve Croatia, Serbia and Montenegro. We are proud of this initiative because it was built  on our desire to promote unity in diversity as a platform for sustainable peace and building a prosperous future for our  country and our region in line with the principles and goals enshrined in the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development.
8.4 In the same spirit, we are devoted to safeguarding Bosnia and Herzegovina’s rich and diverse cultural heritage,  both tangible and intangible, while respecting the cultural, ethnic and religious pluralism that has characterized and  shaped our society and our identity for centuries. No matter the complex challenges we face, we remain committed to  advancing intercultural and interreligious dialogue, tolerance and harmony as pillars of our State and of our society.  Many of you remember the sad moment of 9 November 1993 when the Old Bridge in Mostar, which Ms Bokova  mentioned today, was destroyed. But I am sure you also remember the moment of 23 July 2004 when we jointly  celebrated the inauguration of the reconstructed Old Bridge. That moment was a milestone in our recent history. It is  testimony to the resilience of the human spirit and the power of culture to rebuild bridges that connect peoples and  communities in every sense. The reconstruction of the Old Bridge was the result of international efforts led by the World  Bank and UNESCO, coordinated by Bosnia and Herzegovina and supported by Italy, Turkey, Croatia, the Netherlands,  and many other international partners.
8.5 The rebuilding of the Old Bridge initiated a process of fruitful regional cooperation in preserving cultural heritage.  An excellent example of that regional cooperation was the initiative by Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Croatia and  Montenegro, and developed under the umbrella of UNESCO, to inscribe the medieval tombstones that we call “steći” on  the World Heritage List. The serial property of 28 component sites, comprising 4,000 medieval tombstones located in  four neighbouring States, was inscribed on the World Heritage List on 15 July 2016. These tombstones are especially  remarkable for their interfaith character, as they were used for burial by three medieval religious communities: the  Orthodox Church, the Catholic Church and the Church of Bosnia. Bosnia and Herzegovina is proud to have coordinated  this regional initiative that affirms the importance of culture as a platform for promoting reconciliation, cooperation and  development. The two most recent nominations from Bosnia and Herzegovina to UNESCO’s Memory of the World  Programme, the Sarajevo Haggadah and the manuscript collection of the Gazi Husrev Library in Sarajevo, are another  great example of our contribution to the preservation of cultural diversity.
8.6 Ladies and gentlemen, Bosnia and Herzegovina has decided to bid for membership of the World Heritage  Committee at the forthcoming elections to be held in Paris on 14 November 2017. We want to express our deepest  gratitude to all the countries that have supported our candidacy. We believe that our commitment to safeguarding and  restoring the cultural heritage of Bosnia and Herzegovina, as well as our experience and expertise in preserving and  managing diversity, make Bosnia and Herzegovina a credible and committed candidate for membership of the World  Heritage Committee. Our main objective as a member of this body will be to protect and enhance the credibility of the  World Heritage Convention as the most important global instrument for transmitting cultural heritage to future generations  as a shared resource of our humanity.
8.7 The human race is a mosaic composed of a rich variety of peoples, cultures and religions. Our responsibility is  to protect and nurture that mosaic. The historical experience of Bosnia and Herzegovina offers valuable lessons in  finding a model for harmonious coexistence and the thriving of such diversity. Bosnia and Herzegovina’s unique multi- ethnic and multi-religious culture has been woven through a thousand tears of tolerance and respect among its citizens,  regardless of their ethnic and religious affiliations. For hundreds of years, the citizens of my country have lived in  harmony, helping each other to build houses of worship that almost physically lean on one another. Bosnia and  Herzegovina is a meeting point of civilizations and the bridge between East and West. It is the westernmost expression  of Orthodox Christianity and Islam in Europe. It also marks one of the easternmost reaches of Catholicism, and has, for  almost half a millennium, been home to a small but thriving Jewish community that remains an integral part of our society.  Ethnic or religious exclusiveness and extremism are absolutely unacceptable from our point of view because they are  contrary to the values we have nurtured for centuries.
8.8 Throughout our history, the citizens of Bosnia and Herzegovina have paid a high price for protecting their  pluralistic community and identity, and for defending the idea of cultural, ethnic and religious pluralism and diversity in  our society. But no matter the price, we have never given up on these founding principles of our State in our societies.  We have always understood that the preservation and success of our pluralistic culture as a specific microcosm is  important for the whole world. Today, a battle is ongoing in our world between those who seek to protect and enhance  the pluralistic character and tolerant spirit of their countries and societies, and those who are causing and deepening  cultural, ethnic and religious divides and conflicts. We must not allow the idea of cultural pluralism to be defeated. Its  defeat would produce dangerous ruptures at the world’s most delicate fault lines. The dark and dangerous ideologies of
47  isolation, disintegration and exclusiveness must not be allowed to prevail over the progressive ideologies of inclusion,  integration and pluralism that seek to liberate human potential.
8.9 Those of us who believe that cultural pluralism is one of humankind’s most precious treasures must press on  with our efforts to bridge cultural, ethnic and religious divides and build a pluralistic inclusive and tolerant international  society in which diversity is a source of our common prosperity, a driver of cooperation instead of conflict, and the bridge  instead of a wall between countries and peoples. Our world is embroiled in conflicts that are fermented by cultural, ethnic  and religious divides and geopolitical competition for control of territories and access to resources. The root of many of  these conflicts is discrimination, marginalization, failures of governance, impunity, deep-rooted poverty and lack of  opportunities, all aggravated by the growing effects of climate change. Political, economic and social injustice and  inequality contribute to the rise of despair and hopelessness, creating a fertile ground for the emergence of extremism,  intolerance and violence. Lack of education, ignorance, refusal to engage in dialogue, to understand and embrace and  bridge cultural, ethnic and religious differences give rise to prejudice, xenophobia and hate in our societies and  communities. We cannot keep the impact of these modern conflicts and discordance outside State borders, nor can we  ignore their global consequences in our increasingly interconnected world. Our approach to tackling these challenges  must be holistic, coordinated and unified.
8.10 Culture, science, education and communication are the pillars of such a comprehensive approach to addressing  the conditions conducive to the spread of extremism and radicalism, intolerance and xenophobia in our societies. In  particular, we must work harder to expend access to quality education and opportunities for our younger generations.  Young people offer immense potential for our world. If educated and gainfully employed, they will be a major generator of  stability and prosperity. If uneducated, jobless, robbed of dignity and living in poverty and despair, they can easily  succumb to extremism and thus become a generator of instability. Sustaining a systematic investment in quality  education is one of the best ways to create and expand opportunities for young generations.
8.11 UNESCO has a critical role to play in the achievement of Sustainable Development Goal 4 concerning quality  education. Quality education must not be a privilege of some, but a right for everyone, accessible with equality, equity  and inclusiveness. The lagging of undeveloped and underdeveloped countries behind the developed world is directly  linked to their lagging in access to education, research and innovation, especially in the fields of science and technology.  That is why our first and foremost objective must be to gradually reduce and ultimately close the educational, scientific  and technological gaps in our world. We must ensure that each society, each citizen and each child in the world, no  matter who or where they are, fully and equally benefit from the scientific and technological advances of our civilization.
8.12 UNESCO plays a crucial role in this regard by working to create inclusive knowledge societies and by promoting  sharing of and access to knowledge and information, especially to scientific and technological research and innovation.  To sustain and achieve prosperity for all, we must put the most pressing needs of our citizens, human development and  human rights, at the centre of our actions. Human dignity, freedom and equality are the most powerful drivers of peace  and development. There can be no lasting peace and security when injustice and inequality are an everyday experience  for millions of people, when basic rights are denied to so many human beings, when so many men, women and children  around the world live in fear and insecurity. The resulting grievances make our States unstable and our societies  vulnerable. Injustice and inequality diminish the human potential of our communities, putting development and prosperity  beyond their reach. Thus, the road of progress must go from ending poverty to reducing inequality to building just,  tolerant, open and inclusive societies. One of our most important missions in the time to come will be to build bridges  between cultures, religions and civilizations, to foster dialogue and understanding, to promote trust and respect, to build  bridges in order to provide solutions to problems the true nature of which we have only begun to understand.
8.13 We all celebrate the rebuilding of the Old Bridge in Mostar, but it could not have been rebuilt without the  assistance of the international community, based on their understanding of the cultural significance of the Old Bridge.  Bosnia and Herzegovina did not recover alone. Aware of the temporary difficulties it faced, necessary dependence  succeeded in turning that outside help to the advantage of its citizens as a whole. Today, many other countries in our  world need help in building or rebuilding bridges in their societies. Bosnia and Herzegovina, the home of great bridges  and bridge-builders between peoples, cultures and religions, stands ready to help in these building and rebuilding  challenges in the twenty-first century. UNESCO’s role in this regard is more important today than ever before. This  Organization has always been the best framework for building bridges of international cooperation in education, science,  culture and communication. Let us keep it so. Thank you very much.

10.2 Afghanistan was honoured to become the 52nd Member State of the United Nations family on
19 November 1946, joining the 51 States that initially founded the United Nations. Since then, Afghanistan has sought to  deliver on its responsibilities and honour its international commitments. Afghanistan is also honoured to have been  among the first States to become a member of the important and valuable agency of UNESCO, on 4 May 1948. My  country has been one of the most active members of this United Nations agency, and it is a great honour and privilege  for me to represent Afghanistan and to be here with you today at the Leaders’ Forum.

10.4 Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen, the Afghan nation, besides enjoying a rich cultural and scientific  background, unfortunately inherited a bitter historical experience of conflicts, oppression, exclusivism, prejudice and  inequality. Fortunately, during these years, the international community, and most notably the United Nations, its  agencies and Afghanistan’s partners including the United States of America, the European Union, Japan, China, India,  Australia, the Islamic countries and our neighbours stood by us and supported the programmes of our Government. 10.6 Excellencies, ladies and gentlemen, I would like to take this opportunity to inform you about the challenges,  commitments, and performance of the Afghan Government in three key areas related to UNESCO’s fields of competence:  education, freedom of expression and the media, and archaeology and historical heritage. Afghanistan has a rich history  and civilization. Hundreds of famous philosophers, scientists, poets and experts in culture and art in human history  belong to a geographical area that today is called Afghanistan. Unfortunately, illiteracy is our biggest challenge today in  addition to other problems we face such as war, poverty, terrorism, unemployment and migration. However, a large  portion of our current pains are owed to developments that were imposed on us in the recent past. Due to its geopolitical  and geostrategic location, Afghanistan in its recent history has been a victim of the games and goals of conflicting  regional and global powers, and is now facing an imposed war of terrorism and radicalism. Fortunately, despite these  problems, pains and hardships, the Afghan people’s first ambition is to become literate and to gain knowledge in order to  effect positive change and make progress.
10.7 At the time of the collapse of Taliban regime, Afghanistan had less than one million boys going to 3,400 schools,  where 20,700 teachers were teaching and no girl had the right to education. At that time, there were no national standard  curriculum and textbooks. In literacy programmes, only 22,000 male students received education through literacy  courses and no woman could access such programmes. Not one female teacher was available in all of the schools.  However, in the post-Taliban era, these statistics have improved in an unprecedented manner. For example, in 2016, the  number of students reached more than 9.2 million, while 39% were female students, and more than 17,000 schools were  being used by more than 200,000 teachers, of which 33% were female. In terms of literacy, in the year 1395 of the  Afghan calendar (2016), 18,729 literacy courses were established to teach 417,808 students, 55% of whom were women.  From this number, 319,877 students could graduate. I should mention that one of the important literacy programmes,  managed by UNESCO and funded by Japan, Sweden and Finland, is scheduled to end in the near future. I wish to  warmly thank these three countries and UNESCO for their support, and strongly request UNESCO and our international
49  partners to provide further funding for this programme until we can achieve an increased level of literacy as we have  pledged in our international commitments.
10.8 The Afghan Government has undertaken many actions for literacy in recent years. These include the  preparation and implementation of a national mobilization plan for the elimination of illiteracy, offering incentives for  literacy in public offices, the design of a plan for private sector engagement in the implementation of literacy programmes,  the signing of a number of Memoranda of Understanding for joint cooperation in better implementation of literacy  programmes between and among ministries and independent offices, the holding of cabinet-level committee meetings  under the chairmanship of the Second Vice-President, and the holding of numerous meetings at provincial, district, and  village levels. The Government of National Unity is paying great attention to the issue of literacy, with the Afghan First  Lady, Ms Rola Ghani, recently appointed as Literacy Ambassador, and President Ghani also paying special attention to  the expansion and strengthening of literacy programmes in the country.
10.9 Unfortunately, despite all this progress, lately we have been receiving alarming news about the education sector  and the number of children who are being deprived of attending school. According to a Ministry of Education report, the  number of children who are deprived from attending school is close to three million, most of them girls. Furthermore,  Afghanistan is ranked among those countries with the lowest literacy rates in the world. The literacy rate for men (15  years and above) is 62% and for women is 18%, while the average percentage for low-revenue countries is around 70%  and 57% respectively. Only one out of every five women is literate in Afghanistan, and the literacy rate for women in rural  areas is three times lower than that of women in urban areas. A report released three weeks ago by Humans Rights  Watch stated that the number of girls deprived of education in Afghanistan has increased in recent years, while efforts to  obtain the required conditions for girls’ education have diminished. According to the report, issues such as poor security,  lack of lecture classes, lack of teachers and under-age marriages have prevented an estimated 206,000 girls from  attending school. The report highlights the deterioration of the security situation in Afghanistan as having a direct impact  on women’s participation in educational institutions.
10.10 The renovation and reform of our educational structures and laws is one of the most important priorities of the  Afghan Government. We are aware of the flaws in our old educational system. As well as working to reform the  educational structures, we are trying to change our approach to literacy and education. We have modified and finalized  the third educational strategic plan that will be used for the next five years (2017-2021). Moreover, revision of our  curriculum and educational textbooks in line with global standards and Afghanistan’s international commitments is  another necessity. A special high council on human resources has also been established under the chairmanship of  H.E. President Ghani to lead the education and higher education sectors. Therefore, the Afghan Government requests  UNESCO to assist us in promoting literacy, educational curriculum revision, developing efficient policies, training  educational curriculum specialists, female teacher capacity building and providing education for vulnerable groups of  society.
10.11 War and insecurity, widespread poverty, lack of buildings for more than 50% of schools, lack of professional  teachers, lack of educational equipment and radical and anti-science views of some circles, are just some of the most  important challenges facing education in Afghanistan. Despite the Afghan Government’s and international society’s  persistent struggle against terrorism, war and insecurity still remains the most important challenge in Afghanistan. We  are facing terrorism and an undeclared war that has roots outside our borders, where thousands of radical terrorists from  all over the world and drug mafias wage war against us with the support of some regional governments. We are on the  frontline of war against terrorism and defend all humanity from terrorism. In this respect, our expectation from the  international community, friendly countries and international organizations is to provide strong support to Afghanistan in  its war against terrorism and force supporters of the Taliban and the Islamic State in Iraq and the Levant (ISIL, also  known as Daesh) to stop supporting terrorism and come to the table for negotiation and dialogue. In that way, and by  destroying the roots of terrorism, we can pave the way for an education for all that is equal, balanced and enduring.
10.12 In terms of freedom of expression and the media, Afghanistan has witnessed rapid growth in the press and  media field in the last 15 years. During the Taliban regime, only one publication named “Sharia” and one radio station  called “Radio Sharia” existed in all Afghanistan. Today, there are 366 radio channels, 203 television channels, 72  newspapers, 354 weeklies, 344 magazines, 73 news agencies and 189 publishing companies, all active. Taken together,  some 2,455 media outlets and institutions in various categories are registered and licensed in the country.
10.13 More than anything else, the growth of media needs a conducive political atmosphere. Experience shows that  when there is an open and tolerant political system based on democracy, with good conditions for culture to flourish, a  free press and media activities thrive; when there is dictatorship and tyranny, the light of the media dies out. The rights  and freedom of people can be supported by freedom of expression. The emergence of media is equal to the emergence  of civilization, civil society and the spirit of tolerance, endurance, and the strengthening of national and transnational ties  among all ethnicities and social and cultural groups. Rapid and salient growth of media in Afghanistan over the past 15  years is mostly a result of this factor. The Afghan Government is an accountable one. It not only welcomes critical media  reports, but also considers freedom of expression and free media as a powerful tool for the provision of justice, the rule  of law and the protection of the country’s national interests. Therefore, we defend this human value and fundamental  human right.
10.14 We have also taken serious measures in providing access to information, another human right derived from the  right of freedom of expression. We have ratified the law of access to information, with all public officials obliged to  provide access to information based on this law. An access to information commission has been formed, as well as a  joint commission to provide safety to media outlets and journalists. The latter commission is comprised of members of  the media and Government under the chairmanship of the Second Vice-President. Furthermore, specific centres for
50  journalists’ safety have been established in Kabul and the provinces, and cases of violence against journalists are being  prosecuted seriously by judicial institutions. That is why terrorist groups are targeting freedom of expression and the  media and regularly threaten journalists. Scores of journalists and affiliates to media outlets have been killed and injured  or have been threatened or intimidated. Terrorist groups have proven beyond doubt that they are not bound to any  democratic value or freedom of speech and have consistently targeted the media in their attacks.
10.15 Our country has more than 5,000 years of cultural heritage and has witnessed numerous civilizations during  different periods of human history. A small but important part of Afghanistan’s archaeological heritage is kept in the  National Museum of Asian Arts here in Paris that I visited two days ago. There are artefacts from 200 BCE up to the  eighth century from different areas of Afghanistan such as Bamiyan, the Shotorak region of Kapisa, Fondokistan of  Parwan, Bagram, the Gandahara region, and Jalalabad. They have been transferred from Afghanistan to Paris by the  French Archaeological Delegation in Afghanistan (DAFA). I would like to take this opportunity to sincerely thank the  French Government and DAFA for 95 years of archaeological activities in Afghanistan.
10.16 There are few countries in the world like Afghanistan in terms of natural resources, cultural and social diversity,  sub-cultural differences and coexistence of various ethnic groups. Afghanistan has beautiful scenery and interesting  landscapes. It also has many ancient historical monuments and sites, all reminders of different parts of Afghanistan’s rich  history. These include the destroyed Buddha statues of Bamiyan, the cities of Zuhaak and Gholghola and Chehel Borj in  Bamiyan, the Herat Central Mosque and fortress, the minarets and tomb of Khawaja Abdullah Ansari in Herat,  Balahesaar, Sultan Mahmood’s tomb, Sanayee tomb in Ghazni, Kherqa-e-Mobaraka (the sacred cloak of the prophet  Muhammad). Other sites of interest include the Baba Garden in Kandahar, the Shrine of Ali in Mazar-e-Sharif and other  historical remnants which exists in most of our provinces. Despite the Afghan Government’s attempts to protect historical  sites, many have been impacted by years of war and violence, and other natural and human hazards. In some cases,  they face the threat of destruction.
10.17 Some of our historical sites such as the Minaret and Archaeological Remains of Jam and the Cultural  Landscape and Archaeological Remains of the Bamiyan Valley have already been inscribed on the UNESCO World  Heritage List, and we expect the City of Herat, Bagh-e-Babur and Band-e-Amir to join the List too and to receive more  support in strengthening our archaeology centres. We request UNESCO and the international community to assist us in  protecting our historical heritage. Today, I would like to thank Japan, Germany, Italy, France, China, the Republic of  Korea, UNESCO, the Agha Khan Foundation and other international partners for having organized an event on the  reconstruction of the destroyed Buddhas of Bamiyan, in Tokyo, in September 2017. Although the Buddhas of Bamiyan  were destroyed by the Taliban, Bamiyan is the only city in central Asia and the Middle East combining historical value,  antiquities, indigenous culture and handicrafts and was chosen as part of the Creative Cities Network by UNESCO.  Earlier this year, the film “My City Bamiyan” was awarded first prize ahead of 54 other films from member countries of the  Creative Cities Network in the Small World Film Festival organized by UNESCO. Bamiyan was also chosen as the  cultural capital of the South Asian Association for Regional Cooperation (SAARC) in 2015.
10.18 Finally, ladies and gentlemen, I would like to emphasize that terrorists and radical groups who are enemies to  education and freedom of expression, are also enemies to historical and archaeological heritage. The Taliban destroying  the ancient Buddhas of Bamiyan in 2001 and ISIL targeting other historical and archaeological sites in Syria are  unforgivable crimes. Let us lead a global cultural movement against terrorism by repairing destroyed historical sites and  protecting remaining heritage and strengthening the cultures of all nations. UNESCO should take the lead in this  movement. We believe a world with UNESCO is more beautiful and brighter. A world without UNESCO would be a dark  and lightless world. In a world without education, science and culture, only terror, fear and violence would rule. Thank  you for your attention.
