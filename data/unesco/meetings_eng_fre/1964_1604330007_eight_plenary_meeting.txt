2.4 Unesco, like all other international organizations, has to deal with Member States  which are at different levels of development. Its activities, therefore, are of two types:
(1) those which serve the needs of all mankind, no matter to which country they belong; and
(11) those which cater to the requirements of each individual country according to the posi-  tion it has reached, I propose, inter alia, to examine the various parts of the programme  from the point of view of the requirements of countries which are more or less at the same  stage of development as my'country.
2.5 In the developing countries, whether of Africa or Asia or Latin America, there is a  coexistence of centuries. On the one hand, living in an era which is between the jet age and  the space age, we read about controlled satellites, planet probes and the probability of hu-  man exploration of the moon. On the other hand, the majority of our people are illiterate,  following their traditional ways of looking after livestock. The foundations required for  Wving in a modern age are, indeed, very weak in our country. Many of our educated people  are dazzled by the material advances made by some countries and wish to enjoy the same  standards of living as prevail in those countries. But they have to learn that it is only by  hard work and striving that they can telescope the period of two centuries which these coun-  tries have taken to reach their present standards into a shorter period of two decades or  so.
2.6 Anthropologists tell us that Somali society 1s a "pastoral democracy", It is a well-  knit community with a highly organized system of social security, well adapted to the phy-  sical environment of the country. This society is breaking up and our task is to combine  what is best in that society with the best that inodern science and technology have to offer.  This is a problem which the Department of Social Sciences of Unesco can study, to see how  the principles which have bound Somali society in the past can be adapted for living in a  modern society. My government, therefore, whole-heartedly supports the study on the  theme “Economic and social problems of the newly-independent countries" which is pro-  posed under the social sciences programme for 1965-1966,
