SUMMARY RECORDS OF THE MEETINGS  OF THE  OFFICIAL AND EXTERNAL RELATIONS COMMISSION  FIRST MEETING  Friday, 19 November 1948, at 8.45 p.m.  Chairman: Mr. D. KR. HARDMAN (United Kingdom)  Item 1 of the Agenda :  Session.  Opening of the  Dr. BEEBY (Assistant Director-General) opened  the session on behalf of the Director-General.  Item 2 of the Agenda Election of the  Chairman, Vice-Chairman and Rapporteur.  Dr. BEEBY (Assistant Director-General) in-  formed the Commission that the Nominations  Committee had suggested that a representative of  the United Kingdom should act as Chairman of  the Official and External Relations Commission.  The suggestion was unanimously adopted and  Mr. D. R. Hardman was elected Chairman.  The CHAIRMAN informed the Commission that  the Nominations Committee had suggested that  [ran should supply the Vice-Chairman.  The suggestion was accepted and Dr. G. H. Sa-  dighi was unanimously elected.  The CHAIRMAN reported that the Nominations  Committee had suggested that the Rapporteur  should be supplied by France.  M. SEYDOUX (France) regretted that he could  not accept and suggested that Italy should supply  the Rapporteur.  The suggestion was unanimously adopted and  M. F. G. Mameli was elected.  Item 3 of the Agenda : Adoption of the  Agenda (document 3C/OXR/1) (rev.).  Subject to the provisions of paragraph 1.2. A.4.  of document 3C/BUR/1 (rev.), the Agenda (doc-  ument 3C/OXR/1 (rev.)) was adopted. Under the provisions of document 3C/BUR/1  (rev.), the CHAIRMAN proposed that item 4 (a)  should be remitted for consideration to a joint  meeting of the Official and External Relations  Commission and the Programme and Budget  Commission.  The proposal was adopted.  Item 4 (b) of the Agenda  concerning Reports by  (document 3C/OXR/8).  Future policy  Member States  M. de BLONAY (Head of the Bureau of External  Relations) submitted document 3C/OXR/3 and  added that, out of forty-four Member States,  twenty-three had submitted reports, of which  fourteen had not arrived until the beginning of  the current session and were shortly to be dupli-  cated.  M. SEYDOUX (France) felt it necessary to  clarify certain terms in the proposed resolution.  He approved of the second paragraph (“Consi-  dering that, in accordance with Article VIII of  the Constitution...”), and agreed that Unesco  could not work without documentation from  Member States. He thought, however, that para-  graph (i) on page 2 was too wide and perhaps a  trifle vague; it demanded considerable work  from Member States and their national admini-     (°2) The Agenda of the Official and External  Relations Commission appears as Annex I to the  Commission’s Report, to be found in Volume II, p. 59.
355        strations. The larger States were fully occupied,  while the smaller States had few staff. Only such  information as directly interested Unesco should  be asked for and the demands for information  from Member States should be adjusted to the  means at the latters’ disposal.  Dr. FARAG (Egypt) agreed with the delegate  of France and proposed that a brief questionnaire  should be drawn up on the most urgent matters  on which a reply was sought from Member States.  The CHAIRMAN felt strongly that it was 1m-  possible to draft a satisfactory questionnaire at  short notice.  Following the same line as the delegate of  France, Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) sug-  gested that the second half of paragraph (1) on  page 2 (after “for 1949”) should be struck out and  replaced by “designed to furnish an account of the  activities of National Commissions”. In point of  fact Unesco’s success or failure depended primarily  on the activities of National Commissions.  Mr. CHERRINGTON (United States of America)  approved the amendment suggested by the  delegate of the United Kingdom and stressed the  desirability of fixing a time-limit both for the  receipt of reports from Member States and for the  reception by Member States of the report on the  work of the General Conference.  The CHAIRMAN felt that the wording of the  amendment proposed by the delegate of the  United Kingdom was as vague as, or vaguer than,  the original; he therefore suggested that the  latter should be retained unamended. With  regard to the suggestion by the delegate of the  United States of America, experience seemed to  indicate that the fixing of a time-limit was useless  and that there would always be laggards.  Dr. PARRA-PEREZ (Venezuela) thought it  essential to amend paragraph (1) at the foot of  page 2. The fact was that the Government of  Venezuela was swamped with a vast number of  questions, and he thought it desirable for the  report to be confined to matters connected with  the activities of National Commissions rather than  all educational, scientific or cultural activities.  M. SEYDOUX (France) proposed a draft reso-  lution based on the Director-General’s text :  “ .. that the Governments of Member States be  desired to forward, as required under Article VIII  of the Constitution, reports for the year 1949 ...”.  The reports should include in particular :
1. information on the laws and ordinances  affecting Unesco in force in the territory of Mem-  ber States;
2. details of the steps taken by Member States  for the setting up of National Commissions.  Mr. CHERRINGTON (United States of America)  was in agreement with the substance of the amend-  ment submitted by the French delegate and pro-  posed to submit a draft resolution at a later  meeting. In addition, he emphasized that it was  desirable that the Reports should deal not only  with the growth of National Commissions but  with the practical results achieved by them.  M. SEYDOUX (France) hoped to reach agree-  ment with the delegate of the United States of  America on a final text.  Dr. FARAG (Egypt) agreed with the draft reso-  lution submitted by France but thought that the  implication of the words “laws and ordinances”  was too limited, as they referred solely to the  legislative aspect of the matter; he therefore sug-  gested the phrase “laws, ordinances and other  measures”.  M. COWELL (United Kingdom) asked for a  drafting committee to be set up to prepare a text,  in the light of the various views expressed, for  later discussion by the Commission.  M. SEYDOUX (France) agreed that a drafting  committee might be set up, but it should be a  temporary one, as there were already a multitude  of committees.  Dr. PARRA-PEREZ (Venezuela) proposed that  the drafting committee should consist of the re-  presentatives of Egypt. France, the United King-  dom and the United States of America.  Adopted.  Item 4 (c) of the Agenda : Consideration of  Draft Regulations concerning Recommen-  dations to Member States and Inter-  national Conventions (document 3C/OXR/4).  M. de BLONAY (Head of the Bureau of External  Relations) submitted document 3C/OXR/4.  The CHAIRMAN suggested that the Commis-  sion should first of all discuss the document in
4 + —                                general terms and thereafter deal with it item by  item.  Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) agreed with  the Chairman and pointed out that delegates had  not had time, in the ten days it had been  available, to digest a document representing ten  months’ work by the Secretariat. He therefore  proposed that its discussion should be postponed.  Dr. FARAG (Egypt) asked the Chairman  whether the consideration of the question could  be entrusted to the Procedure Committee, of  which he was himself a member.  The CHAIRMAN pointed out that the subject  was not included in the agenda of the Procedure  Committee and that, in any case, that Committee  was not in a position to discuss it. He proposed  that document 3C/OXR/4 should be submitted to  Member States with a request for their remarks  before 15 June 1949, so that the Executive Board  could consider the matter at its July session before  submitting it to the Fourth Session of the General  Conference.  Adopted.  SECOND  Item 4 (d) of the Agenda Reports to  Member States on Conferences and Meet-  ings held on education, science and culture,  whether sponsored by Unesco or not.  Mr. INSKIP (Union of South Africa) asserted  that his country felt strongly that a number of  conferences had been held unknown to it; it had  no information regarding their results. South  Africa would like to know the date, subject and  results of conferences, and the names of those  attending.  The CHAIRMAN referred the matter for consi-  deration to the special committee which was lo  examine clearing-house activities (exchange and  documentation).  Dr. KINANY (Syria) remarked that the question  was to some extent related to the recommen-  dations to, and undertakings by, Member States to  keep their fellow-members informed of the confer-  ences or meetings they convened on matters  affecting Unesco.
