16.1 Mr. RICHARDSON (United States of America):  Mr. President, fellow delegates, the United States Government accepts with the utmost  seriousness its responsibility to participate creatively and constructively in the work of  Unesco. Our citizens are, we think, entitled to share with the peoples of all the Member  States a well-founded satisfaction in the part they have played in this noble world-wide effort  at practical co-operation in building the human framework for peace.
16. 2 Like each of your governments, the United States Government, ‘if it is to perform all  of its varied responsibilities effectively, must manage {ts affairs with the utmost care and  prudence. Like each of your government, the United States Government must take care  that available resources are committed to such purposes and in such amounts and at such  times as will most assuredly contribute effectively to the achievement of our goals, both  domestic and international, Like each of your governments, the United States Government  must review its priorities and budgetary and programme strategies to assure that its re-  sources, never anywhere near as great as the totality of the demands upon them, are al-  located so as to acheive maximum impact in overcoming the manifold problems we must  attempt to address.
16.3 In the past two years, thc discharge of this responsibility by the United States Govern-  ment has resulted in a major revision in federal priorities and strategies. Military and  space expenditures have been rapidly and systematically reduced in both absolute and rela-  tive terms. Expenditures for domestic programmes to meet problems our people feel to  be urgent in education, health and social welfare have rapidly and systematically increased.  For the first time in recent history expenditures for the latter - that is to meet domestic  human needs - exceed expenditures for defence, At the same time, downward pressures  on the total of government spending plans, have had to be intensified as never before to  combat a very serious inflationary threat. All programmes have therefore been reviewed  to eliminate or postpone every expenditure which cannot be justified on the ground of an  urgent national priority. This drive to assure that expenditures are confined within levels  consistent with maintenance of a healthy economy has gone so far that, for example, the  federal building construction programmes and projects at home and abroad have been, with  minor exceptions, cancelled or indefinitely postponed.
16.4 Thus though United States Government resources are larger, our essential problems  are the sarne as those of each of your governments - we must make each dollar go further  in effective attack on priority problems than ever before if we are to do the job our citizens ’  expect of us. In our case we also have to take into account the fact that maintenance of a  healthy economy in the United States is one of our international, as well as domestic, re-  sponsibilities.
16.5 Under these circumstances we approach the planning and budgetary decisions with  respect to international organizations and programmes, with the same serious attention and  care we try to give to all other decisions, large and small, which have financial implications.  To do less would be to apply criteria to our internatioral responsibilities we cannot afford  to apply at home. And that, neither our tax-paying cltizens nor our Congress would permit.
16.6 In the case of Unesco we have reviewed the programme and budget proposals with  great care,, with total sympathy | with their objectives and, I may. say in my own case, with  growing admiration for the systematic, progressive and on the whole careful planning ,  which seems to characterize them. While we have, here and there, found items with .  which we take issue - we shall, for example, . question in ‘the Administrative Commission  the need to begin constructing à new headquarters building in the 1971-1972 biennjum - Ne.  do not take serious issue with the overall priorities, strategies qr the rationale of pro-
12  posed programme levels. We recognize, as does everyone in this room, that these levels  are modest indeed measured against the needs so eloquently, persuasively and frequently  set forth from this podium. And we especially welcoine the determination which the  Director-General expressed last week to establish new restrictions on staff levels in the  Secretariat,
16.7’ But, Mr. President, the United States delegation must correétly and accurately re-  present to you the views of our government, and especially of our Congress, which in our  system has primary responsibility for determining both our federal revenues and our  federal expenditures. Our delegation must therefore reflect the genuine and 'serious con-  cern with which my government regards thie tendency for the budgets of nearly all inter-  national organizations to which the United States contributes, including Unesco, to expand  steadily and substantially. We cannot view'this tendency with approval in the light of all  of the other responsibilities the United States Government must discharge. Therefore,  Mr. President, the United States delegation will regretfully abstain in the vote you are  about to conduct on the provisional budget ceiling for 1971-1972,
16.8 1 spoke last week in this Conference about the need as I saw it to intensify efforts in  our. countries, especially the industrialized countries, to obtain a revived and intensified  public commitment to Unesco.
16. 9 Taking into account this need and with sincere respect and appreciation for the views  of all those who have advised us to contribute to unanimity in this vote, we are convinced  that abstention is more corisistent with what is expected of us by the United States Congress  as well as by the fiscal authorities of the Executive Branch of the United States Government.  Therefore it is also, in our view, more likely to assure an appropriate United States con-  tribution in the long run to that increased vigour and strength in Unesco which all of us in  this hall seek to achieve. Thank you, Mr. President.

40.1 Mr. VERINGA (Netherlands): ;  Mr. President, my government thinks that the Specialized Agencies should continue to  assume the rôle they have played in the past - an essential rôle in the United Nations sys-  tem - by promoting international collaboration, by co-ordinating national activities and by  executing operational programmes, and that they should make more headway in this regard.
40.2 As far as Unesco is concerned, I think that in theory an annual budget of about $45  million is insufficient if our Organization is to fulfil in a satisfactory way the objectives of  its Constitution, especially having regard to the real needs for international co-operation  in the fields of competence of the Organization.
40.3 However, this need for financial growth which my government sees clearly in theory,  must be correlated in practice with the intellectual, administrative and operational capacity  of our Organization, in order to respond to the needs of Member States and of the interna-  tional community. In formulating a judgement on this capacity the documents we have be-  fore us, the Long-Term Outline Plan for 1971-1976, the Draft Programme and Budget for
1971-1972 and the reports of the Director-General play an important rôle, On the basis  of these documents we can generally get an idea of the efficiency of the Organization and  of the concrete results of its undertakings.
40.4 In th’s connexion, I am of the opinion that progress has td be made in the direction  of more realism in the reports; by this | mean that there is need for more evaluation,  such as was excellently carried out on the subject of literacy in document 16 C/5.
