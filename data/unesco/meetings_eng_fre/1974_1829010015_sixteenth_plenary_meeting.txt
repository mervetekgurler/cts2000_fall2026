1, Le PRESIDENT. -  Mesdames et Messieurs, au moment où s'oavre cette séance de nuit, je donne la parole à  M, Averoff, représentant de la Grèce, povr lui permettre d'exercer son droit de réponse,
2,1 M, AVEROFF (Grèce). -  Monsieur le Président, Messieurs les délégués, il m'est extrêmement désagréable de monter  à cette tribune pour vous entretenir de questions qui touchent À la politique, J'aurais aimé dialo-  guer avec mes collègues tures, parler avec eux de rapprochement dans le domaine de l'éducation  et de la culture, mais je me vois obligé de prendre quelques minutes de votre temps pour démentir  ce que je crois être inexact et dire ce que je crois être vrai,
2,2 Le délégué de Chypre a véritablement le droit de ge plaindre, et il l'a fait, Pour commencer,  la délégation turque a prétendu que la délégation de Chypre ne représentait ici que la communauté  grecque, ce qui est contraire aux décisions de notre Conférence générale et de l'Assemblée géné-  rale des Nations Unies,
2,3 Autre point : le refus opposé par la Turquie à toute solution pacifique est en contradiction avec  les dispositions du Traité de garantie, qui prévoit non pas une action militaire mais des démarches  et des mesures prises en commun avec les deux autres puissances garantes. Des consultations ont  bien été entamées, mais la Turquie a précipité unilatéralement son action militaire, Et comme  S. Exc. M, le représentant de la Turquie a mis en cause le gouvernement hellénique, j'ai demandé  et obtenu d'exercer brièvement mon droit de réponse,
2,4 Je n'en abuserai pas, et me bornerai à un seul sujet, Comment peut-on affirmer que les ma-  nuels d'histoire, tant à Chypre qu'en Grèce, insufflent aux enfants la haine du Turc ? Nous met-  tons volontiers nos manuels scolaires à la disposition de l'Unesco à des fins de vérification.
2.5 Qui plus est, des savants chercheurs chypriotes étudient en ce moment les traditions et la cul-  ture musulmanes, Messieurs, la culture qui doit être sauvée à Chypre esi trois fois millénaire :  autochtone grecque dans la haute antiquité, puis classique, hellénistique et byzantine, franque sous  le règne des Lusignans, elle conserve enfin de beaux vestiges d'art musulman, .
2,6 Un autre point : le délégué turc vous a laissé entendre que l'invation de l'île était une simple  opération de police destinée à sauvegarder la minorité turque, Le gouvernement ture'a soutenu qu'il  était respectueux de la souveraineté, de l'intégrité et de la légitimité dans un pays indépendant qui
16 fait partie du système des Nations Unies, et qu'il était intervenu pour rétablir l'ordre dans une fle  de 600, 000 habitants qui, par le fait de l'invasion, compte aujourd'hui 200,000 réfugiés dont les  souffrances sont indescriptibles, Je pose donc à cette assemblée les questions suivantes :
2,7 Primo : où est l'humanisme, le souci de l'éducation, quand des miliers d'enfants sont privés  de scolarité ? Ce qui a été dit des victimes et des destructions scolaires pourrait donner 1'impres-  sion que le conquérant souffre davantage que l'occupé ; on dirait presque que c'est Chypre qui a en-  vahi la Turquie !
2.8 Secundo : où est le respect de l'environnement, alors que les forêts dont Chypre était si fière  ont été volontairement et parfois systématiquement incendiées ?
2.9 Tertio : où est enfin la justice élémentaire, quand 70 % des richesses sont exploitées par une  minorité qui les occupe et qui ne représente que 18 % de la population totale ?
2,10 11 y a encore un argument auquel j'aimerais répondre, cette fois par la bouche même de  M. Waldheim, secrétaire général des Nations Unies, On nous a dit que pendant onze ans la mino-  rité avait été opprimée par les Grecs, qu'elle était "'enclavée", Or, je vais vous lire trois lignes  du rapport Waldheim daté de novembre 1968 : "At the present tirne, the only restrictions on the  freedom of movement of the population in Cyprus are those which continue to be applied in the areas  under Turkish-Cypriot control",
2,11 Je termine, Monsieur le Président, dans le délai imparti, en langant une idée, Pour prouver  la bonne volonté de la délégation hellénique, je propose à M. le Directeur général de créer une  grande Commission de membres totalement impartiaux, qui ivaient sur place dresser un bilan des  catastrophes, dans tous les domaines qui relèvent de la compétence de l'Unesco et pour tout ce qui  doit être protégé par elle, Cette commission devrait pouvoir circuler librement, et j'ose espérer,  ou plutôt je crois fermement, que le gouvernement ture, faïsant preuve de la même bonne volonté,  ferait tout pour qu'elle puisse s'acquitter de sa difficile tâche,
2.12 Merci, Monsieur le Président,

Je donne la parole à M. Carlton Gomes, chef de la délégation de Trinité-et-Tobago.
4,1 Mr. GOMES (Trinidad and Tobago):  Mr. President, distinguished delegates, I wish on behalf of my delegation and the Government  and people of Trinidad and Tobago to join with other speakers in congratulating Mrs. Jébord onher  election to the Presidency of this eighteenth session of the General Conference of Unesco. I see  her election as not only underscoring the equality of the sexes but also as an endorsement of her  own reputation and of the service she has given to the programmes of Unesco and to the cause of  international understanding and peace.
4.2 1 should like also on behalf of my Government to record its appreciation of the devoted service  given by the retiring Director-General, Mr. René Maheu. I think the most fitting tribute we can  all pay to him {s to continue the work for which he gave so much of himself for over 20 years. I  can only hope that his talent and undoubted experience will not be lost to the international community  and that when the history of Uneaco is written his will be an honoured place,
4.3 I consider it an honour to be able to join with the other delegations In welcoming to this Confer-  ence and to the Unesco community the representatives of the people of independent Guinea-Bissau,  of San Marino and of the Democratic People's Republic of Korea. The challenge of these countries,  as indeed of ours, is to assist and to promote a world order in which each has a part to play. All  of us must join in the development of that order as the present climate will not permit of half  measures, double standards and vacillation. We must all move forward together in search of a  new policy which has as its objective the welfare of mankind.
4.4 Mr, President, the developments among States most certainly point to the fact that peoples,  whoever they are, wherever they are, cannot and will not be suppressed, cannot forever be frus-  trated socially or culturally, All natlons, young and old, developed and developing, are struggling  for genuine independence, an independence which goes further than political forms and symbols.  This search is seen in the increasing activity withinall States, particularly among the youth. New  methods of expressing a genuine humanisra towards one another and a form of government in which  the plang of the ruler and ruled are more closely orchestrated, are beingpromoted, These develop-  ments are not restricted to colonial or nco-colonial States, but are discernible in every country.  One therefore sees Unesco with a continuing rôle in the promotion of these developments. Indeed,  the Constitution states: "It ie in the minds of men that the defences of peace must be constructed",
4.5 My country, Trinidad and Tobago, has had the benefit of Unesco's help in the formulation of  its 16-year Education Plan. The Plan is based on 15 objectives, the main targets being: primary  and junior secondary education for all; senior secondary education (academic and technical) for 40%
402   of the eligible age group (now being reviewed in the light of the demands made by the population for  more school places at that level): expansion of the Library Service; increased in-service training  programmes to include members of the supervisory service. Unesco has also assisted in the exe-  cution of this Plan, Between 1968 and 1974 help was given with regard to curriculum planning,  advice on technical and vocational education, and test planning, to name a few areas. Iam sure  that opportunities will be made available from time to time for more detailed exchanges of ideas  and information,
4.6 Distinguished delegates, my delegation fully endorses the proposals set forth in documents
18 C/4 and 18 C/5, and would strongly urge Member States to match their concern and approval  with the necessary funds, I cannot too strongly emphasize that man's hope for a better world lies  in a truly educated population. The maintenance and development of a civilized world lies in a  truly educated community, in which each person is his brother's keeper, in which responsibilities  and rights are accepted, observed and honoured.
4.7 Mr. President, my brief remarks here tonight relate mainly to education. At the risk of  repetition I would like to put some {ssues to this Conference which I feel must concern all of us  and which can bear further detailed examination and consideration by Unesco in both ita short-  term and long-term programmes.
4.8 First Is the need for liberal education, whatever the field of study, for as I see it this is the  surest foundation for the fostering of a society of civilized human beings and for facilitating adapta-  bility in a world of rapid changes, increasing technology and new work situations. Second is the  question of adequate training and retraining of teachers in the light of new demands and new needs,  Third is the satlafying of demands for more school places at all levels and the question of main-  taining standards in the process of the democratization of education. There is also the need to  guard against the oversupply of personnel in particular fields and the need, and a most important  need at that, to break down the prejudices between the so-called academic studies and vocational  and technical education. Fourth is the need for continuing education. This cannot be considered  a luxury in these changing times. Yesterday's challenges are with us today and will increase  tomorrow and in the years ahead. Fifth is the search for improved methods of assessment, Sixth,  there is need to pay increased attention to the disadvantaged of any society and to the special needs  of the developing societies. Seventh, there must be continuing programmes in curriculum renewal.  Eighth, there should be increased research into cheaper school structures. Ninth, special studies  should be undertaken on the problems of youth and the proper use of increased leisure which is now  Available to all. Tenth, there is an urgent need to review the administrative machinery in education  in order to make it more readily responsive to the needs of the people who serve within the system  and of the general public. There must be an effective marriage between theory and practice, A  conscious effort must be made to keep the entire system informed of plans and programmes, What  touches all must involve all. The crisis in communication is a real problem and calls for detailed  examination and resolution if any appreciable march forward is to be made.
4.9 Mr. President, one sees and feels an immense goodwill at this Conference. We must now  seek to sustain and nourish this goodwill in all fields of human endeavour. This must mean a real  appreciation of the habits and customs of other countries. My own country has, in its small way,  shown how peoples of different ethnic backgrounds can live together and forge common cultural  links, as is evidenced in our celebration of Carnival and in many other Trinidad festivals.
4,10 Mr, President, the main purpose of education and indeed of Unesco must be the continued  building of the bonds of friendship which will ultimately result in reducing world tension in all  countries, developed and developing, rich and poor. There is no doubt in my mind that at what-  ever level we operate, our aim must be the undying vision of creating a new and real humanism.  This {e the cause for which we must all labour,
4.11 To Mr. M'Bow my couniry's best wishes are extended on his nomination as the next Director-  General of Unesco. His will be no easy exercise, given the imponderables associated with such a  high office, but I am sure he can rely on the tremendous goodwill of a dedicated body of interna-  tional civil servants as well as the co-operation of the countries of the Unesco community.
4,12 Finally, Mr. President, I wish to indicate my country's support of Unesco and its subsidiary  agencies and to offer to the Intergovernmental Oceanographic Commission, as a co-ordinating  centre for CICAR or its successor body, our Natlonal Institute for Marine Affairs in Trinidad. I  trust’ that this small contribution will further assist in fostering international co-operation in the  area of Marine Science.
4.13 Mr. President, the years ahead will be difficult ones, but with the goodwill, determination  and courage of all, we will overcome our problems for they are in the main made by man and must  be solved by man,
