(5. 1) Mr. BETAïKCUR MEJIA (Colombia) (Vice-Chairman of the Executive Board) (Translation  from the Spanish):  Mr. President, Mr. Director-General, fellow delegates, 1 should like first to convey to the  President of the General Conference the congratulations of the Executive Board on his election to  preside over the debates of this third extraordinary session, and to offer him our best wishes for  its complete success.
(5. 2) It falls to me, as Vice-Chairman of the Executive Board, in the absence of the Chairman,  H. E. Mr. Fûad Sarrûf, who is unable to be present, to submit on his behalf and on that of the  Board information on the study made for the purpose of finding ways of dealing with the financial  and budgetary situation by which the Organization has been confronted due to factors beyond its  control, such as inflation and the devaluation of the currency of accounting.
(5. 3) At the 92nd session of the Executive Board, held in April and May last, the Director-  General presented, for consideration by the Board, document 92 EX/15 concerning the effects of  the devaluation of the US dollar on the financing of the operation of the Organization and its activi-  ties. According to Unesco’s Financial Regulations, the Organization’s unit of account is the US  dollar. On the other hand, 75 per cent of its expenditure is payable in French francs and 5 per  cent in other currencies likewise affected by devaluation, with the result that 80 per cent of the  budget is affected by the devaluation of the US dollar. The budget for 1973-1974 was established  on the basis of an operational exchange rate of 5. 01 French francs to one US dollar. Consequent-  ly the 10 per cent devaluation of the dollar on 12 February 1973 seriously affected the Organiza-  tion’s budget.
(5. 4) The Director-General estimated that, as a result of this devaluation, an additional sum of  $10,257,000 would be required in order to maintain the programme level as approved by the  General Conference. He added that, due to inflation, the additional costs entailed during the bien-  nium would amount to $7,691,000. Lastly, he drew the Board’s attention to the cash problem  occasioned by the delay of Member States in paying their contributions.
(5. 5) The Director-General suggested measures to the Executive Board which might be taken to  remedy the situation. The Board undertook a detailed examination of the situation, in view of the  fact that it affected the implementation of the programme approved by the seventeenth session of  the General Conference. After checking, with the help of the Finance and Administrative Com-  mission, the figures submitted by the Director-General, and finding that they entirely reflected  the actual situation, the Board investigated ways of coping firstly, with the consequences of in-  flation, secondly, with the effects of devaluation and lastly, with the cash situation.
(5.6) The Board realized from the outset how seriously the increase of costs due to devaluation  and inflation, amounting in a11 to $17,948,000, would affect the Organization, and it was for this  reason that it discussed the possibility of convening an extraordinary session of the General Con-  ference, which was the only body competent to adopt decisions of substance for dealing satisfac-  torily with the financial situation, after the Board had exhausted a11 the means within its  competence. The Board however considered that, in regard to the decision to be taken, it was  advisable to wait and see what fluctuations would occur in the monetary situation between that
3XCf2  time and the autumn session, and then to decide on the basis of a realistic assessment, on the  most appropriate additional measures.
(5. 7) In order to help meet the additional costs due to devaluation, and availing itself of the  authorization it had been given by the seventeenth session of the General Conference, the Board  provisionally approved a supplementary budget of $2, 988,850, corresponding to 2. 5 per cent of  the current budget. In addition, it asked the Director-General to submit to it “a report on the  financial situation of the Organization as of 1 June and revised as of 1 August 197 3”. It further  requested him, in anticipation of the need to curtail activities SO as partially to eliminate the  deficit, to submit “a table of programme activities for 1973-1974 which would have to be deferred  or cancelled if a decision to reduce the programme were contemplated, with an indication of the  impact on the growth of the programme approved by the General Conference at its seventeenth  session” ; and it earnestly advocated that the Director-General continue and further strengthen  the present policy of financial austerity in order to achieve additional savings where possible.
(5. 8) As regards the possible convening of an extraordinary session of the General Conference,  The Board instructed the Director-General that, in informing Member States concerning the finan-  cial situation of the Organization, he should immediately notify them of the possibility of the  Executive Board’s convening an extraordinary session of the General Conference in the month of  October SO as to enable the Conference, in that event, to take the necessary measures for ensur-  ing the implementation of the Organization’s programme. Lastly, the Board invited the Director-  General “to contribute, within the organizations of the United Nations system, to every effort  towards finding solutions to ensure the future financial viability of the organizations of the system  and the integrity of their programmes, and to submit a report on this question to the Executive  Board at one of its subsequent meetings”.
(5. 9) In regard to the financing of the additional costs of $7,691,000 due to inflation, the Board  thanked the Director-General for the information he had provided concerning possible savings  amounting to $2,052,000, and noted that these would not appreciably affect the development of the  programme. Since the General Conference at its last session had approved an Appropriation Re-  serve of $4,309,564 within the current budget, the Board authorized the transfer of this reserve;  it had also adopted the system of semi-full budgeting, under which only inflation costs in the first  year of the biennium would be covered by the Appropriation Reserve, while the amounts not covered  by the latter would have to be financed from savings within the budget, and the Board invited the  Director-General to apply the criteria it had laid down at its 88th session, as follows:  “(i) programme activities which, due to external factors, had failed, or might fail to pro-  duce the expected results;  (ii) programme activities which had already achieved their purpose and were not there-  fore planned in the future;  (iii) programme activities which, in the rational execution of the Medium-Term Plan,  might be postponed to the next biennium”.
(5. 10) TO remedy the cash situation, the Board urged a11 those Member States which had not  already done SO to pay as soon as possible their contributions both to the Working Capital Fund  and to the Budget; and it asked the Director-General to transmit this appeal to the States in ques-  tion. These decisions were approved in decision 6. 12 of the 92nd session of the Executive Board.
(5. 11) At the beginning of its autumn session, which has just corne to an end, the Executive  Board examined item 7. 2 of its agenda, calling for a review of the general financial situation of  the Organization, and following logically from decision 6. 12 referred to above. The Director-  General presented first document 93 EX/15, Part 1, which contained a review of the financial  situation as of 1 June; then this same document revised as of 1 August; and lastly as of 1 September.  This document showed how the fluctuations of the US dollar had modified the estimated additional  costs; in May these had risen to $10,257,000 and by 1 August, with the fa11 in the rate of exchange  of the dollar in relation to the French franc, had reached $19,356,000; later, when the value of  the dollar rose by 20 points, the figure fell again by 1 September to $15,852, 900. The additional  costs due to inflation amounted to $7, 847, 800, or $156,800 more than the sum estimated in  decision 6. 12 of the 92nd session of the Executive Board.
(5. 12) In document 93 EX/ 15, Part II, the Director-General reported on the measures taken to  give effect to the decisions of the Board regarding the savings which could be made on the imple-  mentation of the programme, which amounted to $552,686; the savings resulting from austerity  measures, which amounted to $1,500,000; and the additional savings requested, which the Director-  General reckoned at $1,480,750, through an increase in the efficiency of the Secretariat. These  sums, combined with the use of the Appropriation Reserve of $4,309,564, were equal, as of 1 June,  to the inflation costs, i. e. $7, 843,000.
(5. 13) The document under reference provided the Board with the relevant details in regard to  the programme sectors affected, transfers between sectoral budgets, and the use made of the  Appropriation Reserve, as approved by the Board.
(5. 14) Document 93 EX/15 Part III contains a list of possible reductions in the programme cal-  culated to produce a saving of $2, 117,015, which the Director-General presented in conformity  with paragraph 17 of decision 6. 12 referred to above, indicating the impact which these reductions  would, if approved, have on the growth of the programme. These possible savings, together with  those described to meet the costs of inflation, in effect cancelled growth, since they entailed a  reduction of from 5. 6 to 0. 79 per cent of the overall recosted base, the Education and Communi-  cation sectors being the most affected. In this document, the Director-General presented his  practical comments on the consequences for the execution of the programme that would derive  from approval of additional reductions equivalent to the above-mentioned sum.
(5. 15) At the opening of the discussion on this item of the agenda, the Director-General made an  extensive survey of the situation, referring specifically to the documents mentioned above and  explaining to the Board in detail the measures he had taken to give effect to the decisions adopted  by the Board at its previous session. Then, after stressing the even more difficult situation  with which the Organization was faced in September, he presented a number of suggestions, to  which the Board gave careful consideration and on which he proposed to enlarge later. As regards  the cash situation, he mentioned the ready response that had been made to the Board’s appeal to  Member States for a prompt payment of their contributions; as a result, in the space of four  months, the Organization had received the sum of $30,000,000, representing both arrears and  contributions due for the current budgetary period. Lastly, the Director-General spoke of the  possible measures to be taken, including the necessary convening of an extraordinary session of  the General Conference to enable the Board to make definite suggestions to the Conference, which  could then consider approving the special appropriations required for ensuring the execution of  the programme within acceptable limits.
(5. 16) The Board embarked on a long debate, lasting for 11 plenary meetings, in which 34 mem-  bers took part. It was agreed to decide initially whether or not an extraordinary session of the  Conference should be convened, the first step being to ask the Finance and Administrative Com-  mission to study the figures submitted by the Director-General. Following a thorough study, the  Commission, through its Chairman, Mr. Hector Wynter, informed the Board that the net costs  quoted by the Director-General - $7,847,900 on account of economic inflation and $15,852,100  on account of devaluation - were based on technically sound and reasonable estimates in the light  of the information currently available.
(5. 17) The Board took the view that, having made savings and curtailments in the programme,  arranged for the transfer of the Appropriation Reserve and established a prcvisional extra-  ordinary budget, it had exhausted its possibilities for action and that only the General Conference,  with its sovereign powers, had competence to approve an extraordinary budget to caver the deficit.  The Board recalled that at its previous session, it had likewise considered this possibility, but  had left the matter in suspense in the hope that the monetary situation would become more favour-  able to the interests of Unesco. Since the situation had not improved, it was necessary to convene  an extraordinary session of the Conference, for a specific purpose, i. e. “consideration of the  financial situation of the Organization caused by the prevailing inflation and the devaluation of the  currency of accounting, and measures to be taken in respect thereto”; the Board drew up its  provisional agenda, consisting of this item, together with the other items with which you are  acquainted, and fixed the dates of the Conference: from 23 to 27 October. This decision was  approved by 34 votes in favour and none against, with 3 abstentions.
(5. 18) Following the establishment of the net amount of the costs due to devaluation, and before  deciding on the size of the supplementary budget to be proposed to the General Conference, the  Board held a full discussion on the two different points of view expressed: on the one hand, that  additional savings could be made on the amount of $2,217,000 mentioned in document 93 EX/15,  Part III, without substantially altering the programme; on the other, that the additional savings  made to finance the costs due to devaluation would not only result in a considerable curtailment  of activities fundamental to Member States, but would also virtually cancel the 5. 6 percent growth  rate which had been fixed by the General Conference, and which was already lower than the 7 per  cent rate indicated as a working hypothesis by the General Conference at its sixteenth session.  Other members of the Board referred to the possibility of finding an intermediate solution, At  this juncture, 1 should like to mention the very important speech on this suhject delivered hy  H. E. Mr. Toru Haguiwara, President of the General Conference, with his customary authority  and sense of realism, which elucidated the discussion by reference to the debate and the vote in  the General Conference when the budget ceiling was approved.
(5. 19) Dr. Sarrûf, Chairman of the Executive Board, spoke of the Board’s heavy responsibility  in the matter under discussion; he thought its members should endeavour to find formulas which  would represent a consensus, and this, judging by the speeches made, he believed to be possible.  He then proposed the establishment of a Working Party,’ which would have the support of the Board  and he responsible for performing this delicate task, in which the Director-General offered his  full collaboration. This Working Party consisted of Messrs. Abdelwahab El Borolossy (Arab  Republic of Egypt), Tooryalay Etemadi (Afghanistan), Josef Grohman (Czechoslovakia), Aklilu  Habte (Ethiopia) - replaced subsequently by Mr. Yuda Komora (Kenya) - Leonard Martin (United  Kingdom), and Hector Wynter (Jamaica). After four days of concentrated work, involving meet-  ings in the mornings, afternoons and evenings, the Working Party, through its Chairman,  Senator Wynter, submitted a practical proposal, reflecting the spirit of co-operation of its mem-  bers and of the Director-General, to the effect that further savings amounting to $1,600,000  would be made, on the basis of the following criteria:  “(i) any savings that may occur within the specific budget of a particular project should in  general be used to help the Organization’s financial deficit and should not, save in ex-  ceptional circumstances, be used to reinforce the budget of another project;  (ii) postponement to the second half of the biennium of staff appointments when the execu-  tion of the programme activities in question has not yet begun;  (iii) certain reductions should be effected when this Will not appreciahly harm the integrity  of the programme;  (iv) postponement to the 1975-1976 biennium of a part of the total appropriation for a  specific activity. ”
(5. 20) The application of these criteria would entai1 a minimum of detriment to the execution of  the programme and would make it possible to identify, in the table submitted by the Director-  General in Annex II of document 93 EX/15, Part III, savings amounting to the agreed sum.
(5. 21) The consensus reached by the Executive Board, thanks to the Working Party, was con-  firmed by the Board by 27 votes in favour and none against, with six abstentions, as is shown in  decision 7. 2, Parts II and III.
(5. 22) In Part II of this decision, the Board expressed its appreciation to the Director-General  for the action taken in response to its resolution 6. 12 adopted at the spring session. It referred  to the proposed reductions which would have an adverse effect on the growth rate; reaffirmed  the need to absorb the effects of inflation by means of savings and curtailments in the programme  while endeavouring not to harm its integrity; recognized that, in addition to the measures taken  to offset the costs of inflation for 1973, it would be necessary in 1974 to absorb additional costs  by similar means; and approved transfers from the Appropriation Reserve. as well as transfers  and adjustments between the various appropriation lines.
(5. 23) In Part III, the Board accepted the figure of $15,852,100 as of 1 September as represent-  ing the extra cost to the budget arising as a consequence of the devaluation of the unit of account;  noted the savings and transfers effected to meet the costs of inflation ($7,847, 900); anticipated  that additional savings might have to be made in the 1974 programme to meet further inflation,  and reaffirmed that these inflationary costs must be absorbed more especially by savings and  economies. The Board was accordingly of the opinion that it would not be feasible to finance the
3XC12  whole of the deficit due to devaluation by economies in the programme since such economies would  have a detrimental effect on its execution, but that additional economies would have to be made in  order to counter the effects of monetary fluctuations. The Board therefore invited the Director-  General to make further savings to the amount of $1,600,000, by identifying reductions which  would result in the least damage to the coherence and mutually reinforcing aspects of the principal  segments of an integrated programme and which would not reduce Unesco’s capacity to co-operate  with and assist other international organizations and regional institutions. Lastly, the Board  decided to submit to the General Conference at its third extraordinary session the relevant supple-  mentary estimates.
(5. 24) 1 should like now to refer to the figures which the Board proposes in document 3 XC/4 as  supplementary appropriations, and to the additional contributions of Member States. Firstly, in  paragraph 28, Part 1, sub-paragraph (a), the Board recommends that the General Conference  give its final approval to the supplementary estimates provisionally approved by the Executive  Board. In suh-paragraph (b), it requests approval of the supplementary estimates totalling  $14,252,100 - this amount being obtained bydeductingfrom the net devaluation costs - $15,852,100 -  the sum of $1,600,000 which the Director-General has been asked to save, specifying its impact  on the programme, as set forth by him on pages 3 and 4 of document 3 XC/3.
(5. 25) In Part II, the Board asks that the contributions resulting from the assessments on new  Member States for the years 1973-1974 may be accepted as miscellaneous income and used to  reduce assessments on Member States in that financial period. Sub-paragraph (d) indicates that  the supplementary appropriation Will be financed by contributions from Member States in accor-  dance with the scale of assessments; and in sub-paragraph (e) the sum of $1,600,000 derived  from contributions to finance this supplementary appropriation i s regarded as miscellaneous in-  corne. Thus, by subtracting from $14,252,100 the $1,600,000 of miscellaneous income, the  additional contribution is reduced to $12,652,100. This is because, in the event of the rate of exchange  of the dollar in relation to the French franc improving, this sum may be reduced; and, in the  contrary event, as stated in sub-paragraph (g), the Director-General would propose to the Execu-  tive Board and, if necessary, to the next session of the General Conference, appropriate measures  to tope with the situation.
(5. 26) In Part III of the draft resolution, the Executive Board, conscious of the possible finan-  cial difficulties with which the General Conference and the Director-General may have to contend,  despite the approval of supplementary appropriations, because the rate of payment of the supple-  mentary contributions may not be sufficiently rapid, deems it necessary to authorize the Director-  General to negotiate and contract short-term loans, within the limits of the approved supplementary  appropriations, SO as to ensure the proper execution of the programme. And lastly, in order to  reduce the possible costs of financing loans, the Board proposes appealing to Member States to  consider the possibility of offering loans to Unesco on conditions more favourable than those pre-  vailing on the commercial market or, if necessary, of guaranteeing the commercial loans.

7.1 Le DIRECTEUR GENERAL :  Monsieur le Président, Monsieur le Vice-Président du Conseil exécutif, Mesdames  et Messieurs les délégués, j’ai l’honneur de soumettre à votre bienveillante considéra-  tion les prévisions supplémentaires assorties de propositions connexes (document 3 XC/3)  que j’ai établies conformément à l’article 3. 8 du Règlement financier et à la décision 7. 2  prise par le Conseil exécutif à sa 93e session. C’est en vertu de cette décision, vous le  savez, que la Conférence générale est actuellement réunie en session extraordinaire pour  examiner la situation financiere créée par l’inflation existant dans le monde et par ladé-  valuation de la monnaie de compte de l’Organisation et pour arrêter les mesures à prendre  à cet égard.
7. 2 Le vice-président du Conseil exécutif, S. Ext. M. Betancur Mejia, vient de vous  expliquer de manière très complète par quel processus et pour quelles raisons impé-  rieuses le Conseil est parvenu à cette décision. Il vous a aussi expose, avec une par-  faite précision, les mesures qu’il vous est recommandé de prendre pour faire face ZI  la situation, mesures dont le détail vous est presenté, en méme temps qu’un projet de  résolution soumis à votre approbation, dans le document 3 XC/4.  ‘7. 3 M. Betancur Mejia a souligné - et je lui en suis fort reconnaissant - que ces  recommandations sont la traduction concrète de l’accord complet qui est intervenu  entre le Conseil, au sein duquel aucun vote négatif n’a été enregistré, et le Directeur  géneral. A mon tour je voudrais dire combien je me rejouis de cet accord qui ne doit  rien à l’improvisation ou à un compromis superficiel, mais est le fruit d’une recherche  réaliste et d’un examen approfondi auxquels se sont livrés, chacun pour sa part, le  Secretariat et le Conseil, ainsi que d’une volonté commune de compréhension réciproque

7. 33 Je voudrais maintenant parler brièvement du deuxième des problèmes sur lesquels  votre attention doit étre attirée et qui est celui de la trésorerie de l’organisation. C’est  un problème qui, pour étre moins pressant que les difficultés proprement budgétaires,  se posera cependant très bientôt. En effet, la situation de la trésorerie est extrémement  précaire et cela de manière constante. Cette précarité est due, non pas au fait que les  Etats membres ne paient pas leurs contributions - les arriérés étant exceptionnels ou  provisoires -, mais à ce que le calendrier de leurs versements ne correspond pas à  celui de nos besoins. Ce décalage est d’ailleurs naturel puisque les premiers sont  fonction des procédures parlementaires ou budgétaires nationales, tandis que les se-  conds dépendent évidemment de l’échelonnement dans le temps de l’exécution du programme.
7. 34 C’est précisément pour pallier les inconvénients résultant de cet état de choses  qu’a été institué le Fonds de roulement. Or, vous le savez, celui-ci est très insuffisant  puisqu’il n’est actuellement que de 4 millions de dollars, chiffre qui parai’tra encore  plus dérisoire lorsque le budget total sera passé, comme je le souhaite, après le vote  des crédits additionnels, de 120 millions de dollars à 134.252.000 dollars. Il y a là  une inadéquation, une disparité, qui est extrémement genante du point de vue de la bonne  gestion de l’Organisation et il conviendra que la Conférence générale y remédie le plus  tôt possible en vue d’assurer à notre trésorerie la souplesse indispensable. De fait, il  y a tout lieu de penser que, vu son faible montant, le Fonds de roulement sera très  prochainement épuisé et le demeurera pendant une grande partie de 1974. Dansquelques  semaines, en effet, la trésorerie sera déficitaire parce que, face au solde non encore  versé (19 millions de dollars) des contributions pour 1973 qui représente 35 % du total  des sommes à recevoir, le volant de sécurité dont nous disposons est seulement de
4 millions de dollars.
7. 35 Monsieur le Président, Mesdames, Messieurs, il est dans ces conditions abso-  lument indispensable que j’aie l’autorisation - que vous seuls pouvez me donner - de  contracter des emprunts à court terme pour faire face aux besoins de trésorerie et je  remercie le Conseil d’avoir reconnu cette nécessité dans la section III, paragraphe (h),  du projet de résolution qu’il soumet à votre approbation. Du point de vue pratique, je  tiens à vous informer que le Secrétariat a déjà effectué diverses études et procédé à  certaines consultations dont il ressort que, compte tenu du caractère très variable de  nos besoins et de la cherté actuelle du loyer de l’argent, les emprunts en question ne
3XCl2  seraient pas contractés pour un montant et à un taux détermines une fois pour toutes,  mais revétiraient la forme de “crédits rotatifs” utilisables selon les besoins (revolving  credits) et portant intéret à des taux fluctuant en fonction de l’évolution du marché. Ce  seraient là les meilleures conditions possibles pour des emprunts commerciaux. Mais  il n’en serait pas moins préférable - et à mon sens naturel - que les Etats membres  étudient la possibilité, comme le Conseil exécutif a bien voulu vous proposer de le leur  recommander au paragraphe (i) du projet de résolution, de consentir à l’Organisation  des préts assortis de taux plus favorables que ceux du marché commercial, préts qui  ne porteraient au reste que sur des sommes relativement faibles. Sur ce point aussi,  des négociations officieuses sont en cours et j’ai bon espoir qu’elles pourront aboutir  à des propositions nous évitant de recourir, au moins pour une partie de nos besoins,  à des emprunts bancaires de type commercial.
7. 36 Pour terminer, je voudrais dire quelques mots du troisième problème - celui-là  à plus long terme - que soulève la situation financière actuelle. Comme M. Betancur  MejTa a eu raison de le souligner à la fin de son exposé, il s’agira d’éviter à l’avenir  l’apparition de déficits dus à la dépréciation de la monnaie de compte de l’Organisation.  M6me si les perturbations du système monétaire international sont passagères et méme  si un nouveau dispositif suffisamment stable est finalement mis en place, il serait bon  que les organisations du système des Nations Unies recherchent dès aujourd’hui les  moyens de se prémunir contre des maux du genre de ceux auxquels elles sont aujourd’hui  confrontées, Naturellement, les solutions à imaginer ne devront pas s’appliquer uni-  quement à lWnesco, mais mutatis mutandis à l’ensemble des institutions du système  dont elle fait partie et qui sont toutes affectées à des degrés divers et de manière diffé-  rente par les fluctuations du dollar des Etats-Unis. A cette fin, il y a lieu de procéder  à des consultations en vue de coordonner les mesures qui pourraient éventuellement  étre prises.
7. 37 Toutefois, la recherche de la coordination ne doit pas exclure, pour telle ou telle  organisation, la possibilité de prendre des initiatives, faute de quoi on risquerait fort  de voir se perpétuer le statu quo. C’est dans cet esprit alliant la volonté de concertation  à l’action individuelle que des consultations ont déjà commencé entre les secrétariats  des organisations du système des Nations Unies, consultations auxquelles j’ai person-  nellement participé. Et il n’est pas indifférent de noter que 1’Unesco a été la première  à entreprendre l’étude de cette importante et difficile question dans le cadre d’un comité  spécial du Conseil exécutif qui s’est réuni au mois d’août 1972. Depuis lors, les échanges  de vues se sont étendus à la totalité du système des Nations Unies sous les auspices du  Comité administratif de coordination (CAC) lequel a envisagé plusieurs possibilités qui  sont présentement étudiées par le Comité consultatif pour les questions administratives
3XC/2  et budgétaires (CCQAB) de l’Assemblée générale des Nations Unies. Bien entendu,  j’ignore quel sera le résultat final des consultations en cours, mais le problème me  semble suffisamment simple pour que deux types de solutions apparaissent dès mainte-  nant pouvoir étre retenus.
7. 38 Selon une première hypothèse, la monnaie de compte de l’organisation interna-  tionale resterait une monnaie nationale et, dans ce cas, je ne verrais aucune raisonde  renoncer à utiliser le dollar des Etats-Unis. Toutefois, on risquerait alors de connaftre  à nouveau des situations dans lesquelles, en cas de hausse du dollar, lesEtats membres  auraient à verser des contributions supérieures à ce dont l’Organisation a besoin pour  faire fonctionner ses services et exécuter son programme, ou, en cas de dépréciation  de cette monnaie, à combler le déficit budgétaire par une combinaison variable de ré-  ductions d’activités et d’ouvertures de crédits additionnelles. A mon avis, la seule  façon de ne plus se trouver en butte à de tels problèmes, tout en gardant le système  actuel, ce serait pour la Conférence générale de “geler” le montant en valeur absolue  des contributions au niveau résultant du taux de change du dollar en vigueur au moment  de l’adoption du budget.
7. 39 Par contre, si on se refuse à adopter cette mesure qui permettrait d’éviter les  perturbations résultant de l’emploi comme monnaie de compte d’une devise nationale  au cours nécessairement variable, il faudra faire appel à une unité de référence inter-  nationale dont la valeur sera indépendante des fluctuations des marchés des changes.  Dans l’état actuel du monde, seuls les droits de tirages spéciaux (DTS) dont le Fonds  monétaire international assure la gestion me paraissent pouvoir remplir cette fonction  d’unité de compte.
7.40 Voilà donc la position que j’ai cru devoir prendre - en tant que Directeur général  de l’Unesco, mais sans engager les instances souveraines de l’Organisation - à l’occa-  sion des discussions qui ont eu lieu entre les secrétariats des organisations du système  des Nations Unies. Et sauf objections ou directives contraires de votre part, j’envisage  de poursuivre mes efforts dans ce sens au cours des prochains échanges de vues entre  les membres du CAC.
7.41 En conclusion, Mesdames et Messieurs, tout en formulant le voeu que ce dernier  problème soit examiné à la dix-huitième session de la Conférence générale, je me per-  mets de souhaiter, en ce qui concerne les questions assurément plus urgentes de la  trésorerie et surtout du déficit budgétaire, que vous adoptiez le projet de résolution qui  vous est présenté par le Conseil exécutif. Et compte tenu de ce que, comme je l’ai dit,  ce texte reflète fidèlement l’accord qui s’est fait à la fois entre les membres du Conseil  et entre le Conseil et le Directeur général, je serais particulièrement heureux que le  m@me consensus se manifeste au sein de la Conférence générale et se traduise par un  vote aussi unanime que possible.

(11. 1) Mr. ZEMSKOV (Union of Soviet Socialist Republics) (Translation from the Russian):  Mr. President, delegates, we are assembled here to discuss the subject of Unesco’s budget-  ary deficit resulting from the devaluation of the US dollar and inflation. The delegation of the  USSR takes a serious view both of the financial position of the Organization and of the causes res-  ponsible for it. That is only natural. The Soviet Union - a country with the most advanced type  of social and State structure, unaffected by any of the financial and currency crises and other UP-  heavals to which capitalist society is subject - takes a completely impartial view of the situation  which has now arisen in Unesco, for which neither it nor the other socialist countries are in any  way responsible.
(11. 2) The Soviet Union, as an active member of Unesco and a substantial contributor to Unesco’s  budget, cannot remain indifferent to its fate or to the policy it pursues. It is for this reason that  we consider it important, at this stage, to make a realistic assessment of the situation on account  of which it was decided to convene this extraordinary session.
(11. 3) The USSR delegation considers that it would be incorrect to represent Unesco’s present  difficulties, as several speakers at the 93rd session of the Executive Board did, as a “crisis” or  a “catastrophe”. It must not be forgotten that Unesco’s budget for 1973-1974 already exceeds the  budget for the previous biennium by an enormous sum - 30 million dollars. It is common know-  ledge, also, that a considerable number of Member States have not yet paid a11 or part of their  contributions for the previous biennium - 1971-l 972 - and that some of them have not paid for  several years past. In these conditions, ladies and gentlemen, it scarcely seems right to propose  meeting the deficit by calling for supplementary contributions from Member States. As you know,  when the Soviet delegation raises the problem in Unesco of the rational and strictly economical  use of the Organization’s resources, attempts are sometimes made to misrepresent the very basis  of our position, to sow doubts about the Soviet Union’s attitude to Unesco’s programme and about  our readiness to help the developing countries in their praiseworthy struggle to set up an indepen-  dent economy, eradicate illiteracy, and achieve progress.
(11. 4) We would like to stress once again that the Soviet Union - the first workers’ State, founded  by the great Lenin - has from the very first days of its existence, not by words but by deeds,  afforded disinterested assistance to the peoples of the developing countries in their struggle  against colonialism and for the right to be their own masters. The Soviet Union did not desist
3XC/2  from its self-sacrificing efforts even in the most difficult periods of its history, as the peoples  of Asia, Africa and L,atin America are well aware. At the XXIVth Congress of the CPSU, the  Secretary-General of the Central Committee of the CPSU, L. 1. Brezhnev, emphasized particular-  ly that “despite a11 difficulties and even in some instances defeats, social changes of various kinds  are taking place throughout large areas of the world. The workers are winning important victories  in the struggle for their rights, for true freedom and for the defence of human dignity. The  patriots of countries still groaning under the colonial yoke are continuing their courageous fight  for liberation. Our country “, L. 1. Brezhnev stressed, “is doing everything to support this just  fight. The USSR’s political and economic co-operation with the countries which have won their  liberation has developed still further in recent years. Our trade with them is expanding. With  our co-operation, dozens of industrial and agricultural firms have been set up in many Asian and  African States. We also contribute towards the training of qualified personnel for these countries.  And a11 this is done in the interests of both sides”.
(11. 5) The Soviet Union’s efforts in this field have now assumed particularly large proportions.  It was the Soviet Union that proposed placing on the agenda of the XXVIIIth session of the United  Nations General Assembly the important and urgent question of “Reduction of the military budgets  of States Permanent Members of the Security Council by 10 per cent and utilization of part of the  funds thus saved to provide assistance to developing countries”. Our proposa1 was formulated in  clear and precise terms by a member of the Politburo of the General Committee of the Party,  USSR Foreign Minister A. A. Gromyko, in his address to the United Nations General Assembly.  The Soviet Union has for many years past been pressing for the rationalization of Unesco’s ex-  penditure, SO that the bulk of its resources may be spent on developing the Organization’s pro-  gramme, instead of being swallowed up by the bureaucratie machinery. It is no secret that over
60 percent of Unesco’s budget goes to pay for the staff and other administrative expenses, whilst  less than 40 per cent is spent on assistance to the developing countries. It is understandable that  a11 Member States would like to see this position changed.
(11. 6) Now, ladies and gentlemen, let us turn our attention to practical matters. This session  has been presented with the figure of $12,600,000, representing the deficit of the Organization,  which it is proposed to caver by supplementary contributions from Member States. We are aware  of course, that this figure was originally even higher, SO that we are bound to admit that the  Unesco Secretariat and the Executive Board have already made some effort to find means within  the Organization for overcoming the financial difficulties. But at the same time, we must say  ihat our delegation is convinced that neither the Secretariat nor the Executive Board has by any  means exhausted the possibilities of effecting interna1 economies to reduce the budget deficit further.
(11. 7) Before analysing these unexploited possibilities for effecting economies in specific sectors  of the Organization’s activities, we would like to suggest certain general ways in which the  General Conference might obtain supplementary interna1 resources. This could be done, we consider,  by “freezing” a number of vacant posts in the Unesco Secretariat; eliminating or at least redu-  cing the reclassification of posts; cutting down the number of conferences, meetings and missions;  reducing the number of publications and documents; greatly reducing the number of consultants  employed by Unesco; stopping the construction of new buildings for Unesco; reorganizing cer,tain  units of the Organization; and cutting down the number of ineffectual, overlapping programmes.
1 would like to ask delegates to note particularly that our proposals relate mainly to cuts in adminis-  trative and various other unproductive expenses of the Organization, which should not impair the  quality of Unesco’s programmes or affect them quantitatively to any extent.
(11. 8) Following up the views we expressed at the 93rd session of the Executive Board, we would  like today to make a number of practical suggestions which, if carried out, would, in our view,  not only achieve considerable financial savings but also improve the programme. 1 would like to  emphasize that it is a case of improvements as well as of financial savings. In the opinion of the  Soviet delegation, it is essential to strike a balance, in the interests of the Organization, between  the regular budget and extra-budgetary resources. There has in the past few years been a dan-  gerous tendency to transform various sectors of Unesco into executors of projects. Take, for  instance, the development programmes of the UN and the International Bank for Reconstruction  and Development. According to the 197 3 -1974 programme, the Education Sector is to administer  extra-budgetary funds of UNDP, amounting to more than twice as much as its own budget. 1 shall  give you the figures - $57,900,000 and $27,200,000 respectively. At the same time, considerable  sums from Unesco’s budget are used to caver part of the administrative expenses involved in such
3XC/2  Projects as those of UNDP and IBRD. This is, in our opinion, an abnormal state of affairs. It is  essential that the UNDP, IBRD, the UN Fund for Population Activities and various other funds  should fully caver a11 expenses entailed in the execution of the projects and programmes of those  organizations, and they should not be even partly met from Unesco funds.
(11. 9) As we have already mentioned the Education Sector of Unesco, we may point out that this  sector is required to deal with research on fundamental problems of universal interest. But in  fact the programme of the Education Sector consists mainly of minor projects. Unesco’s research  in this sector is done mainly by consultants who are not essential to the Organization and who  cause it considerable expense. The questions worked out by the consultants are discussed in nu-  merous working groups, seminars and conferences. Ladies and gentlemen, this method of work  is both expensive and ineffectual. This research could be done more cheaply and even more effet-  tively by specialized institutes in the Member States of the Organization. Activities relating to  educational publications also need organizing. There should in the future be a uniform plan for  publishing. This plan should include, firstly, publications giving information about the most ad-  vanced international experience in education. Our delegation considers that the proposals for  certain reductions in the programme of the Education Sector, contained in the Secretariat’s  document 93 EX/15, Part III, Annex II, are by and large acceptable. Apart from this it would be  easy and not prejudicial to the work, to eliminate certain somewhat far-fetched projects such as,  for instance, the project for “associating schoolchildren and students in the preparation of the  curricula and in the running of educational institutions”, the project on “the rôle of modern com-  munication media in the development of new forms of expression and communication among the  Young”, and various other similar projects. At the same time, it would be possible to reduce  subventions to such non-governmental organizations as, for instance, the International Federation  for Parent Education and the International Federation for School Correspondence. These measures,  like those for regulating the staff of the Education Sector, would enable further savings to be made  in Unesco’s budget.
(11. 10) In the opinion of our delegation, considerable economies could also be made in the Science  Sector. We are doubtful, for instance, about the usefulness of retaining certain types of activities  in the fields of ecology, hydrology and geology: certain types of activity which duplicate measures  taken under important long-term programmes such as, for instance, “Man and the Biosphere”,  the International Hydrological Decade, and the International Biological Correlation Programme.  It would be useful, in this connexion, to combine parallel programmes in these areas so as to con-  centrate efforts and exclude duplication within the same sector. In our opinion, the following  activities could be eliminated without substantially harming the programme:  the project on Misuse of Science by Society (with the exception of questions concerning the  preservation of the environment);  programmes constituting pre-investment activities designed to obtain new extra-budgetary  resources;  the working out of indicators of models for planning the development of science and technology  in the developing countries should be curtailed.  Being in favour of the accelerated development of science and techology in these countries, we  bave always been against evolving a pseudo-scientific “typology of underdevelopment”. The project  in question is in fact only a modification of this kind of typology. in our opinion it would also be  possible to curtail various other projects and research plans, which duplicate the fundamental  scientific programmes of this sector. This would effect considerable savings.
(11. 11) In the present serious financial position of the Organization, we would like to suggest  several changes in the Social Sciences and Culture Sector which, we think, would lead to consi-  derable financial savings whilst at the same time having a beneficial effect on the future activity  of this sector. We propose that more effective use be made of the large subvention given to the  International Council for Philosophy and Humanistic Sciences. We think that it would be possible  to postpone for five or six years work on the publication of the third volume of “Main trends of  research in the social sciences and humanities”, since this publication, apart from other consi-  derations, requires further consideration and summarization. Then also, in our opinion the  records of certain meetings and symposia could for instance be brou@ out not in the form Of  separate publications but in the periodical publications of the Council for Philosophy and Humanis-  tic Studies and of the International Social Science Council. We are in favour of amalgamating
3XW2  some parts of the programme of the Social Sciences Department, such as “Methods for applica-  tion of the social sciences” and “Problem-oriented social science research and teaching”. This  Will make it possible to improve the quality of the programme. We also propose combining such  projects as “Evaluation techniques”, “Socio-economic indicators” and “Development planning and  management”. It would also be a good thing to combine two divisions - “Application of the Social  Sciences” and “Methods and Analyses in the Social Sciences”. It would be logical to bring together  a11 projects dealing with questions of the quality of the environment, population and family plan-  ning and place them in the section on population and family planning. In precisely the same way,  questions concerning human rights and problems of peace could be integrated into the philosophy  section, which is responsible for the execution of the very important project “Forum on Peace”.
(11. 12) It would be possible to save considerable sums by making the programme more effective  and by improving administrative arrangements in the Department of Culture within this Sector.  It cari scarcely be necessary to allocate three to five programme specialists for every project,  especially since numbers of the people employed in the Department of Culture are only adminis-  trative personnel, and not specialists in any particular field of culture. The Slav and the Balkan  and South East European projects could be combined without harming the programme. This would  also make it possible to reduce the number of staff. The Department of Culture could operate  more effectively even with far fewer specialists than at present, provided that some of them had  much higher professional qualifications than is the case at present.
(11. 13) Economie resources are also seriously underemployed, in our opinion, in the Communi-  cation Sector, the Administration Sector and General Services. As you see,ladies and gentlemen,  our proposals are designed to produce savings primarily in administration and also to eliminate  or combine overlapping projects. By giving serious consideration to the matter and implemen-  ting various practical proposals made by our delegation, and also by making the interna1 savings  which, we believe, many other delegations here Will also propose, the General Conference could  reduce the present budgetary deficit at least from $12,600,000 to $lO,OOO,OOO without harm to  Unesco’s programme. The Soviet delegation proposes that further effective measures in this  direction should be taken.
(11. 14) Mr. President, ladies and gentlemen, after again stressing that our attitude to Unesco’s  present financial difficulties is absolutely impartial, we should like to state that, in the event of  an appeal being made to a11 of us and in particular to the Secretariat of the Organization to make  an extra effort, we shall nevertheless be prepared to do our part towards overcoming these  difficulties.
(11. 15) Allow me, in conclusion, ladies and gentlemen, to express the hope of the Soviet delega-  tion that the leaders of Unesco Will take decisive measures to ensure that Unesco’s budget is used  more effectively and more economically in the future. Thank youI Mr. President.

13. 15 We also take it that in this exercise the basic and collective aim is to see that  the Approved Programme and Budget are not affected in any way. It is this principle  which, in our view, necessitated calling on the General Conference - the supreme body  of the Organization - to take a stand.
13. 16 But we must confess to feeling disappointed at the line of action already taken  in regard to this problem. We feel that the General Conference has not been given  enough options to determine the basis of action in the light of its previous decisions on  what is at stake - the Programme and Budget.
13. 17 The proposals before us have already, in principle, undermined, prejudiced and,  I venture to say, violated the authority of the previous General Conference decisions or  resolutions.
13. 18 This evasion of our commitments when it suits us seems to be developing into  an accepted method of procedure in dealing with issues which we know are of major im-  portance to some Member States. As a result, the idea of the General Conference being  the supreme organ of the Organization is becoming nothing but a mockery.
13. 19 In practice, Member States may approve a policy decision one day and two days  later do a11 they cari to undermine that same decision.
13. 20 And it is strange that, despite these contradictions, which often stem from mis-  trust, suspicion, hunger for power and material superiority, we still attend Unesco  meetings and conferences like this one, if only to pretend to make some temporary ac-  ceptable sense out of them in order to keep the Organization going.
13. 21 We seem to have lost the original sense of direction and purpose and instead we  have acquired a system of making decisions which soon after being passed Commit no-  body.
13. 22 This is the way we see it and this is what worries us. It worries us because we  are convinced that this approach to common problems cari have disastrous effects on  certain aspects of our national development plans.
13. 23 We are convinced that if the General Conference was truly accepted as apolicy-  making body, respected by a11 Member States and free from lip service SO often dicta-  ted by ideological considerations, the financial question could have been solved without  resorting to cuts in the programme - cuts which have been effected without due regard  and respect for Member States whose national plans reflect confidence in the Unesco  Programme and Budget approved at the seventeenth session, We believe that theprob-  lem could have been treated with respect for the balance of development.
13. 24 It is staggering to think that we, the same people who talk SO much in terms of  development as a means to human understanding, cari,, at the same time, recommend  reducing the means of achieving what we SO eloquently preach.
13. 25 Mr. President, the Director-General was right when, during one of his brilliant  addresses, he said “Thus, by an irony of fate, on the one hand, we were thinking about  the major problems to the solution of which Unesco might make an important contribu-  tion and the additional means to be employed to that end, while on the other, we were  being invited to consent to a reduction of the resources placed at our disposa1 by the  General Conference. ”
13. 26 We venture to say, Mr. President, that the proposals as they stand cannot be  said to be completely free from arm- twisting and pressures presented in the sweet  name of Consensus. May we make it clear at this point that we do not accept this con-  sensus presented by the Vice-Chairman of the Executive Board. We do not accept it  because in our view accepting this consensus means one thing and one thing only: sup-  porting those who, since the seventeenth session, have looked for every possible chance  and opportunity not only to reduce the resources legally placed at the disposa1 of theOr-  ganization but also to see that the capacity of renewal of the programme is sloweddown.
13. 27 We are right, we must be right, when we further assert that the measures we  are taking on this issue are bound to affect the operational programmes to a greater ex-  tent than we are led to believe. No doubt it is the developing countries that Will feel it,  whether they like it or not. In fact, evidence to this effect is already manifesting itself.
13. 28 We are, of course, assured that a11 efforts Will be made to see that no serious  damage is done to the programme. We do not accept this assurance when we know that  in practice it Will not give us that extra project in Education, Science, Culture andCom-  munication.
13. 29 In fact, by implication, the handling of the inflation and devaluation issues clearly  gives us both a medium- and long-term warning that integration of Unesco projects in  our national plans should be treated with the utmost caution because one cannot take it  for granted that the Approved Programme Will remain free from manipulations bysome

13. 30 We would like to conclude by regretting that the General Conference is, if 1 may  say SO, being hoodwinked into taking a step backwards. This step Will probably uphold  the image of Unesco here at Headquarters and may be among developed countries, but
1 hesitate to speculate on the reaction of developing countries which are likely to belick-  ing their wounds after the cuts.
13. 31 Mr. President, we intended to request as much time as the Executive Board had  on this matter in order to open a fuller discussion, but we have not done SO and are not  going to do SO because we are anxious to respect the gentlemen’s agreement that this  Conference should end on the day foreseen. We are aware, too, of the fact that most  of the members here work to tight schedules and therefore it would not be right for us  to disrupt that order.
13. 32 We do hope, however, that at the 1974 regular session, it might be possible for  us to request a general study in principle on the legality of the General Conference de-  cisions and their legal implications for Member States in regard to the Approved Pro-  gramme and Budget. In our view some frank discussion of this would go a long way to  help our future outlook on the Organization and what it stood for, what it stands for and  what it will stand for.
13. 33 In the meantime, we reserve our right to take a stand in the light of the delibera-  tions and the final resolution. We also reserve the right to take the floor again if we find  it absolutely necessary and, of course, if we are permitted by you, Mr. President.  Thank you very much.
