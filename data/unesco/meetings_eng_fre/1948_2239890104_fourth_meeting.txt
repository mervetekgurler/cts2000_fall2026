3C/PRO/1) (continuation).  Section IV. Credentials (continuation).  Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) submitted a  re-drafted amendment, embodying the conclusions  reached in the discussion of Rule 22, as follows :  I. The credentials of delegates and alternate  delegates must be authenticated in writing either by  the Head of the State, by the Head of the Govern-  ment, by the Minister of Foreign Affairs or the  Minister of Education, or by one of their colleagues  of full Ministerial rank in charge of that Department  of the Government of the Member State chiefly  responsible for the relations between that Govern-  ment and Unesco.
2. The General Conference may, in exceptional  circumstances, accept provisionally credentials authen-  licated in writing by the Minister Plenipotentiary  representing the Member State in the country in  which the Conference is held. Member States  and others admitted to participate in the Confer-  ence by means of such provisional credentials shall  take steps immediately to provide proper credentials,  and the Director-General shall present a report at the  next Session recording the action taken by such Mem-  ber States in order to conform with this Rule.
3. Delegations present at the General Conference,  provided with properly authenticated credentials, shall  enjoy voting rights, rights of nomination for office  and other benefits enjoyed by Member States and  their representatives composing the General Confer-  ence,
4. These rights and benefits cannot be secured by  the mere presentation of credentials in the absence of  a delegation which refuses to attend the Conference,  notwithstanding the fact that those credentials would  have been recognized as valid, had the delegation been  present.  Paragraphs (5) and (6) were to read as para-  graphs (2) and (3) of Rule 22 in document
3C/PRO/1.  Paragraph (3) and (4) of the amendment were  based on the observations made in the Confer-  ence, following the report on the credentials of  the Czechoslovak delegation.  The amendment embodied in the first two para-  graphs made it clear that any member of the  Government could sign credentials, but did not  increase the number of issuing authorities.  M. SEYDOUX (France) was anxious that there  should be a written agreement on the subject of  credentials, so that they should commit the  signatory States. Credentials issued over the  signature of the Minister Plenipotentiary in the  country in which the Conference was held, for  instance, should be regarded as full credentials  only in exceptional cases and provisionally.  Mr. RIBBLE (United States of America) pointed  out that Unesco might be compelled, in important  cases, to Investigate the administrative status of  the Minister Plenipotentiary concerned.  The CHAIRMAN thanked the delegate of the  United Kingdom for preparing the new Rule 22,             providing for the issue of credentials (a) in  normal circumstances, and (b) in exceptional  circumstances, and defining the rights which  could be exercised in virtue of such credentials.  M. SEYDOUX (France) wished to consider the  amendment in detail when the French translation  had been distributed. It would be useful to know  whether delegates who did not hold credentials  issued by the Head of the State or the Minister  of Foreign Affairs, and who were not pleni-  potentiaries, could take part in inter-governmental  conferences: and whether a member of the  Cabinet, unconnected with Unesco (e.g. the  Minister of Food), could issue credentials. He  also thought that a shorter text might be desirable.  The CHAIRMAN did not think it necessary to  provide for exceptional cases, since every case  was submitted individually to the Conference for  decision.  Mr. RIBBLE (United States of America) entirely  agreed.  The CHAIRMAN adjourned the discussion until  the French translation of the amendment and  alterations to it had been distributed.  Dr. MANALAC (Philippines) was anxious that  absence should, in itself, entail disqualification;  in other words, that a reply stating that a  delegation would not attend the Conference and  that, though desirous of doing so, it was unable  to be present, should make it unnecessary for  the Conference to furnish proof of a refusal.
