(3.2) The Committee elected as {ts Vice-Chairmen, Dr. Petar Vutov, Head of the delegation  of Bulgaria, Mr. Diallo Issa Ben Yacine, Head of the delegation of Guinea, and Dr, Alejandro  Roces, Head of the delegation of the Philippines, In accordance with the Rules of Procedure,  the Nominations Committee considered the Executive Board's report, presented by its Chair-  man, Dr, Rodolfo Bardn Castro, who gave invaluable help in the preparation of the List of nom-  inations for the election of the President and 15 Vice-Presidents of the General Conference,  and of the Chairmen of the Commissions and Committees.
(3.3) In pursuance of the decision taken by the Nominations Committee, I have the honour to  submit its proposals. The Committee recommends the  following 15 countries for the offices of Vice-Presidents: Brazil, Cameroon, France, Greece,  Honduras, India, Italy, Japan, Pakistan, Poland, Senegal, Uganda, United Kingdom, United  States of America, and Venezuela, I should like, here, to thank the delegations of Morocco  and of the United Republic of Tanganyika and Zanzibar for withdrawing in favour of Pakistan  and Uganda respectively,

Cookey (Nigeria); and, for the Administrative Commis-  sion, Professor Bedrettin Tuncel (Turkey). (3,5) With regard to the chairmanship of the Legal Committee, Dr, Rodolfo Barén Castro ex-  plained to the Nominations Committee the reasons why the Executive Board had made no propo-  sal for that office. The Nominations Committee has decided not to recommend a candidate for  this office at this meeting. In conformity with the resolutions adopted by the General Confer-  ence at its twelfth session, the Legal Committee is at present studying the possibility of pre-  senting to the General Conference a draft resolution with the object of increasing the Committee's  membership. In order to respect the principle of geographical distribution, while taking due  account of the candidates’ competence, Ît was considered advisable to recommend that the Legal  Committee defer the election of its officers until the Conference has taken a decision on the mat-  ter and, to ask it, In that case, to elect a Vice-Chairman or temporary Chairman to serve for  the time being.
(3.6) In accordance with the proposal of the Chairman of the Executive Board, and with the ob-  ject of allowing the Commissions and Committees full freedom, as in tho past, to elect their  Vice-Chairmen and Rapporteurs, as well as the Chairmen of their Working Parties, the Nomi-  nations Committee recommends that such elections take place in those Commissions and Com-  mittees. I have to convey the Nominations Commitice's hope that, when proceeding to those  elections, delegations will take full account of the candidates! competence, the principle of geo-  graphical distribution and that of the diversity of cultures. (5.5) Fundamental political, soc{al and economic changes have occurred and are still taking  place in the life of the nations. A world-wide soclallst system has come into being; the liqui-  dation of colonialism is being completed; young States have emerged; the peoples of many  countries have entered on the path of independent development, Boundless prospects of soclal,  economic, technical and scientific progress have opened up to mankind. (5.6) In the history of civilization, science has always played a progressive rôle. In our time,  under new social conditions, {t is becoming a directly productive force, since every human ac-  tivity fs, directly or indirectly, closely bound up with the application of the achievements of sci-  ence and technology.
(5.7) Hence the special importance of two major problems: firstly, the extension of theoretical,  fundamental research in such branches of science as physies, chemistry and biology; secondly,  the application of science to production. This is a two-way link, essential also for the progres-  sive development of scientific and technical thought. There is a very close connexion between  theoretical research and the practical application of the results of that research, i.e. produc-  tion. In the past, after Galvant's important discovery of electrical phenomena in living organ-  sims, several decades were to pass before the appearance of the first electric lamp, but in our  time any important discovery enters very quickly into the production process, outstripping the  most optimistic hopes of its inventors.
(5.8) It is common knowledge that the development of mathematics has determined the growth  of mathematical logic and cybernetics which, in conjunction with the uchievements in electronics,  have led to the manufacture of computers and automatic-control machines which play so import-  ant and vital a part both In science and in technology. Achievements in theoretical and experi-  mental physics have given man mastery over the nuclear energy of the atom, and are now making  it possible to accomplish the tasks involved in gaining control over thermonuclear processes  and in their utilization as a mighty and practically inexhaustible source of peaceful energy  Achievements ou the theoretical side of the natural sciences have ushered in the era of the ex-  ploration of the cosmos, with the aid of artificial satellites, rockets and spaceships, and led to  the creation of the indispensable premises for a deeper penetration of the cosmos, a knowledge  of the natural phenomena of the universe, bringing the planets of the solar system nearer, so to  speak. The development of modern biochemistry, biophysics and microbiology has providedthe  basis for the development of those branches of industry concerned with antibiotics, vitamins,  ferments and pharmaceutics which are so extremely important in the Lives cf peoples. Thus,  theoretical research conditions practical scientific achievement, and technical progress may at  times even lead to the creation of new branches of knowledge and of production.
(5.9) While economic independence is essential to the achievement of full politicalindependence,  economic progress is unthinkable without the development of natural and applied sciences in every  country and without the rapid translation of results into practice.
