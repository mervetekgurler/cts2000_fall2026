10.1 Mr, CONTON (Sierra Leone):  Madame President, I believe that approximately half of the 120 members of Unesco  are on your list of speakers in this debate, I wish to address myself, particularly to the  other half - these who are not on the list, some of whom, I hope, may still be open to  persuasion,
10,2 I want to address them in the form of four questions which I shall invite them to study  with me for a few seconds, These are all questions raised by the documentsnow before  us, draft resolutions 143, 160, 207 and the draft resolution annexed to the Sixth Report of  the Legal Committee, My four questions, Madame President, are the following,
10,3 First of all, what are the sponsors of draft resolutions 207 and 143 seeking to do?  Secondly, is what they propose lawful? Thirdly, is what they propose wise and prudent?  And finally, is the way they propose to do it the best way?
10,4 As to the first question, Madame, what are the sponsors of draft resolutions 207 and
143 seeking to do? It may first of all be worth emphasizing that no one is trying at this  stage to expel Portugal from Unesco, The purpose of draft resolution 143 is to condemn  racialism and colonialism and to say to the Government of Portugal that, since member-  ship of Unesco carries obligations as well as rights, until that government accepts those  obligations - particularly the obligation to behave in conforinity with the Charter, declara-  tions and resolutions of the United Nations of whose family Unesco is a member - Portugal  will not be allowed to enjoy any of the rights of membership,
10,6 As to the second question, is what the sponsors of draft resolutions 207 and 143 pro-  pose lawful, I shall answer by pointing out that the annex to the Sixth Report of the Legal  Committee makes reference to Article V of the Constitution, whereas, in my view, the  relevant Article, now that the matter has moved to the General Conference itself, is not  Article V but Article IV of the Constitution, Paragraph B,3 in the English version, found  on page 10 of the Manual of the General Conference, states clearly hat the General Con-  ference, and I quote: "shall summon international conferences of States in accordance  with the regulations to be made by it,"
10,6 As the distinguished delegate from Nigeria has said, at its seventh sessionthe Gene-  ral Conference made precisely such regulations, and it amended them at ita twelfth session,  Rule 3.1(a) of the Rules of Procedure for the Calling of International Conferences of States,  found on page 75 of the English version of the Manual, states equally clearly that, "The  General Conference: shall decide which States shall be invited",
10,7 I wish particularly to stress, Madame President, that this rule does not add that this  clause itself cannot be used to prevent Member States from attending, Nor doesit exempt  the General Conference from its application, We are therefore left with the simple phrase,  "that the General Conference shall decide which States shall be invited to international con-  ferences of States",
10,8 No one can deny that a conference attended by delegates from 120 States 18 an inter-  national conference of States, unless English words have lost all their meaning,
10,9 This means therefore that, if we apply the letter of the law alone at this stage the  Constitution gives to this General Conference what it does not, or may not, give to the Ex-  ecutive Board, namely, a completely free hand to decide which States shall and which States  shall not be invited t” its conferences, As the Government of Portugal itself has said, this  is the most important right that it would lose if draft resolution 143 1s adopted, because it  has itself said that it receives at present no assistance from Unesco,
10,10 My third question was: Is such an action wise and prudent, even though it may well  be lawful? Here, Madame President, we must leave the letter of the law and turn to its  spirit, I am certain that the supporters and sponsors of draft resolutions 207 and 143 are  as fully concerned by the spirit as by the letter of the law, Indeed, my delegation is doubly  “ pleased that this matter has moved from the Legal Committee - which was quite rightly  concerned with the mechanics of the law - to this General Conference, which must be con-  cerned also with questions which transcend the mechanics and look towards the purpose of  the rule of law,
10,11 This brings me, Madame President, to the question: What is the purpose ofthe rule  of Jaw? In my view it is twofold, First of all, to protect human rights and, secondly, to  protect public order, The law has a duty to the individual, to protect his rights; it has a  duty to society, to protect public order, That Portugal hus flouted the Universal Declara-  tion of Human Rights by denying the people of its African territories the right to decidewho  shall govern them - and this is quite different from the right to choose persons to represent  them in the government of Lisbon - is a fact so clear that I think I need spend no time on it,  As to public order, I want to spend a little time on this, because human rights can only sur-  vive where there is public order,
10,12 What, Madame President, would be the effect on public order in the corridors and  the halls of Unesco of allowing Portugal to participate in the conferences of this Organiza-  tion, against the wish of so many other Member States? I wish to suggest, Madame Presi-  dent, that the effect would be to create such a degree of public disorder in Unesco as to  cripple it, This 1s not merely because of the number of delegations which will not share a  conference table with a country they regard as an enemy of human rights, but becausethese  same delegations, by and large, represent precisely those areas of the world where Unesco  does what we all agree is its most important work, K is this conviction, Madame Presi-  dent, that the law supports us one hundred per cent in its letter, in its spirit and in ite  purpose, that unites the African countries with their many friends on the other continents,  in supporting draft resolutions 207 and 143,
10,13 I repeat, therefore, that what we are trying to do is not only lawful but is wise by  reference to the highest values we know - those of human rights and public onder,
10.14 My fourth and final question, then, is whether - even though this may be 80 - weare  using the best methods to achieve our purpose? Certain delegates have levelled against us  the charge of emotionalism, 1 suggest that the best test of whether a man is acting emo-  tionally or rationally is the methods that he selects to achieve his purposes, The whole  world knows that the opponents of colonialism and racialism who are represented here have  determined to fight their enemies, not by force, but by constitutionalism, Step by step,  this constitutional battle has been fought in the organs of the United Nations, tempting  though it has been to resort to force elsewhere, The battle has now reached its Specialized

10,16 But perhaps an equally important aspect of this question of methods is the one that  has prompted some delegates to ask why, if we are so certain of our legal grounds, we do  not allow this matter to go forward to the International Court of Justice, as the majority of  members of the Legal Committee have advised, The reason, Madame President, is a  simple one, The best legal opinion comes from lawyers who know, not only the law, but  also their clients and the ideals which inspire thelr clients, and the society in which their  clients live, The client in this case is Unesco, The members of the International Court  of Justice distinguished as we do not deny they are, as individual lawyers, are not familiar  with Unesco, they do not know their clients, they do not understand the things that Unesco  believes in and seeks to do, they do not know the way that we seek to do them.
