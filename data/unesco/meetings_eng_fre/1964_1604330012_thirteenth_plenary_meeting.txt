La lecture et l'écriture  sont une formalisation de la raison et constituent en elles-mêmes une première logique for-  melle, Au contraire, l'homme qui ne sait ni lire ni écrire agit par intuition globale, par  rapprochements analogiques, ce qui explique l'importance de l'empirisme magique dans les  sociétés archaïques, Placé devant une difficulté, il ne recourt pas à l'analyse rationnelle  mais à la mémoire, instrument-clé du comportement adaptatif, là où n'existe aucun autre  moyen d'enregistrement de l'expérience, L'écriture utilisée pour la conservation de la pen-  sée libère l'homme des limites, du poids, de la non-interchangeabilité de la mémoire indi-  viduelle, L'homme du milieu naturel use de la mémoire et recherche le précédent analo-  gique qui lui servira de modèle pour l'action présente, D'où, dans les sociétés préindus-  trielles ou non industrielles, l'importance du stock traditionnel de dictons et de proverbes,  de cette "sagesse des nations', véritable manuel de règles d'action. 20.1 Dame Mary SMIETON (United Kingdom):  Mr. President, I should like to make clear at once that having listened to the Director-  General and to this debate, the United Kingdom is of the opinion that the level of the Regular  budget for 1965-1966 should be fixed at $48.12 million. And I would like to refer again, as  I have done on several occasions in the Executive Board, to the factors that we have taken  into account in reaching this view.
20.2 The first factor is that it is no good providing finance for a programme unless it is a  programme that is thoroughly well worked out. The projects in such a programme must be  clearly defined, they must be part of a coherent plan, and they must be of a kind which are  best carried out by an international agency. Also they must be capable of advancing effec -  tively the agreed aims and purposes of Unesco, including notably the degree to which they  will help the developing countries to make progress in the fields of education, science and  culture.
20.3 Second, we must be sure that the programme which is approved is one which it is  within the capacity of the Organization to carry out effectively and efficiently. By this I  mean that it must be supported by a well qualified staff, properly recruited and trained and  in post at the right time; that the various conferences and meetings will be adequately or -  ganized and documented; that Unesco's publications will be of high quality and objectivity;  and, most important, we must be sure that the projects, whatever they are, that we carry  out as agents are efficiently executed and managed. :
20.4 Thirdly, and this has been stressed much today, we must try to achieve a budget level  which commands to the maximum extent possible the support of all the governments who by  their assessed payments finance Unesco. In this connexion, Mr. President, we might per-  haps just remind ourselves that Unesco is not the only Specialized Agency for which Member  States find contributions and that in addition many governments, including that of the United  Kingdom, make substantial voluntary contributions for the Special Fund and the Technical  Assistance Fund.

20.5 Before coming back to these three factors, as I would like to do in a moment, ! should  say that the strong view of my Government, which is obviously shared by many of us, is that  whatover budget level is finally adopted for 1965-1966 at this Conference, it is imperative  that some improvements should be sought in our budget procedures for the future. We  believe that we ought to look a good deal further ahead than we do now, and make forward  plans so that our decisions on future. budgets will be made on a better and firmer basis than  at present, and, just as important, so that Unesco itself can regard its biennial programmes  as part of a continuous programme of development, We believe too that our methods of bud-  geting must be improved and that we should not in future be faced with last-minute additions  which make an orderly method of obtaining properly-considered government views impos-  sible, I am sure the General Conference will agree that the Executive Board was right to  decide that one of its urgent tasks next year is to examine these procedures in co-operation  with the Director-General. We must exercise due foresight in including known costs at the  proper stage, we must also be realistic in recognizing that there may well be factors, ur-  gent emergency needs, cost-of-living increases which cannot be foreseen, or items that can-  not be estimated with sufficient accuracy in advance to enable provision to be made in the  budget. For these, under our current procedures, we must look to the Executive Board to  exercise its discretion in deciding whether and to what extent drawings must be made from  the Working Capital Fund. For this particular biennium we may have to have recourse to  the Working Capital Fund more than we should had the budgetary procedures which I have  outlined above been followed.
20.6 1 return now to the immediate issue of the 1965-1966 Budget. Taking the first two  factors I have mentioned - the need for a well-conceived and coherent plan, and the need  for efficient and effective organization and staffing ~ my Government at first took the view  that the programme proposed by the Director-General in 13 C/5 was not within Unesco's  capacity to carry out without too great a risk of inefficiency through the kind of overstrain  which wastes resources. We still think we are running a risk but we are prepared to accept  the Director-General's assurances and to abide by the wishes of the majority of our collea-  gues on the Executive Board for a programme of this size.
20.7 This brought us to support a budget level of $48.12 million. It is now being suggested  by the Director-General - and this first came up at the Executive Board immediately prior  to this Conference - that additional non-programme items should be included in the budget  figure and that the budget level should be $48.92 million. Mr, President, we cannot go  along with this, we think, with other delegations that have spoken, that as the programme  is carried out in the coming biennium, and as the Organization continues with its own house -  keeping operations during this period, there will be possibilities of adjustments which make  it unnecessary for the Conference to vote here and now a budget level in excess of $48.12  million. We recognize that this means that it will almost certainly be necessary, on this  basis, to have recourse to the Working Capitrl Fund if, as the United Kingdom of course  expects, the programme proposed by the Dire ‘tor-General is to be safeguarded and carried  out. But we believe that recourse to this fund, if necessary, is the wiser course in the situa-  tion in which we are placed in this biennium since none of us can know here and now whether  the whole of the $48.92 million will in fact be needed.
20.8 To sum up, Mr. President, the United Kingdom is, as I have said, in general and  warm support of the programme proposed by the Director-General, but we consider that  this programme can be started and carried through, if need be with some recourse to the  Working Capital Fund, on a budget level of $48.12 million.
20.9 May I, in conclusion, make some comments on Mr. Zavala's interesting proposal that  we vote in stages, first on the figure of $48.12 million, and then on the figures which the  Director-General has filled in for us in paragraph 7 (a) and (b) of the Executive Board reso-  lution (document 13 C/6 Add. Rev.). I was not quite sure how he applied this to point (c) of  this paragraph (draft proposals and amendments submitted by Member States) but Professor  Carneiro has raised this issue very clearly in his statement.
20,10 Unesco has applied in the past and I believe applied very wisely that self-discipline,  which has been so helpful to the Programme Commissions of the past, of knowing at the  beginning the total sum within which the Commission must work, and of knowing at the end  of its work that it may have to cut back the total volume of individual proposals that it has  approved in order to bring the whole within the Hmit of the budget determined at an early  stage of the Conference. It was precisely in order to achieve this and te let the Programme  Commission know exactly where it stands that the time-table was arranged so that the Com-  mission does not start work on the programme proper until the so-called provisional budget  level, all of it, has been fixed. The Programme Commission will, I am sure, examine  crefully all the new proposals made by Member States. There is no question of these being  ignnr:d, But in so far as the Programme Commission does adopt these, then it must find  the means of accommodating them within the figure that has been fixed in our vote today. I  do therefore most strongly support the Director-General's point on this, as the only means  to achieve our business in an orderly fashion.
20,11 As regards the proposal for voting successively on the first $48.12 million budget  figure and then on (a) and (b) of paragraph 7 of the Executive Board's resolution, I have been  thinking about this. A vote on whether we should include the $250,000 for paragraph 7 (a)  would not of course be a vote on whether that money should be found for that purpose, we all  know that it has to be found and similarly with the $650,000 maximum figure for paragraph
7 (b). The proposal therefore is in fact a proposal for voting successively on a number of  separate total budget figures. This is a quite different practice from that adopted in the  past, which I think has served us well, by which we vote first on a draft resolution on the  budget total which is furthest from the substantive proposal before the Conference, and in  order thereafter. I believe that we should be wise to follow this practice and vote on clear  proposals before us for budget totals, The Chairman will no doubt give his ruling with ex-  pert advice on what is the substantive proposal, but I would have thought that the substantive  proposal before us was for a budget of $48.12 million as proposed by the Executive Board.

Then, said  Dr. Zavala, let us vote on the $550,000 which has to do with the salaries of our Secretariat  and personnel and which the Director-General explained to us with great care, was a dif-  ferent kind of question in a different category and could be handled in a wholly different  manner.
43.3 I think a great many of us, on Dr. Zavala's proposal, would have voted for the  $48,120,000. Some of us would perhaps have then voted to add the $250,000. Many of us,  including Mr. El Fasi and those who support his point of view, would have voted to add the  $550,000, and still others of us would have voted to go on up to $50 million. This was Dr.  Zavala's proposal as I understood it. Due to a procedural ruling from the Chair he with-  drew his proposal on this method of voting. But this does not mean the withdrawal of the  proposal that emanated from the Executive Board, after four long meetings and countless  discussions, to the effect that our budget should be $48,120,000. This is the basic pro-  posal. 1 am astonished that the Director-General can get up this morning, and, as Dr.  Kuypers has brilliantly explained to us, come completely out of the blue with a brand-new  propesal which he (the Director-General) says he has been studying for two years, but  which he has never discussed with the Executive Board since he agreed with the Executive  Board on the proposal for $48,120,000. Believe me, his control of the Executive Board is  such, and his influence over the members of that Board is such, that if he had not agreed  you would have had objections on the Executive Board to this figure; and this figure would  not now be placed before the General Conference without the objection of one single mem-  ber of the Executive Board.
43.4 Why do we have an Executive Board? Let us abolish the Executive Board and save my  time. I am not prepared to spend four months here in Paris over the last two years debat-  ing these subjects, calling on the Director-General, discussing this question of the budget  and finally come up with a unanimous proposal - I am prepared to do that and have it voted  down by the General Conference; this is the privilege of the General Conference - but I am  not prepared to go through all that and then have a new proposal brought out of the blue this

The basic proposal before this Conference,  I submit, is the proposal recommended without any adverse vote of any kind by the Execu-  tive Board,
43.5 Now, Mr. President, I had hoped very much and I have devoted the last two years to  the hope that we could come through this Conference with agreement on the budget, without  argument about it. The problems I spoke on this morning were those of Chile,  Bolivia and Paraguay, and of the United States. But President Johnson has been cutting  expenses in every department of our Government. It is not only good government and good  management, but it is good politics in a campaign year. He would admit it if he were here.

I ask you to think of the increase from a level of $39 million for  the current biennium to $48.12 million. ‘This is a tremendous increase, and it is coupled  with $50 million extra coming in from the United Nations and the Special Fund, of which, as  I have told you before, the United States is contributing a full 40% voluntarily - not by the  vote of a conference such as this, not because somebody is pointing a gun at us and saying  put the money up or a lot of people will not like you; no, voluntarily, 40%. We are giving  far more to this Special Fund - which comes voluntarily from us into Unesco to be adminis -  tered by the Director-General and his staff - far more than our contributions to Unesco.
43.7 I think it is terribly unhappy when we come here prepared to support these enormous  increases inthis budget, with enthusiasm, with goodwill, and then have suddenly presented tous  and to this General Conference a brand-new proposal, as Dr. Kuypers pointed out, which  had never been revealed to us, And then we are told that this is the basic proposal! This,
1 submit, is not the basic proposal. The basic proposal I deem to be the proposal of the  Executive Board. We cannot continue in this manner in the making of our budgets. We  cannot slip from figure to figure, from week to week, from day to day; we must have some  regularity in the making of our budgets. | suggest, Mr. President, that we take as our  basic figure the figure recommended by the Executive Board. If Dr. Zavala's procedure is  not to be followed, then I think we must at least vote on the recommendation or the Execu-  tive Board. If it is rejected, if the $50 million figure is accepted, and I understand the  deep needs of our membership - if that figure is accepted and we know how to administer  it - that presents another problem to the United States, another problem to this Conference.  But I would like to proceed regularly and to have the recommendations of our Executive  Board come before this meeting and be treated with the respect that I think they deserve.

Monsieur le Président, Mesdames et Messieurs les délégués : La délégation cubaine estime  opportun d'expliquer en quelques mots l'attitude qu'elle a adoptée dans le vote qui vient d'avoir  lieu, Il y a peu de temps, à cette même tribune, nous prenions position en faveur du plafond  budgétaire proposé à la Conférence par le Conseil exécutif et le Directeur général ; en d'autres  termes, nous avions opté pour un chiffre de 48, 925, 000 dollars, Toutefois, au cours du premier  vote, nous nous sommes ralliés au chiffre de 50, 000, 000 de dollars proposé à la Conférence par  les délégations de nombreux pays d'Afrique et d'Asie, ainsi que de divers pays d'autres continents,  L'explication est simple et nous n'avons en réalité rien fait d'autre que d'appliquer les thèses que  nous avons défendues à cette tribune, Nous avons dit au début que la tâche gigantesque que peut et  que doit accomplir l'Unesco justifie, exige même peut-être, un budget élevé ; nous avons déclaré  également que nous votions pour un budget de ce genre, notamment parce qu'il permettrait de faire  face à des nécessités que nous estimons réelles et inéluctables et qu'en outre, nous étions ferme-  ment convaincus que ce budget serait utilisé au mieux par les dirigeants de l'Unesco pour lesquels  nous avons une grande estime etun profond respect, Notre vote estdonc conforme aux principales  thèses que nous avons soutenues, et la délégation cubaine est convaincue qu'avec ce budget élevé  l'Unesco pourra mener à bien l'excellent programme que le Conseil exécutif et le Directeur nous  ont proposé à la séance d'aujourd'hui.
