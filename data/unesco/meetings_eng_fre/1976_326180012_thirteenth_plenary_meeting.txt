8.6 Mr. President, I should now like to make a few brief observations on some of the basic items  of documents 19 C/4 and 19 C/5, the details of which will be discussed by the representatives of  our delegation in different Commissions. The Director-General has indeed shown great depth of  understanding and wisdom in analysing the contemporary problems in his introduction to the Drag  Medium-Term Plan for 1977-1982. Planning being basically an exercise in resource manipulation  it is appropriate that Unesco should attempt this first comprehensive plan in a medium-term per.  spective. The overall objectives along with emphases on rural development, the interdisciplinary  approach, the development of people and societies, and endogenous development, are undoubtedly  most pertinent strategies. But, considering the nature of some given objectives such as respect  for human rights, the rule of peace, the role of international law and balance between man and  nature, it is doubtful whether such eternal themes can be set in atime frame. Hence my delegation  believes that most of the principles of action as set out in the Medium-Term Plan are applicable  only on the conceptual level. It cannot be denied that the too numerous study and research pro-  grammes listed in the targets may further illuminate the complexity of the problems but the need,  we believe, is for action programmes, Viewed in this perspective, the Draft Medium-Term Plan  can be considered as a useful basis for clarifying the broader objectives rather than as a document  of indicative planning. However, we do fully endorse the emphasis on self-reliance and peace  emphasized in this document, guided as we are by the words of our King Birendra: "Our strategy  is to bring about development largely through the mobilization of our own resources, supplemented  where necessary by international co-operation. These tasks call for a condition of peace, As we  are a peace-loving people, we seek to work for peace, not in isolation from the rest of the world,  but in collaboration with the whole world".

26.11 Mr. President, my delegation wishes to express its general satisfaction with and support  for a number of matters being discussed on the Conference Agenda. The proposals contained in  the Draft Programme and Budget, document 19 C/5, outlining the planned activities of the Organi-  zation, and in document 19 C/4, the Medium-Term Plan providing a framework and material for  dealing with contemporary problems on a continuing basis, are consistent with the objectives of  many countries in their search for a better education and quality of life for their citizens, My  delegation wishes to support the adoption of these documents. The Director-General's proposals  to make the Organization more effective are of particular importance to my country because any  action leading to efficiency in the Organization and the elimination of unnecessary expenditure,  means that more funds would be available for the use of Member States in greatest need, My dele-  gation appreciates the efforts of the Director-General in this regard as well as his efforts to keep  the budget ceiling low while providing the maximum services to member countries on a restricted  budget.
26.12 Mr. President, my delegation wishes to refer to document 19 C/60 dealing with the report  of the Director-General on geographical distribution of staff. My country is among those which  fall in the category of unrepresented States. My delegation supports any action which has the effect  of raising the lowest quota. There is no justification for perpetuating a system which does not  facilitate Member States enjoyment of their right to participate in the Secretariat of the Organiza-  tion of which they are members. My delegation feels that with positive efforts on the part of the  Secretariat, Unesco can attain the goal of representation of all Member States in its operations.  On 20 October 1976 my country's delegation to the United Nations General Assembly expressed in  strong terms before the Fifth Committee the dissatisfaction of small countries of the Caribbean  over the relatively low percentage of posts allocated to those countries in the Secretariat of that  Organization. The delegation drew attention to the urgent need to increase the number of these  countries! personnel in senior posts and to the principle that senior posts should not be considered  the special preserve of any nationality or any group of countries. Mr. President, my delegation  wishes to restate that position in this Organization because it is imperative that the cultural per-  ceptions of developing countries should be important inputs in Unesco's operations. It therefore  calls on the Director-General to accelerate the steps towards ensuring that there are no countries  unrepresented in the Secretariat.
26.13 My delegation welcomes the increased attention being given to the promotion of culture. It  sees increased cultural awareness as an integral and vital part of national development; it considers  that governments have a duty to promote, in a positive way among its citizens, the appreciation of  their own cultural heritage, and respect for the cultural heritage of others. My delegation there-  fore wishes to support Unesco's efforts in providing means whereby Member States may increase  the exchange of cultural property.
26.14 My delegation wishes to use this opportunity to express a word of tribute to the Director-  General, Mr. M'Bow, for the success he has so far brought to this Conference. He is a man of  initiative and drive, and devotion to service. This is surely characterizing his term of office as  Director-General, He has consistently shown himself to be in the forefront of action in finding  solutions to the innumerable problems which develop in this complex Organization. Mr. President,  like previous speakers, I wish to thank sincerely the Government and people of this fine country,  Kenya, for their hospitality and for the facilities which they have provided for this Conference. I  wish also to express the hope that prosperity will continue to reign in this pleasant land. I wish  finally, Mr. President, to reaffirm my Government's support for this Organization and the prii-  ciples to which it is dedicated. My Government looks forward to a deepening of relations with  Unesco and to greater participation in all spheres of its activities. Thank you.
