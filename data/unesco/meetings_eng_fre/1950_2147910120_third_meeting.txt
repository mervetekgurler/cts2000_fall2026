[25] Mr. de LACHARRIERE (France) pointed  out that it was not the business of the Committee  to discuss the cultural value of the Spanish  language, since that could not be called in ques-  tion; nor was it to prevent the mass of the  Spanish-speaking peoples in any way from col-  laborating in Unesco’s work. Unesco’s Courier  had long been published in Spanish; and the  translation into Spanish of other Unesco publica-  tions which aimed at promoting cultural progress  in the Latin-American countries was quite a dif-  ferent question from the one now before the Com-  mittee, namely the use of Spanish as a working  language of Unesco’s General Conference.  He thought that the Committee should consider  only the practical aspect of the problem: it was  a question of facilitating the business of inter-  national meetings attended by the representatives  of governments which had to decide on a pro-  gramme of action in the shortest possible time  and with the minimum of expenditure. Certain  countries, including France, had always been  concerned to reduce administrative expenditure  to a minimum, so as to devote the greatest pos-  sible part of the budget to programme activities.  It was in that practical spirit that the French  delegation would associate itself with the delega-  tions of Norway, Sweden, Turkey and the United  Kingdom, and vote against the Mexican proposal.  [26] Dr. CHEDIAK (Cuba) said the Mexican  proposal was first mentioned at the Mexico City  Conference, again at Beirut and again in Paris;  he believed it had been received with sympathy  by other delegates, but the only result of all the  discussions seemed to be requests for a delayed  decision. Unesco’s aim was to reach the masses,  and his delegation considered that the most ade-  quate vehicle to that end was a language which  they understooa. Dr. Chediak considered it  absolutely necessary and vital for Unesco’s needs  that the Mexican delegation’s proposal should be  adopted.  Other speakers had said that the greatest  obstacle was expense; the Director-General had  studied the problem, and figures were given in  document 5C/PRO/5; it would therefore be un-  necessary to re-submit the question to the Budget  Committee. It had also been said that, if Spanish  were used, it would delay Unesco’s work; but at  that time, the United Nations had not taken a  decision regarding the use of Spanish as a working  language; its use had, however, since been  approved by the United Nations and other Special-  ized Agencies. It would be lamentable to para-  lyse Unesco’s work for budgetary reasons, yet  that was what would happen if Spanish did not  become an official working language. The coun-  tries of Latin America wished to be extremely  friendly towards Europe, and French and English  were used in their conferences; it was thought  that there should be a certain amount of  reciprocity in Europe. As to the cost of putting the proposal into effect,  savings could doubtless be made in other direc-  tions so as to counter-balance the additional  expense. It would be useless to refer the proposal  to the Budget Committee, since document 5C/  PRO/5 contained an exhaustive survey of the  financial consequences of adopting Spanish as a  working language of the General Conference.  The Committee had all the data and could give  its decision without further delay.  In conclusion, Dr. Bernal Jiménez pointed out  that the adoption of Spanish as a working lan-  guage would be the best way of influencing the  peoples who wished to obtain greater access to  culture.  [B] Dr. BARCIA (Uruguay) believed that no  contact could be made with the peoples of Latin  America unless Spanish were used. The sug-  gestion had been made that China might use this  as a precedent, but although the population of  China was greater than that of Latin America,  the uniformity of language was by no means the  same; nor did China consist of several States  with international status. Finally, Mr. Castro  Leal drew attention to the fact that the obstacles  to the adoption of Spanish as a working language  of the General Conference had been overcome by  other Specialized Agencies; the reasons which led  them to adopt Spanish were equally applicable in  the case of Unesco.
