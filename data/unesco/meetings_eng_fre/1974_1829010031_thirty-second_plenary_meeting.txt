Certes, dans ces projets de résolution, figurent quelques points  sur lesquels nous avons des réserves à faire, mais, dans leur ensemble, ils sont conformes aux  principes et aux objectifs de l'Organisation concernant Je problème de la paix et de la lutte contre  le colonialisrne et le racisme.

36.1 Mr. JONES (United States of America):  Thank you, Madam President. \Vith your permission, 1 should like first of all to oulline the  views of my Government and of the United States National Commission on the relation of Unesco  to the building of peace. Unesco's rôle in the building of peace is distinctive. It should pursue  this special rôle with single-minded resolution, knowing that, if it does not do this job, no other  agency will, Unesco's rôle which is filled by no other international agency, is the promotion of  non-political co-operation and collaboration in the fields of education, science and culture, Other  agencies of the United Nations have functions that are also indispensable, But they are quite dif-  ferent functions, Unesco must realize that if it takes on others' responsibilities, it will but {m-  pair îts own capacity to do what it alone should and can do. It can invite a lack of faith in the  value of its own activities when it seeks to substitute other activities for them, or exhaust its  energies by taking on responsibilities that other agencies of the United Nations system are better  able to fulfil,
36.2 In performing its distinctive rôle, a primary instrument available to Unesco is the stimula-  tion and organization of practical intellectual collaboration, This collaboration must extendacross  frontiers and be active among the learned men and civic leaders who work in Unesco's fields of  competence, International understanding requires the practice of co-operation among those whose  major occupation in life is to search for understanding, in research, teaching, or the practice of  the arts and sciences, The development and promotion of education, science and culture are dis-  tinctive means by which Unesco seeks to create a climate and tradition of peace, For Unesco can  build a just peace by enriching the qualities of man's life and promoting throughout! the world re-  spect for justice, the rule of law, individual human rights and the fundamental freedoms affirmed  by the United Nations Charter,
36. 3 In furthering education, science and culture, Unesco indeed is not only building a path to  peace, it is practising peace in the here and now, and demonstrating in the concrete what peace  is, and what peace is for. To help create a climate of peace, Unesco must resist firmly the temp-  tation to embark on excursions into international political issues, It should avoid war-like words  uttered in the name of peace. It should avoid statements like some of those made in this hall last  night, and some that were uttered this morning, which, using artificial stereotypes, seek to divide  mankind into two castes: the persecuted and the persecutors, or the virtuous and the wicked. These  are not distinctions worthy of men who have thought seriously about the human condition, or who  know the elementary facts of human life, Resolutions passed by Unesco indeed cannot build peace,  but they may do quite the opposité; for, if this General Conference seeks to impose cultural or  ideological uniformity, it certainly will net be fulfilling the rôle assigned to Unesco. It should in-  stead concentrate on well-defined projects and activities capable of arousing widespread and honest  support and of achieving practical realization,
36.4 Unesco should not be looked upon as vested with a monopoly of initiative or responsibility  in its assigned fields of activity, The goal is international co-operation, not direction or manage-  ment by Unesco. Our Organization must recognize that intellectual collaboration and co-operation  take place outside Unesco's framework, for example, through regional and bilateral programmes,  and this too serves the Unesco idea, At the same time we must also recognize that the other agen-  cies of the United Nations system, in pursuing their own tasks and laying and enlarging the ground-  work for peace, will find themselves inevitably, to some extent, in an area of commor interest,  and vice-versa. This calls for close collaboration and co-operation within the United Nations sys-  tem. This is not said, I hasten to affirm, in criticism of what Unesco has done, but rather to  underscore in advance one of the principal guidelines under which our Organization hhs worked  over the years,
