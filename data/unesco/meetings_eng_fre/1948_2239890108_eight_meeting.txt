Interpretation of the Constitution (new  Rule 33 of the Rules of Procedure sub-  mitted by the delegation of the United  Kingdom) (document 8C/PRO/10).  Mrs. BRUNAUER (United States of America)  thought it should be possible, where the question
469  of interpretation was not a matter of over-  whelming importance, to arrange within the  Organization for its solution, provided there was  substantial agreement in the Conference. The  matter would be referred to the General Confer-  ence, and if that body was in substantial agree-  ment on the interpretation given, it could be left  there.  Mr. PILE (United Kingdom) thought that the  first clause of the proposed new Rule 33 followed  the United States proposal. He suggested the  omission from paragraph (3) of the words  “… and with the approval of a two-thirds majority  of its members...”. That would meet the United  States proposal that, where there was a conflict  of views, the Committee should merely report to  the General Conference. 1. At plenary meetings of the General Confer-  ence, a majority of the Member States attending the  Session shall constitute a quorum.
2. At meetings of Commissions, Committees and  subsidiary bodies of the Conference, a majority of the  Member States which constitute that body shall form  a quorum. Agreed.  Rule 94. Annual election of members of the  Executive Board (Item 8 in document
8C/PRO/15).  Before opening discussion on the draft sub-  mitted by himself and the Rapporteur, to replace  the existing Rule 94, the CHAIRMAN asked  whether the delegate of Belgium wished to  maintain the amendment he had submitted  (Annex VIII of document 3C/PRO/T).  M. MAST (Belgium) did wish to maintain his  amendment, which aimed at underlining the  autonomy and independance of the Executive  Board.  After an explanation from M. de BLONAY  (Head of the Bureau of External Relations), the  Belgian amendment was put to the vote, and  rejected by eight votes to six.  Dr. CHEDIAK (Cuba), speaking on the  proposed new wording of Rule 94 suggested by  the Chairman and Rapporteur, thought it would  limit the sovereignty which Article V, para-  graph 1, of the Constitution had given to the  General Conference, enabling it to nominate,  from among the members of the delegations,  those who might be members of the Executive  Board.  After a discussion on the question of a time-  limit, Mme SAUNTE (Denmark) proposed the  deletion of the words “‘at least twenty-four hours”  in paragraph (2).  That suggestion was supported by Mr. THERON  (Union of South Africa).  It was agreed to delete the words “at least
24 hours”: Rule 94 as amended would then  read :
1. Each year, during its ordinary session, the Gen-  eral Conference shall elect by secret ballot six mem-  bers of the Executive Board for a term of three years.
2. Only delegates nominated either by the Nomi-  nations Committee or by two delegations, before the  ballot, may stand for election.
3. The General Conference shall follow the pro-  cedure set forth in Rules 30, 86 and 88.  Approved.  New Rule on Suspension, suggested by the  Canadian delegation (document 3C/PRO/7,  Annex II, item 26).  After an exchange of views, it was agreed to  re-word paragraph (c) of the Canadian draft, in  accordance with a suggestion made by Mr. PILE  (United Kingdom), so that the whole Rule would  read as follows :  No Rule of Procedure may be suspended unless :  (a) the motion for suspension has been introduced  in writing and handed to the Director-General;  (b) copies of the motion for suspension have been  circulated to all delegations not later than two days  preceding the meeting;  (c) suspension is already provided for under any  existing rule, or unless the motion for suspension is  adopted by a two-thirds majority of members present  and voting.  Approved.  It was also agreed that, as a consequence of  the above Rule, a new sub-paragraph (g) would  be added to Rule 80.  Rule 96.
