[84] Mr. GOROSTIZA (Mexico) recalled that the  monuments of art and history, which were the  common heritage of the whole human race, were  in danger through lack of the financial resources  necessary to enable experts and laboratories to  assemble scientific data. The Mexican delegation  accordingly proposed the adoption of an inter-  national convention under the terms of which  each signatory country would introduce a tax of  approximately $3, payable on arrival by all  tourists, who would receive in exchange a card  affording them free entry to all national museums  in the country. Funds secured in this way would  be allocated for the preservation and restoration  of monuments of archaeology, history and art. A  contribution equivalent to not less than 20 per cent  of the vield of the tax would be paid weekly to  Unesco’s International Committee of Museums and  Monuments. A conservative estimate of Unesco's  revenue under this proposal would be $6,000,000.  Thus the project was one whose repercussions on  the budget took the form of substantial receipts,  while its urgency and importance were such that  there was no need to emphasize them.
