5.1 Mr Puna (Cook Islands) :    Madam President, Madam Director-General, Excellencies, distinguished representatives, youth delegates,  ladies and gentlemen. I bring you warm greetings from the Cook Islands. When I spoke during the general policy debate  at the 38th session of the General Conference in 2015, UNESCO and the Cook Islands were both celebrating  anniversaries: UNESCO, its seventieth anniversary, and the Cook Islands, our fiftieth anniversary of self-government and  free association with New Zealand. We both reflected on milestones and achievements, set new goals, and explored  opportunities for the future. The question has to be asked now: where are we? Have we found our place in the new  global agenda? Do we have mechanisms in place to ensure that UNESCO is contributing effectively and efficiently to  global outcomes? Are we, as Member States, supporting the work and mission of UNESCO through our own policy
157  priorities to ensure that the Sustainable Development Goals are indeed globally achieved? Our own reflections in  preparation for this meeting again raised the questions of how our country can better contribute to the UNESCO agenda  and how the programmes and initiatives of UNESCO can support our own development pathway.
5.2 I am pleased to confirm that the Cook Islands honoured the commitment it made at the last session of the  General Conference. We have ratified the Convention for the Safeguarding of the Intangible Cultural Heritage. We have  re-established our Te Reo Maori Language Commission, our indigenous language commission, to ensure both the  protection and currency of our language. We also established the Cultural and Historical Places Board of Trustees to  support the protection and maintenance of valued places in our community and ensure that their stories are captured and  told for the next generation and beyond. The truth is that we highly value our traditional knowledge in any form, process  or practice. This traditional knowledge is part of our DNA. It is in our genes and therefore, particularly in the area of  conservation, drives our approach to the twenty-first century. We are small. In fact, we are very, very small. Yet we think  and we talk big. But we do not just talk. We walk the talk. You may recall my reference earlier in the week to the  establishment of Marae Moana – our Cook Islands Marine Park – dedicating our entire exclusive economic zone of 2  million square kilometres to sustainable development. Traditional knowledge and management practice are fundamental  concepts of Marae Moana.
5.3 We are also committed to the protection of our environment through the conversion of our energy sources to  renewable energy. Back in 2010, we made a very strong policy commitment to convert 50% of our energy sources to  renewables by 2015, and 100% by 2020. Ladies and gentlemen, Excellencies, we achieved our 50% target on time, in
2015. And we are on target to achieve our 100% target of conversion by 2020. Interestingly, we started this work with our  most isolated and fragile islands and communities, to ensure their energy independence – an achievement that is even  more significant when one considers the difficulties of transport and other services to remote communities, especially in  times of high risk due to tropical storms and cyclones.
5.4 The Cook Islands, as a member of the Pacific Heads of Education Systems Small Working Group, has been  engaged with the development of a new regional education framework, that will support the Pacific region as a whole in  working towards Sustainable Development Goal 4. My Government has made a commitment of over 4% of gross  domestic product (GDP) to education. That commitment has been realized, and it is my intention to continue it. We are  particularly focused on developing responses to SDG Goal 4.7, supporting the development of life skills in our young  people. We know that the issue of supporting our youth in a fast-changing twenty-first century is one that the whole  Pacific region, and I sure many of us, are dealing with. On this note, I am encouraged by the strong voice of youth we  heard on Monday, and their request to be seen as true partners, and not only as recipients or beneficiaries, in the global  discussion.
5.5 Within the Pacific region, we know each other well. We were all delighted on Monday at the inclusion of New  Caledonia as an Associate Member of UNESCO. We continue to work together for the betterment of our region, while  respecting the sovereignty and unique cultures and contributions of our individual countries and peoples. To this end, the  Cook Islands is humbled to have the unanimous support of all Pacific Islands countries and territories in our candidacy  for the Executive Board of UNESCO. As Pacific islanders, we often talk about our concept of family and what it means to  be part of the family. The Cook Islands stands ready to work with all members of the UNESCO family to ensure our world  will provide a safe, nurturing, understanding, peaceful and affirmative place for the next generation and beyond. This is  my commitment and the commitment of the Cook Islands. Thank you.

It seems that humanity has never been  so in need of vigilance in recalling the importance of the culture of peace and dialogue, which is our primary goal. 53.2 Madam President, the 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, adopted by the United Nations General  Assembly, obliges us to wage a devoted and decisive struggle to address the key problems we are facing today: building  peace and eradicating poverty, gender inequality, unequal opportunities for education, climate change and a healthy  environment. Montenegro strongly supports the principles of a new long-term strategy and gives its full contribution to the  implementation and achievement of its key objectives.
53.3 We recall here UNESCO’s precious support in the activities related to the natural, cultural and historical area of  Kotor, inscribed on the World Heritage List in 1979, the same year a devastating earthquake struck Montenegro. Thanks  to UNESCO-funded experts who offered immensely important assistance and support, the worst consequences of that  natural disaster were successfully remedied, enabling the authenticity and integrity of Kotor to be preserved. Since the  last session of the General Conference, two other extremely important cultural assets of Montenegro have been added to  UNESCO’s World Heritage List both as serial and multinational nominations: Stećci medieval graveyards and Kotor’s  Fortress as a part of the system of Venetian fortifications from the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries. Stećci are
184  medieval graveyards of Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Croatia and Serbia, while the Venetian fortifications are a  joint initiative of Italy, Montenegro and Croatia. We do believe that these enrichments enhance UNESCO’s world  heritage, as the UNESCO presence in those sites will be a boost to the prosperity of local communities. We are  particularly pleased at the possibility of the Forte Mare Fortress in Herceg Novi joining the Venetian fortifications already  inscribed, as recently announced by a decision of the World Heritage Committee.
53.4 Preserving nature and providing a healthy environment is an equally important contribution to UNESCO policies  in Montenegro. Montenegro has recently ratified the Paris Agreement on Climate Change, thereby expressing  unambiguously our responsibility for the environment. The Durmitor National Park is not only a unique natural  phenomenon that is on the UNESCO World Heritage List – it is also an area in which there are medieval graveyards, and  which is part of a biosphere reserve – the Tara River Basin – defined within UNESCO’s Man and the Biosphere  programme. In this programme, Montenegro has made significant progress this year thanks to continuous cooperation  with the UNESCO Office in Venice, our reliable and long-term partner. We also hope that UNESCO’s World Heritage List  will be further enriched with some other sites from Montenegro’s Tentative List in the near future.

It is necessary to make additional efforts to reduce  the number of children and young people who do not have access to education, to improve the position of young people  and achieve gender equality. Montenegro, as a part of UNESCO’s Associated Schools Project Network, is dedicated to  climate change challenges and sustainable development through education.
53.6 The UNESCO conventions in all domains represent the key principles on which Montenegrin public policies are  based.
