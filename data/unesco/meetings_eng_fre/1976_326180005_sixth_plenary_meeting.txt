(10.9) Mr. President, allow me to make a brief survey of what my country has been able to achieve in the  field of education since His Majesty Sultan Kabous came to power. Since the great Omani upsurge of 1970  a modern system of education has been developing at maximum speed, in an attempt to compensate for the  backwardness imposed on Omani citizens for long years past. The strategy of action at this time was suited  to the stage of upsurge, when education was extended to almost all parts of the Sultanate. While the number  of schools in 1969/1970 was only three boys' schools, that number rose in 1976/1977 to 238 schools, com-  prising 105 for boys, 54 for girls and 79 coeducational. During the same period, the number of classes  rose from 24 classes for boys to 1, 976 classes for boys and girls. The number of pupils rose from 900  boys in the year 1969/1970 to 68, 700 boys and girls in 1976/1977. The development which has occurred  during the two school years, from the eighteenth session of Unesco's General Conference in 1974 up to the  present, is illustrated by the fact that the number of schools has increased by 35 per cent, and the number  of students by 40 per cent. The number of teaching staff has risen from 2,115 to 2,659, representing an  increase of 26 per cent. This speedy quantitative increase has brought education to a stage which required  a stand for reconsideration, revision, with a view to working for the development of education in sucha way  that it may achieve its objectives in keeping with the general trend in the Sultanate, that the new stage of  development may be based on sound planning and not left to haphazard growth. On this premise, a new  strategy was embodied in a five-year plan for*the Ministry of Education, covering the period from 1976/
1977 to 1980/1981. The purpose of this plan is to improve the standard and quality of education and to  make education an effective instrument for the achievement of the aims of Omani development.

12,2 Mr, President, since the last session of the General Conference two years ago in Paris, the  world has witnessed a continued plethora of political, social and economic problems and crises.  The imbalance of international relations, inflation, recession, inequalities of development, monet-  ary and energy inadequacies, needless to say, compel all of us gathered here today to take a fresh  look at the life of our Organization and at the role it should and can play to make this world a bet-  ter place to live in, Therefore, Mr, President and fellow delegates, may we together in frankness  and openness, address ourselves to the various issues before us, remembering that we represent  an infinitely rich variety of peoples, many systems and patterns of life, each of which has its own  style, integrity and identity, and every one of which is to be respected and recognized in its own  right, Only in such a framework can just and innovative solutions be found and set in motion, I  suggest that today we reflect and we listen again with our minds and hearts, to the aspirations and  feelings of the peoples of our respective homelands, of our neighbours, of our mutual world. Then  We may act, with determination and vision in our work here,
12.3 I wish, in this connection, to pay tribute to the Director-General, Mr, Amadou-Mahtar M'Bow,  for his devoted efforts in making Unesco a viable instrument for achieving peace and international  order, The preparatory work for this Conference, which includes such important documents as  Moving Towards Change'', the Draft Medium-Term Plan (1977-1982) (document 19 C/4), the  Evaluation of Unesco's Activities in the Perspective of Development, and the Draft Programme  and Budget for 1977-1978 (document 19 C/5), contain a rich variety of ideas and food for thought. And again, we endorse the use  of what has been called indigenous expertise in the context of international collaboration, The  experts of the developing countries have much to offer, as well as to learn, Further, we appeal  that the funding patterns for Unesco be greatly improved through a renewed determination to make  the Organization survive and live.
12.4 The Draft Programme and Budget itself is worthy of our closest attention, since it reflects  the careful and expanding role of this Organization in the world today. The Secretariat hag obvi-  ously prepared a preliminary document which, in specific and tangible terms, represents the  vision of a very vital future for Unesco, The shifting role and the revised programme designs are  laid out in actual terms of activities and costs, of new emphases, as well as sustaining what ig  worthwhile of the past.
12.5 Let us look for a moment at the Organization's specific role in the field of human rights,  As the Director-General, Mr, M'Bow, has so often said, now is the time, as never before, to  protest against prejudice and injustice, which are unfortunately becoming more and more wide-  spread. Human rights do not exist merely on paper, They exist in the hearts and minds of all  people, in all nations, And, to Unesco itself, in a remarkable and perhaps unique way, falls the  responsibility of fostering the growth and strengthening the reality of these freedoms through  education, through science, and through culture. The impressive array of conferences, seminars  and meetings, the exchange of and utilization of short- and long-term consultancies, the ever-  broadening collection of available materials, attest to the rigorous role of the Organization in  this field.
12.6 In a very real way this effort ties in with man's quest for peace in this world. Today, peace  and stability can only be based upon human rights and freedoms. Again, the Organization has  shown a profound awareness of its responsibilities in this quest. We know that an enlightened edu-  cational programme is not a luxury but a necessity, Therefore, the key problems in education -  functional illiteracy, poorly trained teachers, archaic curricula, a shortage or absence of books  or instructional materials, a shortage of school buildings - are being stressed in the activities set  out in the Draft Programme and Budget. We look forward to participating in a number of these  programmes, In science, a highly specialized branch of education, it is also necessary that in  order to flourish any society must have scientific capabilities as a part of its strength and self-  identity. Further, it is paramount that individual cultures not only avoid debasement and, worse  still, extinction, but that they be able to arrive at the discovery of their cultural identity and to  build and sustain themselves. In Indonesia, we are conscious of the imperative of self-reliance  and national sovereignty in all phases of life, economic, social, political, scientific and cultural,  This national resilience, or power of self-renewal, is a prerequisite for equity and mutual respect  in the family of nations, Underdevelopment, inadequacies and specific problems can in fact be  solved best by domestic indigenous measures.
12,7 Recent world events, recession and inflation, the continuing crisis of financing, the constant  call for trained manpower, the exploitation of natural resources, are all based irreversibly upon  the world economic situation, Consequently, the call for a new economic order, a massive re-  balancing of the wealth and resources of the world, deserves full support. This will necessitate  thorough and drastic changes in economic thought and adjustments in the thinking of all people.  And, yet, Mr, President, as an educational policy-maker with a medical background, as a univer-  sity teacher and as a politician, I still feel that unless we prove ourselves able to transcend the  technicalities of contemporary work in education, science and communication, towards those objec:  tives of Unesco which are no longer ideals but are, in fact imperatives for human survival, we will  regress in becoming a generation of failure. We should therefore endeavour to develop our cultural  identities and richnesses, not merely as elements for display and dramatic representation, but in  an attempt to translate them into a universal, cultural behaviour which makes it possible for man  tolive in dignity and peace. The challenge for mankind today is not limited to achieving scientificand  technical excellence, Important as these are, they have to be inspired by cultural values and correspond-  ing cultural behaviour which enrich and introduce harmony in the life of the human species.

In the world of today, in the untiring search  of individual nations and international organizations alike for a new economic order, we need,  Mr, President, a concerted effort for the culturalization of human affairs, Let us now in these  days, more than ever before, discuss and deliberate in the spirit of culture which must bear the  fruits of peace, prosperity and understanding among all peoples.

16.2 Before I deal with any other matter I wish, on behalf of the Government of Sweden, to extend  a warm welcome to the countries that have joined Unesco since the last session of the General  Conference: Grenada, Mozambique, Papua New Guinea, the Seychelles and Surinam and to our  prospective member, the People's Republic of Angola,
16,3 Sweden has always strictly adhered to the principle of universality in the membership of the  United Nations and its agencies. To be "the centre for harmonizing the actions of nations’ - in the  words of Article 1,4 of the United Nations Charter - the United Nations and its agencies must be  organizations where all the States of the world have an opportunity to make their voices heard This is why we oppose any proposal or decision to limit the membership of the United Nations.  organizations or to restrict the participation of States in our deliberations and regional activities.  Support for the United Nations and its Specialized Agencies is a cornerstone of Swedish foreign  policy. The readiness of the Swedish Government remains to contribute towards making the United  Nations system an organization for the broadest possible co-operation between all countries in  order to promote security for human beings all over the world.
16.4 And now I turn to the matters of development and to the role of Unesco in today's world,  Recently a well-known British magazine asked a class of schoolchildren what they understood by  development, One twelve-year-old boy said: "People should not be hungry, They should not be  ill all the time. They should have housing and schools'. The idea that development means meet-  ing first of all the basic needs of man is now beginning to be the insight of all who take part in the  discussion on development. The widening gap between rich and poor countries has for a long time  been the central theme of the international development dialogue, Our primary concern must be  to correct the staggering inequality that exists between men, and to establish a world economic  order that meets the just demands of the poor countries, A continued growth of productive capa-  city in the poor countries is required as part of these efforts.
16,5 In the last few years greater attention has been focused on the distribution of resources with-  in countries. There are a number of reasons for this. For the developing nations, substantial  evidence indicates that although impressive growth has taken place in many of them, broad groups  of the population have not had a reasonable share in the benefits of increased resources - or may  not have benefited at all. This situation affects in my opinion one basic assumption for develop-  ment co-operation. In the opinion of Sweden a redirection of both international and national pol -  icies in favour of the poor is necessary. In the context of international co-operation for develop-  ment we need a set of common objectives such as those provided by the so-called basic-needs  strategy which was adopted by the World Employment Conference last summer, in which nearly  all countries took part. Basic needs include essential services that should be provided by the  community for all its members such as safe drinking water, sanitation, health and educational  and cultural facilities. Here and today it is appropriate to emphasize in particular the educational  and cultural services, since these are the fields where Unesco has a special responsibility in the  overall endeavours to combat poverty. A basic needs-oriented policy must also involve the parti-  cipation of the people in the decision-making process - young and old, men or women, To facili-  tate such participation it is of great importance that adequate information and communication  facilities are provided,
16,6 The decisions to be taken by this Conference will affect not only the six years ahead. They  will also form the basis for the work of this Organization for the rest of this century. The Swedish  delegation will be active in putting forward proposals for a Unesco policy which will focus the atten-  tion of this Organization on the basic needs of education and culture as well as on the application of  science in the development effort. Therefore Sweden will support primarily activities aiming at  meeting the basic needs of education of children and of adults, including handicapped groups. We  will support programmes which mean the waging of war against the evil of illiteracy and which in  our opinion increase the ability of poor people to fight hunger and thirst, illness and cultural  starvation,
16.7 In his Introduction to the Draft Medium-Term Plan, the Director-General rightly points out  that the General Conference has on many occasions stressed the need to avoid the dispersion of  efforts; he has referred to the dissipation of limited resources on projects that are starved of funds  and have consequently failed to contribute towards achieving the general aims of Unesco, There-  fore I submit that it is now urgent to reach a concentration of our efforts. This is where the pro-  cess of setting priorities must come in, This process is sometimes painful, To set priorities is  to choose, and - when resources are limited as they are indeed in the case of Unesco - this means  to say no to a number of things in order to be in a position to say yes to a few, but basic, things.  If you give priority to everything, you will give precedence to nothing, In using the yardstick of a  basic-needs strategy this Conference has to go through the objectives proposed in the Plan and ask  itself on each and every item: is this what we have to concentrate on now, as it is our task to do  first things first? Through the discussions of this Conference we may not reach final conclusions  in this respect, The Director-General should in any case be given firm guidance to continue this  important task, In his endeavours, he should see to it that the programmes and the priorities of  Unesco form an integrated part of the development efforts of the whole United Nations system,
16,8 Mr, President, let me bring up a subject that has to do with the confidence to be vested in  Unesco as an Organization which - in the words of Julian Huxley, the first Director-General -  cannot "espouse one of the political and economic doctrines competing in the world today to the  exclusion of the others!!, I refer to one of the dimensions of basic human needs, namely the parti-  cipation of the people in making the decisions which affect them. In this process, the mass media  certainly can contribute to the mobilization of the people in the development efforts and support  their participation, The fact that the mass media play an important role in forming opinions has  often led to attempts by authorities to control them, Those in possession of power know that the
347   freely spoken or written word is a mighty force, capable of changing societies and of making people  question established truths. In many countries it is the newspapers that exercise this important  scrutinizing function, They can put the spotlights on forgotten problems, on abuses of power, and  on corruption, A society that does not permit open eriticism of its leadership and its institutions,  becomes a rigid and oppressive society. Criticism, which at first seems exaggerated, negative  and even destructive, may prove useful in the long run, Criticism is always uncomfortable for  those that hold economic and political power - but it is precisely in order to control and balance  their exercise of power that one needs a free and independent press and a free debate,
16.9 Recent developments in the field of mass media are in many ways alarming, Monopoly trends  emerge in both developed and developing countries. The more such trends grow, the greater are  the risks of one-sidedness and uniformity. It is deplorable that the gap between rich and poor  countries in the field of mass media is widening - even if the figures show an increase in newspaper  consumption and radio-listening in the developing countries. Among other things the high costs of  distribution a d of newsprint have hampered the increase in newspaper reading in those countries,  Another part of the problem is the dominating role played by a few international news agencies in  the flow of information between countries and between continents. This dominance has led to an  imbalance in the flow that must be cured, The growing demands for a new order in the field of  information that is raised by the non-aligned nations are understandable. It is, however, also im-  portant to condemn censorship and other forms of interference by any government in the contents  of the mass media, In too many countries there are limits set to the free flow of information and  to free debate.
16,10 Unesco is a natural forum for lively discussion on these and other problems in the mass  media field, A starting point for such further discussion must be that the right to communicate  also implies the right to critical scrutiny of the national power structure. It is a crime to throw  a journalist in jail because of his writing - a crime not only to the journalist but also to those who  are deprived of the right to receive the information in order to form an opinion of their own, There  are of course many other functions to be fulfilled by mass media - functions which should respond  to the basic communication needs in the different societies. The media should, however, never be  reduced to propaganda mouth-pieces of the powerful,
16,11 I have mentioned some reasons why the Swedish delegation is strongly opposed to the adop-  tion of the Draft Declaration on Fundamental Principles Governing the Use of the Mass Media,  This draft is based upon the unacceptable principle that governments should have a responsibility  for the contents of the mass media. Such a declaration would mean the acceptance of a mass  media ideology directly contrary to the principles set out in the Universal Declaration of Human  Rights. The principle that the authorities refrain from interfering in journalistic activities does  not leave governments as passive bystanders in the development of mass media, The monopoly  trends in the newspaper field as well as the dependency on commercial interests can be counter-  acted with special supporting measures. In the international sphere there are good reasons to  support the co-operation in the news agency field recently initiated by the non-aligned nations,  This could become a valuable addition to the international agency services - but it can never be  accepted as something purporting to replace existing news services.
16.12 If a declaration concerning the mass media should at all be adopted in Unesco, it must be  based on a balanced ideological foundation, As I see it, there are a lot of issues concerning the  mass media that remain to be discussed further in Unesco. This session of the General Confer-  ence should, therefore, not impose a deadlock on those discussions,

(28.1) H.E. Mr. BEN ABDELJLIL (Morocco) (Translation from the Arabic):  Mr. President, distinguished delegates, this nineteenth session of the General Conference of Unesco  coincides with the 30th anniversary of the foundation of our distinguished Organization. This age is  usually-associated with the full bloom of youth and the threshold of old age, with all the connotations of  enthusiasm and the beginning of maturity. Thus, our Organization witnesses a stage in whichithas reached  a degree of consciousness and activity equal to the maturity of its views and its deep insight. This session  also coincides with the convening of the Conference in Africa for the first time - in this good country, 80  beautiful, with its well-known tradition of struggle. On behalf of my country as well as on my own behal,  I have pleasure in congratulating you, Mr, President, on the great confidence placed in you by our Confer-  ence at its present session. I also wish to congratulate the Director-General who supervises the peu  of the Organization and is responsible for its future. We congratulate ourselves on the progress achieved  by our Organization along the road to improvement and advancement, and on the great hopes we place in i  for the establishment of peace, justice and brotherhood.
(28.2) Mr. President, we are all pleased with the sound line now being followed by Unesco; since the last  two sessions it has come to realize the responsibilities which it should assume. Over the past 30 years,  Unesco has succeeded in spreading a consciousness among individuals and peoples, in awakening and devel:  oping in oppressed nations the ambition for serious, constructive work, and in raising vital issues wh  respect to culture, science and communication. It has acted as an authority, a stimulant and a pioneer (0!  the people who had struggled for liberation, had then found themselves robbed of their personality and,  sometimes, powerless to emerge rapidly from the state of backwardness, ignorance, ill-health mpd pit.  Thanks to its officials and experts, the various conferences and meetings it organizes, and the technica !  and scientific assistance it offers, Unesco has been able to guide, assist and stimulate co-operation,  petition, the exchange of expertise and experience. However, it soon sensed that a great danger threaten
362         its existence; namely, dispersal of effort, submersion in minor issues, proliferation of operati 1 activi  ties. The fact is that, by extending its assistance to the establishment of numerous a a or  international centres and institutes, by offering aid to governmental and non-governmental et RE  organizations, by establishing a large number of Regional Offices in the sphere of education and science  and by publishing numerous specialized reports, studies books and periodicals, it has carried out the first  stage of its responsibility, that is to say, the achievement of an upsurge and the facilitation and encourage-  ment of decentralization. It is now clear that the Organization should move on with its activities to the  ond stage, which differs completely from the first and consists in complete dedication to standardization  normalization, comparison and serving the major issues which preoccupy mankind and threaten its exist-  ence. It is no longer possible for Unesco, now that its Member States number 140, now that the horizons  of knowledge and experience in the domains of education, science, technology, culture and communication  have broadened, to maintain absolute centralization and continue in partial operational works.
(28.3) Consideration of man, individually and socially, as the pivot of life and the supreme object of all  progress and development, has moved Unesco to follow immediately the United Nations in the service of  development, the preservation of peace, and the promotion of human rights through education, culture  science and communication. The definition of the problems and objectives which won the approval of the  members of our Organization, so that those problems and objectives became the linchpin of medium-term  planning, of co-ordination between the sexennial plan and the Unesco draft programme for the coming  biennium - all this helps to provide the necessary framework for action, brings viewpoints closer, and  creates circumstances calculated to develop in the world a new character - one in which justice, peace  and fruitful co-operation prevail, banishes the spectre of war, diminishes disparities between individuals,  groups and peoples, and eliminates discrimination, racialism, Zionism and ignorance. Our Organization's  co-operation with the United Nations in working to establish peace and to create a new world in which justice  and equality shall prevail, disparities shall be removed and man shall enjoy his rights, based on a new  world economic order, is a credit to it, exalts it, and adds new fields of activity to those of its present  competence, whose object is the happiness of man and the protection of his civilization.
(28.4) Among the problems worthy of attention and which concern mankind as a whole, is the preservation  of the glorious and authentic relics of Jerusalem without alteration, and the follow-up of all the resolutions  of the eighteenth session pertaining to this subject.
(28.5) The demographic explosion in the world and the spread of the mass mediahave brought human beings  closer to one another, both materially and morally, and have conduced to a solidarity in the world in re-  spect of its future destiny. The availability of raw materials and energy, the creation of a dialogue between  rich and poor nations, betweeen North and South, between East and West, are issues which no one has the  right henceforth to ignore or disregard. It has thus become the duty of every State to participate in study  and research with all other States on methods designed to banish the spectre of war, to find food for all,  to provide sufficient water for agriculture, industry and man's utilities, to free man's environment from  pollution, poisoning and slow destruction. Unesco's contribution to the Second Development Decade is the  crowning of the concerted efforts exerted by the organizations of the United Nations system, whose results  are beginning to be apparent despite the paucity of the funds allocated to them as compared with the immense  allocations made for armaments.
(28.6) The spirit manifested in the sexennial plan and the draft programme in dealing with the present world  situation is worthy of every consideration, and calculated to open new hopes for humanity, despite the  major problems now facing it, and the great dangers which can destroy the human race, unless the world  becomes enlightened and replaces mental bigotry and deviation by balance and tolerance. The invitation to  dialogue and co-operation between nations and, at the same time, the inducement to give promience to  their cultural identity are two inseparable aspects, which are non-contradictory and which achieve unity  in diversity. The confirmation of respect for cultural identity in the development of societies, and the  utilization of science and technology in ways fitted to the identity of the environment and the circumstances  peculiar to each State, are two basic conditions in the balanced natural development of each nation. This  development depends, however, on regional and international co-operation which is able to promote and  hasten it, and to ensure its success.
(28.7) Morocco neglects no opportunity of expanding and encouraging this co-operation. Noteworthy  among the measures taken by the Kingdom of Morocco is the proposal it made to the International Con-  ference of Ministers and Senior Officials responsible for Physical Education and Sport, convened at Unesco's  Headquarters last April, which provided for the establishment of an international fund to raise the standard  of sports and develop sports installations in the developing countries. This proposal was approved by the  conference and taken into consideration by the Director-General of Unesco; it was also mentioned in the  Director-General's address at the closing of the conference. The Moroccan delegate announced his govern-  ment's readiness to make an immediate contribution to this fund.
(28.8) Mr. President, my country is proud to belong to two regions, the African regionand the Arabregion,  which are both striving by the side of the countries of the Third World to raise the standard of their people,  and to eliminate all kinds of disparity and injustice and all causes of conflict. My country works with all  its might to promote co-operation within the compass of the two regions. Morocco has recently had the  honour to host the Conference of Arab Ministers responsible for Science Policies, with the assistance and  supervision of Unesco and ALECSO. I personally had the honour to preside over the conference and to chair  the permanent committee to follow up the work of the conference. A number of recommendations were  passed, which will be submitted for consideration by your distinguished Conference so that you may adopt  whatever resolutions you may think advisable, with a view to Arab co-operation for economic and social  development, which can only be achieved through the support of each country's infrastructure in the sphere  of science, technology and the development of human resources.
(28.9) In conclusion, I am also pleased to draw attention to the report prepared by the group charged by the  Conference to consider the major international problems and Unesco's contribution towards finding solutions  to them. I shall mention only two of the enlightened ideas, as follows: the first is to call on future genera-  tions to restrict immoderate consumption, and to reduce the depletion and pollution of nature, which would  force future generations - whose number will be doubled - to live in decaying environments; the second is  that scientists should direct their research in accordance with their country's circumstances and in the
363   spirit of their particular environment, because there is a type of science monopoly, far more serious than  the brain drain, which prevents us from benefiting from the diversity of circumstances of life and the  various cultures capable of creativity. Thank you all for your kind attention. God be with you.
