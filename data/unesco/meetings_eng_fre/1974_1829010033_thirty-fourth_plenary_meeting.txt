9,2 Madam President, when Unesco was founded just under thirty years ago it was a small  Organization a d the interests it represented were virtually homogeneous, At the time what  was best for the Organization probably mattered more than what was best for the world. This was  the time when idealism rather than realism was a guiding principle in the Organization's activities,  At that time, high and outstanding qualities of individuals was the most important qualification for  membership of the Executive Board, The Board was then conceived as a kind of élitist club and  for a long tinie this concept was used by interested groups to keep others out of it, Unesco was  then not truly living up to all its constitutional duties,
9.3 With the accession of more members to Unesco over the years, however, the Organization  became and is now heterogeneous, truly universal and true to its Constitution, Unesco's  programmes of activities, the prime function of the Organization, are now found in all its Member  States and are of concern to all of them as contributors to its budget.
9,4 The Executive Board members elected by the Member States at General Conferences have  become custodians of the interests of all Member States which are governments, including the  governments of their own countries, in between General Conferences, It istherefore inconceivable  that an Executive Board member can faithfully discharge his functions, whataver his personal  qualifications, once he ceases to enjoy the confidence or supportof his government. Unesco itself,  an intergovernmental organization, would find itself in an equally embarrassing situation,
9.5 Madam President, we therefore see in the Swedish proposal an effort to further adapt the  Executive Board to the changes that have taken place in Unesco, towards heterogeneity and  universalism, and to marryidealism with realism, We see in that proposal an attempt to bring  together what is best for the world represented by Member States and what is best for the  Organization, We also see In it an attempt to save the Organization, Member States and the mem-  bers concerned from possible embarrassing situations,
