1. The PRESIDENT: .  The meeting is called to order. First 1 give the floor to the Deputy Director-General for a  statement concerning items 12; 4 and 63. - )
2.1 Mr. FOBES (Deputy Director-General):  Madam President, yesterday the Conference had a discussion on items 12, 4 and 63, that is,  the item on ways and means by which Unesco should contribute to the establishment of a new  economic order, and the item on ways and means by which Unesco could participate in the current  reflection on possible changes in structures of the United Nations system. At the close of that  exchange - which incidentally dealt not only with document 18 C/103, but also with three draft  resolutions (18 C/PLEN/DR. 13, 14 and 15), as well as with an amendment to one of those resolu-  tions which is found in 18 C/PLEN/DR. 16 - the Conference was considering the establishment of  a working group to take another look at the draft resolutions, in an effort to bring them before  the plenary for final decision in a form which would secure wide support.
2.2 It seemed, however, that it might be useful for the Conference to hear the views of the  Director-General in the light of the debate that has taken place. That is the purpose of my re-  marks, which presumably would lead you back to the possible establishment of a working group,  as seemed to be generally agreed yesterday.
2.3 Most of my remarks will deal with the question of the establishment of a new economic  order and Unesco's contribution to that, but before touching upon that main subject I would say a  few words on 18 C/PLEN/DR. 14, which is a draft resolution submitted by a number of countries  and which deals with reorganization of the Secretariat. It was explained by the sponsors that  they felt that, after many years, reorganization was necessary and would help the Organization  to participate in the creation of the new economic order, and for that reason they submitted this  draft resolution under agenda items 12, 4 and 63. The reaction of the Director-General to this  draft resolution is that he could accept it, but wonders whether it is entirely necessary, since he  has already made known his intention to examine carefully the structures and procedures of the  Secretariat. In fact I bèlieve he will have something to say on that subject in a closing declara-  tion that he will make at the end of the Conference next week. In that sense, since he already  knows that decentralization is a subject that the Director-General has been asked to pursue, and  since rotation of staff is among the subjects discussed by the Administrative Commission and  recommended to the Director-General, the points that one finds in 18 C/PLEN/DR. 14 in effect  have already been made and hardly need to be recalled. His examination of the Secretariat and  its structure and methods of operation will be on the broadest possible scale and will lead him,  while it proceeds, to talk to the Executive Board. He wanted me to give that assurance when  you consider the need for such a draft resolution as 18 C/PLEN/DR. 14.
2.4 In respect to the amendments to 18 C/PLEN/DR. 14 which you will find fn 18 C/PLEN/DR:16,  he has some problems and in fact some objectloris to the additional paragraphs, which he would  like the General Conference to consider. In the propbsed additional sub-paragraph (d} which you  find in 18 C/PLEN/DR. 16, the Director-General would be invited to observe more effectively the  principle of the equality of cultures in geographical distribution, First of all, this is a reference  to a principle which {s not found in the Constitution, .whereas that of geographical distribution is.  Secondly, this equality of cultures in geographical distribution is to be taken into account not only  in recruitment of staff but also in internal promotion of staff at all levels in the Secretariat. It  seems to the Director-General that this requirement would.run counter to the Constitution and to  the Staff Regulations as approved by.the General Conference, whereby, subject to the highest .  standards of integrity and competence, and fo geographical distribution, appointments and promo-  tions of staff of the Secretariat are left entirely to the Director-General. In particular, the pro-  motion of staff and thé transfer of staff inside thé Secretariat are not subject to this consideration  of geographical distribution, which is important at the recruitment stage.
2.5 Secondly, Madam Président, in sub-paragraph (e) one finds the request that the Director-  General cbserve more gtrictly the provisions of the Constitution with regard to intellectual étan-  dards and competence in recruiting staff for all positions of responsibility. The Director>General  feels that such a request is not necessary and may not be entirely appropriate and proper to make  to him. Similarly on the final paragraph which {tis proposed to add - but this is'a relatively  minor point - he'does of coursé report to the Executive Board regularly, and to the General Con-  ference,’ on geographical distfibulton of staff and'on Appointments of staff. He feels it unnetes-  sary té réquest this'again in this resolution, partiéularly as it seems superfluous to réquest that  the Directér-Gerérat make an objective report. I think it will be found that all his reports are  presumed to be objective.
2.6 Madam President, these are all the remarks I have to make on 18 C/PLEN/DR. 14 and the  proposed amendments in 18 C/PLEN/DR. 16. They are observations of the Director-General,  which we would hope would be taken into account when a working group examines the resolutions  on behalf of the plenary, Also, consideration might be given to the possibility of waiting for the  remarks of the Director-General next week, when he will discuss his intentions in respect of the  review of the Secretariat as to structures and procedures.
2.7 I turn now to what is perhaps the heart of the matter before us, what is called a new inter-  national economic order. And I would like to make three points. First | would like to describe  the background which has led to the present situation on the question of establishing a new inter-  national economic order, and what is likely to happen within the United Nations system in the  next eleven months. Secondly, the Director-General would like to suggest the nature of the  action which it is desirable for this General Conference to take. And thirdly, I will offer his  specific comments on the two draft resolutions before you, that is 18 C/PLEN/DR. 13 and
18 C/PLEN/DR. 15. But I think I should stress that events within the United Nations system, to  examine its own structures and to see what it can do to bring about a new economic order, are  going to enroll very rapidly in the next eleven months. And therefore your broad guidance and  your general authorization for the Director-General to act when these rapid events occur is  important.
2.8 First of all, let me describe how we got to the present situation. The subject of a new inter-  national economic order and new structures for the United Nations system really arose first in
1973, when the United Nations Economic and Social Council and the General Assembly decided to  prepare for the mid-term review and appraisal of the Second Development Decade. At that time,  after the Economic and Social Council had set in motion this mid-term review and appraisal, the  General Assembly adopted a resolution, number 3172, In this resolution the General Assembly  decided to hold a special session, In September 1975, to examine the political and other implica-  tions of the state of world development and international economic co-operation, expanding the  dimenslons and concepts of world economic and developmental co-operation. The special session  was to consider new concepts and options in order to assist in the evolution of a system of world  economic relations based on the equality and common interests of all countries. I am emphasiz-  ing this, Madam President, to show that in 1973 the General Assembly had already decided on a  special session and had already decided that that special session should examiue the economie  structures of the world,
2.9 Also during that same year, the Economic and Social Council had asked for reports on agree-  ments among the agencies of the United Nations system and for reports from all those agencies as  to how their operational activities were contributing to the Development Decade, and taken several  other actions which showed that the process of examination of the United Nations system was  already launched. I am emphasizing therefore, that even before the special session of the Gen-  eral Assembly this spring, which led to a Declaration on a'new international economic order, the  process of re-examination of structures was already launched; and in a sense, therefore, even  if there had been no Declaration on a new international economic order this spring, the issue  would have been before this General Conference in any event, and the Conference would have been  called upon to give guidance to the Director-General and the Executive Board as to how they should  participate in all the preparatory work which is going to lead up to that special session of the  General Assembly in September of next year.
2.10 I should say here that after the General Assembly had passed resolution 3172 last year,  and after the special session had adopted by consensus the Declaration on a New Economic Order,  the Economic and Social Council in July of this year adopted a resolution'which is referred to  quite properly in 18 C/PLEN/DR. 13. It is Economic and Social Council resolution 1911, and it  is really under that resolution that we have been proceeding to prepare for this coming special  General Assembly session in September next year, because it is that resolution that finally makes  it clear that each Executive Head of an agency in the United Nations system should go before his  governing body, give it a report on the reorientation and adaptation of his programme of work -  which we now have done in document 18 C/103 - and that each agency should then submit a report  on the implementation of the Declaration on the New International Order to thé Economic and So-  cial Council next year, before the spécial session of the General Assembly. The Director-General  is prepared to present that report on behalf of Unesco in the light of whatever comments and guid-  ance you give him on document 18 C/103. That is the background as to why the issue is before  you. It would have come before you even had there been no Declaration on a New International  Economic Order,
2.11 Now I would like to say what is likely to happen in the next eleven months. At the General  Assembly in New York right now there is a draft resolution which would confirm the preparations  which have already been decided or initiated by the Economic and Social Council, namely: that  there be a preparatory committee of governments for the special session of the General Assembly  next year; that the Economie and Social Council Committee on Programme Co-ordination will  hold a special session fn February next year on structures of the United Natfons system - so as  you can see, we will already be asked for any Unesco comments we have to offer in February, at  a meeting of an ECOSOC committee; and presumably, that the first meeting of the preparatory  committee of governments for the special session of the General Assembly would take place in  April. So after the February meeting, at which we should be prepared to present some Informa-
30   tion, we will have the preparatory committee of governments in April, when they will also want  to know about Unesco's contribution towards the establishment of a new economic order and re-  view of structures. And the third event will of course be the Economic and Social Council regu-  lar session in July, just before the special session of the Assembly in September, In between  these meetings, of course, the Executive Board will be meeting in April and May and will be  kept informed by the Director-General as to the status of preparations and as to exactly what he  has provided to the Economic and Social Council, or the preparatory committee of governments.
2.12 I should say here, perhaps, that the so-called ACC, or Committee of the Executive Heads  of all agencles, met recently in New York, took note of the need to prepare reports for next  year's General Assembly, considered that the members of ACC should exchange notes on what  thelr organizations were dolng, and in general be ready to respond if the Economic and Social  Council wanted comments and judgements from the heads of agencies. I say this to stress the  importance of the guidance that you give the Director-General so that he can participate in any  action by the ACC. Those are the principal facts, background and current situation.
2.13 My second point deals with the nature of desirable action by this Conference. You will re-  call that [ mentioned that events are going to move very quickly. We need an authorization for  such reports to the Economic and Social Council - either its Committee on Programme Co-  ordination, or its preparatory committee for the special session of the Assembly. We need not  only an authorization for such reports as we may be asked for, but an authorization, in effect,  to participate in the meetings of those bodies and to respond at any time during their debates.  We will, of course, furnish to the Economic and Social Council all relevant reports and docu-  ments, and your resolutions, including document 18 C/103, In addition to this directive to  furnish reports as requested, we would therefore welcome such supplementary advice as you  deem appropriate. For example, one can already see from your debates that there was general  support for one of the items which the Director-General mentions in document 18 C/103, namely,  the importance of joint medium-term planning. in the United Nations system. We would also, of  course, take as advice already, even in the absence of a resolution, the strong support voicedby  speakers here that Unesco should emphasize the socio-cultural dimensions of a new International  Economic Order.
2.14 You might also wish to provide a directive to the Executive Board on what it might do. We  will, of course, keep the Board informed, at its meeting in May, as to the state of affairs, but  this Conference might wish to ask or authorize the Executive Board itself to respond to any re-  quest coming from the Economic and Social Council.
2.15 My last point, Madam President, is to comment on the draft resolutions which are before  you, and I refer particularly to 18 C/PLEN/DR. 13 and 18 C/PLEN/DR. 15. I would not comment  on the elements of substance in 18 C/PLEN/DR. 13, I mean on the interpretation of the Declara-  tion or on certain statements of principle which the sponsors are laying before you, because that  is not appropriate for the Director-General. I would say that other paragraphs, however, which  do not interpret the Declaration on the New Order, or which offer principles for adoption by this  Conference, and those further paragraphs which simply draw interpretations or implications for  Unesco from the decisions of the General Assembly or the Economic and Social Council, do not,  in the view of the Director-General, raise any questions of interpretation or practicability. For  example, he notes especially the penultimate paragraph of the preamble, which considers that  Unesco is directly concerned by the Declaration and the Programme of Actlon, particularly in  connexion with access to science and technology, the training of personnel required for national  development and the promotion of exchanges of information. This is the kind of directive, or  interpretation of implications for Unesco of what the General Assembly has done, which seems  entirely appropriate and practical and upon which the Director-General could easily react in any  reports that he makes to the Economic and Social Council, That is the only comment the  Director-General wished to make on 18 C/PLEN/DR. 13,
2.16 As to 18 C/PLEN/DR. 15, the Director-General notes that it provides guidance - and here  he is thinking particularly of paragraphs 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 and paragraph 8 - which could assist  him in preparation for the special session of the General Assembly. These are paragraphs which  try to draw the implications for Unesco of the actions of the General Assembly, particularly the  Declaration on the New International Economic Order. And the only observation that he would  make is that paragraph 8 singles out educational planning, scientific and technical identification  of obstacles to development, inventory of natural resources and co-ordination of science policles,  which could clearly be important among Unesco's contributions to a New Order, and thereforeare  very relevant. He would simply want to say that, if this text were to be adopted by this Confer-  ence, he would not regard this listing of four points as exclusive or limiting, for it would seem  that there are a number of other aspects of Unesco's programme which would also make a valu-  able contribution to the New Order.
2.17 Finally, the Director-General, on 18 C/PLEN/DR,15, would say that since he is likely to  be called upon to offer advice on the structures of the United Nations system, the views expressed  in paragraphs 6 and 7 of 18 C/PLEN/DR. 15 would be helpful to him if the Conference, in fact,  were to adopt those paragraphs. They express a view about structures and procedures, particu-  larly as to how éo-ordination would take place in the United Nations system, which, if it were the  Conference's decièlon, he would act upon and convey to the Economic and Social Council and the.  General Assembly.
2.18 Madam President, I have tried to provide comments by the Diréctor-General of a nature 29  that would help a working group to examine these draft resolutions, as | say, to see how they  might be put in form to gather the maximum possible support in this Conference. Thank you.
