(2,1) Mr, ROMANOVSKY (Union of Soviet Socialist Republics) (Translation from the Russian):  Mr, President, ladies and gentlemen, the Soviet delegation has already stated, ina speech  made when the agenda was under consideration, that the Soviet Government proposed that the  question of "Unesco's tasks in the promotion of peace and peaceful coexistence and co-operation  among States with different social and economic systems" be discussed at the General Confer-  ence mainly because it held that, at the present time, there 1s nothing more important tor the  peoples of all countries than the struggle to preserve and promote peace and to bring about the  triumph of the principles of peaceful coexistence,
(2.2) Peaceful coexistence is the alternative to war; it is the settlement of all disputes between  States through negotiation on the basis of equality of rights, mutual understanding and trust be-  tween countries, non-intervention in one another's internal affairs, and the recognition of each  people's right to independent management of its own affairs, strict regard for the sovereignty  and territorial integrity of all countries and the development of broad international co-operation  based on full equality and mutual advantage,
(2,3) The Soviet Union is convinced that the coexistence of States with different social and eco-  nomic systems is in the interests of all peoples, for not only does it deliver mankind from  the threat of thermonuclear war but it also creates more favourable conditions for the final abo-  Ution of colonialism, for economic development and social progress in newly-independent States,  and for the flowering of education, science and culture,
(2.4) It is stated in Unesco's Constitution that the purpose of our Organization is to contribute  to international peace and the general welfare of mankind by promoting collaboration among the  peoples of the whole world In regard to education, science and culture, There is nothing cas-  ual about this, for Unesco's activities and prospects of further development depend upon one  essential condition - the preservation and consolidation of peace,
(2,5) Unesco, in fact, is itself an embodiment of the principle of the peaceful coexistence of  States with different social systems, and it can and should make its contribution to the streng-  thening of this principle, Its position and decisions with regard to this matter are of greatim-  portance for various sectors of the population and primarily for the intellingentsia, who playan  essential part in the shaping of public opinion,
