Stressing the same difficulties, Mr. SHARIF (Pakistan) said that in his opinion other persons than those mentioned in Rule 22 should be authorized to sign credentials. Dr. BRULL (Cuba) pointed out that, according to the Cuban Constitution, in the absence of a Minister of State, the Under-Secretary (who was also the Head of Personnel) was authorized to sign on the Minister's behalf. He therefore thought that this delegation of signature should extend to credentials. The SECRETARYread out the list of countries in the second category: Afghanistan Hungary Australia Iran Austria Iraq Belgium Israel Bolivia Lebanon Brazil New Zealand China Pakistan Colombia Peru Cuba Turkey Egypt United Kingdom France Uruguay Haiti Venezuela On the proposal of the CHAIRMAN, the Com- mittee unanimously decided to recommend to the General Conference that it should provisionally accept the credentials of the above-mentioned delegations. Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) proposed that the Committee should meet a short time before the close of the session to examine the credentials of the delegations which would have taken the necessary steps in the meantime to comply with Rule 22. The CHAIRMAN seconded Mr. Cowell's proposal, but suggested that the meeting should not be held just before the end of the Conference. The SECRETARY announced that he had ar- ranged a third category including a number of non-Member States who had sent observers: Ceylon Korea Chile Nepal Eire Nicaragua Finland Sweden Jordan Vatican Mr. BIRECKI (Poland) having raised the ques- tion of representatives from Republican Spain, the CHAIRMAN replied that it was not for the Com- mittee to decide on the advisability of sending such and such an invitation, and M. de BLONAY (Head, Bureau of External Relations) said that, in accordance with a decision of the Executive Board, representatives of Republican Spain had been invited to follow the work of the Conference in an unofficial capacity, as guests of honour. The meeting rose at noon. SECOND MEETING Tuesday, 4 October 1949, at 9.30 a.m. Chairman: The SECRETARY stated that, at the time of the Committee’s previous report, twenty-three regu- lar ones had come in, as well as a telegram from Canberra regretting that the credentials of the Australian Delegation were delayed in the mails. In two special cases the credentials did not exactly comply with Rule 22: the French credentials were a certified copy of a document signed by the Minister of Foreign Affairs of France and the credentials of the Pakistan del- egation were signed by the Prime Minister of that country acting probably as its Foreign Professor JAkoB NIELSEN (Denmark) Minister. Both these credentials could therefore be considered as regular. The credentials of Afghanistan, Australia, Bolivia, Colombia, Egypt, Iraq, Turkey and Venezuela were quite irregular. As the Secretary was speaking, new credentials, those of Lebanon, had arrived and he submitted them to the Chairman. The CHAIRMAN pointed out that the Lebanese credentials were sufficient since they were signed by the President of the Republic with whose signature he was quite familiar.            Dr. QUDRATE-KHUDA (Pakistan) declared that he had received a telegram from Karachi that regular credentials, signed by the Foreign Minister of Pakistan, were on their way. Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) stressed that, if the present Pakistan credentials were signed by the Prime Minister who was in charge of the Foreign Ministry during his colleague’s absence in the United States, they were already sufficient. The CHAIRMAN proposed that the French, Lebanese and Pakistan credentials should be added to the list of regular ones. Agreed. The SECRETARYindicated why the credentials he had mentioned were irregular: Afghanistan had communicated a telegram from the Minister of Foreign Affairs and a letter from the Embassy; Australia, * a letter from the Embassy; Bolivia, a telegram from the Minister of Foreign Affairs; Colombia, a letter from the Minister of Foreign Affairs followed by a letter from the Embassy stating that regular credentials would follow; Egypt, a telegram from the Minister of Foreign Affairs; Iraq, a letter confirmed by a letter from the Iraqi Embassy in France; Turkey, * two successive telegrams from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and a letter from the Turkisk Embassy in Paris; Venezuela, * two telegrams from the Ministry of Foreign Affairs announcing the sending of full credentials, followed by a personal letter from Dr. Parra Pérez. The SECRETARY said that Rule 22 of the Rules of Procedure stipulated that the credentials of delegates and alternate delegates should be “issued” either by the Head of the State or by the Minister of Foreign Affairs. Moreover, although the authority issuing the telegram could always be called into question, it should be noted that the telegrams sent by the above-mentioned countries all bore the seal “Etat” or “State”, which showed that they had been issued by an official person- ality at least, and probably by the Minister who had signed them. Subject to those remarks, the Secretary agreed with the proposal by the Chairman to maintain the rights of those States provisionally. Their credentials had not been communicated in due form, but they could be considered as offering sufficient guarantees to permit the States to take part in the voting. The CHAIRMAN recalled that, at its previous meeting, the Committee had decided to draw the attention of the Conference to the fact that the Rules of Procedure were insufficient. The Secre- tariat was preparing a report on the matter, which included a suitable amendment. He thought that, since regular credentials for the eight delegations which still lacked them were announced, a definite proposal by the Committee to the Conference that the eight delegations be allowed to vote, had become a necessity. Agreed. Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) was very glad, on behalf of his Delegation, to follow the Chair- man’s lead. He declared that he was in favour of an amendment to Rule 22. Matters would be greatly simplified if one of the Under-Secretaries, Counsellors or other high permanentofficials could sign credentials on behalf of the Foreign Secre- tary. The CHAIRMAN fully agreed with Mr. Cowell, but he did not think it was the Committee’s rôle to propose such an alteration of Rule 22. The meeting rose at 10.15 a.m.
