2.1 Kr CHAMPATONG (Thailand), Chairman of Commission II:  Thank you, Mr President. It is an honour to present the report of Com-  mission II, which is the result of excellent team work among the delegations of  Member States, the members of the Secretariat and the officers of the Commission,  in particular the Rapporteur, who was totally dedicated to his task. Through you,  Sir, I wish to commend to the appreciation of the plenary all those involved for  their valuable contributions, their hard work, and above all, for the friendly  enthusiasm which permeated our meetings. This created an excellent atmosphere of  co-operation and professional understanding.
2.2 Mr President, as the delegates have the report before them and have had  time to study it, it does not seem necessary for me to make a. detailed presen-  tation. Rather, I would like to make a few general remarks.
2.3 Following from what I have just said about the friendly spirit of co-  operation, which I am sure will have left a lasting impression on all who par-  ticipated, I would like to refer to the general consensus concerning the appro-  priateness and quality of the three major programmes we examined; there was  unanimous support for the underlying themes of Education for all; the Formulation  and application of educational policies; and Education, training and society. All  the modifications made were done in a spirit of broadening the consensus achieved  by the Executive Board. Here, I should like to pay tribute to the working group  set up by the Bureau of the Commission, which greatly facilitated the Com-  mission's task by examining certain draft resolutions which had broad financial  implications. As a result of its efforts, the group was able to present a report  which met with the full agreement of the authors of these draft resolutions and  the subsequent unanimous approval of the Commission.
2.4 From my own personal point of view, I welcomed the stress that our meet-  ings laid on primary education. I also appreciated the great emphasis placed on  literacy, especially the launching of an appeal to the United Nations General  Assembly to proclaim an International Literacy Year. The strong emphasis given to  technical and vocational education, which in the current world economic situation  may pave the way towards increased productivity in many Member States, is a wel-  come one. I should like you to take note of the various references in our report  to the importance of the activities for the education of women and girls. The  increased emphasis on informatics in education is also timely.
2.5 Finally as far as my general remarks are concerned, I would like to say  how encouraging it was that despite overall financial constraints, many Member  States made offers to assist each other and to.co-operate with the Secretariat in  the execution of the programme.
2.6 Mr President, as you have already indicated, the material which Com-  mission II brings to the plenary for its consideration today consists basically  of two things - firstly, a report on our discussions and decisions; and secondly,  a series of recommendations. Because the General Conference merely takes note of  the narrative report of our work whereas it has to consider separately each of  our recommendations, I will, with your permission, Mr President, first present  the report of Commission II and then present individually our various  recommendations.
2.7 Mr President, I thus hereby invite the General Conference to take note  of the report of Commission II as contained in document 23 C/112, Parts I, II and  III together with the relevant modifications found in document 23 C/112 Add.  & Corr. Some of these modifications are merely typographical or other errors dis-  covered by the Secretariat, while the others are those decided upon by the Com-  mission when it adopted its report.
