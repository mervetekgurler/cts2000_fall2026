1.1 The PRESIDENT:  Ladies and gentlemen, I wish you a very good morning. I declare open this fifteenth  plenary meeting.
1.2 Today's agenda consists of the examination of draft resolutions pertaining to those parts of  the Draft Medium-Term Plan which were discussed here in plenary. This will be a complex and  difficult task, involving the consideration of draft resolutions concerning Major Programmes I,  'Reflection on world problems and future-oriented studies'; VIII, 'Principles, methods and ‘  strategies of action for development'; XII, 'The elimination of prejudice, intolerance, racism and  apartheid'; and XIII, 'Peace, international understanding, the freedom of the peoples and human  rights'. There are also several draft resolutions which affect the structure of the Draft  Medium-Term Plan or concern several major programmes.
1.3 However, I have some fresh proposals for you. At the fourth meeting of the General  Committee held this morning, the General Committee was informed of a very constructive initiative  taken by the African electoral group in which all groups have wholeheartedly participated. An  informal working group has been constituted under the chairmanship of Mr Iba Der Thiam. This  group was convened in view of the very large number of draft resolutions which we have before  us, some of which could involve extremely long discussions. Our timetable of work, which allows  one day for their consideration, would make it almost impossible to discuss them adequately in  plenary. Nor would there really be enough time to transfer certain draft resolutions to the  Drafting and Negotiation Group.
1.4 Mr Thiam's informal group has achieved excellent results. It has reached agreement on the  draft resolutions relating to Major Programmes I, VIII, XII. For lack of time, Major  Programme XIII has not yet been examined.
1.5 Mr Thiam has therefore proposed that the working group continue its work and that the  plenary meeting be postponed until the informal working group is ready with its proposals  concerning all the draft resolutions, including those concerning Major Programme XIII. The  informal working group would be able to make proposals to the plenary which would have been  accepted by all. This procedure is intended to gain the maximum of time, because we can expect  that the results of the informal working group, based on an exceptional spirit of compromise and  consensus, will be unanimously accepted by the General Conference.
1.6 It should be clear, however, that the proposal does not in any way call into question the  existence of the Drafting and Negotiation Group, which could indeed be asked by you to deal with  draft resolutions in respect of which the plenary might not be able to accept the proposals of the  informal working group.
1.7 The General Committee has discussed Mr Thiam's proposal and fully endorsed it.
1.8 I therefore propose that this plenary meeting be adjourned and that we meet again only at
9 a.m. tomorrow morning. This will enable the informal working group to complete its work and  prepare a report to the plenary. Before dealing with the report of the wc*Kking group, we would  examine the Report of Commission I and adopt the draft resolutions the Commission has  proposed.
1.9 Ladies and gentlemen, do you agree with my proposal? I see no opposition. Thank you very 15  much.
1.10 It is so decided.
