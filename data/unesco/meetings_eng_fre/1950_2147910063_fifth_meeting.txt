[1] In reply to the Mr. Roseman’s question  regarding exhibitions, Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau  of Administrative Management and Budget) ex-  plained that the need for an ‘exhibition was  determined on the basis of programme require-  ments. Sometimes a specific instruction was  contained in a programme resolution while on  other occasions, the Executive Board authorized  an exhibition as being necessary for the effective  fulfilment of a programme resolution. A member  of the staff of the Department of Mass Communi-  cation undertook the co-ordination necessary  within the Secretariat to prepare such exhibitions.  The best possible use was made of services within  the Organization, and outside assistance was only  contracted for in very special circumstances.  [2] In reply to Mr. WEEDEN (Australia),  Mr. THOMAS (Director, Department of Cultural  Activities) promised to supply a financial state-  ment relating to the International Theatre Institute  and an indication of the amounts and probable  sources of income of the International Music  Council.  [3] In reply to further questions, Mr. Thomas  explained that fees for the Translation of Great  Works together with publishing costs were  shared on a fifty-fifty basis by Unesco and the  Government concerned. On the basis of 450 cop-  ies, the present cost to Unesco per book trans-  lated and published was on an everage $610. In
1951, the Secretariat proposed to participate in  the translation of thirty books.  [4] For the information of Mr. POLLOCK  (Canada), Mr. THOMAS explained that Unesco  did not contribute funds or services to the actual  work of colour reproduction, but merely facili-  tated sales; in return, it was the custom of pub-
492  lishers to grant a reduction on sales made to  National Commissions through Unesco. With  regard to Museum, the present number of sub-  scribers totalled 1,100; this showed a large  increase on last year, and it was hoped to reach  the figure of 2,000 very soon.  [5] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) asked  whether it would not be possible to save the  $4,500 provided for the proposed meeting under  items 4.251 and 4.252 by asking the writers and  artists concerned to send written reports which  Unesco would circulate instead of holding a  meeting.  [6] Mr. THOMAS (Director, Department of  Cultural Activities) explained that the Fourth  Session of the General Conference had expressed  a strong desire that Unesco should assist and col-  laborate closely with the International P.E.N. Clubs,  and Unesco had been particularly requested to  organize this meeting.  [7] However, it was agreed that there was some  confusion in the wording of the resolution, which  might be clarified by the Programme and Budget  Commission.  Museums AND Historica Monuments [III.4.E].  [8] Mr. ROSEMAN (United States of America)  asked the Secretariat to supply a breakdown of  the total costs in this section as proportioned be-  tween items 4.3 (Museums) and items 4.4  (Historical Monuments).  [9] Mr. WEEDEN (Australia) also requested a  financial statement on International Council of  Museums.  [10] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom)  having remarked on the large amount requested  for travel in paragraph III (page 186), Mr. THO-  MAS (Director, Department of Cultural Activities)  explained that, although this project had been  studied and discussed by Unesco for some time,  it had not been possible before to take any specific  action. In 1951, it was proposed to implement a                                              proposal made by several Member States to bring  an exhibition of Mexican Art to Europe. [11] Replying to Mr. WEEDEN (Australia),  Mr. THOMAS explained that the phrase “or the  provision of services” in item 4.31 referred merely  to the provision by Unesco of office space and  local telephone facilities in Paris.  [12] Mr. KIRPAL (India) having asked what  form of assistance to Member States ($10,000)  was proposed under item 4,42, Mr. THOMAS si id  that, on the request of a Member State, Unesco  would send a mission of three people for three  months. He emphasized however that the re-  questing Member State would be expected to pay  the expenses of the mission during its stay.  [13] On item 4.41, Mr. SEDAR SENGHOR  (France) suggested that, instead of establishing  a new Advisory Committee, Unesco should take  advantage of the experience of one of the nor  governmental organizations already established.  In this way he thought that the necessity for  holding an annual meeting at a cost of $8,000  could be avoided.  [14] Mr. THOMAS (Director, Department of  Cultural Activities) explained that this would not  be an entirely new advisory committee. Before  the war, an International Office of Museums had  existed under the League of Nations; it was on  the experience of this body, and on a decision of  the Third Session of the General Conference, that  it had been decided to re-establish a body of this  nature. The Secretariat was convinced, more-  over, of the necessity for having an advisory body  entirely at the disposal of Unesco. A non-govern-  mental organization could not meet this require-  ment.  LIBRARIES AND DOCUMENTATION SERVICES [111.4.F].  [15] Mr. POLLOCK (Canada) raised the ques-  tion of the International Book Coupon Scheme.  His Government felt that this scheme must stand  or fall on its administrative efficiency. The  object must surely be to ensure the greatest pos-  sible distribution of books at the lowest possible  administrative cost. He would be glad of further  information as to the total cost of this scheme in
1949 and 1950, as it appeared that far too few  Book Coupons were being distributed in relation  to the high costs of planning, launching and ad-  ministering this scheme. Mr. Pollock also re-  quested information as to the disposition of the  soft-currencies received by Unesco in exchange for  Book Coupons.  [16] Mr. WEITZ (Head, Bureau of Adminis-
493  trative Management and Budget) could not agree  that the administrative costs were so extremely  high. He estimated that present overheads  amounted to approximately 3 per cent of the total  turnover, although, in view of the revolving  capital involved, it was difficult to estimate over-  heads accurately. He explained that the amount  of dollars made available by Unesco for this  scheme was fixed in consultation with the Ex-  ecutive Board on the basis of the amount of dol-  lars available to the Organization, having regard  to the hard-currency requirements of Unesco it-  self. Moreover, there was no danger that the  Organization would not, in the normal course of  its business, be able to utilize all the soft-  currency it acquired in operating this scheme.  [17] Mr. THOMAS (Director, Department of  (Cultural Activities) added that a total of four  staff members were employed on the administra-  tion of this scheme, and reminded delegates that  it had now been extended to cover not only books  but also documentary and scientific films, etc.  [18] The CHAIRMAN assured the delegate of  Canada that his remarks on this subject would be  included in the general report of the Committee  to the Programme and Budget Commission. [26] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom)  pointed out that, although his Government had  supported publication of the International Copy-  right Bulletin (item 4.61), they now felt that the  scope of the Bulletin extended far beyond the  limits originally intended and should therefore  be reduced with consequent economies. In ad-  dition, his Government felt that it should not be  necessary for Unesco to convene both the com-  mittees referred to under items 4.62 and 4.621:  the work of preparing for a Universal Copyright  Convention could be accomplished by the Secre-  tariat with the assistance of one or other of these  expert committees.  [27] Mr. THOMAS (Director, Department of  Cultural Activities) replied that there was a big  question of time and rhythm of work involved.  This was a very complicated matter and, at the  rate of work planned, he felt that it would not be  possible for the Secretariat to work without con-  sulting an expert committee in 1950 and again  in 1951. In addition, it must be remembered that  participants in such committees not only provided  the Secretariat with expert advice and opinions,  but also conveyed the views of their governments  with regard to the proposed Universal Copyright  Convention. The second committee of experts  could not be convened until after the next session  of the General Conference had given a decision  with regard to the establishment of an Inter-  Governmental Conference to prepare the text of  the Universal Copyright Convention.  [28] Sir Ronald ADAM (United Kingdom) felt  that some economies should certainly be made  under this project.  [29] It was decided to give the matter further  consideration after the Programme and Budget  Commission had reported on the programme.
