16.4 However great my pride and gratitude at  representing among you for the first time an African  country freshly admitted to the family of nations, I  shall not conceal either my astonishment or pleasant  surprise to be able to recall the time, not so long |  ago, when Léon L'Africain was able to write in all  sincerity : "The people of Tropical Africa are rural  folks, without reason, mind or skill, and not efficient  at any thing at all".
16,5 Today, fortunately, deeper knowledge of the  people of Africa has led the world to discover what  has lain hidden in our societies and in our souls in  terms of humanity and individuality, and to realize  how much they have to contribute to the enrichment  of world civilization. A new era is dawning   PLENARY MEETINGS  - your welcome {s proof of it - the era of the African  contribution to the symphony of cultures,
16.6 But before conveying the contribution of the  Republic of the Niger, let me say a few words about  the country which I represent, Small in population  - 2,800,000 inhabitants - it is large in area -
1,200,000 square kilometres, The Republic of Niger  occupies a central position between the Sahara and  the African savannah, and its geography in large part  determines its destiny,
16.7 A meeting place of Oriental and African cul-  tures, the Niger is a country where Western culture  reaches only a minute section of the population, Even  today, despite our own efforts and those of France,  only 7 children out of every 100 can go to school.  That fs an indication of the immensity of the task  awaiting us in the field of education, We have drawn  up a five-year programme which aims at raising the  achool enrolment rate to à minimum of 30%. During  this five-year period, we intend to provide every  village of more than 2,000 inhabitants with a one-  room school, eventually arriving at a one-room  school for every village of more than 1,000 inhab-  ftants, Thus we hope that, by October 1964, there  will be 120,000 children enrolled in 2,800 classes,  whereas at the present time there are 22,000 school  children divided into 610 classes,
16,8 Parallel to these measures, which will be  carried out in accordance with traditional methods,  the Niger Government has been anxious to start a  large-scale drive against illiteracy, using audio-  visual methods, particularly the radio, which is an  excellent medium in a country where the population  is so widely scattered. That drive has just begun.  Personally, I should like experts to come and pay us  a visit in a few months! time to study the results;  this would greatly benefit other countries which are  also struggling to accustom their people to using the  basic media for the communication of culture.
16,9 Another aspect of our educational programmes  to which I should like to draw attention is that of  schcol enrolment in the nomadic area. The problem  is difficult because, here, we are confronted with a  people who are constantly on the move in search of  water and pasture-land for their cattle, Noting that,  hitherto, efforts in this field have not ylelded satis-  factory results in any country, we are developing a  new idea, based essentially on the principle that  education should not be divorced from the human,  social and, especially, economic background. With  this in mind, we are planning to set up an experi-  mental community development centre in a limited  area. We shall try to persuade a nomadic group to  gettle for a time in an area well equipped with water  supplies, and help them to develop stock-breeding  possibilities to a maximum, as a means to a higher  standard of living; on these bases, we hope to build  an integrated programme of instruction and education,  In order to carry out this programme in the shortest  possible time, we intend to appeal to Unesco for  assistance.
16.10 Still in connexion with education, I should  like to recall here that my Government has presented  a programme, which is modest, of course, but desig-  ned to meet the most urgent needs; we do not think,  therefore, that its adoption will meet with any dif-  ficulties.
16,11 I also take this opportunity to present to you  a project which is not included in the supplementary  programme, but to which we attach special import-  ance: I refer to the construction in our young Re-  public of a Centre for training, information and  exchange, a Centre for research and practical work  & place where all could meet whose needs, skills  and aims converge on the same goal - the smooth  and efficient development of the arid lands of Africa.
16.12 For this vast group of countries, skirting  the South of the Sahara from East tu West Africa, is  original both in mentality and in situation. Midway  between the vast desert spaces of the North and the  tropical regions of the South, the Sahel, a marginal,  unpredictable area, which has great potentialities but  can also run counter to expectation at any moment,  presents a type of existence which 1s unique in the  entire arid zone, Let a few extra millimetres of  water fall, and life is assured for hundreds of  thousands of men and beasts, But let these few  millimetres fail to materialize, and there is famine  and desolation, Apart from the magic action of our  so-called rain-makers, it is the task of modern  science to conquer the desert, to feed man, to cure  the sick animal.
16,13 But science and technique cannot be imagined  without the education of those who are - or will be -  their first beneficiaries, Nor can they be imagined  without unity, understanding and the mutual help of  all those people linked by a common destiny and for  whom rain, fire and sun may ba sources of joy or  misery.
