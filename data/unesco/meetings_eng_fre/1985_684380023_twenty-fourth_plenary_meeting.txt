2.1 Mr LEIBBRANDT (Netherlands) :  Thank you, Mr President. I have asked for the floor to make a few obser-  vations about the report of the Legal Committee. As you may have noticed from  paragraph 9 of the report of this Committee, dealing with the establishment of  observer missions, some members could not agree with the conclusions set out in  paragraphs 7 and 8. I would like now to point out for what reasons the Nether-  lands delegation could not agree with these conclusions, that is, the conclusions  set out in paragraphs 7 and 8.
2.2 First of all, the Legal Committee was asked to deal only with the legal  aspects of the questions submitted to it. It is our opinion that the Committee  has gone beyond those aspects. There is no basis either in the Constitution or in  the bylaws of Unesco for discriminating between a State which has withdrawn from  the Organization and a State which is not yet a member. Moreover, this rule of  non-discrimination is one of the guiding principles of the Vienna Convention of
1975, which deals with the observer status of States in international organiz-  ations, and should be of guidance to us in this matter. I therefore do not agree  with the distinguished representative of Australia, who just took the floor  before me, that the Vienna convention should not be mentioned any more in this  meeting. We consider the principle of non-discrimination of paramount importance.
2.3 Coming now, Mr President, to the rights and obligations as established  in law and practice, we find the following: point one, to establish an observer  mission, notification only, not a request, is required. The Director-General  stated as much in his oral report to the 121st session of the Executive Board,  and I quote: 'the Head of the organization to whom the appointment of an observer  mission has been duly notified acknowledges receipt of this notification to the  State concerned. The mission is considered to be established once such receipt  has been acknowledged and may thereupon begin to discharge its functions'. My  second point is, Mr President, that, as regards the rights and obligations, it is  up to the Executive Board to consider the granting of facilities, in which  respect again it should be guided by the principle of non-discrimination. And  finally, Mr President, the State which has established an observer mission and  which is granted facilities should negotiate the contribution towards the payment  of those facilities. Apart from these rights and obligations, there are no  further reciprocal rights and obligations established in international law or  practice. It is for this reason that the Netherlands delegation considers the  points set out in paragraph 8 of the report of the Legal Committee objectionable  in law. And we consider it of the utmost importance that Unesco should not estab-  lish procedures with built-in road-blocks as regards the establishment of an  observer mission and which might lead to discriminatory decisions.
2.4 Mr President, I now come to the matter of the possible referral to the  International Court of Justice of the question of the payment by the Unite6  States of America of contributions for the year 1985. We have taken note of the  conclusion of the Legal Committee that there are no legal obstacles to such a  referral, but also that the International Court may give only an adivsory opinion  and that extra costs and extra time are involved. As far as the desirability of  referral to the Court is concerned, the Netherlands delegation is of the opinion  that it is not a wise thing to do. Apart from the already mentioned point that it  concerns only an advisory opinion and that extra costs are involved, we feel  strongly that it would create another obstacle for the desire to return to uni-  versal membership of Unesco.
2.5 Mr President, I am now concluding my remarks, and I would like to  reserve my right to intervene again in the debate at a later stage. Thank you  very much.

56.1 Mr MABILANGAN (Philippines) :  Thank you, Mr President, for giving me the floor at this stage: I will  probably save the time of the Conference because I shall be speaking on behalf of  other countries. I will try to be as brief as possible, as others have already  spoken extensively on the subject before us.
56.2 Let me state again that I have the honour to speak on behalf of  Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and my own country, the Philippines, as Member  States of the Association of South-East Asian Nations. We would like to commend  the Administrative Commission and the Legal Committee for having done the best  that they could in their respective reports in the light of the terms of refer-  ence given to them.
56.3 Mr President, on the question of reviewing the reciprocal rights and  obligations of Unesco and a withdrawn Member State requesting observer status, we  wish to present the following points that the General Conference may wish to take  into account. Firstly, do we want the United States to come back into Unesco?  There seems to be no question that we all do, and moreover that none of us will  be happy to see any other Member State go. Secondly, the United States, by seek-  ing observer status, has thereby manifested its continuing interest in the Organ-  ization. Such interest would and should be promoted through the contacts facili-  tated by the ex-Member State's observer status, both within the Organization and  with the other Member States. We all hope, of course, that this will lead as soon  as possible to the re-entry of the United States into the Unesco family. Thirdly,  the financial implications of a Member State's withdrawal should not be a para-  mount factor in considering the matter at hand.
56.4 Mr President, at present there is no legal text to distinguish between  observers which have not been Member States and those that have been and have  withdrawn. We believe we should not attempt to draw that distinction at this  time. Non-Member States that have requested to become observers have been allowed  to do so. A Member State that has withdrawn becomes a non-member and should be  treated like other non-Member States in the matter of observer status. Later,  perhaps, the Executive Board could draw up more definitive guidelines to confirm  the relationship between Unesco and a Member State that has withdrawn and wishes  to become an observer.
56.5 With respect to the question of seeking an advisory opinion from the  International Court of Justice, we agree that the Organization may request the  International Court of Justice to give such an advisory opinion, but is it de-  sirable at all or even necessary to do so? Has Unesco exhausted all the possi-  bilities through negotiations? What does Unesco stand to gain from an advisory
887    opinion? We believe it would be more prudent and practical for the Organization  to pursue bilateral contacts and negotiations with the withdrawn State or Member  States concerned. Many speakers have already dealt with the legal and other  implications of resorting to the International Court of Justice, and I do not  wish to go over the same ground. I merely wish to emphasize on behalf of  Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines the desirability of pursuing  bilateral negotiations.
56.6 On the question of staffing, I do not think that anyone can disagree  that staff recruitment should be governed by two general principles: equitable  geographical distribution, which is the prevailing practice; and priority for  nationals of Member States over those of non-Member States in so far as employ-  ment at Unesco is concerned. However, with respect to staff already employed at  Unesco, we concur with the view of the Director-General and other Member States  which have spoken earlier that they should be uniformly treated as international  civil servants. Therefore, in case of retrenchment, the only criteria that should  apply in determining who should be retained are those which should apply to  everyone on the staff regardless of nationality, namely, competence, loyalty to  the Organization, and actual need for their respective services. In conclusion,  allow me to state that these views represent the consensus of Indonesia,  Malaysia, Thailand and the Philippines. Thank you, Mr President.
