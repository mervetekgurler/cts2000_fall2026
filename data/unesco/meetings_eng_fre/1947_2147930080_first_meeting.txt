SUMMARY RECORDS OF THE MEETINGS OF THE NOMINATIONS COMMITTEE FIRST MEETING Thursday, 6 November 1947, at 5 p.m. Chairman : Mr. S. D. PIERCE (Canada). The meeting was opened by M. MARITAIN (France), in the presence of the heads of the dele- gations of the Member States taking part in the Second Session of the General Conference, or their representatives. The Committee proceeded to the election of its Chairman; Mr. S. D. Pierce (Canada) was elected unanimously. At the request of the Chairman, Sir John MAUD (United Kingdom) defined the role of the Executive Board in the matter of nominations. The Board had thoughtit necessary to set up a committee of five mem- bers to consider possible nominations for the offices of President and Vice-Presidents of the Conference. This committee had made certain suggestions to the Nominations Committee, with which rested the ultimate responsibility for submitting nominations to the General Conference. The committee had suggested a representative of Mexico for the office of President. Asregards the Vice-Presidencies, the committee was unanimous in its choice for six ofthe seats to be filled, proposing the representatives of the following countries : Belgium, France, India, Lebanon, Poland and the United States of America. After reminding the meeting that the General Committee of the Conference, which would meet every morning, would consist of the President and Vice-Presidents of the Conference and the Chairmen of the Commissions and Committees, Sir John MAUD (United Kingdom), communicated to the Committee the following suggestions : that the Chairmanship of the Credentials Committee should be given to a representative of Turkey; that of the Committee on Procedure to a representative of the United Kingdom; that of the Programme and Bud- get Commission to Dr. C. E. Beeby, head of the delegation of New Zealand; that of the Admini- strative and External Relations Commission to Dr. Han Lih-Wu, head of the Chinese delegation. The Committee decided, by acclamation, to nominate the representative of Mexico for the Presidency of the Conference. With regard to the Vice-Presidents, the CHAIR- MAN asked that a vote should be taken, to begin with, on the six countries whose names had first been proposed, that is : Belgium, France, India, Lebanon, Poland and the United States of America. The Committee declared unanimously in favour of these six countries and the nominations were adopted. The Committee then proceeded to the nomination of a seventh country for the office of Vice-President. Dr. MARTINEZ BAEZ (Mexico) suggested that Peru should be nominated. This proposal was supported by the delegate of EGYPT. At the request of the delegate of the UNION OF SOUTH AFRICA, the CHAIRMAN read the list of the countries which had furnished Vice-Presidents for the Conference the previousyear : Brazil, China, the Philippines, Saudi Arabia, the Union of South Africa, the United Kingdom and the United States of America. Peru was unanimously nominated for the seventh office of Vice-President of the Conference. The Committee then proceeded to the election of the Chairmen of the Commissions. — 615 —

A Committee of the Executive Board had done preliminary work to this effect, and he called on Dr. MARTINEZ BAEZ, Vice-Chairman of the Executive Board, to present the suggestions of this Committee. The delegate of the UNION OF SOUTH AFRICA rose to question the wisdom of the Committee in presenting the delegates with a fait accompli, thereby depriving them of the possibility of offering their opinion on the nominations. The CHAIRMAN remarked that Sir John Maud’s motion was simply a means of co-ordinating the vast task of nominations and left the way open for amendments on specific nominations. He also wished to raise an objection to the fact that no office had been allocated to Lebanon in the suggestions of the Executive Board Committee.
