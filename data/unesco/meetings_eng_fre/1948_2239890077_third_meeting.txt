Item 4 (f): Amendment to Financial Regu-  lations (document 3C/ADM/7). |  Financial Regulation 27.  Mr. PILE (United Kingdom) outlined to the  meeting his understanding of the proposed  amendment. The sum of money which remained  to an organization at the end of à calendar or  fiscal year divided into money for which no  obligations had been incurred—surplus money—  and money for which an obligation had been  incurred, although no money had actually been  paid out. It was possible to interpret the term  “obligation” in two senses : firstly, in the strict  sense of an expenditure to which one was  committed by a binding legal contract with a  third party and, secondly, in the sense of an  expenditure to which one was morally bound  without legal commitment. As he understood it,  the effect of the proposed amendment would be  to make available to Unesco for three years all  funds “obligated” in both senses of the world,  both to meet obligations and to incur them  within that period. On the ground that the  implementation of the amendment would in effect  lead to the creation of two or three funds quite  separate from the Budget and not subject to the  control of the General Conference, he proposed  that approval for the use of funds, “obligated” in  the wider sense of the word but not expended  in one year, should be sought from the General  Conference in the form of a re-appropriation for  the next year.  In answer to a question from Mr. HULTEN  (United States of America), M. PEISSEL  (Comptroller) conceded that it would not be  necessary to amend Regulation 27 of the Financial  Regulations as it stood, if the proposal of the  delegate of the United Kingdom were adopted.  He attempted to indicate to the meeting, however,  the difficulties with which the Secretariat was  facied in carrying out the programme and which  had led them to propose an amendment which  involved the acceptance of the term “obligation”  in its wider as well as its strictly legal sense.  Taking as an example the sending out of an edu-  cational mission, he explained to the meeting  that, without the adoption of the amendment,  the Secretariat might be faced with the alternatives  of entering into an insufficiently considered legal  contract or abandoning a project which had been  approved by the Conference. In order to facilitate  the implementation of the programme, the  Auditors had accepted provisionally in 1948 the  wider sense of the term “obligation” with reference  to programme activities, but had asked that the  Secretariat should obtain a clear definition from  the General Conference.  M. YALCIN (Turkey) was opposed to the  acceptance of the broader interpretation of  “obligation”, as it appeared in the proposed  amendment.  On the ground that it was necessary for the  proper carrying out of the decisions of the General  Conference by the Secretariat, the CHAIRMAN  asked the meeting to accept the proposed inter-  pretation of the term ‘“‘obligation” to include a  duty imposed upon the Secretariat as well as a  legal obligation incurred by it. The proposal was seconded by Mr. CAIRNS  (New Zealand) and adopted.  Item 4 (g) : Re-appropriation of certain  categories of miscellaneous income (docu-  ment 3C/ADM/12).  Mr. WEITZ (Acting Head of the Bureau of  Administrative Management and Budget) ex-  plained that the item had arisen out of a dual  problem which the Organization had encountered  during the past two years. In accordance with  the Financial Regulations and customary national  practices, miscellaneous income served to reduce  the contributions requested from Member States.  There were two activities in Unesco’s programme,  however, in which it was desirable that miscel-  laneous revenue should not be re-appropriated to  the general budget, but to the activities from                 which the income was derived. One of the  activities was the publications programme; it  was often difficult to estimate in advance what  the demands would be for certain documents,  or which documents it would be necessary to  distribute in additional numbers, owing to the  important information which they contained. It  was therefore suggested, in the draft resolution  on page 3 of document 3C/ADM/12, that a special  fund should be established to which the revenue  derived from the sales of Unesco’s publications  should be credited. The resolution further  suggested that the revenue derived from reim-  bursable loans of Unesco personnel should also be  credited to the special fund; Unesco was often  obliged to take on temporary staff and wished to  be able to pay them from the income derived  from lending staff to other Specialized Agencies.  Mr. HULTEN (United States of America) feared  that his delegation would be obliged to vote  against the resolution because, although the sums  involved were small, problems might arise if, in  future, they became larger.  Mr. PILE (United Kingdom asked whether, if  the resolution were adopted, it would prejudice  the findings of the Programme and Budget  Commission. He feared duplication of work if  the same problem was put to two different  Commissions.  The DEPUTY DIRECTOR-GENERAL agreed  that it would be wise for any decision of the  Administrative Commission on the matter to be  communicated to the Programme and Budget  Commission. Personally, however, he did not  think that such a technical problem would be  affected by the discussions of the Programme  and Budget Commission.
