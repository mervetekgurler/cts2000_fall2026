The CHAIRMAN announced that the Sub-Com- mittee would re-examine the revisions proposed to the resolution in section 28 of document 4C/8 (rev.), a redraft of which had been distributed by the Secretariat. Mr. ABRAHAM (United States of America) sug- gested that the wording in the second paragraph: Authorizes the Director-General, in consultation with the Executive Board, to provide technical assistance to non-Member States of Unesco where such assistance is expressly requested of Unesco by the Technical Assistance Board might be too precise and should be generalized to say that assistance would be provided in ac- cordance with any provisions formulated by the Technical Assistance Board. The SECRETARY pointed out that precise lan- guage was purposely used, because very definite instructions had been given by the Second Ses- sion of the General Conference in regard to ser- vices to non-Member States, and the Director- General would prefer that any authorizations and exceptions should be very definitely stated. Unesco would, of course, explain its position to the Technical Assistance Board, Unesco’s position being no doubt similar to that of the other Spe- cialized Agencies. Mr. PILE (United Kingdom) called attention to three factors which he said should be considered in the present situation: (1) that the instructions given by the Second Session of the General Con- ference referred only to the regular programme of Unesco, whereas this was a special programme;
(2) that non-Member States would probably be putting money into the project; and (3) that an anomalous situation would arise if a State applied directly to Unesco for aid in Unesco’s field and if Unesco could not act because it did not have the authority. He said that this point could be provided for in two possible ways in drafting the text: (a) by stating that Unesco would follow the common decisions adopted by the Technical Assistance Con- ference, or (b) by instructing the Director-General to provide technical assistance to non-Member States if authorized by the Technical Assistance Board. Mr. Pile considered the first method (a) to be the better. On the CHAIRMAN’s invitation, the SECRE- TARY explained that the Executive Board, in con- sidering that point, had decided that it could not
364         go as far as Mr. Pile had suggested, in view of the definite instructions given at Mexico City. He also pointed out that, at Lake Success, a vague deci- sion, or no decision at all, might be made. It was considered safer, therefore, for Unesco to draft its own resolution with the understanding that it would observe the same general policy as the other Specialized Agencies and discuss all requests received with the Technical Assistance Board. Mr. CUMMINS (Australia) suggested adding the phrase … in conformity with any principles or directives which may be promulgated by the United Nations, without further change in the wording. Mr. JAUME (France) said that if they abided by the provisions of paragraphs 4 and 5 on page 14 of document 4C/8 (rev.) under the heading “Co- ordination of Effort”, the requests for technical assistance would in most cases fall within the purview of the Secretary-General of the United Nations; and it was to be presumed that 90 per cent of the requests for assistance would be the responsibility of several Specialized Agencies. But, to meet cases in which a request would be within the exclusive domain of Unesco, he thought that they should introduce a clause releasing the Director-General from the obligation to refer it to the United Nations, and he proposed that the Secretariat should be instructed to draft such a clause. The SECRETARY stated that the view of the Director-General and of the Executive Board was that, in cases of common action with other Spe- cialized Agencies, Unesco would take parallel action with them and that, in cases of requests from non-Member States for isolated action by Unesco, a negative viewpoint would be taken, in conformity with the instructions given at Mexico City, and seeing that the non-Member State so requesting could well be expected to become a Member of Unesco. Calling attention to the phrase “in consultation with the Executive Board”, the Secretary expressed the belief that the Director- General would prefer to have this definitely stated. He then mentioned that it had been suggested to insert the words “of method” after the word “changes”, in the first paragraph of the revisions. He felt, however, that it might be better not to add this limitation, because of the possibility that at Lake Success the whole plan might be limited either geographically or in substance.
