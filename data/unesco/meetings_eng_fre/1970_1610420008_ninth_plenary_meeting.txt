6.4 The Greek concept of education and culture was founded on the invention of "logos" and  on the positive assertion of the value of man, It has come down to all of us through the  centuries as a permanent legacy of moral and spiritual strength making it imperative that
9  we inwardly strive for the humanization of man, This implies that all men have the right,  indeed the duty, to demand education, and, what is mort, education that should never at  any peint be considered completed, The process of the pertection and liberation of man  can be likened to a ladder which man is ever climbing, with always one more step to go. The technological progress of  our age, with its astounding achievements, brings in its wake the very real danger of break-  ing man's historical conscience and making him a slave-to his own achievements. 6.5 The democratization of education is an urgent obligation of the State to its citizens. It  implies the broadening and extension of the school system to afford opportunities to every-  body but it also entails a revolutionary adaptation of teaching methods and of the approach  to pupils and students, Basic education, general and vocational, should be free and com-  pulsory for as many years as it is accessible, And the means by which pupils can learn  well and learn more should be made available, But above all, the system should provide  for able and enlightened teachers, well-trained and regularly retrained, who should be in  a position to help the growing child emerge as a responsible citizen, morally and intel-  lectually equipped to take his proper place in society,
6.6 For it can be argued that the democratization of education, which requires that all  children, whatever their abilities, should be accepted and helped to grow and develop ac-  cording to their own individual potentialities, is essentially in the hands of the teacher,  For this reason the teacher, more than anybody else, has a real need for life-long educa-  tion, and must be afforded every help in his work so that he may be able to adjust his con-  tribution to the particular circumstances of his country, to the demands of his time and  the requirements of life around him. Whether education keeps up with life or not depends  not so much on the curriculum, although that must be constantly reviewed and revised, as  on the qualities of leadership that the teacher brings to his work,
6,7 Life-long education, on the other hand, which has been considered a human need since  the time of Solon and Socrates, is a much broader proposition and calls for universal im-  plementation, It is only through the right kind of education, continuously provided, thata  person can keep up with progress, can be stimulated to seek know'edge as part of the pro-  cess of self-improvement and can constructively and responsibly contribute to and enjoy  life around him; and only through the right kind of continuous education will the walls of  prejudice be pulled down, the ideals of democracy and peace be fully realized, and happi-  ness, built upon freedom and maturity, be attained through courage. For courage, as the  product of human virtues par excellence, is the justification of education, of "Paideia”".

Quite appropriately, therefore, the  draft programme methodically pursues the spiritual integration of the various disciplines  and activities relative to the scientific study and understanding of the environment, the so-  cial and human sciences and philosophy and culture. The initiatives proposed in conjunc-  tion with efforts to formulate systematic methods of promoting cultural development through  the exchange of experiences and information on an international scale, and the utilization  of our respective cultural heritzea, require special attention and support, But in order to  face up to the responsibilities that our collective enterprise entails, it is essential that na-  tional endeavours should systematically be promoted so that every country can enter into  dialogue with the others from a secure position, free of threat to itself, and to the civiliza-  tion of man, of absorption or extinction, Each one's contribution is valuable and necessary,  In order to bring about progress within a nation as well as to promote the spirit of co-
9  operation and ‘acceptance between ‘nations, it is necessary that each one of us consider  truth, whatever its source, as a national value, For deep within national tradition, you  will discover man in all his purity and spontaneity, The Greek thinker Ion Dragoumis, 50  years ago, ‘in an address to his nation, recommended: ''Return to the folk-songs, fo popu-  lar art, to the simple life of the village, in order to discover your language and soul; and  if you have inside you impetus and inspiration, with these elements, you will create every-  thing, tradition and civilization, truth and philosophy...
