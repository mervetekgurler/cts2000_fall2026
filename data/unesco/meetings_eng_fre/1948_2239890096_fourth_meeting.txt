FOURTH MEETING  Thursday, 2 December 1948, at 10.30 a.m.  Chairman: Mrs. Louise WRIGHT (United States of America)  How Unesco should improve its publications  policy and carry out its publications pro-  gramme in 1949 (continuation).  The CHAIRMAN announced that the Admini-  strative Commission had agreed to pass on  without comment to the plenary meeting of the  Conference the resolution referred to it by the  Joint Committee of Fifteen on the subject of the  scope of Unesco’s exchange of information  activities. The General Committee had ruled that  the Report of the Joint Committee should be  submitted direct to the General Conference in  plenary meeting, having first been forwarded to  the Budget Sub-Commission, in order that any  budgetary implications contained in the Report  could be considered by that body.  Resolutions appended to the Report of the  Chairman of the Sub-Committee on Publi-  cations (document 8C/OXR/18, Annex II) (con-  tinuation).  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) gave a brief sum-  mary of the discussions which had taken place  and the amendments which had been introduced  when the resolutions were considered by the  Official and External Relations Commission.  With regard to Resolution 1, the Commission had  agreed to ask the Joint Committee to consider  the whole question of the languages in which the  National Commissions Newsletter should be  published. Resolutions 4 and 5 had been slightly  amended. Endorsement of those resolutions was  sought from the Joint Committee, which was also  required to submit to the Budget Sub-Commission  any clauses containing budgetary implications.  Resolution 3 (recommendation for an enlarge-  ment in the scope of the Courter and an increase  in its production).  While warmly supporting the proposal for the  further development of the Courier (which he  regarded as the best medium of general publicity  possessed by Unesco), Mr. COWELL (United  Kingdom) thought it would be impossible for
434  the Secretariat to provide the funds necessary  for the production and transport of the millions  of copies required by National Commissions. He  asked for information on the conditions under  which the Courier had been produced in 1948.  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) pointed out that, if  the recommendation of the meeting of represen-  tatives of National Commissions were adopted (to  develop the Courier from a simple reporting organ  into an international newspaper), more funds  and more staff would be needed; at the moment,  the Courier was produced as a six to eight-page  monthly magazine in English, French and  Spanish, by one individual.  Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of America)  had spoken in favour of the resolution in the  course of the debate at the meeting of represen-  tatives of National Commissions, but proposed  that, in view of the difficult situation facing the  Budget Sub-Commission, it would be preferable  to hold over any change in the form and content  of the Courter until 1950.  Mr. HOLCROFT (New Zealand) thought that  it would be foolish, at a time when National Com-  missions were being asked to explore all possible  ways of making the general public conscious of  Unesco’s aims and activities, not to develop an  instrument already to hand for the purpose.  Whatever else had to be sacrified to the budget  cut, the proposal for improving the quality of  the Courier and increasing its distribution should  not be quashed. He hoped to see included, in  the future, articles of general interest in the fields  of education, science and culture, and suggested  that outstanding persons visiting Unesco should  be invited to contribute to it.  In answer to the CHAIRMAN, Mr. KAPLAN  (Secretariat) reported that the Courier was not  budgeted for as a separate item. However, most  of the figure budgeted for under the item  “contractual printing” (document 3C/6, Part III.  I. 2., p. 73) was devoted to the Courier. The  Budget Estimates for 1949 already provided for  a second editorial assistant to work full time on  the production of the Courter, and arrangements  had been concluded with an American printing                      (N N  &  ™   b y   Un       firm which would increase the supply of copies  of the Courier to the American public by about
500 per cent in 1949.  Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) maintained  that the problem of additional cost involved in  the resolution under discussion could be met if,  as he had already suggested, a special fund were  created, to which revenue derived from the sale  of Unesco publications was credited, for future  use in the production of Unesco publications.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa), who  deplored the fact that, in a far-distant country  like his own, it was impossible for the public to  obtain information about Unesco’s activities,  proposed that the Secretariat should cut down  the volume of its documentation by half, in order  to meet the general desire for increased production  of the Courier. He warmly supported the views  expressed by the delegate of New Zealand regard-  ing the importance of the journal.  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) pointed out that,  while the Courier, to be topical, needed to be  distributed quickly, it was difficult to find agents  willing to handle such a cheap publication.  Mr. Kaplan reminded the meeting that the  representatives of National Commissions had  rejected the proposal that Member States should  adopt their own methods of reproduction of  Unesco publications.  On the suggestion of Mr. HOLCROFT (New  Zealand), the meeting agreed to resume discussion  on that point.  Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) proposed that  the meeting should ask the Secretariat to pursue  the possibility of persuading National Commissions  to consider very carefully whether they could not  undertake to reproduce publications from master  copies supplied by the Secretariat.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa) seconded  that proposal and suggested that the recommen-  dation should not be confined to National Com-  missions, but extended to include governments of  Member States. He remarked that his further  suggestion that Member States should use micro-  film had also been rejected by the meeting of  representatives of National Commissions.  Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of America),  seconded by Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom),  proposed that, wherever feasible, National Com-  missions or national governments should arrange  with the Secretariat for the reproduction of the  Courier.  Mme SAUNTE (Denmark) and Professor CHEN  Yuan (China) raised the problem of the trans-  lation of the Courier into languages other than  French, English and Spanish.  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) thought that it  would be possible to help National Commissions  for that purpose from funds allocated under the  budget of the Public Information Bureau for 1949.  Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom) proposed a  formal resolution on that point, as follows :  That the Director-General should negotiate with  Member States and National Commissions in order to  develop plans for the wider circulation of the Courier,  and that these plans should include proposals for  facilitating the reproduction of the Courier by Member  States or National Commissions, either in the present  working languages, or in translations which they  should themselves produce. Subject to the fore-going reservations, the  Committee agreed to forward resolution 3 (i) to  the Budget Sub-Commission.  Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of America),  seconded by Mr. HOLCROFT (New Zealand) and  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa), proposed  the endorsement of resolution 3 (ii), Mr. Holcroft  making a special plea for the importance of an  improvement in the quality of the Courier. Discussion then took place on resolution 4,  together with a resolution proposed by the South  African delegation, which had been forwarded to  the Committee by the Official and External  Relations Commission.  Resolution 4 read as follows :  That, in order to provide Member States and  National Commissions with factual reports on the  Er   Po pe s  TE N  E m     development of work undertaken by the Secretariat,  the expert Committees which it summons, and by  Member States and National Commissions, the  Director-General shall produce and distribute a bi-  monthly Official Bulletin. The Committee agreed to approve resolution 4,  amended to cover the points raised by the  delegation of the Union of South Africa, and to  forward it to the Budget Sub-Commission.  Resolution 5 (Handbook of National Commis-  sions).  Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of America)  pointed out that the Handbook of National Com-  missions would contain information on the or-  ganization, officers, and method of operation of  National Commissions already established, and  should prove of great interest to Member States.  In answer to Mr. COWELL (United Kingdom),  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) informed the meeting  that it should be possible to publish the first  Handbook in 1949.  The Committee agreed to endorse resolution 5,  and to forward it to the Budget Sub-Commission,  with the recommendation that adequate bud-  getary provision should be made for the pro-  duction of the Handbook.
436  Résolution 6 «(Distribution by National Com-  missions).  While admitting that the distribution of  Unesco publications had not so far been as wide  as desired, Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) drew the  meeting’s attention to the steps being taken to  rectify that. Efforts were being continued to  intérest commercial agents in handling Unesco  publications, although that was not easy as, so  far, Unesco had very few publications suitable  for sale. It was hoped to introduce, in the near  future, a system of depository libraries—libraries  all over the world which would receive Unesco  publications and documents and act as distri-  bution centres. Attempts were also being made  to extend subscription lists for Unesco pub-  lications.  The CHAIRMAN remarked that she could not  obtain copies of Unesco publications in Chicago.  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) stated that, when  a publication was produced, the number of  copies printed was sufficient only to cover the  immediate purposes of the Organization. lt was  found impossible, with the existing budgetary  provision, to produce sufficient copies to meet  the needs of enquirers. From the Public Infor-  mation point of view that was a disaster, as it  meant that, having roused public interest, it  was impossible to provide the public with copies  of the publications. An illustration was the  publication of the Report of the Commission on  Technical Needs. Great interest in that pub-  lication had been aroused among the Press, but  in one month it was out of print. The attitude  of the General Conference to publications had  previously been such that the Director-General  had not felt justified in asking for a larger  appropriation for the item in the budget.  Mme SAUNTE (Denmark) considered that it  would be better to cut down the number of  Unesco publications in 1949 by 25 per cent, rather  than that the frustrating situation should  continue.  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) pointed out that  that would be extremely difficult, because, if  the General Conference requested the Secretariat  to undertake a particular piece of work, it was  imperative that the results of that work should  be published. He agreed that, if a Publications  Revolving Fund were established, it should be  possible to deal with the problem of insufficient  numbers of copies. ‘Paper shortage was no  longer a problem for the Secretariat. In view of the  budgetary situation, the Australian delegation did  not think Unesco could be called upon to do more. Mrs. O'HARE McCORMICK (United States of  America) stressed her belief that the solution of  the problem of how to get Unesco’s message  transmitted to the largest possible public was of  prime importance to the Organization. Hitherto  the Press had not participated in Unesco’s public  information activities in the way it should. She  proposed the holding of a Conference of ten  outstanding figures in the publication world to  advise Unesco on the best means of developing  its public information policy.  That motion was seconded by Mr. ROBERTS  (Union of South Africa).  Mr. KAPLAN (Secretariat) welcomed the  suggestion of the delegate of the United States  of America. General Conferences had not so far  understood the importance of Unesco’s public  information work, even though Unesco depended  for its very survival upon public opinion.  Nevertheless, he was not discouraged, because he  thought that the Secretariat of Unesco was  evolving in such a way that the Public Infor-  mation Bureau would soon be acting as the  technical centre of a vast distributor of infor-  mation, which would be Unesco itself. He  thought that the conference proposed could be  included under item 2.235 of the Programme :  In the development of the press work of the Mass  Communication Projects Division, the Director-  General is instructed to consult with a Press and  Publication Committee of experts from organizations  of publishers and journalists.  Mme SAUNTE (Denmark) pointed out that the  resolution had been changed so that it covered  the work of the whole Organization.  The decision was approved.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa) and  Mr. EISENHOWER (United States of America)  thought that, if such a conference were held,  experts would be prepared to serve without pay. Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa) pointed  out that a great opportunity was being lost, at  Unesco conferences, for personal contacts between  specialists, because the Secretariat did not supply  information on delegates’ background. In view of the discussion which had taken  place in the Administrative Commission on the  Joint Committee’s resolution relating to the  re-organization of Unesco’s Department of  Exchange of Information, Mr. ROBERTS (Union  of South Africa) asked for clarification of how  the resolution should be interpreted.  The CHAIRMAN said that, in drawing up the  resolution, the Committee had wished to secure  an investigation into the activities of the  Department, and had expressed a desire to see  services re-allocated rather than reduced.  Mr. ROBERTS (Union of South Africa) was  satisfied with that explanation; he did not wish  protracted discussion on the resolution to take  place in plenary meeting.
